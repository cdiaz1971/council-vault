# Housing Production Target Memo  
  
## Executive Summary  
  
The proposed [[Housing Production Target Resolution]] establishes:  
  
- An annual production goal of **1,400 housing units per year through 2035**  
- A baseline affordability requirement of **15% of units at ≤80% AMI**  
- Within that, **33% of affordable units at ≤60% AMI** (effectively 5% of total units)  
  
These figures align with documented local housing need, regional inclusionary housing norms, economic feasibility research, and established housing finance structures.¹²³  
  
---  
  
## I. Rationale for 1,400 Units Per Year  
  
The City's Housing Needs Assessment projects demand for approximately:  
  
- **13,774 additional housing units by 2035**  
- Approximately **1,377 units annually**  
- About **8,133 cost-burdened households** earning below $75,000 annually  
  
The 1,400-unit annual target aligns with documented demand projections and supports Maryland's housing target framework.⁴  
  
---  
  
## II. Rationale for 15% at ≤80% AMI  
  
National inclusionary housing programs commonly require:  
  
- **10%–20% affordability**  
- Targeting households at **60%–80% AMI**  
  
Examples include:  
  
- Montgomery County, MD: 12.5%–15%  
- Fairfax County, VA: 12.5%  
  
The 80% AMI baseline captures workforce households such as:  
  
- Teachers  
- Nurses  
- Public employees  
  
Research indicates deeper affordability mandates can reduce production unless offset by subsidies or density incentives.²³  
  
---  
  
## III. Rationale for 33% of Affordable Units at ≤60% AMI  
  
Requiring one-third of affordable units at 60% AMI:  
  
- Ensures measurable deep affordability  
- Preserves financial feasibility  
- Aligns with the **Low-Income Housing Tax Credit (LIHTC)** program  
- Matches state subsidy structures  
  
Tiered affordability models are widely used to balance production and depth.¹  
  
---  
  
## IV. Supply–Price Relationship  
  
Economic research consistently finds:  
  
- Housing supply constraints increase prices across income levels  
- Maintaining production volume reduces long-term rent growth pressure  
  
Production is therefore a core affordability strategy.³  
  
---  
  
## V. Preservation and Anti-Displacement  
  
Preserving naturally occurring affordable housing (NOAH):  
  
- Is often more cost-effective than new subsidized construction  
- Reduces displacement risk  
- Complements new housing production  
  
The framework therefore combines **production expansion with preservation strategies**.⁶
