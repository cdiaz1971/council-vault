# Progressive Policy Ideas Master Note

Index of progressive policy ideas screened against Frederick's actual city authority, budget posture, and political risk. Source material is ProGov21's policy roadmaps (Immigration, Food, Child Care, Job Quality, Housing, Public Health, Equity in All Policies) plus prior council research on affordability beyond housing. Each entry below gets its own note when there's bandwidth to develop it; this note tracks the list and the reasoning for inclusion.

## Near-term, low-lift

### [[Language Access Ordinance]]
City authority, no preemption risk, no new revenue required. Requires city agencies to survey language needs and provide accessible oral/written services. Directly targets District 2 demographics (foreign-born population 1.4x county rate). Saint Paul and Madison have working models.

### [[Double Up Food Bucks - SNAP Matching at Farmers Markets]]
Doubles SNAP/EBT value at farmers markets. Invests in resident purchasing power rather than mandating anything from retailers, avoiding the small-grocer pushback risk identified on Golden Mile. Pairs with the existing YMCA produce market (FEED grant).

### [[NourishMD Connector Role for Golden Mile Retailers]]
No city dollars, no ordinance. Routes existing small grocers and markets to a state capital grant program ($25k-150k per business) most likely don't know exists. Direct investment in businesses already serving the corridor rather than competing with them.

### [[Responsible Bidder Ordinance]]
Applies only to money the city already spends through procurement. Requires contractors to comply with labor law, participate in apprenticeship programs, pay fair wages. No preemption exposure, no new revenue needed.

## Medium-term, tied to active threads

### [[Zoning for Farmers Markets, Food Co-ops, and Food Hubs]]
By-right zoning for farmers markets, food cooperatives, mobile markets, and food hubs in underserved areas. Folds directly into the LMC reform process already underway -- this is the concrete zoning language version of "name food access as a criterion in LMC deliberations." Burlington, Fresno, and Minneapolis have model code.

### [[Reserved-Slot Childcare Contracts]]
City contracts directly with existing providers for a guaranteed block of slots, paid whether or not the seat is filled in a given month -- the model that actually unlocks new provider capacity rather than just subsidizing existing capacity. Texas's contracted-slots framework is the closest working model. Blocked until county childcare waitlist data comes back; targeted for FY28.

### [[Shared Services Alliance for Home-Based Childcare Providers]]
Partnership structure where in-home and small-group childcare providers pool costs for purchasing, admin, and training. Complements the zoning audit already queued for LMC reform -- opens new capacity rather than subsidizing existing capacity. Seattle's Sound Child Care Solutions and Fairfax's Mixed Delivery program are the cited models.

### [[Community Benefit Agreement Framework for Data Center Approvals]]
Ties directly into the active data center opposition fight. If a project clears the Planning Commission, a CBA turns the fight into negotiating leverage -- wage floors, local hiring, union rights -- rather than pure opposition. Milwaukee's Deer District CBA is the model. Already have a vault note on data center CBA models generally.

### [[Local Food Preference in City Procurement]]
Gives local, sustainable food producers preference in competing for municipal procurement dollars. Could ride alongside the Responsible Bidder Ordinance in the same procurement reform rather than being a separate legislative fight. Cleveland is the cited model.

## Leverage for active legislation

### [[Rent Escrow Account Program - Alternative Enforcement Mechanism]]
LA's REAP model: tenants pay reduced rent into city-held escrow when landlords fail to remediate code violations; city can fund remediation itself and recoup costs via lien. Worth raising with Rachel Gray as an enforcement mechanism that doesn't depend on the private right of action she already stripped from the Real Rent Ordinance draft.

### [[Anne Arundel County Landlord Licensing Precedent]]
Anne Arundel mandates landlord licenses as a prerequisite in the eviction process -- a real Maryland county precedent using licensing as leverage rather than treating it as a cost to minimize. Ammunition against Brehm's rental licensing rewrite, which proposes fee reduction on a pass-through-to-renters rationale.

## Screened out (tracked here so we don't re-litigate)

- **Universal/subsidized child care funded by sales tax or property tax levy** -- no authority; city can't change sales tax.
- **Local minimum wage / paid sick leave ordinances** -- likely state preemption; unverified whether Frederick (vs. a charter county) has any carve-out authority. Check with Gray before treating as live.
- **"Healthy baseline" licensing mandate for food retailers** -- regulatory mandate on existing small grocers, the exact dynamic we're trying to avoid on Golden Mile.
- **Legal defense fund / universal representation for immigrants facing deportation** -- already decided against for this budget cycle (Immigrant Legal Services Appropriation shelved at FY2027 close).
- **K-12 sensitive locations protection from ICE** -- FCPS/county school board authority, not city council; a supportive resolution is the only lever available and it's symbolic.
- **Reproductive health roadmap generally** -- different domain (rights protection, not affordability); worth its own thread alongside CVA/immigration work if opened, not folded in here.
- **City-run or city-sponsored grocery store** -- screened out after reviewing the actual Golden Mile retail landscape; risks direct competition with existing small, mostly immigrant-owned markets.
