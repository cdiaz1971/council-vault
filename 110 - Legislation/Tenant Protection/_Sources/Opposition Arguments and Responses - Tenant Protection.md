# Opposition Arguments and Responses - Tenant Protection

## What Rockville's landlords actually said

The best preview of Frederick's opposition is the record from Rockville, where the same package was debated in 2025 and 2026. When the draft was introduced in May 2026, landlords said:

- **The law is biased toward tenants.** This was the general objection.
- **They opposed the two-year lease offer.** They mainly wanted to keep the ability to raise rent mid-term. Rockville answered by allowing increases during a two-year lease only if they are written into the lease up front.
- **They opposed the eviction-history question ban.** Some suggested a look-back limit instead of a full ban.
- **They opposed tenants making repairs without landlord approval.** Rockville answered by making repair and deduct a City-authorized step after the landlord misses a deadline.
- **They raised relocation costs and who decides fault.** Rockville answered by excluding conditions the tenant caused and natural disasters.
- **They asked to keep returned check fees and higher fees for repeat lockouts.** Rockville allowed bounced check and credit card processing fees.

**RealPage** submitted written testimony against the algorithmic pricing ban. Rockville responded by limiting the ban to devices that use non-public competitor data. RealPage has sued other cities, including Berkeley, over broader bans.

**Tenants**, for their part, were disappointed the package left out rent stabilization and said six months of displacement before permanent relocation help kicks in is too long.

**Lesson for Frederick:** Rockville passed its package by answering each specific objection with a design fix, not by debating in the abstract. Frederick's draft should include those fixes from the start, so the same objections have less to grab onto.

## Who is likely to oppose

**Organized landlord and real estate interests.** Expect opposition from the Maryland Multi-Housing Association, which represents large apartment owners and managers statewide; local realtor and property manager groups; and large out-of-area owners of Frederick complexes. Expect them to coordinate on testimony and to focus on cost, small landlords, and the claim that the City is overreaching its authority.

**Software and screening vendors.** RealPage, which has a record of testimony and lawsuits, and possibly tenant screening companies affected by the limits on using eviction records.

**Small local landlords.** These are the most sympathetic opponents. Their concerns are paperwork, the two-year lease, relocation exposure, and fair chance screening if it applies to them.

**The Administration.** The Mayor's relationship with you is strained, and housing has been a point of difference. Expect questions about staffing, cost, and timing, and possibly a push to wait for the licensing rewrite.

**Pressure from the left.** Tenant advocates who want rent stabilization or good cause eviction may call the package too weak.

## Arguments and responses

**"This will raise rents."**
- Law-abiding landlords face almost no new cost under the package. The costly provisions (refunds, repair and deduct, relocation, penalties) apply only to landlords who break the law.
- Fees moving into base rent makes the price visible and comparable. Hidden fees cost buyers more. The StubHub experiment found people spent 21 percent more when fees appeared at checkout.
- Laws much stronger than this one, such as good cause eviction, show no effect on new construction (University of Minnesota, 2025) or on supply (Seattle and New Jersey just cause laws).
- **Don't** promise lower rents. Promise honest prices, fair access, safe homes, and help when enforcement forces a move.

**"Landlords will leave the market."**
- Rockville's economic analysis judged the package roughly neutral overall.
- The evidence on supply loss comes from rent control (San Francisco, 1994 expansion), which this package doesn't include.
- Montgomery County, Prince George's County, Takoma Park, and Rockville all regulate rentals more heavily than Frederick and still have active rental markets.

**"State law already covers this."**
- The package covers what state law doesn't.
- State criminal screening rules skip landlords with five or fewer units.
- State law expressly allows local lease content and local notice protections.
- State remedies require the tenant to go to court. The package lets the City enforce them.
- Deposits, the late fee cap, and court procedure stay with the state.

**"This is anti-landlord" or "this is biased."**
- Rockville's Commission gives landlords two of seven seats, and Montgomery County's gives them four of twelve voting seats.
- A landlord who advertises honestly, fixes things on time, and treats applicants fairly will barely notice this law.
- The people who lose under it are competitors who undercut honest landlords with hidden fees and deferred repairs.

**"Small landlords can't comply."**
- The City provides the disclosure form, model lease, and translations for free.
- Disclosure takes minutes per lease.
- The heavier provisions can include small-landlord paths, like Minneapolis's choice between City screening criteria and an individualized review, or Rockville's one-unit exemption from the algorithmic ban.
- The fair chance extension, the one rule aimed squarely at small landlords, is severable.

**"The two-year lease locks landlords in."**
- It is an *offer*. The tenant may decline, and the landlord may offer a shorter term for a stated reasonable cause.
- Any increase can be scheduled in the lease up front.
- Turnover is expensive for landlords (vacancy, cleaning, marketing), and a two-year tenant is a predictable one.

**"Banning eviction-history questions hides bad tenants."**
- Most Maryland eviction filings are collection tools that end with the tenant paying and staying. More than 400,000 were filed in FY2023.
- A filing the tenant won or that was dismissed says nothing about risk.
- The package still lets landlords consider actual eviction judgments within a look-back period (the Philadelphia model). Rockville's landlords asked for exactly this compromise.

**"Tenants will make unauthorized repairs."**
- They can't under this package. Repair and deduct happens only after a City inspection finds a violation, the landlord misses the City's deadline, and the City authorizes a licensed contractor.

**"Relocation makes landlords pay for things that aren't their fault."**
- Relocation is owed when a unit is declared unfit or the landlord loses a license. Both are the landlord's responsibility.
- Natural disasters and tenant-caused conditions are excluded.

**"The City can't afford to enforce this."**
- Licensing revenue ran about $1.73 million against a $300,000 projection in FY2026 through the third quarter.
- Seattle funds three enforcement staff with a fee of about $11 per unit.
- Effective dates are staged so staffing comes before the heavier provisions.
- The lien protects the City's relocation advances. Minneapolis recovered only a quarter without one.

**"Wait for the licensing rewrite."**
- Renters shouldn't wait on a separate bill with its own timeline.
- The package is drafted to work with whatever licensing structure the Council adopts.
- The package also fixes the coverage gap that licensing leaves open (the eight-year certificate of occupancy exemption).

**"This exceeds the City's authority" or "Aleti."**
- The package avoids a private lawsuit entirely. Remedies run through City orders, license conditions, municipal infractions, and liens.
- Two Maryland municipalities, Rockville and Takoma Park, already use these tools.
- The provision with the most authority risk is a commission that awards money, and it can start as a hearing officer or come in a later phase. See [[Maryland Legal Baseline and Local Authority - Tenant Protection]].

**"Government shouldn't interfere in private contracts."**
- Maryland already shapes every lease: it bans confession of judgment, caps deposits, and requires the Bill of Rights to be attached.
- Contracts work when both sides know the terms and can enforce them.

**From allies: "Why no rent stabilization?"**
- There aren't five votes to override a veto on it this term.
- The package builds the data a future stabilization debate needs: rent history disclosure and unit-level rent and eviction reporting at license renewal.
- Good cause eviction is a 2027 General Assembly ask.
- Passing what can pass now is how the next step becomes possible.

## Lines to use

- "The price is the price. What you're quoted is what you pay."
- "Know before you sign."
- "A fair shot at a home, and a fair chance to stay in it."
- "When a landlord breaks the law, the City makes it right, not the tenant, and the family doesn't end up on the street."
- "Honest landlords will barely notice this law."
- "Rockville did this in July. Frederick renters deserve the same."

## Lines to avoid

- Claiming the package will lower rents.
- Calling it rent control, or letting opponents call it that without a response.
- Attacking landlords as a group. The package's frame is honest landlords versus bad actors.
- Citing figures marked "verify" in the research note until they are confirmed.
