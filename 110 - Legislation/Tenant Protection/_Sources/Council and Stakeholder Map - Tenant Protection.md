# Council and Stakeholder Map - Tenant Protection

Leanings below are working judgments from vault profiles and past discussions, not commitments. Confirm each one in conversation.

## The vote count

There are seven Council members. **Five votes override a mayoral veto**, so five is the real target, not four.

| Member | Seat | Likely lean | What they care about | How to approach |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| César Díaz | President, District 2 | Sponsor | | |
| Katie Nash | Vice President, District 1 | Likely yes | Housing, "responsible growth," practical budgeting | Early briefing. Current tension with Hempel Irani and Shackelford after the ethics complaint, so keep the package separate from that fight. |
| Sarah Hempel Irani | District 5 | Likely yes | Most progressive member. Immigrant inclusion, has named rent stabilization. Carrying the rental licensing rewrite in committee. | Co-sponsor candidate. Coordinate early on definitions and coverage so the two bills fit together, and so she sees the package as complementing her bill, not competing with it. |
| Libby Taylor | At-Large | Likely yes | Policy implementation: "what does this actually produce for residents." LMC modernization. | Lead with outcomes and the measures section. Her Medicaid policy background fits the fiscal and implementation framing. |
| Peter Brehm | District 3 | Lean yes, with concerns | Affordable housing, sustainability. Member of the Mayor's Affordable Housing Task Force. Drafted the earlier licensing rewrite and has argued licensing is already overburdened. | Show that enforcement runs on existing licensing with staged dates and dedicated funding, not a new bureaucracy. |
| Derek Shackelford | At-Large | Swing | Reliable on equity and housing, but called affordability "complex" and didn't endorse rent stabilization. Focus on enforceability and landlord impact. Member of the Affordable Housing Task Force. | Lead with simple compliance for honest landlords, small-landlord paths, and Rockville's precedent. Equity data (ALICE, children in rentals) matters to him. |
| Joe Adkins | District 4 | Swing to no | Retired 35-year City planner, sole Republican, self-described moderate. Supply-side on housing, no record on renter protections. Focus on process and fiscal responsibility, and equity for neighborhoods outside downtown. | Technical briefing on administration, staffing, and costs. His code enforcement background makes the repair deadlines and inspection pieces familiar ground. Don't count on his vote. Treat a fair hearing from him as a win. |

**Path to five.** Díaz, Nash, Hempel Irani, and Taylor are the likely base. Brehm is the most likely fifth vote, and Shackelford is the sixth that makes it comfortable. The package's severable structure is what lets Brehm and Shackelford amend the edges and still vote yes.

## Community allies to bring in

**Legal and housing services**
- Maryland Legal Aid (Frederick office)
- The Access to Counsel in Evictions provider for Frederick
- Beyond Shelter Frederick, which runs emergency rental assistance
- The Housing Authority of the City of Frederick (HACF), for its residents and voucher holders

**Immigrant and community organizations**
- Centro Hispano de Frederick
- Spanish Speaking Community of Maryland
- Asian American Center of Frederick
- RISE Coalition of Western Maryland
- Mobilize Frederick
- Golden Mile Alliance, which covers renters on the Golden Mile corridor

**Statewide advocates**
- Maryland Renters Alliance and the Public Justice Center, who have worked on state tenant law and can testify

**Faith communities.** Several council members have church ties, and faith voices can reach the swing votes.

**Renters themselves.** Stories from Frederick tenants about hidden fees, screening denials, slow repairs, and displacement after a license action will carry more weight than any study. Collect a few before the workshop. Spanish-language listening sessions would build the case for the translation rule.

## Likely opponents

- The **Maryland Multi-Housing Association** and large apartment owners and managers.
- **Local realtor and property manager groups.**
- **RealPage** and other pricing software vendors, over the algorithmic ban in Real Rent.
- **Small local landlords.** They are sympathetic and should be met with, not dismissed.
- Possibly the **Administration** on timing and cost.

Arguments and responses are in [[Opposition Arguments and Responses - Tenant Protection]].

## Peer contacts

**Ryan Trout**, Director of Housing for the City of Rockville and a Frederick resident, shared Rockville's Chapter 18 model. He is the best source for:

- how Rockville handled landlord objections
- staffing plans
- the relocation amounts the City Manager must set
- early implementation after January 1, 2027

He also proposed the $15 million Frederick Housing Accelerator Fund to the Government Operations Committee in September.

## Suggested order for organizing

1. **Hempel Irani and Nash.** Settle how the package fits with the licensing rewrite and get early co-sponsorship. Talk to them separately given the current tension.
2. **Taylor.** Walk through outcomes and measures.
3. **Clara Jackson.** Assign follow-up research on the parts you've chosen and start the renter landing page and handbook.
4. **Ryan Trout.** Get Rockville's implementation lessons before staff raise capacity objections.
5. **Brehm and Shackelford.** Brief them once the coverage and funding answers are solid.
6. **Staff briefing** with HHS, code enforcement, Finance, and legal together, so capacity questions get answered in one room.
7. **Community partners.** Brief them and collect renter stories.
8. **Adkins.** Technical briefing.
9. **A workshop** on the full package. Real Rent was workshopped July 9, so this would cover the expanded version.
