# Tenant Protection Ordinance Outline

This is a planning outline, not a draft. It lists the concepts, subject areas, and design questions for one tenant protection ordinance with the Real Rent Ordinance at its core. Ordinance language gets written with César in chat. Status lives in the [[Council Tracker]].

## 1. Concept

**One package, three promises:**
1. **The price is the price.** What a renter is quoted is what they pay (Real Rent).
2. **The rules are knowable.** A renter can see the terms, the history, and their rights before signing, in a language they read.
3. **The rights are enforceable.** Violations produce refunds and consequences without each tenant having to sue.

**Working title options:** Frederick Renters' Rights Ordinance, Real Rent and Renter Protection Ordinance, or Fair Renting Ordinance. Keeping "Real Rent" in the title preserves the recognition already built at the July 9 workshop.

**Framing for the vote.** Real Rent's frame carries the whole package: it does not tell landlords what to charge, it tells them to be honest about it and to follow rules other Maryland cities already use. Nearly everything in the core package is disclosure, process, or enforcement, not price regulation. Rockville's adopted Chapter 18 is the proof that a Maryland municipality can do this.

## 2. Design principles

- **Real Rent is the spine.** Every other part attaches to Real Rent's definitions and enforcement article rather than standing alone.
- **Build on state law, do not repeat it.** Maryland already covers deposits, application fee basics, late fee caps, criminal screening for larger landlords, entry notice, mold, AC in larger buildings, abuse-based lease termination, retaliation, and self-help eviction (see [[Tenant Protections]] Section 2). The ordinance should extend coverage, close gaps, or add enforcement, and otherwise point to state law.
- **Cover every rental unit.** Real Rent Revision 1: no carve-out for buildings whose certificates of occupancy are eight years old or newer. The newest buildings are where fee stacking is worst.
- **Enforcement that survives *Aleti*.** Administrative restitution, license conditions, municipal infractions (now up to $5,000 under Chapter 570 of 2025), records presumptions, and liens, rather than a locally created private lawsuit. Legal risk is laid out below for César to weigh.
- **Language access is built in, not added later.** Model forms and notices in the most common languages in Frederick.
- **Fund the enforcement.** Name a responsible department, staff, a reporting date, and a revenue source.
- **Equity lens.** Renters at the ALICE threshold, immigrant households, renters with records, seniors, and people with disabilities benefit most from disclosure and fair process. Flag any provision whose compliance cost is likely to pass through to rent.

## 3. Vehicle and structure (options)

| Option | What it means | Tradeoff |
|---|---|---|
| A. New article(s) in Chapter 12.5 | Real Rent becomes Article VII (Rodent Control now holds Article VI), with further articles for screening, leases, relocation, organizing, data, enforcement | Keeps everything tied to licensing. Licensing exemptions must be overridden for coverage. |
| B. New chapter, "Landlord-Tenant Relations" | A standalone chapter modeled on Rockville Chapter 18 that cross-references licensing | Cleanest long-term home. Bigger lift and more renumbering. |
| C. Real Rent now, package as a second ordinance | Pass Real Rent on its current timeline, introduce the rest separately | Protects Real Rent's timing. Two campaigns instead of one. |

**Questions for César:** which option; whether to wait for or work around Council Member Hempel Irani's licensing rewrite (the Affordability Agenda currently excludes it); and whether expanding scope is worth pushing Real Rent's First Read past December 2026.

## 4. Proposed parts

Each part lists what it would cover, where the idea comes from, what Maryland already does, and open questions. Tiers: **Core** (in the first package), **Strong add** (include if the vote count holds), **Optional or later**.

### Part 1. General provisions (Core)
- Findings: Frederick rental data (ALICE share, cost burden, vacancy rate), fee prevalence, the market-failure argument for all-in pricing (Seattle's recitals are a model), algorithmic pricing exposure, displacement during enforcement.
- Purposes, including "housing stability and tenant agency" (Rockville § 18-2).
- Definitions: Covered Unit (all rental units with narrow exceptions), Total Rent, Mandatory Fee, Optional Fee, Variable Fee, Algorithmic Device, Non-Public Competitor Data, Tenant Organization, Displaced Tenant, Fit for Occupancy (tied to RP § 8-212), Written Notice.
- Applicability, state-law-prevails clause, liberal interpretation, no waiver of rights, severability, effective dates.

### Part 2. Real Rent: pricing and fees (Core, the existing ordinance plus revisions)
- Everything in [[Real Rent Ordinance]] with the seven fixes in [[Real Rent Ordinance Revisions]]: universal coverage, administrative restitution instead of a private action, one-sentence anti-circumvention rule, algorithmic pricing ban, utility and RUBS rules, application fee alignment with RP § 8-213, renumbering.
- **Additions from peer law:**
  - Closed list of permitted mandatory fees, with any other fee presumptively prohibited (Seattle 7.24.040.A, Rockville § 18-163(c)).
  - Optional fees only with written informed opt-in, free opt-out at any time, at cost when a third party provides the service, and unbundling of bundles (Seattle, Rockville).
  - Banned fees: paying rent by non-card methods, services previously included, landlord duties and repairs, common-area access, mail and packages, roommate changes, and fees for preparing or serving notices (Rockville, Seattle 7.24.034).
  - No charge billed more than 120 days late (Rockville).
  - Fees increase at most once per 12 months with 90 days' notice (Rockville).
  - Refund of anything collected above lawful rent and fees (Rockville).
  - Pet fee and deposit limits (Rockville monthly fee plus escrowed deposit, or Seattle's deposit-only rule).
  - Base rent only in nonpayment cases already applies under state law. Consider a rule that payments apply to rent before fees (National Tenants Bill of Rights).
- **State overlap:** late fees (5% cap) are state law, so leave the cap alone and ban notice fees instead. Deposits stay out.
- **Open questions:** Takoma Park Chapter 6.16 as the closest in-state fee model to compare; whether to cap specific fees (key, lockout) or limit them to actual cost.

### Part 3. Transparency before and during the lease (Core)
- A standard disclosure of no more than two pages, before the first page of every lease and with every listing: rent, concessions, utilities, every fee, total monthly cost with estimated utilities (Seattle 7.24.039). City model form, translated.
- Advertised prices include all mandatory fees and say whether utilities are included (Rockville § 18-180(b)).
- **Three years of rent history** for the unit and the building's average annual increase (Rockville § 18-180(d)).
- Code citations in the last 12 months, pending code or court actions, utility shutoff notices, and foreclosure filings (Chicago RLTO 5-12-095, 5-12-100).
- Notice of ownership change, a 24-hour local contact, and a copy of the rental license (Rockville §§ 18-116, 18-117, 18-127).
- Proposed lease two days before signing, executed copy within three days, renewal offer rent matches the lease (Rockville § 18-147).
- Annual updated disclosure and a monthly statement of variable charges (Seattle).
- **State overlap:** state law already requires lease contents, RUBS disclosures, and prior-year utility totals for landlord-billed utilities, and says local jurisdictions may require additional lease content. That sentence is the hook for this part.

### Part 4. Fair applications and screening (Strong add)
- No eviction-history question on applications (Rockville § 18-149(b)).
- Written screening criteria available before applying, written reasons on denial, and a chance to correct errors (National Tenants Bill of Rights, Seattle).
- Written reason when a guarantor is required (Rockville).
- Accept portable screening reports without a fee (state law allows it at landlord option; consider requiring it).
- Extend the state conditional-offer criminal screening process to landlords with five or fewer units, or adopt Montgomery County's Housing Justice Act limits (misdemeanor look-back, excluded minor offenses).
- **State overlap:** source-of-income discrimination, subsidy-holder credit protections, and conditional offers for larger landlords are already state law. Local value is coverage of small landlords, transparency, and enforcement.
- **Open questions:** whether a fair chance extension draws more opposition than it is worth in round one; how to verify compliance (Rockville checks standard applications at license renewal).

### Part 5. Lease terms and leaving early (Strong add)
- Offer of a two-year lease with a challengeable reasonable-cause exception, and any mid-term increase disclosed in the lease up front (Rockville § 18-146(1)).
- One rent increase per 12 months for a continuing tenant (Rockville § 18-147(d)).
- Required lease content: a short Frederick renter rights summary, City contacts, the City's inspection right.
- **Translation:** when a lease is negotiated in another language, a full written translation before signing (California Civil Code model). Directly serves households protected by the [[City Values Act]].
- Banned terms beyond state law: attorney fees unless court-awarded, reasonable, and reciprocal, and not treated as rent for redemption (Rockville, Montgomery County); court costs above actual; possibly mandatory arbitration and class action waivers (National Tenants Bill of Rights).
- **Early termination** on 30 days' notice, charge capped at one month's rent: job relocation, unit unfit for 60 days, license suspended or revoked, unrepaired health and safety violations, death of a primary wage earner, change in disability status, inpatient treatment, other cause beyond the tenant's control (Rockville § 18-146(18)). Add: offer of an affordable or subsidized unit, and seniors moving to care or senior housing (Clara's survey, New York RPL § 227-a).
- **State overlap:** abuse-based termination and military reassignment are already state law.

### Part 6. Repairs and habitability (Strong add)
- **City-authorized repair and deduct:** after a missed correction deadline on a City inspection, the City authorizes the tenant to use a licensed contractor and deduct up to two months' rent (Rockville § 18-115(g)). Runs through the City, not a private claim.
- Response-time standards by severity (Montgomery County: 24 to 48 hours for life-threatening, 15 to 30 days otherwise, faster for repeat violations).
- Maintenance and repair history disclosed to new tenants (National Tenants Bill of Rights).
- A local procedure for enforcing the state mold law, which passed without implementation funding (Rockville staff report).
- **Optional:** cooling standard for buildings under 10 units (state AC rule covers 10+), radon testing for basement and ground-floor units (Rockville § 18-182).
- **Open question:** what Frederick's property maintenance code already requires for heat, cooling, locks, and pests (verify before drafting).

### Part 7. Displacement and relocation (Strong add, possibly phased)
- **Temporary relocation** when the City finds a unit unfit: hotel, per diem, or comparable housing, plus moving and storage, owed even when the tenant is behind on rent (Rockville § 18-178(b)).
- **Permanent relocation** on license revocation when the owner stops renting, long displacement, or redevelopment that forces out lower-income tenants: deposit with interest, prorated rent, three months of fair market rent, moving costs, and an added amount for seniors, people with disabilities, and families with children (Rockville § 18-178(c)).
- City-paid relocation costs billed to the owner and collected as a **lien** if unpaid. Minneapolis recovered only about a quarter without one.
- Right to return on substantially similar terms.
- Connects to the post-revocation stewardship item in [[Housing -- In-Progress Plans]] (receivership or nonprofit/CLT purchase right).
- **Open questions:** this touches licensing Article V, which is the subject of the separate licensing rewrite; a relocation dollar amount tied to HUD Fair Market Rent for Frederick; whether the City fronts payments and from what fund.

### Part 8. Tenant organizing and anti-retaliation (Strong add)
- Stated rights to organize, meet, distribute information, and act collectively, independent of management (Rockville § 18-161).
- Free use of meeting rooms and common areas for tenant organization meetings; posting and distribution rights; access for outside organizers invited by tenants (Rockville staff recommendation, National Tenants Bill of Rights).
- Tenant organizations may file complaints on behalf of members who authorize it (Rockville § 18-161(d)).
- Local anti-retaliation covering harassment, privacy violations, service cuts, and unreasonable increases, with a presumption of retaliation for adverse action against organizers (National Tenants Bill of Rights).
- **Name inquiries about or disclosure of immigration status as harassment.** State law already treats threats to report immigration status as retaliation and as a crime when used to extract money or labor.

### Part 9. Rental data and reporting (Core, low cost)
- Unit-level reporting at license issuance and renewal: owner and agent contacts, building facts, fee schedule, included utilities, current rent, rent increases, and evictions (Rockville § 18-195).
- Standard lease filed with the City.
- Annual public report with a date and responsible director, feeding the annual housing production and affordability report in [[Frederick Affordability Agenda 2026-2029]].
- Public dashboard with rent increases shown as five-year tiers, not raw percentages, to avoid helping price coordination (Rockville staff).
- Beneficial ownership disclosure for LLC owners (National Tenants Bill of Rights; connects to [[Corporate Landlords and Private Equity]]).
- **Optional:** voluntary rent guidelines included in every rent increase notice (Rockville Article V), as a data step short of stabilization.

### Part 10. Enforcement and remedies (Core)
- **Who:** a named department and director with investigation authority, including building-wide and company-wide investigations (Seattle 7.24.170). Which Frederick department fits is a verify item.
- **Records:** three-year retention of ads, leases, disclosures, fee charges, and vendor receipts, with a rebuttable presumption of violation when records are missing (Seattle 7.24.045).
- **Restitution:** administrative orders to refund prohibited fees with interest, as a license condition for licensed units and through municipal infraction consent orders otherwise (Real Rent Revision 2). Consider Seattle's refund-plus-double model and its penalty reduction for restitution paid within 10 or 15 days.
- **Cure-and-repay:** a short window to stop a fee and repay every affected tenant in the building before penalties escalate (Seattle 7.24.060.E).
- **Penalties:** municipal infractions up to the new $5,000 state ceiling; per-unit, per-month amounts from Real Rent ($500 / $1,000 / $2,500); repeat-violation escalation.
- **Licensing consequences:** mandatory denial after repeated uncured violations or a serious health and safety citation (Rockville § 18-118(b)); enhanced inspection sampling for buildings with failures.
- **Adjudication:** use the existing Technical Code Appeals Board, or create a landlord-tenant commission on the Rockville or Takoma Park model with authority to hear complaints and order relief.
- **Debt and liens:** unpaid restitution and relocation costs collectible as a debt to the City or a property lien, with tenant money held in trust and paid out (Seattle 7.24.240, Rockville § 18-178(d)).
- **Referral:** post the Attorney General's Consumer Protection Division contact with the Total Rent disclosure, preserving tenants' existing state consumer protection remedy.

### Part 11. Education, language access, and administration (Core)
- City model disclosure form, renter rights summary, and handbook, translated into the most common languages spoken by Frederick renters (data to confirm).
- A renter landing page (Clara's step 1) and handbook (step 3).
- Correct the City's tenant-rights web page, which still lists a two-month deposit maximum.
- **Funding:** dedicate part of rental licensing revenue (FY2026 ran well above projection) or a small per-unit regulatory fee, as Seattle did at about $11 a unit for three positions.
- Annual report date and named responsible director.

### Part 12. Effective dates and phasing (Core)
- Staggered effective dates: disclosure and fee rules first (aligned to lease renewal cycles), data reporting at the next license renewal, relocation and commission provisions after regulations and staffing are in place.
- Fix Real Rent's internal effective-date conflict (120 days vs. effective on approval).

## 5. Deliberately out of scope

| Item | Why it stays out | Where it goes |
|---|---|---|
| Rent stabilization | Authority exists for municipalities, but five veto-proof votes do not | Keep the data in Part 9; revisit after 2029 |
| Good cause eviction | 2026 state bill failed and authorized counties only | State delegation ask, 2027 session |
| Security deposit amounts | State law (RP § 8-203) | Education only |
| Lower late fee cap | State 5% cap; notice fee ban is the cleaner local tool | Possible state ask |
| Eviction court procedure, sealing, counsel eligibility | Court and state program rules | Education; possible City-funded counsel supplement |
| Local private right of action | *Aleti* risk | Enforcement through Part 10 instead |

## 6. Legal risk by part (for César's decision)

| Part | Risk | Main question |
|---|---|---|
| 2 Real Rent fees and disclosure | Low to moderate | Regulates advertising and licensing conditions, not rent levels. Takoma Park and Rockville regulate fees. |
| 2 Algorithmic ban | Moderate | Rockville precedent; RealPage has sued other cities over broader bans. Keep the non-public data definition. |
| 2/10 Coverage of unlicensed units | Moderate | Relies on general police power instead of the licensing hook. |
| 3 Disclosures | Low | State law expressly allows additional local lease content. |
| 4 Screening | Moderate | Interaction with state criminal-screening law for small landlords. |
| 5 Lease terms and early termination | Low to moderate | Rockville and Montgomery County have similar rules. |
| 6 City-authorized repair and deduct | Low to moderate | Runs through City inspection authority, not a private claim. |
| 7 Relocation and liens | Moderate | Lien authority and Charter support to confirm. |
| 8 Organizing | Low | Rockville precedent. |
| 10 Commission with money awards | Moderate to high | Whether Frederick's Charter supports an administrative body ordering damages. |
| Private right of action | High | *Aleti*. Not proposed. |

## 7. Politics

- Likely yes on Real Rent: Díaz, Nash, Hempel Irani, Taylor. Swing: Shackelford, Brehm, Adkins. Five votes survive a veto (from [[Real Rent Ordinance Revisions]]).
- Expect the landlord objections Rockville heard: general bias, two-year lease offers, the eviction-history ban, tenant repairs without approval, and relocation cost and fault. Expect tenants to ask why rent stabilization is not included.
- Parts 2, 3, 9, and 11 are the easiest to defend. Parts 4, 7, and 10 (commission) are where swing votes are most likely to peel off. A package can let swing members support the core while amending the edges.

## 8. Research still needed

1. Frederick baseline: Chapter 12.5 current text after Rodent Control, property maintenance code requirements, which department would enforce, Technical Code Appeals Board powers.
2. Takoma Park Chapter 6.16 and its Commission on Landlord-Tenant Affairs, as the in-state municipal model alongside Rockville.
3. Montgomery County Chapter 29 and the Housing Justice Act, for screening and repair timelines.
4. Frederick data: rental units by building age and size, fee prevalence (a sample of current listings), evictions by address, code complaints, languages spoken by renter households.
5. Rockville implementation after January 1, 2027: staffing, caseload, landlord response.
6. Access to Counsel eligibility wording (Area Median Income vs. state median) before it appears in public materials.
