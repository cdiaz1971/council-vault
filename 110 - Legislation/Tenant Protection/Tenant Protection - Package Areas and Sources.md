# Tenant Protection - Package Areas and Sources

The areas we will work on as part of the tenant protection package, grouped by part, with the laws each idea comes from. The body below is written to stand alone for staff.

## 1. Real Rent (Honest Pricing)

- Advertised rent includes every mandatory fee (Rockville, Seattle)
- A closed list of fees landlords are allowed to charge (Rockville, Seattle)
- Optional services need written opt-in and can be cancelled at any time for free (Rockville, Seattle)
- A ban on junk fees, such as fees for paying rent, for notices, or for repairs the landlord already owes (Rockville, Seattle)
- A ban on rent-setting software that uses competitors' private data (Rockville)
- Clear rules for how utilities are billed to tenants (Maryland)
- Limits on how often fees can go up, with advance notice required (Rockville)

*Law references*
- Rockville City Code Chapter 18, § 18-163 (fees), § 18-180 (advertised prices), § 18-148 (rent-setting software), adopted July 2026, effective January 1, 2027
- Seattle Ordinance 127497, Council Bill 121254 (fee disclosure, permitted and prohibited fees)
- Seattle Ordinance 126803 (notice fee ban)
- Maryland Real Property § 8-212.4 (utility billing disclosure)

## 2. Know Before You Sign (Disclosure)

- A standard two-page City disclosure form given before any application fee and again before signing (Seattle)
- The total monthly cost, including estimated utilities (Seattle, Rockville)
- Three years of rent history for the unit and the building (Rockville)
- Recent code violations, pending enforcement actions, utility shutoff notices, and foreclosure filings (Chicago)
- The owner, the managing agent, a 24-hour local contact, and the rental license number (Rockville)
- Notice to every tenant when the building changes owners (Rockville)
- The proposed lease at least two days before signing, and the signed copy within three days (Rockville)
- A written translation of the lease when it was negotiated in another language (California)

*Law references*
- Seattle Ordinance 127497 (two-page disclosure and City model form)
- Rockville City Code Chapter 18, § 18-180 and § 18-147(g) (fee, utility, and rent history disclosure), § 18-117(c) (ownership change), § 18-127(2) (24-hour local contact), § 18-147 (lease delivery timelines)
- Chicago Residential Landlord and Tenant Ordinance, § 5-12-100 (code violations and shutoff notices) and § 5-12-095 (foreclosure)
- California Civil Code § 1632 (lease translation)

## 3. Fair Screening and a Stable Home

- Written screening criteria posted before any application fee is charged (Seattle)
- Limits on denying applicants over eviction filings that were dismissed or are old (Rockville, Philadelphia)
- A written reason for any denial, and a chance to fix errors in the screening report (National Tenants Bill of Rights, Maryland)
- A written reason whenever a guarantor is required (Rockville)
- The fair chance criminal screening process extended to small landlords (Maryland, Seattle)
- An offer of a two-year lease, with any planned increases written into the lease up front (Rockville)
- Rent goes up no more than once in any 12 months (Rockville)
- Leaving the lease early without penalty when life changes (Rockville, New York)
- Fair lease terms on attorney fees and court costs (Rockville)
- The right to organize, meet, and file complaints together (Rockville, New York City, National Tenants Bill of Rights)
- Protection from harassment over immigration status (Maryland)

*Law references*
- Seattle First-in-Time Ordinance and Fair Chance Housing Ordinance
- Rockville City Code Chapter 18, § 18-149(b) (eviction history), § 18-146(1) (two-year lease offer), § 18-147(d) (one increase per year), § 18-146(18) (early termination), § 18-146 (attorney fees and court costs), § 18-161 and § 18-181 (organizing)
- Philadelphia Renters' Access Act (2021)
- National Housing Law Project, National Tenants Bill of Rights (2024)
- Maryland Real Property § 8-213 (application fees and screening), state fair chance screening rule for landlords with more than five units, and the Maryland Tenants' Bill of Rights, October 2026 edition (immigration status threats as retaliation)
- New York Real Property Law § 227-a (early termination for seniors moving to care or senior housing)
- New York City Tenant Bill of Rights (organizing)

## 4. Rights That Hold Up (Enforcement and Relocation)

- City-authorized repairs when a landlord misses a City deadline to fix a violation (Rockville)
- Repair deadlines based on how dangerous the problem is (Montgomery County)
- Refunds ordered by the City for illegal fees, with a reduced penalty for landlords who repay quickly (Seattle)
- Required recordkeeping, and a presumption of a violation when records are missing (Seattle)
- Escalating penalties, and consequences for the rental license (Rockville, Maryland)
- A place to bring complaints, either a Landlord-Tenant Commission or a hearing officer (Rockville, Montgomery County, Connecticut, Kansas City)
- Temporary and permanent relocation help when a home becomes unfit or is lost (Rockville, Seattle)
- A right to return once the unit is repaired (Rockville, Ontario)
- Unpaid relocation costs collected through a lien on the property (Rockville)

*Law references*
- Rockville City Code Chapter 18, § 18-115(g) (City-authorized repair), licensing denial and enhanced inspection provisions, Commission on Landlord-Tenant Affairs, § 18-178 (relocation, right to return, and lien)
- Montgomery County Code Chapter 29 (repair timelines and Commission on Landlord-Tenant Affairs)
- Seattle Ordinance 127497 (restitution, records presumption, and penalty reductions)
- Maryland municipal infraction penalty limit of $5,000, Chapter 570 of 2025
- Connecticut local Fair Rent Commissions
- Kansas City Office of the Tenant Advocate
- Seattle Municipal Code Chapter 22.212 (relocation assistance)
- Ontario Residential Tenancies Act (right to return after repairs)

## 5. Applies Across the Whole Package

- Covers every rental unit, newer buildings included (Rockville)
- Rental data reported at each license renewal, plus an annual public report (Rockville)
- Model forms and a renter rights summary in the languages Frederick renters speak most (Seattle, New York City)
- A named department in charge, assigned staff, and a funding source (Seattle)
- Effective dates that start in stages (Frederick design)

*Law references*
- Rockville City Code Chapter 18 (licensing covers every rental facility) and Article V, § 18-195 (data reporting)
- Seattle Ordinance 127497 (translated model forms, enforcement staffing funded by a per-unit fee) and the Seattle Renters Handbook
- New York City Tenant Bill of Rights one-page summary
