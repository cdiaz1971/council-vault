# Legislative Findings -- Rental Junk Fee Ordinance

# LEGISLATIVE FINDINGS AND RECORD  
## Rental Housing Fee Transparency and Consumer Protection Ordinance

---

## I. Purpose

The Mayor and Board of Aldermen of the City of Frederick adopt the following findings to establish a clear legislative record supporting the regulation of rental housing fee practices, to prevent unfair and deceptive practices, and to ensure transparency in housing costs.

These findings are intended to demonstrate the factual basis, policy rationale, and regional context supporting the ordinance.

---

## II. Findings: Deceptive Pricing Practices in Rental Housing

The Mayor and Board of Aldermen find that:

1. **Separation of mandatory fees from advertised rent obscures the true cost of housing.**  
   Rental listings frequently advertise a base rent that does not reflect the full amount a tenant must pay to occupy a unit.

2. **Tenants are routinely unable to determine the total cost of tenancy prior to application or lease signing.**  
   Mandatory fees are often disclosed late in the leasing process, after time and money have already been invested.

3. **This pricing structure distorts market competition.**  
   Properties that exclude fees from advertised rent appear artificially cheaper than comparable units that include costs upfront.

4. **Hidden and fragmented pricing disproportionately harms working-class renters.**  
   Tenants with limited financial flexibility are less able to absorb unexpected or undisclosed costs.

5. **The use of non-rent fees functions as a de facto rent increase without transparency.**  
   By shifting revenue into separate charges, landlords can increase total housing costs while avoiding direct comparison on advertised rent.

6. **These practices are analogous to deceptive pricing models in other consumer markets.**  
   Similar fee structures have been regulated in industries such as ticketing, hospitality, and telecommunications due to their misleading nature.

---

## III. Findings: Common Fee Inflation Practices

The Mayor and Board of Aldermen further find that rental housing providers have increasingly adopted fee structures that:

1. **Impose mandatory administrative and processing fees unrelated to tenant-selected services.**

2. **Charge recurring "technology," "convenience," or "platform" fees for standard functions such as rent payment or account access.**

3. **Add separate charges for routine property operations**, including:
   - Trash collection  
   - Pest control  
   - Common area maintenance  

4. **Use third-party billing systems to impose additional "utility administration" or "service" fees**, increasing tenant costs beyond actual utility usage.

5. **Require payment method fees**, even for standard payment options such as electronic bank transfer.

6. **Structure fees to evade comparison shopping**, including:
   - Low advertised rent paired with high mandatory add-ons  
   - Conditional "discounts" that mask true pricing  

7. **Apply fees broadly across tenants**, indicating that such charges are not optional in practice, regardless of how they are labeled.

The cumulative effect of these practices is to increase the total cost of housing in a manner that is opaque, inconsistent, and difficult for consumers to evaluate.

---

## IV. Findings: Regional Policy Trends

The Mayor and Board of Aldermen find that jurisdictions in the region have begun addressing rental fee practices as a consumer protection issue.

### Washington, DC

Washington, DC has adopted policies requiring:

- Greater transparency in rental pricing  
- Disclosure of total housing costs  
- Restrictions on certain non-optional fees  

These measures reflect a recognition that fragmented pricing undermines fair housing markets.

### Montgomery County, Maryland

Montgomery County has taken steps to:

- Strengthen tenant protections  
- Improve disclosure requirements in rental transactions  
- Address unfair or misleading fee practices  

These efforts demonstrate a regional trend toward regulating rental pricing transparency through local authority.

---

## V. Findings: Municipal Authority and Interest

The Mayor and Board of Aldermen find that:

1. The City has a legitimate and substantial interest in preventing unfair and deceptive trade practices in the rental housing market.

2. The City is authorized to regulate rental housing through licensing and related enforcement mechanisms.

3. Requiring that all mandatory charges be incorporated into a single total price is a reasonable and narrowly tailored method of:

   - Promoting transparency  
   - Protecting consumers  
   - Ensuring fair competition  

4. This ordinance does not regulate the amount of rent charged, but rather the manner in which rental housing is priced, advertised, and administered.

---

## VI. Policy Determination

Based on the foregoing findings, the Mayor and Board of Aldermen determine that:

- The separation of mandatory fees from rent is a widespread and harmful practice  
- Disclosure requirements alone are insufficient to correct market distortions  
- A clear standard requiring all-in pricing is necessary to ensure transparency and fairness  

Accordingly, the City adopts the Rental Housing Fee Transparency Ordinance to:

- Eliminate deceptive pricing practices  
- Establish consistent and enforceable standards  
- Protect tenants from hidden and unavoidable costs  

---

## VII. Conclusion

The Mayor and Board of Aldermen conclude that this ordinance is necessary and appropriate to protect the public welfare, ensure fair housing practices, and promote transparency in the rental market within the City of Frederick.
