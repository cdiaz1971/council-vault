# Maryland Legal Baseline and Local Authority - Text Amendment Filing

Presented as legal risk for César's decision. City Attorney review is advisory, not a gate.

## State law

- **The legislative body sets the procedure.** Land Use § 4-203: "A legislative body shall provide for the manner in which its zoning regulations and the boundaries of districts and zones shall be established, enforced, and amended." The statute does not say who may initiate an amendment. Choosing who files is the kind of procedural choice the statute leaves to the City.
- **The public hearing right stays.** § 4-203 also requires "at least one public hearing on a proposed zoning regulation or boundary at which parties in interest and citizens have an opportunity to be heard," and the regulation cannot take effect until 10 days after the hearing. The amendment keeps both the Planning Commission and Council hearings. Residents lose a filing route, not their right to be heard.
- **Map amendments are a different track.** § 4-204(b) governs piecemeal map amendments (change or mistake findings, the 12-month refiling bar). The submitted text does not touch map amendments, so a property owner can still seek a rezoning of their own land.
- **Planning commission.** § 2-102 sets planning commission composition and terms. Nothing in it guarantees private parties a right to bring text amendments to the commission.

## City Charter and Rules

- Council members introduce ordinances, which bear their names (Charter § 15.1(a)). Council may compel inclusion of legislation on the agenda (§ 15(a)) and sets its own rules (§ 11).
- The Council President presides (§ 9.1). The Mayor is not a member of Council. Under the pre-2024 structure the code's "Mayor schedules a hearing by the Board of Aldermen" language fit a Mayor who presided over the Board. The submitted text conforms § 306 to the current structure. *(Verify the Mayor's presiding role before December 2024 against the old Charter before saying it publicly.)*
- Council Rules adopted in early 2026 require a member's text amendment to be filed at least 10 days before introduction, copied to members, and posted on the City website, and they exempt a member acting in an official capacity from the text amendment application fee (FNP, February 10, 2026). The submitted text relies on that existing Rules framework.
- The ordinance is subject to the Mayor's veto. Override takes five of seven within 30 days.

## Risk by provision

| Provision | Risk | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Limit filing to elected officials and the Division | Low | § 4-203 leaves procedure to the City; peer jurisdictions do the same ([[Peer Jurisdiction Text Amendment Filing Rules]]) |
| Non-voting Council review before the Commission | Low | Text amendments are legislative, so pre-hearing discussion does not raise the prejudgment problem it would in a quasi-judicial map or site plan case |
| Council President receives the recommendation and schedules the hearing | Low legal, moderate political | Internal allocation of Council business; political attack is "power concentration" |
| Division filings get notice only | Low | Staff applications still go through the full Commission and Council hearings |
| Silence on pending applications | Moderate | A change to filing rules could be read to reach applications already accepted. Applicants generally have no vested right in a pending legislative change, but a retroactive reading invites a fairness claim. The Housing Certainty Act vesting regime (G-26-11) applies to development applications, and whether it touches a text amendment application at all is a Rachel Gray question. The data center fight is being carried by the moratorium, not this bill |
| No scheduling deadline | Low legal | "As soon as practicable" already governs the later hearings; the review meeting has no clock. Political, not legal, exposure |

## Questions for the City Attorney (advisory)

1. Does the amendment apply to text amendment applications already accepted as complete on its effective date, or only to applications filed after?
2. Can the Council address scheduling deadlines and sponsorship disclosure through its Rules of Procedure rather than the LMC?
3. Does "elected City official" include the Mayor, and could the Mayor's filings also go to the Council review meeting?
