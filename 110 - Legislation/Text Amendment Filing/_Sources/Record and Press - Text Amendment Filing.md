# Record and Press - Text Amendment Filing

## October 22, 2026 workshop, as reported

FNP, "Proposed change would restrict zoning amendment process to city officials, staff," Katelynn Winebrenner, October 23, 2026. Paraphrase unless quoted:

- **Framing.** The draft limits text amendments to City officials and planning staff; private parties would go through Council members. The paper reported the change as eliminating the $4,400 fee.
- **Brehm.** Residents told him code changes were "one-off wonders based upon specific development proposals." On keeping a route for private ideas: "So I don't think we want to eliminate that." His goal is a level playing field, with everyone having the same opportunity through an elected representative. He does not expect the change to add much time.
- **Díaz.** A member's authority to submit comes from serving on Council. The review is mainly to let Council know what is planned and get feedback. Members would filter requests using their judgment and knowledge of the code. He hopes a code review would reduce the number of applications. Members need to know what is coming so they can answer the public.
- **Hempel Irani** was the only other Council member at the work session. She appreciated the idea of lowering barriers, said text amendments are mainly brought by developers and the cost keeps ordinary residents out, and asked whether a historical look at the code could reduce the number of text amendments.
- **Lisa Graditor** (McCurdy, Dean and Graditor) said every text amendment she has worked on saved City resources, because developers paid for her firm and for consultants' case studies, renderings, and traffic and engineering work.

**Read on the record.** Attendance was thin: four of seven members (Nash, Taylor, Shackelford, Adkins) were not reported as present, so they have not engaged on the record. The first public opposition voice is the land use bar, and its argument is cost shifting, not property rights.

## Earlier coverage that frames the fight

- **FNP, September 26, 2026,** "City council members wary of proposed change to planning process." Not readable this session. Timing points to the September 24 work session on Brehm's site plan review ZTA (§ 309), not this bill. Read it before the Commission hearing; opponents may blur the two Brehm amendments together.
- **FNP, December 30, 2025,** "Resident-led effort for Frederick to regulate industrial contamination stalls." Kevin Sellner's brownfield amendment; the $4,400 fee "would have been a showstopper for us as residents." Strongest evidence that resident ideas already depend on Council sponsors.
- **FNP, February 10, 2026,** Council Rules changes: members file text amendments 10 days before introduction, posted online, no fee for members acting officially. Context: the ethics complaint against Nash over a data center ZTA introduced without text to colleagues.
