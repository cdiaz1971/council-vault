# Text Amendment Filing - Opposition Research

Positions are projections from the vault record and public reporting unless a quote is cited. Profiles in `195 - Political People` hold the full background. Vulnerabilities are listed so you know the terrain, not as lines to use. Conflict-of-interest flags are monitoring notes, not findings.

## Attack lines, ranked by danger

| Rank | Attack | Who carries it | Why it is dangerous | Your answer (detail in [[Text Amendment Filing - Talking Points]]) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | "Council strips residents' right to propose zoning changes" | Land use bar, possibly civic groups, a headline writer | It is literally true of the text and fits in a headline | Residents can't use the route now ($4,400 plus counsel); Sellner went through Council members; route becomes free; hearings stay |
| 2 | "Developers will launder amendments through friendly members, like the 2025 data center draft" | Opponents of the bill, and possibly data center opponents who fear the next one | Has a real Frederick example attached to your VP | The review meeting puts every sponsor and draft in public on day one; disclosure commitment; Ethics Ordinance applies |
| 3 | "Power grab by the Council President" | Adkins, possibly the administration, possibly Shackelford | Fits Adkins' § 9.1 charter push and an open-ended scheduling clause | Next-available-workshop commitment; Rules deadline; Council elects its President and can compel agenda items |
| 4 | "Cost shift to taxpayers; developers paid for the studies" | Graditor and the land use bar | Already on the record (FNP Oct 23) | Sponsors can still receive privately prepared studies; staff time saved on non-viable applications; get the real fee figures |
| 5 | "Retroactive attack on a pending applicant" | Trammell Crow, Bruce Dean | Litigation-minded industry; Quantum Maryland already sued the County | Bill is about future applications; pending applications are a legal question; data center is on the moratorium track |
| 6 | "Fold it into the LMC rewrite" | Taylor, Adkins, staff | Sounds reasonable and kills momentum | Rewrite has no adoption date; this stops the code from changing underneath the rewrite |
| 7 | "Slows pro-housing fixes" | Housing advocates, builders | Splits a natural ally | GG Frederick precedent had Council sponsors ready; Council housing package |

## Development community and land use bar

**McCurdy, Dean and Graditor (Bruce Dean, Lisa Graditor).** Most directly affected actor. Represented Trammell Crow on PC25-626ZTA (Dean's firm drafted it, and an almost identical draft reached Council through Nash in August 2025) and Wormald on the § 816 amendment (Graditor as agent). Drafting and carrying text amendments is a line of business for the firm.
- *On the record:* Graditor at the October 22 workshop said each amendment she worked on saved City resources because developers paid for her firm's and consultants' work ([[Record and Press - Text Amendment Filing]]).
- *Expected arguments:* cost shifting; loss of expertise; politicization; delay; due process for pending applicants.
- *Leverage:* repeat players with staff, the Commission, and members; credibility on technical questions; litigation-ready clients.
- *How to engage:* do not attack the firm. Concede their work product is often good and say it can still come in, with a public sponsor. Make the argument about who answers for a citywide rule.

**Trammell Crow (PC25-626ZTA applicant).** Only applicant with a live stake. Will watch for any reading that reaches its pending application, and has counsel who will testify. Keep the bill off its merits.

**The Wormald Companies, GG Frederick Holdings.** Recent private applicants. GG Frederick won 6-0, so it can argue the old route works. Low likelihood of organized opposition; possible letters.

**Frederick Health.** Filed a private text amendment to allow hospitals in mixed-use districts, recommended 3-1 by the Commission and later approved by Council. An institutional, sympathetic applicant. If opponents want a "good private amendment this would have blocked" example, this is it. Answer: an institution of that standing would have found a sponsor in a day, and the public would have heard about it earlier.

**Builders and business groups** (Frederick County Building Industry Association, Frederick County Chamber of Commerce). No recorded position. Projection: letters opposing on red tape and investment-climate grounds, likely at the Commission hearing. Watch for testimony requests and sign-up lists.

## Administration

**Mayor Michael O'Connor.** Loses the code's role in receiving the Commission recommendation and scheduling the Council hearing. Keeps filing authority and the veto.
- *Interest:* executive authority over the land use calendar; relationship with development interests; institutional standing (MML President 2024-2025, Moore ally).
- *Expected posture:* low-key opposition or a request to keep the scheduling role, possibly through staff comments. A veto is possible but would put him on the side of keeping a $4,400 door that only developers use.
- *Leverage:* under the **current** code, he schedules the Council hearing on this bill after the Commission recommends. He can slow it.
- *Vulnerabilities (context, not lines):* MPDU fee-in-lieu record, where most developers pay instead of build; structural arts funding conflict documented in his profile.
- *How to engage:* frame it as aligning the code with the post-2024 Council structure, and offer to keep his filing route visible. If the hearing stalls, Council can compel agenda inclusion (Charter § 15(a)).

**Planning Division (Director Gabrielle Collard) and staff.** Mixed. Gains workload relief and alignment with Council priorities; loses fee revenue and some autonomy, and gains a new duty to give Council notice of staff applications. Staff writes the report the Commission reads, so staff's view matters more than anyone's at step two.
- *How to engage:* meet Collard before the staff report is written. Ask for the five-year filing data, frame the notice requirement as protecting staff from surprise political fights, and ask what would make the review meeting easy to administer.

**City Attorney Rachel Gray.** Advisory. Questions in [[Maryland Legal Baseline and Local Authority - Text Amendment Filing]]. Her answer on pending applications sets your public line.

## Planning Commission

The body that recommends. Its friction with Council (ADU conditions dropped, the Mixed Use Vertical amendment introduced without Council input) cuts both ways: some commissioners may see a Council pre-review as encroachment, others as the Council finally engaging early.
- *Expected arguments:* preempting the Commission's role; delay; the Commission already filters bad ideas.
- *How to engage:* the review is non-binding, so the Commission remains the first body to recommend on the merits. Pitch it as better direction to the Commission. Identify the chair and any commissioner with land use practice ties before the hearing.

## Council members

**Joe Adkins.** Lean no. The only Republican; appointed after losing the 2025 election (30.3 percent); former City Deputy Director for Planning who supported the Planning Commission and oversaw current and long-range planning.
- *Interest:* process expertise, staff relationships, his § 9.1 charter amendment on removing Council leadership, which he wants to discuss scheduling with you.
- *Expected arguments:* concentration of power in the Council President; the review step is redundant with the Commission; belongs in the LMC rewrite.
- *Leverage:* the most credible technical critic on the dais; can slow items through process questions.
- *Monitoring flag:* $500 from developer Marvin Ausherman. Relevant only if an Ausherman-linked application is affected.
- *How to engage:* ask his advice on the review step design and offer the Rules deadline first. Keep the conversation separate from the § 9.1 scheduling talk so neither looks like a trade.

**Derek Shackelford.** Unknown, gettable. Led the two-year data center moratorium, which comes from the same diagnosis: developer applications setting the City's agenda. Ran for Council President in 2021 and 2025 and lost both times, the second to you, and protects institutional position.
- *Expected concern:* Council President authority, possibly framed as concern for colleagues' access.
- *How to engage:* tie the bill to his moratorium, credit his work, and make the Rules deadline his idea if he wants it.

**Libby Taylor.** Lean yes, may want to fold it in. Her modernization plan pauses private text amendments for 270 days once a draft code reaches Council, and her stated view is "Instead of the developers leading the way, it's a way for the city to decide what we want to do."
- *Monitoring flag:* $500 from St. John Properties, a major commercial developer with Frederick holdings.
- *How to engage:* quote her own line back, position this as the interim fix that protects her rewrite, and ask for her support at First Read.

**Katie Nash.** Lean yes, cautious. Has asserted legislative independence from the Mayor before. The August 2025 data center draft she submitted, prepared by Bruce Dean's firm, led to Ethics Commission findings and a cease-and-desist on data center and energy matters. Opponents will cite it as proof that Council sponsorship can be captured.
- *How to engage:* private conversation first. The answer protects her too: the review meeting would have made that draft public on day one. Do not raise it unprompted.

**Sarah Hempel Irani.** Lean yes. Attended the October 22 workshop and said cost keeps ordinary residents out of the process. Note her public criticism of Nash in September; do not assume she and Nash move together.

**Peter Brehm.** Co-sponsor. Framed the bill as a level playing field. Also carries the § 309 site plan review amendment, which the FNP covered under "City council members wary of proposed change to planning process" (September 26). Keep the two bills distinct in public.

## Civic groups and advocates

**Citizens for Responsible Growth (Steve Jakubczyk) and Fellowship of Scientists and Engineers (Dr. Kevin Sellner, Betty Law).** Likely supportive on the data center logic, since they argue residents and Council, not a developer's lawyers, should write the rules. Sellner is the resident whose brownfield amendment went through Council members because of the fee, which makes him the best possible witness. Risk: CRG opposed the ADU and production reforms, and some members may object to losing a resident filing right on principle. Ask Sellner directly whether he will testify.

**Mobilize Frederick (Kathy Kinsey, Karen Cannon, Jenny Willoughby).** Their February 23, 2026 letter on the data center ZTA raised the LMC § 306(b) process argument. Natural ally on process reform. Ask for a letter to the Commission.

**Housing and YIMBY advocates.** Possible concern that builder-led pro-housing fixes slow down. Brief them early with the GG Frederick example and your housing package.

**Immigrant and working-class community groups** (Centro Hispano, SSCM, RISE). Not a natural part of this fight, but the equity case (a free route for residents without lawyers) is theirs. A short statement from one of them would answer the "strips residents" headline better than you can.

## Press

The Frederick News-Post (Katelynn Winebrenner covered the workshop) framed the bill neutrally and reported it as eliminating the fee. Expect the resident-rights angle at the Commission hearing if any resident testifies against. Have the Sellner fee quote and the "free and the same for everyone" line ready.
