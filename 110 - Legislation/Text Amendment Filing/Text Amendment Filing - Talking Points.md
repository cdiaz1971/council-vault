# Text Amendment Filing - Talking Points

Points, not a script. Full remarks, letters, or op-eds get drafted in chat. Facts marked *(confirm)* need a number from Planning before you say them.

## Core message (use everywhere)

**One sentence.** The people who change Frederick's zoning rules for the whole city should be the people who answer to Frederick's voters.

**Three supports.**
1. **Accountability.** Every proposed code change will have an elected sponsor who presents it in public and answers for how it fits the Comprehensive Plan.
2. **Fairness.** Right now the route to propose a code change costs $4,400 *(confirm)* plus a lawyer. Residents don't use it. Developers do. This makes the route free and the same for everyone.
3. **Nothing taken from the public.** Every public hearing stays. Map amendments stay. Anyone can still propose a change. They just do it through someone who has to stand behind it.

**Contrast line.** Citywide rules should be written for the city, not for one site.

## Planning Commission presentation (as applicant)

Order of points, about five minutes:

1. **What it is.** A process amendment to § 306. It changes who signs a text amendment application and adds a public first look by the Council. It does not change any zoning district, any use, or any map.
2. **Why now.** Recent text amendments came from data center, gas station, mixed-use, and hospital applicants, most prepared by the same firm, each rewriting citywide rules around one project. Residents described them as "one-off wonders." The Commission has seen these applications. You know the pattern.
3. **What changes for the Commission.** You still hold your public hearing and make your recommendation. You will receive applications that have already had a public Council discussion of Comprehensive Plan fit, so you get clearer direction on what the elected body is weighing. That speaks to the friction over Commission conditions not reaching Council, as happened with the ADU recommendation.
4. **Peer practice.** Rockville's 2026 zoning ordinance lets only the Mayor and Council apply. Montgomery County and Frederick County put initiation with their councils. Gaithersburg requires every applicant to name a public sponsor. Frederick's version is less restrictive than Rockville's.
5. **Legal footing.** State law, Land Use § 4-203, leaves "the manner in which" zoning is amended to the legislative body and guarantees the public hearing, which this keeps.
6. **The ask.** Recommend approval as submitted.

Keep off: the data center application's merits, Council President authority as a theme, any criticism of staff.

## Council readings (First and Second Read)

- Open by naming what the Commission recommended and why, in its own words.
- Repeat the three supports. Then go straight to the three objections you know are coming and answer them before anyone raises them: residents, scheduling, and laundering (answers below).
- State the commitments: next-available-workshop scheduling, disclosure of who requested any amendment you sponsor, and willingness to put both in the Council Rules for every member.
- Close on accountability: "If an idea is good, it will find a sponsor. If no elected official is willing to put their name on it in public, that tells us something too."

## Press lines

- "This makes the front door to changing Frederick's zoning code free and the same for everyone."
- "Today the people who use this process are developers and their attorneys. Residents who wanted a change already came to Council members, because the fee was a showstopper. We are writing the way residents actually work into the code."
- "No hearing goes away. No map amendment changes. A property owner can still seek a rezoning of their own land."
- "Rockville just did the same thing in its new zoning code. Montgomery County and Frederick County already work this way."
- If asked about the data center developer: "This bill is about how every future text amendment starts. The data center application is a separate question the Council is handling through the moratorium."

## Residents, NACs, and advocates

- "You don't lose a thing you were using. You gain a free way in."
- "Bring an idea to any Council member, not only your own. If one says no, ask another. Every request I sponsor will say who asked for it."
- "You will see every proposed code change at a public Council meeting before it ever reaches the Planning Commission. That is earlier than you see them today."
- "Both public hearings stay, and you can speak at both."
- For data center and growth-management groups (Mobilize Frederick, Citizens for Responsible Growth): "This is the process reform your letters asked for. The developer no longer decides when the City rewrites its rules."
- For housing advocates: "The ground-floor residential change that passed 6-0 had Council sponsors ready before it passed. Pro-housing ideas will have champions. This Council has a housing package moving."

- **Hempel Irani.** Thank her for showing up on the 22nd. Her line on cost keeping residents out is the best quote on the record. Ask whether she will speak to it at First Read. Her question about a historical review of the code is a natural tie to the LMC review.
- **Taylor.** Lead with her own diagnosis: her modernization plan pauses private text amendments, and her line that the city, not developers, should lead. Position this as the interim fix that makes modernization work. Ask what she would need to support it now rather than fold it in.
- **Nash.** Private conversation before the Commission hearing. Opponents will cite the 2025 data center draft. Agree on how she wants that handled: the answer is that the new review meeting would have put that draft in front of the full Council and the public on day one. Do not raise it in public unless asked.
- **Shackelford.** Tie it to his moratorium: both come from the same problem, developer applications setting the City's agenda. Address Council President authority directly, and offer the Rules deadline so his support does not look like a vote for your power.
- **Adkins.** Respect his planning background and ask his view on the review step's design. Keep it separate from the § 9.1 charter conversation. Offer the Rules deadline before he raises it.

## Hard questions and answers

**"You're taking away residents' right to propose zoning changes."**
Residents aren't using that right, because it costs $4,400 *(confirm)* and a lawyer. The one resident amendment in recent years went through Council members for exactly that reason. This makes the resident route free and official, and every hearing where residents speak stays.

**"This hands the Council President control over which amendments get heard."**
The President schedules Council business today; that is what the office does. I've committed to putting every complete application on the next available workshop, and I'll support writing a deadline into the Council Rules for whoever holds this seat. The Council elects its President and can put any item on the agenda itself.

**"Developers will just get a friendly Council member to file for them. Isn't that worse, less transparent?"**
It is more transparent. Today an application goes from a developer to staff to the Commission with no elected official's name on it. Under this, a named official presents it at a public Council meeting and answers for it. I will disclose who asked for anything I sponsor, and I'll support that rule for every member. And members who sponsor are bound by the Ethics Ordinance.

**"Wasn't the data center amendment exactly that, a developer draft submitted by a Council member?"** *(only if raised)*
That draft never had the public first look this bill creates. Under this process it would have come to the full Council and the public at a meeting at the start, with its sponsor explaining it. That is the point.

**"Is this aimed at the data center application?"**
No. It is about how every future amendment starts. Pending applications are a legal question for the City Attorney. The data center question is on its own track through the moratorium.

**"Developers paid for studies and consultants. Now taxpayers will."**
Nothing stops anyone who asks a Council member to sponsor from preparing the supporting studies. What changes is who signs the application and stands behind it in public. And staff will stop spending hours on applications that were never going to fit the Comprehensive Plan.

**"This adds delay."**
One public meeting. Brehm doesn't expect it to add much time, and an early public discussion avoids the delay that comes from finding problems late at the Commission.

**"What about the lost fee revenue?"**
Small, and offset by staff time saved. We have asked Planning for five years of filings and revenue. *(Give the number once you have it.)*

**"Why not wait for the LMC rewrite?"**
The rewrite has no adoption date, and private applications keep coming in the meantime. This is a small process fix that makes the rewrite easier, because the code won't be changing underneath it one site at a time.

**"Why the Council President and not the Mayor?"**
The old language comes from when the Board of Aldermen was the legislative body and the Mayor sat with it *(verify against the pre-2024 Charter before saying this)*. The Mayor isn't a member of the Council now. Text amendments become ordinances, and the Council schedules its own ordinance hearings. The Mayor still files amendments and still has the veto.

**"Can the Mayor still file?"**
Yes. Any elected City official can, and the Mayor's applications get the same public Council review.

**"Why does staff only give notice?"**
Staff applications reflect administration policy and still go through both public hearings. Today Council doesn't even get notice. This adds transparency in both directions.

## Things not to say

- Don't say no Maryland jurisdiction lets residents file. That has not been checked.
- Don't say Frederick County bars private applications. Only that its Council initiates.
- Don't call the review a "hearing." It is a public meeting with no vote.
- Don't name the Nash ethics ruling unless asked, and then only that it is a public document.
- Don't give a fee revenue figure until Planning confirms it.
