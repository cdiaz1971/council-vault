# Title: An Ordinance Establishing Real Rent Requirements for Rental Licensing and Tenant Protection

Introduced by: Council President César Díaz

Date:  

Action: 

Summary: This ordinance establishes Real Rent disclosure requirements that ensure Tenants are informed of the total amount they must pay to occupy a unit, inclusive of all mandatory charges and costs associated with leasing the unit.

THE CITY OF FREDERICK

CITY COUNCIL

ORDINANCE NO: G-26-\_\_\_\_\_\_

AN ORDINANCE concerning the establishment of Real Rent Requirements for Rental Licensing and Tenant Protection

FOR the purpose of requiring Real Rent disclosure to ensure that Tenants are provided the full and accurate cost of occupying a unit, including all mandatory charges and other costs associated with leasing; and requiring Landlords, as a condition of rental licensing, to clearly specify the total cost of the unit to promote transparency and prevent deceptive pricing practices.

BY adding

Chapter 12.5, Article VI

The Code of the City of Frederick, 1966 (as amended)

SECTION I. BE IT ENACTED AND ORDAINED BY THE CITY COUNCIL OF THE CITY OF FREDERICK, that Chapter 12.5, Article VI of The Code of the City of Frederick, 1966 (as amended) is hereby added to read as follows:

## Article VI. Real Rent Requirements for Rental Licensing and Tenant Protection

### § 12.5-89. Findings and Purpose

(a) Findings. The Mayor and City Council find that:

(1) The use of mandatory rental fees charged separately from base rent undermines price transparency, distorts the local housing market, and imposes unjust and unpredictable financial burdens on tenants.

(2) Separating mandatory charges from advertised rent obscures the true cost of housing, prevents meaningful comparison between units, and functions as a de facto rent increase that evades transparent scrutiny.

(3) Rental listings in the City frequently advertise a base rent that does not reflect the full amount a tenant must pay to occupy a unit, and mandatory fees are often disclosed only late in the leasing process, after prospective tenants have already invested time and money.

(4) The use of third‑party billing systems, technology fees, platform charges, administrative add‑ons, and similar mechanisms to impose costs beyond advertised rent is a widespread and harmful practice in the residential rental market.

(5) The City has a legitimate and substantial interest in preventing unfair and deceptive trade practices in the rental housing market and in protecting the public health, safety, and welfare of its residents.

(6) The City is authorized to regulate rental housing through licensing and to exercise its police powers and home‑rule authority to ensure transparency, fairness, and consumer protection in the rental housing market.

(7) Disclosure requirements alone are insufficient to correct market distortions created by fragmented pricing, and a clear, enforceable standard requiring all mandatory charges to be incorporated into a single disclosed price is necessary and appropriate.

(b) Purpose and Intent. This ordinance regulates the manner in which rental housing subject to a City rental license is advertised, priced, and offered to tenants as a condition of rental licensing. It is the intent of this ordinance to ensure that all mandatory charges associated with tenancy are incorporated into a single total price for purposes of advertising, disclosure, billing, and licensing compliance. Nothing in this ordinance shall be construed to: 

(1) regulate or limit the total amount of rent a landlord may lawfully charge;

(2) conflict with or be preempted by any provision of Maryland landlord-tenant law; or

(3) prohibit charges expressly authorized and governed by state law.

### § 12.5-90. Definitions

(a) In general. In this section, the following words have the meanings indicated.

(b) Advertised Rent. “Advertised Rent” means any rental price or cost communicated in a listing, posting, advertisement, or offer, whether online, in print, or in any other medium.

(c) Covered Unit. “Covered Unit” means any residential dwelling unit in the City of Frederick for which a rental housing license is required under applicable City ordinance.

(d) Landlord. “Landlord” means any person or entity that owns, controls, manages, or offers for rent one or more Covered Units, or that employs or engages a property manager to do so.

(e) Mandatory Fee. "Mandatory Fee" means any fee, cost, or charge that a Tenant must pay as a condition of applying for, leasing, or occupying a Covered Unit, regardless of how the charge is named or described. Mandatory pet rent—defined as any recurring charge required in order to keep a pet in a Covered Unit—is a Mandatory Fee and must be included in Total Rent. All Mandatory Fees must be incorporated into Total Rent.

(f) Optional Fee. "Optional Service" means a service that

(1) is affirmatively requested by the tenant in writing;

(2) is not required for tenancy; and

(3) can be declined at any time without penalty, lease denial, delay, or adverse treatment.

(g) Prohibited Fee. “Prohibited Fee” means any Separate Fee that is required, automatic, or imposed as a condition of tenancy. A Mandatory Fee that is not incorporated into Total Rent constitutes a Prohibited Fee.

(h) Separate Fee. “Separate Fee” means any charge imposed on a tenant that is not incorporated into Total Rent.

(i) Tenant. “Tenant” means any person entitled to occupy a Covered Unit under a lease, rental agreement, or other arrangement, whether written or oral.

(j) Third Party Billing System. “Third Party Billing System” means any platform, vendor, or service provider used by a Landlord to assess, invoice, or collect charges from tenants separate from Total Rent.

(k) Total Rent. “Total Rent” means the total periodic charge a Tenant is required to pay for the right to occupy a Covered Unit, inclusive of all Mandatory Fees, however denominated, that are required as a condition of tenancy. This term is used solely for purposes of advertising, disclosure, and licensing compliance and shall not be interpreted to limit the lawful amount a Landlord may charge.

### § 12.5-91. Authority and Applicability.

(a) Authority. This ordinance is enacted pursuant to the City’s home rule authority, police powers, and licensing authority to protect the public health, safety, and welfare, including the authority to prevent unfair and deceptive practices in the rental housing market.

(b) Applicability. This ordinance applies to all Covered Units as defined in § 12.5‑90, to all new leases and lease renewals executed on or after the effective date set forth in § 12.5‑99, and to all Landlords and property managers acting on behalf of Landlords with respect to Covered Units. In any enforcement proceeding, the form, label, or name assigned to a charge is not controlling. The City and any court may consider the substance, function, and practical effect of the charge in determining compliance. A fee shall be presumed to be a Mandatory Fee for purposes of this ordinance if: 

(1) more than 25 percent of tenants in the same property are charged the fee; or

(2) refusal to pay the fee results in lease denial, delay in lease execution, or less favorable lease terms.

(c) Anti-Circumvention. Any lease provision that purports to waive a Tenant’s rights under this ordinance, impose a Prohibited Fee, or otherwise circumvent the requirements of this ordinance is void and unenforceable. It shall be unlawful for a landlord to:

(1) mischaracterize a Mandatory Fee as an Optional Service;

(2) condition lease approval, execution, or renewal on a Tenant’s purchase of or enrollment in any service;

(3) use a third party vendor, affiliate, or contractor to impose charges that would constitute Prohibited Fees if charged directly by the Landlord;

(4) offer a rent discount or incentive conditioned on a Tenant’s agreement to pay Separate Fees; or

(5) structure charges in any manner designed to evade the requirements of this ordinance.

### § 12.5-92. All-in Pricing Requirement.

(a) A Landlord shall advertise, quote, and charge only Total Rent for any Covered Unit. It is unlawful for a Landlord to:

(1) advertise or quote a rental price for a Covered Unit that is less than Total Rent; or

(2) require a Tenant to pay any charge that is not incorporated into Total Rent.

(b) Consolidation of Mandatory Costs. All Mandatory Fees and all other mandatory costs of tenancy shall be incorporated into Total Rent, regardless of the label, name, category, or characterization assigned to the charge.

### § 12.5-93. Prohibited Fees.

(a) Prohibited Fees Unlawful. No Landlord shall charge or collect a Prohibited Fee from a Tenant of a Covered Unit.

(b) Categories of Prohibited Fees. The following charges constitute Prohibited Fees when not incorporated into Total Rent, including but not limited to:

(1) administrative or management fees;

(2) move-in or move-out fees;

(3) mandatory technology, software, platform, or portal access fees;

(4) payment processing fees for any standard payment method;

(5) mandatory pest control, trash, recycling, or common area maintenance fees charged separately;

(6) utility administration, billing, or service fees;

(7) lease renewal, lease preparation, or lease modification fees;

(8) inspection or housing compliance fees;

(9) Non‑refundable pet fees of any kind—including, but not limited to, one‑time pet fees, pet move‑in charges, or any upfront charge related to a tenant’s pet that is not a lawful refundable security deposit governed by Maryland Code, Real Property Article § 8‑203; or

(10) any charge that recovers ordinary costs of property ownership, management, or operations.

(c) Exceptions. Nothing in this section shall prohibit:

(1) charges expressly permitted and governed by Maryland state law, including lawful security deposits and late fees;

(2) application fees governed by Section 12.5-94; 

(3) charges for Optional Services as defined in Section 12.5-90; or

(4) any charge that is incorporated into Total Rent, which shall not be considered a Separate Fee.

### § 12.5-93.1. Pet Deposit Disclosure.

(a) Requirements for Pet Deposits. Any pet deposit charged by a Landlord in connection with a Covered Unit shall:

(1) be documented in writing as refundable at the time of collection, including a clear statement that the deposit will be returned to the Tenant upon termination of tenancy, subject only to deductions for documented pet‑caused damage beyond ordinary wear and tear;

(2) be subject to the same itemization, documentation, and return requirements applicable to security deposits under Maryland Code, Real Property Article § 8‑203 et seq.; and

(3) be disclosed to the Tenant in writing at or before lease signing, including the total amount, the conditions under which deductions may be made, and the timeline for return.

(b) Non‑Compliant Pet Deposits. Any pet deposit that does not comply with this section shall be deemed a Prohibited Fee for purposes of this ordinance.

### § 12.5-94. Permissible Charges.

(a) Application Fees. A Landlord may charge a onetime application fee per applicant household, not to exceed the Landlord’s actual, documented, out of pocket cost to conduct a background or credit screening, and in no event more than the amount permitted under the Maryland Code, Real Property Article. Application fees shall be refunded in full if no screening is performed. A Landlord shall provide documentation of actual screening costs upon request by the applicant.

(b) Security Deposits. Security deposits are governed by Maryland Code, Real Property Article § 8203 et seq. No nonrefundable deposit of any kind may be charged in addition to, or as a substitute for, a lawful security deposit.

(c) Late Fees. Late fees are governed by the Maryland Code, Real Property Article. No compounding, escalating, or additional late penalties beyond those expressly authorized by state law are permitted.

### § 12.5-95. Utility Charges.

(a) Permissible Utility Charges. A Landlord may charge a Tenant separately for utilities only if the charge is based on:

(1) the Tenant’s actual metered usage for the specific Covered Unit; or

(2) a lawful, transparent, and consistently applied allocation formula that is disclosed to the Tenant in writing at lease signing.

(b) Prohibited Utility Practices. Flat rate utility fees that do not reflect actual or estimated usage are prohibited unless incorporated into Total Rent. No administrative, service, billing, convenience, or similar fee may be added to any utility charge.

### § 12.5-96. Rental Licensing Compliance.

(a) Conditions of Licensure. Compliance with this ordinance is a material condition of:

(1) issuance of a City rental housing unit license;

(2) renewal of a City rental housing unit license; and

(3) continued eligibility to operate Covered Units within the City.

(b) Licensing Enforcement. The City may deny, suspend, or revoke a rental housing unit license for any violation of this ordinance, or for any pattern or practice of noncompliance. The City may condition continued licensure on restitution to affected Tenants. Licensing remedies are in addition to any civil penalties or private rights of action available under this ordinance.

### § 12.5-97. Penalties and Enforcement

(a) Civil Penalties. Each Covered Unit in violation, and each calendar month during which a violation continues, shall constitute a separate offense for purposes of this section. Civil penalties shall be paid to the City within 30 days of final determination unless otherwise ordered. Civil penalties for violations of this ordinance shall be assessed as follows:

(1) five hundred dollars ($500) per Covered Unit for a first violation; 

(2) one thousand dollars ($1,000) per Covered Unit for a second violation; and

(3) two thousand five hundred dollars ($2,500) per covered unit for any subsequent violation. 

(b) Private Right of Action. Any Tenant who pays a Prohibited Fee in violation of this ordinance may bring a civil action against the Landlord in a court of competent jurisdiction. Nothing in this section limits any other right or remedy available to a Tenant under applicable law. In any action under this section, the burden of proof shall be on the Landlord to demonstrate that the charge at issue is not a Prohibited Fee. In any action under this section, the prevailing Tenant may recover:

(1) three times the amount of all unlawful charges paid;

(2) statutory damages of not less than one thousand dollars ($1,000);

(3) reasonable attorney's fees and costs; and

(4) such other relief as the court deems appropriate.

### § 12.5-98. Severability.

(a) Any charge not expressly authorized by this ordinance shall be presumed to be a Prohibited Fee unless the Landlord demonstrates by a preponderance of the evidence that the charge is a lawful Optional Service or is otherwise authorized under this ordinance or Maryland state law.

(b) If any provision of this ordinance, or the application thereof to any person or circumstance, is held invalid, the remainder of the ordinance and the application of such provision to other persons or circumstances shall not be affected.

### § 12.5-99. Effective Date.

(a) This ordinance shall take effect 120 days after the date of adoption and shall apply to all new leases and lease renewals executed on or after the effective date.

SECTION III. BE IT FURTHER ENACTED AND ORDAINED BY THE CITY COUNCIL OF THE CITY OF FREDERICK, That this ordinance shall take effect on the date it is approved by the Mayor and all other ordinances or parts of ordinances inconsistent with the provisions of this ordinance will as of that date be repealed to the extent of such inconsistency.
