Title: An ordinance to regulate electric powered micromobility devices

Introduced by: Council Member Peter Brehm

Date:

Action:

Summary: This ordinance regulates the use and operation of certain electrically powered micromobility devices within the City of Frederick.

THE CITY OF FREDERICK CITY COUNCIL

	ORDINANCE NO: G-26-	

AN ORDINANCE concerning the use and operation of electric powered micromobility devices

FOR the purpose of regulating the use and operation of certain electrically powered micromobility devices; protecting the public health, safety, and welfare by banning the commercial rental of certain electrically powered micromobility devices; and otherwise generally relating to the regulation and the operation of electrically powered micromobility devices within the City of Frederick.

BY repealing

Chapter 22 Article VI

The Code of the City of Frederick, 1966 (as amended)

BY adding

Chapter 33 Article II

The Code of the City of Frederick, 1966 (as amended)

SECTION I. BE IT ENACTED AND ORDAINED BY THE CITY COUNCIL OF THE CITY OF

FREDERICK, that Chapter 22, Article VI of The Code of the City of Frederick, 1966 (as amended) is hereby repealed in its entirety.

SECTION II. AND BE IT FURTHER ENACTED AND ORDAINED BY THE CITY COUNCIL OF

THE CITY OF FREDERICK, Chapter 33 of The Code of the City of Frederick, 1966 (as amended) is hereby added to read as follows:

CHAPTER 33. PUBLIC SAFETY

ARTICLE II. ELECTRICALLY POWERED MICROMOBILITY DEVICES

§33.3. Definitions.

(a) 		In general. For the purposes of this article, the following words have the meanings indicated. References to the “Transportation Article” means the Transportation Article of the Maryland Code.

(b) 		“Alley” means a street that is intended to provide access to the rear or side of a lot or building in an urban district and is not intended for through vehicular traffic as defined in Transportation Article §21-101(b).

(c) 		“Bicycle path” means any travel way designed and designated by signing or marking for bicycle use only, located within its own right–of–way or in a shared right–of–way, and physically separated from motor vehicle traffic by berm, shoulder, curb, or other similar device as defined in Transportation Article §21-101(c).

(d) 		“Bike lane” means any portion of a roadway or shoulder designated for single or dual directional bicycle flow.

(e) 		“Downtown business district” those nonresidential properties fronting on the following streets:

(1) Record Street;

(2) South Court Street—from West All Saints Street to West Patrick Street;

(3) North Court Street—from West Patrick Street to Second Street;

(4) South Carroll Street—from East All Saints Street to East Patrick Street;

(5) North Carroll Street—from East Patrick Street to East Church Street;

(6) East All Saints Street—from South Market Street to South Carroll Street;

(7) West Patrick Street—from South Bentz Street (including all nonresidential corner properties adjoining the intersection of Bentz Street) to Market Street;

(8) West Church Street—from North Bentz Street to North Market Street;

(9) East Church Street—from North Market Street to East Street;

(10) Council Street;

(11) West Second Street—from North Bentz Street to North Market Street;

(12) East Second Street—from North Market Street to East Street;

(13) West Third Street;

(14) East Third Street—from North Market Street to East Street;

(15) West Fourth Street; and

(16) East Fourth Street—from North Market Street to East Street.

(f) “Downtown commercial district” has the meaning set forth in Chapter 10, Article 1, §10-1(e) of this Code.

(g) 		“Electrically powered micromobility device" or “EPMD” means a range of small, lightweight, low-speed vehicles—typically all classes of e-bikes, e-scooters, e-cargo bikes, EPAMDs, low-speed electric vehicles, OPDMDs, mopeds, electric skateboards, or any other device using electric power to transport a single person for personal, short-distance and urban transportation. This definition applies to any vehicle powered by electricity that cannot be defined as a motor or fuel driven vehicle. This definition does not include an electrically powered vehicle required to be registered as a motor vehicle under Maryland law, such as an e-moto.

(h) 		“Electric bicycle,” “electric assisted bike,” or “e-bike” has the meaning set forth in Transportation Article §11-117.1.

(i) 		“E-moto,” “e-motorcycle,” or “e-moped” is a self-propelled electric motor vehicle having a seat or saddle for the rider, designed to travel on no more than three wheels, and not operated on rails. Because they do not fit the criteria for a traditional bicycle, electric two-wheelers that exceed baseline e-bike limitations are classified and regulated as standard motorcycles or mopeds. An e-moto has footpegs or throttles and no operational pedals, exceeds 750-watt rating, and can exceed 28 miles per hour.

The term e-moto includes, but is not limited to, electric dirt bike, e-motorcycle, electric moped, and high-powered e-bike.

(j) 		“Electric personal assistive mobility device,” or “EPAMD” means a pedestrian device, such as a Segway, that has two non-tandem wheels, is self-balancing, is powered by an electric propulsion system, has a maximum speed capability of 15 miles per hour, and is designed to transport a person, as defined in Transportation Article, §21-101(j).

(k) 	“Electric scooter” or “e-scooter” has the meaning set forth in Transportation Article,

§11.117.2. The term does not include an electrical assisted bicycle, EPAMD, motorcycle, or low-power scooter.

(l) 		“Low-speed electric vehicle” means a vehicle that: (i) is self-propelled utilizing electricity as its primary propulsion method; (ii) has at least three wheels in contact with the ground;

(iii) does not use handlebars to steer; and (iv) exhibits the manufacturer's compliance with 49 CFR 565 or displays a seventeen-character vehicle identification number as provided in 49 CFR 565.

(m) 	“Moped” has the meaning set forth in Transportation Article §11-134.1.

(n) 		“Other power-driven mobility device” or “OPDMD” means any mobility device powered by batteries, fuel, or other engines––whether designed primarily for use by individuals with mobility disabilities––that is used by individuals with mobility disabilities for the purpose of locomotion. OPDMD include but are not limited to golf carts, electronic personal assistance mobility devices (EPAMDs), such as the Segway® PT, not including a wheelchair, electric skateboards, hoverboards, electric wheelchairs, electric unicycles, and any other mobility device designed to operate in areas without defined pedestrian routes, but does not include ATV’s, off-road vehicles, motorized bicycles, motor scooters, or any other motorized vehicles, whether electric or fuel powered.

(o) 		“Path” means a way especially designed for a particular purpose, such as pedestrian traffic or non-motorized vehicular traffic.

(p) 		“Reasonable and prudent speed” means a speed at which it is safe to operate a vehicle, including an EPMD, considering the actual and potential dangers existing. In addition, the operator of a vehicle (a) shall control the speed of the vehicle as necessary to avoid colliding with any person or any vehicle or other conveyance that is on or entering a travel way; (b) shall drive at an appropriate, reduced speed when approaching and crossing an intersection at which cross traffic is not required to stop by a traffic control device; (c) shall drive at an appropriate, reduced speed when approaching and crossing a railroad grade crossing; (d) shall drive at an appropriate, reduced speed when approaching and going around a curve; (e) shall drive at an appropriate, reduced speed when approaching the crest of a grade; (f) shall drive at an appropriate, reduced speed when traveling on any narrow or winding travel way; (g) shall drive at an appropriate, reduced speed when any special danger exists as to pedestrians or other traffic or because of weather or highway conditions.

(q) 	“Roadway” has the meaning set forth in Transportation Article §11-151.

(r) 		“Regulated event” means a public gathering such as a festival or protest that requires government permits and must follow specific health, safety and security regulations.

(s) 		“Shared use pathway” means a paved, off-road facility designed for travel by a variety of non-motorized users, including bicycles, pedestrians, skaters, joggers, and others.

(t) 		“Sidewalk” means that part of a highway that is intended for use by pedestrians, and that is between the lateral curb lines or, in the absence of curbs, the lateral boundary lines of a roadway, and the adjacent property lines as defined in Transportation Article §21-101(w).

(u) 	“Street” has the meaning set forth in Maryland Transportation Article §11-163.

(v) 		“Trail” means an unpaved thoroughfare or track across land used for recreational purposes such as pedestrian activities, including wheelchair use, skating or skateboarding, bicycling or use of other human-powered vehicles, and where permitted, motorized vehicular activities, including all-terrain vehicle riding, motorcycling, snowmobiling, use of off-road light trucks, or use of other off-road motorized vehicles.

(w) 		“Travel Way” means any sidewalk, path, bicycle lane, bicycle path, trail, or shared use pathway that is owned by the City or provided for public use by easement.

§33.4. Authorization.

(a) 		Authority. The City Council, by resolution, may authorize the Frederick Police Department to designate specific travel ways for EPMD use, with or without restrictions, on a temporary basis for a set period of time under 30 days.

(b) 		Restrictions during special or regulated events. The Frederick Police Department or the Department of Public Works may temporarily restrict the use of travel ways by EPMDs in the downtown commercial or business district, Carroll Creek Park, or any other City park or property during special or regulated events. These restrictions apply only for the duration of the event.

(c) 		Restrictions. Restrictions may include the requirement that a person walk while pushing, pulling, or carrying the EPMD, or reduce the speed of the EPMD to a reasonable and prudent speed as defined herein, or prohibiting use during the event.

(d) 		Posting of signs. The Director of Public Works shall post or cause to be posted signs pertaining to the use of EPMDs, with or without restrictions, consistent with this subsection in a location. A person may not use or operate an EPMD in violation of a sign posted in accordance with this subsection.

§33.5. Use generally.

(a) 	Responsibility. Operators of EPMDs have heightened responsibilities due to the higher speeds of these devices and the potential risks they pose to both operators and pedestrians. In addition, EPMDs are subject to all duties applicable to bicycle operators, as set forth in Section 22‑28.1 of this Code, Maryland law, and any other applicable state or federal laws.

(b) 		EPAMDs and OPDMDs. EPAMDs and OPDMDs designed to support individuals with limited mobility or disabilities, such as power wheelchairs, mobility scooters and walking aids with powered assistance, are not included in this article.

(c) 		Exemption for City employees. The provisions of this article do not apply to City employees engaged in the performance of official duties.

§33.6. Use on public property.

(a) 		E-Moto operation prohibited. It shall be unlawful to operate an e-moto on any travel way or non-motorized trail.

(b) Use on shared use paths. 

(1) A person may use or operate an EPMD on any shared use path in the City of Frederick, except as otherwise provided in this article, or where prohibited by posted signs. This prohibition does not apply to an OPDMD or to the operation of an EPMD as part of an activity or event for which the City has issued a permit authorizing such use.

(2) A person may not use or operate an EPMD on the Baker Park Shared Use Path from Baughman's Lane to Bentz Street.

(c) 		Use on sidewalks. A person may not use or operate an EPMD on a sidewalk in the downtown business district. A person operating an EPMD on a sidewalk outside the downtown business district shall yield the right‑of‑way to pedestrians and give an audible signal before overtaking and passing any pedestrian.

(d) 		Use on City-owned travel ways. Except as otherwise provided in section 33.6(b)(2), a person may operate an EPMD on alleys, bike lanes, roadways, and streets unless the City has posted signs, in sufficient numbers and locations, indicating that such use is prohibited.

(e) 		Carroll Creek Linear Park. A person may not use or operate an EPMD in Carroll Creek Linear Park unless the City has posted signs in sufficient numbers and locations indicating that such use is permitted in some manner.

		§33.7. Use on private property.

(a) 		In general. A person may not use or operate an EPMD on any private property without the permission of the owner or person having lawful possession.

(b) 		Private property open to the public. A person may not use or operate an EPMD on any private property that is held open to the public if the owner or lawful occupant has placed signs in sufficient numbers and locations on such property to provide notice that such use or operation is either prohibited or restricted in some manner.

§33.8. Operating regulations.

(a) 		Reasonable and prudent speeds. Except as provided below, EPMDs must be used or operated at reasonable and prudent speeds consistent with conditions that currently exist and as defined in §33-3(p) above.

(b) 		Yield right of way. Ride on the right side of street lanes and yield the right of way to pedestrians and bicycles in the bike lane or bike path. If riding in the bike lane or bike path, riders shall maintain a safe distance.

(c) 	Dismounting. Dismount when required by posted signs.

(d) 		Parking. Park the EPMD upright on a hard surface at a bicycle rack, at a curb or edge of the road or on the edge of a sidewalk, provided that normal and reasonable movement of pedestrian traffic is not impeded. Violations of this provision shall be subject to a fine established by the City Council for violation of Section 13-31 of this Code.

(e) 		Passengers prohibited. It shall be unlawful to operate an EPMD with more passengers than the device was designed to accommodate by the manufacturer.

(f) 		Pedestrians—right-of-way and audible signal. At all times, operators of EPMDs shall yield the right-of-way to pedestrians and human-powered devices and shall give an audible signal before overtaking and passing a pedestrian or human-powered device.

(g) 		Clearance. When overtaking or approaching a pedestrian, other EPMD, bicycle, or other obstacle, the operator of an EPMD must allow at least 3 feet clearance.

(h) 	Safe operations. EPMDs must be operated on the road or on permitted travel ways and must comply with all traffic laws and posted signage. These laws include, but are not limited to, stopping at stop signs, obeying traffic signals, riding in the direction of traffic, the prohibition on wearing headsets or earplugs in both ears, and yielding to other vehicles and pedestrians. A person may not operate an EPMD while impaired.

§33.9. Required equipment.

(a) 	Helmets. Except as otherwise required by state or federal law, any person under 16 years of age who operates an EPMD, except for an OPDMD, or being carried as a passenger on such micromobility device on a public way, bicycle path or on any other public right-of-way shall wear a helmet. Said helmet shall fit the person's head, shall be secured to the person's head by straps while the micromobility device is being operated, and shall meet the standards for helmets established by the United States Consumer Product Safety Commission pursuant to 16 C.F.R. 1203. These requirements shall not apply to a passenger if the passenger is in an enclosed trailer or other device which adequately holds the passenger in place and protects the passenger's head from impact in a crash. No person operating such a micromobility device shall permit any other person to ride as a passenger on such micromobility device unless such passenger is wearing such protective headgear.

(b) 		Lights. Every EPMD when in use at any time from a half hour after sunset to a half hour before sunrise must be equipped with a lamp on the front that emits a white light visible at a distance of at 500 feet to the front and with a red lamp on the rear that shall be visible from all distances up to 600 feet to the rear.

(c) 		Brakes. An EPMD must be equipped with brakes that allow it to stop from 10 mph within 15 feet on dry, level pavement.

(d) 		Bell. An EPMD must be equipped with a bell or other device capable of emitting an audible signal detectable at a distance of at least 100 feet. An EPMD may not be equipped with, nor may any person use upon an EPMD, any siren or whistle.

§33.10. Modifications. It shall be unlawful to make any aftermarket modifications to an EPMD to increase either (i) the manufacturer designed speed or propulsion power of a micromobility device or (ii) the manufacturer designed passenger capacity of a micromobility device, except as may be allowed by regulation.

		§33.11. Education and Public Outreach

(a) 		Signage. The Department of Public Works, in coordination with the Frederick Police Department, may install signage is strategically placed when needed along travel ways to inform EPMD operators of their responsibilities and any applicable restrictions, may include guidance on appropriate speed. All signage must be clear, easy to read, and include contact information for reporting alleged violations.

(b) 		Public outreach strategy. The Office of Public Affairs, in coordination with the Department of Public Works and the Frederick Police Department, shall develop and maintain a public outreach strategy to educate the community about the provisions of this article and the safe operation of EPMDs. The strategy must include an informational handout available both as a printed flyer and in digital formats through contemporary communication channels, including the City website and social media. All materials must be reviewed, updated, and proactively distributed to the public at least once per year.

(c) 		Training. The Department of Parks and Recreation, or other designated City department, may provide training opportunities for the public on the proper and safe operation of EPMDs. Training may be offered through in‑person classes and/or virtual instructional videos.

(d) 		Collaboration with FCPS schools. The Department of Parks and Recreation, or other designated City department, may collaborate with FCPS schools located within the City of Frederick to provide educational materials and informational sessions to students regarding the provisions of this article and the safe operation of EPMDs.

§33.12. Enforcement and penalty.

(a) 		Municipal infractions. Except as otherwise provided in this subsection, each violation of this article is deemed a municipal infraction punishable by a fine of one hundred dollars ($100.00). 

(b) 		Collision with a pedestrian. A collision between an operator of an EPMD and a pedestrian is deemed a municipal infraction punishable by a fine of one thousand dollars ($1,000.00).

(c) 		Helmets. A violation of section 33-9(a) will be enforced by the issuance of a warning that informs the offender of the requirements of that section.

(d) 		Impoundment. In addition to the penalty set forth in subsection (b) of this section, an electric vehicle may be impounded by an authorized enforcement officer under the conditions set forth in §33-13 below. Upon payment of any fine imposed under subsection (b), the owner of the electric vehicle may reclaim the vehicle.

(e) 		Failure to reclaim. Any EPMD not reclaimed within forty-five (45) days of impound will be deemed abandoned and discarded by the owner thereof and disposed in accordance with Chapter 17, Article III of this Code.

(f) 		Responsibility of Parents. A parent may not allow their minor child to violate any of the provisions of this article. Violation of this subsection is deemed a municipal infraction, punishable by a fine of one hundred dollars ($100.00). 

ARTICLE III. SHAREABLE, DOCKLESS ELECTRIC BICYCLES, AND ELECTRIC SCOOTERS

§33.13. Definitions.  In this article the following words have the meanings indicated.

(a) 		“Commercially rented electric bicycles and electric scooters” means an electric bicycle or electric scooter managed, maintained, possessed, leased, or owned by a private or commercial entity that is offered to an individual for use on a limited basis by paying a fee.

(b) 		“Dockless” means that a vehicle does not require an individual user to return and lock the vehicle to an authorized fixed station once the user has completed use of the vehicle.

(c) 	“Electric Bicycle” has the meaning set forth in 33-3(g) of this Chapter.

(d) 	“Electric Scooter” has the meaning set forth in 33-3(i) of this Chapter

(e) 	“Officer” means a sworn member of the Frederick Police Department.

(f) 		“Shareable” means that a vehicle that is accessed via an on-demand portal, whether a smartphone application, membership card, or similar method; is operated by a private entity that owns, manages, and maintains devices for shared use by members of the public; and is available to members of the public in unstaffed, self-service locations.

§33.14. Offering and penalty.

(a) 		Offering. Commercially rented shareable, dockless electric bicycles and electric scooters are prohibited within the City. A person may not provide or offer such vehicles anywhere within the City.

(b) 		Penalty. Violation of this section is a municipal infraction punishable by a fine of $400.00. Each incident is a separate violation. Each day a violation continues will be deemed a separate offense.

§33.15. Use or operation.

(a) 		Operating. A person may not use or operate a sharable, dockless electric bicycle or electric scooter on a travel way as defined in Section 33-3(y) of this chapter.

(b) 		Penalty. It is a violation of this subsection if an officer has probable cause to believe that the person has committed or is committing a violation of this subsection and is punishable by fine in the amount of $60.00.

§33.16. Impoundment.

(a) 		In general. In addition to the penalties set forth in this article, a shareable, dockless electric bicycle or electric speed scooter offered for a fee or operated in violation of this article may be impounded by an officer.

(b) 		Fees. The owner of a shareable, dockless bicycle or electric scooter may reclaim the vehicle upon payment of an initial impound fee of $40 and a daily storage and administration fee of $5.

(c) 		Notice - issuance. Once a shareable, dockless electric bicycle or electric scooter has been impounded, the Frederick Police Department shall make a good faith attempt to determine the name and address of the owner of such device by serial number, vehicle identification number (VIN), or such other means as are reasonably ascertainable through inspection of the exterior of such vehicle. In those cases where the name and address of the owner of the subject vehicle are determined, written notice shall be sent to the owner by certified mail, return receipt requested, or by personal service acknowledged by signature of the registered owner or other responsible party.

(d) 	Notice - contents. The written notice required by this section must contain:

(1) 		A description of the subject vehicle, including serial numbers, vehicle identification number, or other identifying information,

(2) 	The name and address of the owner of the vehicle; and

(3) 		The dates and descriptions of the violations that establish the grounds for impoundment, the unpaid amounts of the civil penalties for each violation, and the process by which the vehicle may be reclaimed. A copy of each citation or other document providing the required information attached to the notice is sufficient to satisfy this requirement. Where multiple vehicles owned by the same person are impounded on a single day, a single notice listing all impounded devices is sufficient.

(e) 		Failure to reclaim. Any vehicle not reclaimed within forty-five (45) days of impound will be deemed abandoned and discarded by the owner thereof and disposed in accordance with Chapter 17, Article III of this Code.
