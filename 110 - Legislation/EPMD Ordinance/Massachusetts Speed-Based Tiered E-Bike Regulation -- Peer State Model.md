# Massachusetts Speed-Based Tiered E-Bike Regulation -- Peer State Model

## Summary

Massachusetts Governor Maura Healey filed S.3077 in May 2026, proposing a three-tier regulatory framework for micromobility devices based on speed capability. The bill applies the heaviest regulatory requirements -- license, registration, and insurance -- only to the fastest tier, while establishing different rules for lower-speed devices. The bill takes effect January 2028 if passed.

## Key Points

**Tier structure:**
- Tier 3 (41+ mph): Annual registration, liability insurance, license plate required; banned from bike lanes and multi-use paths
- Tier 2 and 1: Subject to separate restrictions; Tier 1 and 2 require riders to follow bike lane and path rules; helmets mandatory for riders 16 and under

The core regulatory logic is speed-based tiering rather than motor type or class labeling -- a different approach than the federal Class 1/2/3 framework.

**Political context:**
The bill was filed by the governor (not the legislature) and is titled "An Act to Enhance the Safe Use of Micromobility Devices (S. 3077)." Effective date: January 2028 if passed, giving cities time to prepare.

## Relevance

Frederick's EPMD ordinance (G-26-___) is still in draft, pending legal review and council feedback. The Massachusetts bill illustrates what a state-level regulatory framework looks like when it's built around speed capability rather than ambiguous class distinctions.

The equity argument César has built for Frederick -- Class 1 and 2 path access is an equity issue -- is structurally supported by the Massachusetts approach, which treats the lower-speed tiers as essentially path-compatible. The Massachusetts bill does not ban lower-speed devices from paths; it reserves the heaviest restrictions for the fastest tier.

The AMAC concerns documented in the EPMD brief (road speed language unenforceable, class distinctions unverifiable at roadside) would be partially addressed by a speed-based approach, since speed is at least theoretically measurable.
