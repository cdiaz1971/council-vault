# City of Frederick Micromobility Ordinance Meeting Brief

Prepared September 23, 2026  
Draft reviewed: UPDATED_CURRENT_PB_EBike Ordinance Draft_20260914.docx

**Overall assessment**

The ordinance would regulate privately owned electric micromobility devices, restrict their use in specified locations, establish equipment and operating requirements, prohibit certain shared dockless rental services, and create fines and impoundment provisions.

It is **not a citywide ban on personally owned e-bikes or scooters**. Its general approach is to allow covered devices in many locations while prohibiting them on the specified Baker Park path, on downtown business district sidewalks, and in Carroll Creek Linear Park unless signs permit use.

The principal meeting task is to distinguish policy choices from drafting defects. Council can decide the desired restrictions, penalties, and rental policy, but the present text needs substantial clarification to communicate and enforce those decisions consistently.

All draft section references below refer to the [September 14 ordinance draft](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1Jv4-3qIrHBrNdWvvm7IzTbv2DfT77l_d/edit).

**1. Legislative structure and effective date**

The draft would:

- Repeal Chapter 22, Article VI in its entirety.
- Add micromobility rules in Chapter 33, Article II, §§33.3–33.12.
- Add shared dockless e-bike and scooter rules in Article III, §§33.13–33.16.
- Take effect on the date the Mayor approves it.
- Repeal inconsistent ordinance provisions to the extent of the inconsistency.

The introductory “BY adding” clause lists only Article II, although the operative text also adds Article III. Section II says that Chapter 33 is being added, rather than clearly describing the addition of articles to that chapter. These provisions should be reconciled with the intended codification.

The ordinance number and passage/approval dates remain blank. The legal-sufficiency signature block does not establish that legal review has been completed.

**Implementation consequence:** The draft contains no delayed start, general education-only period, or requirement that outreach and signs be ready before the ordinance becomes effective.

**2. Which devices are covered**

The umbrella term “electrically powered micromobility device,” or EPMD, includes examples such as e-bikes, e-scooters, cargo e-bikes, electric skateboards, mopeds, low-speed electric vehicles, and certain mobility devices.

However, the definition also excludes electrically powered vehicles required to be registered as motor vehicles under Maryland law. E-motos are separately defined and expressly prohibited on travel ways and nonmotorized trails.

Three distinctions matter:

**E-bikes.** The draft incorporates Maryland’s definition. State law requires fully operable pedals, two or three wheels, and a motor rated at 750 watts or less. Class 1 provides pedal assistance through 20 mph; Class 2 can assist without pedaling through 20 mph; Class 3 provides pedal assistance through 28 mph. These are motor-assistance thresholds, not blanket permission to ride at those speeds everywhere. [Maryland Transportation §11-117.1](https://mgaleg.maryland.gov/mgawebsite/laws/StatuteText?article=gtr&section=11-117.1)

**E-scooters.** The incorporated state provision defines an “electric low speed scooter,” including a weight below 100 pounds, handlebars, a standing platform, and capability up to 20 mph. The draft subsequently excludes a “low-power scooter,” an undefined term that could cause confusion about the very category being incorporated. [Maryland Transportation §11-117.2](https://mgaleg.maryland.gov/mgawebsite/laws/StatuteText?article=gtr&section=11-117.2)

**E-motos.** The draft’s detailed sentence combines no operational pedals, more than 750 watts, and capability above 28 mph. It then includes “high-powered e-bike” among its examples. This leaves uncertainty about a powerful device that retains functional pedals or fails only one e-bike criterion. Council should request a classification rule that does not depend on marketing names.

Additional definition problems include:

- “Single person” in the EPMD definition is difficult to reconcile with cargo bikes and the express allowance for manufacturer-designed passenger capacity.
- The exclusion of “motor or fuel driven” vehicles is imprecise when every EPMD uses an electric motor.
- Low-speed electric vehicles are included as examples, but Maryland includes low-speed vehicles within its motor-vehicle definition. The draft’s registration exclusion may therefore remove devices that its examples appear to include. [Maryland Transportation §11-135](https://www.mgaleg.maryland.gov/mgawebsite/Laws/StatuteText?article=gtr&enactments=false&section=11-135)
- Disability-related devices are both included in the definitions and partially excluded elsewhere.

**Meeting objective:** Obtain a simple classification chart covering ordinary e-bikes, throttle e-bikes, scooters, modified bikes, electric dirt bikes, cargo bikes, and disability mobility devices.

**3. Where devices could be used**

The following summarizes Article II’s proposed rules, subject to its exemptions and other applicable law.

| Location | Proposed treatment | Qualification |
|---|---|---|
| Shared-use paths generally | Allowed | Other ordinance restrictions and posted prohibitions apply. |
| Baker Park Shared Use Path from Baughman’s Lane to Bentz Street | Prohibited | The text identifies this path segment, not all of Baker Park. |
| Sidewalks in the defined downtown business district | Prohibited | The definition’s frontage list determines the boundary. |
| Sidewalks outside that district | Contemplates operation | Riders must yield to pedestrians and signal before passing. |
| Alleys, bike lanes, roadways, and streets | Generally allowed under §33.6(d) | Sufficiently placed City signs may prohibit use; other laws still apply. |
| Carroll Creek Linear Park | Prohibited by default | City signs can permit use in some manner. |
| Private property | Owner or lawful possessor permission required | Publicly accessible private property may have posted restrictions. |
| Travel ways and nonmotorized trails | E-motos prohibited | This is a separate prohibition from the EPMD rules. |

**Downtown boundary concern.** “Downtown business district” is defined through nonresidential properties fronting on 16 listed streets or street segments. North and South Market Street are not listed as frontages; Market Street appears as an endpoint for other streets. East Patrick Street is likewise not independently listed as a frontage.

That does not establish that every corner property on those streets falls outside the district, but it means the text should not be described as a clear ban on all downtown sidewalks.

The property-based definition also raises questions about residential frontage, mixed-use buildings, and continuity along a block. “Downtown commercial district” is separately incorporated from Chapter 10 and used for event restrictions; the sidewalk prohibition uses “downtown business district.”

**Recommended clarification:** Adopt or publish a map with continuous, unambiguous boundaries and confirm that the two district terms are intentional.

**4. Operating and equipment requirements**

Sections 33.8–33.10 would require:

- Reasonable and prudent speed for actual conditions.
- Yielding to pedestrians and human-powered devices.
- An audible signal before overtaking pedestrians or human-powered devices.
- At least three feet of clearance when overtaking or approaching a pedestrian, another EPMD, a bicycle, or another obstacle.
- Compliance with traffic laws, signs, and required dismounting.
- No impaired operation.
- No passengers beyond manufacturer-designed capacity.
- Upright parking on a hard surface at specified locations without impeding normal pedestrian movement.
- Required lighting, brakes, and an audible signaling device.
- No aftermarket increases in manufacturer-designed speed, propulsion power, or passenger capacity, except as allowed by regulation.

**There is no general numerical riding-speed limit.** The 10 mph figure is a brake-testing standard. The 20/28 mph figures associated with e-bike classes are classification thresholds.

The three-foot rule applies to “approaching” as well as overtaking and includes obstacles. The draft does not explain the expected behavior on a narrow path where that clearance is impossible. It should specify whether riders must stop, wait, or dismount.

The passenger subsection is titled “Passengers prohibited,” but the operative language prohibits only exceeding designed capacity. The heading should match the rule.

The modification exception does not identify the regulation, issuing authority, or criteria. The prohibition addresses making modifications; it does not separately and clearly prohibit operating a device already modified elsewhere.

**Equipment details**

| Requirement | Draft standard |
|---|---|
| Helmet | Generally applies to operators and passengers under 16 on specified public ways, with exceptions and an inconsistent final passenger sentence. |
| Front light | White, visible 500 feet ahead. |
| Rear light | Red lamp, visible up to 600 feet behind. |
| Lighting hours | Half an hour after sunset through half an hour before sunrise. |
| Brakes | Stop from 10 mph within 15 feet on dry, level pavement. |
| Bell or other signal | Audible at least 100 feet away; sirens and whistles prohibited. |

These provisions should not be presented as a verbatim restatement of state law. Maryland’s bicycle equipment law uses poor-visibility conditions to trigger lighting, permits qualifying rear reflector/lamp arrangements, and permits rather than requires a bell. The draft imposes different requirements. Counsel should confirm their authority and how overlapping requirements will be explained. [Maryland Transportation §21-1207](https://mgaleg.maryland.gov/mgawebsite/laws/StatuteText?article=gtr&section=21-1207)

**5. Fines and impoundment**

| Conduct or charge | Draft consequence |
|---|---|
| General Article II violation | $100 municipal infraction |
| Collision between an EPMD operator and a pedestrian | $1,000 municipal infraction |
| Helmet violation under §33.9(a) | Warning |
| Parent allowing a minor child to violate Article II | $100 municipal infraction |
| Improper parking | Fine tied to City Code §13-31; no amount stated here |
| Offering prohibited shared dockless rentals | $400; each incident and each continuing day are separately addressed |
| Operating a covered shared dockless device on a travel way | $60 |
| Article III impound fee | $40 initially |
| Article III storage and administration | $5 per day |
| Unclaimed impounded device | Deemed abandoned after 45 days; disposal cross-referenced to Chapter 17, Article III |

**Collision penalty.** Section 33.12(b) makes the collision itself the infraction. It does not expressly require negligent or reckless operation, violation of another rule, injury, or fault by the operator. It also does not expressly identify the liable party as clearly as it could.

The text therefore appears to create a collision-based offense without a stated fault requirement. Whether that is intended and legally appropriate should be resolved directly.

**Impoundment.** Section 33.12(d) refers to conditions in §33.13, which contains definitions, not impoundment conditions. Section 33.16 contains impoundment provisions, but those apply to shared dockless rental devices.

Simply changing the reference to §33.16 would not fully solve the problem for a privately owned device involved in a collision. Article II needs its own clear grounds, authorized officers, notice, release conditions, and review process, or an express incorporation of suitable procedures.

Article II links impoundment and release to the collision penalty. It should not be described as clearly authorizing confiscation for every $100 violation.

Article III requires efforts to identify owners and written notice when an owner is identified. It does not itself expressly provide a deadline for that notice or describe a hearing to contest impoundment. Counsel should identify any procedures supplied by other law and determine what needs to be stated here.

**Parent responsibility.** “Allow” is undefined. The draft does not specify whether knowledge, permission, or failure to exercise reasonable supervision must be established. Council should also clarify how this provision interacts with warning-only helmet enforcement.

**6. Disability access requires substantive revision**

Section 33.5(b) excludes EPAMDs and OPDMDs “designed to support” people with limited mobility or disabilities. Federal accessibility rules also address devices used because of a mobility disability even when those devices were not designed primarily for that purpose.

Federal rules distinguish wheelchairs from other power-driven mobility devices. The draft’s OPDMD definition excludes a wheelchair in one phrase but expressly includes electric wheelchairs later. It also lists powered devices while broadly excluding “any other motorized vehicles.”

The appropriate correction is to align definitions and exemptions with the federal framework, including assessment of legitimate safety requirements for particular devices and facilities. A design-only exemption is not a reliable substitute. [DOJ mobility-device guidance](https://www.ada.gov/resources/opdmds/)

The OPDMD exception in §33.6(b)(1) is attached to the general shared-use-path paragraph. Its relationship to the separate Baker Park prohibition is unclear. The exemption should be stated consistently across the affected location restrictions.

**Meeting question:** Will the City Attorney and accessibility staff confirm that the ordinance protects qualifying disability-related use consistently, including use of devices not originally marketed as disability aids?

**7. Shared dockless rental policy**

Article III combines three defined concepts: commercial rental, shareability, and docklessness.

“Shareable” involves on-demand access, private management, and unstaffed self-service locations. “Dockless” means users need not return and lock the device to an authorized fixed station.

Accordingly, the offering prohibition is best understood as targeting **commercial shared dockless services**. It should not be summarized as an explicit prohibition of every rental e-bike, staffed rental shop, or docked system.

Important distinctions remain:

- The offering prohibition applies anywhere within the City.
- The operation prohibition applies to a defined “travel way.”
- The operation clause does not repeat the commercial-rental qualification.
- “Travel way” does not expressly list ordinary streets, alleys, or roadways, although another subsection permits EPMDs on those facilities.
- The operation penalty is worded as though an officer’s probable cause makes the conduct a violation. The underlying offense and the standard for issuing a citation should be separated.

Council should state whether it intends a targeted dockless-service ban or a broader rental policy and revise the operative language accordingly.

| §33.12(d) | Refers to §33.13 for impoundment conditions | Supply the correct substantive impoundment framework. |
| §33.13 definition of electric bicycle | Refers to §33-3(g), the EPMD definition | Electric bicycle is currently §33.3(h). |

| §33.8(e) | Heading says passengers are prohibited | Match heading to capacity-based restriction. |
| §33.16(a) | Uses “electric speed scooter” | Use the intended defined term consistently. |

The reasonable-speed reference to subsection **(p) is correct** under the current Word numbering.

Additional cleanup is needed for “shared use path” versus “shared use pathway,” “bike lane” versus “bicycle lane,” “shareable” versus “sharable,” section punctuation, and incomplete wording in the signage provision.

**Helmet ambiguity deserves separate attention.** The opening sentence generally addresses people under 16, but the final sentence prohibits carrying “any other person” without a helmet. That could be read to impose an all-age passenger requirement. The enclosed-trailer exception also needs reconciliation with Maryland’s express coverage of children riding in bicycle trailers. The draft’s opening state-law qualification matters, but clear operative text would be better. [Maryland Transportation §21-1207.1](https://mgaleg.maryland.gov/mgawebsite/Laws/StatuteText?article=gtr&section=21-1207.1)

**9. State-law and existing-code coordination**

Maryland generally permits e-bikes where bicycles may travel, while providing specific rules for bicycle paths and natural-surface trails. Class 3 path access is more restricted unless specified conditions or local authorization apply. State law also prohibits someone under 16 from operating a Class 3 e-bike on a public highway, while allowing an appropriately accommodated passenger under 16. The draft’s helmet rule does not replace that age restriction. [Maryland Transportation §21-1205.2](https://mgaleg.maryland.gov/mgawebsite/laws/StatuteText?article=gtr&enactments=false&section=21-1205.2)

Maryland authorizes local bicycle regulation on highways under local jurisdiction. That supports local regulatory authority but does not independently resolve every device classification, equipment requirement, or facility restriction proposed here. [Maryland Transportation §25-102](https://mgaleg.maryland.gov/mgawebsite/laws/StatuteText?article=gtr&enactments=false&section=25-102)

The published City Code identifies existing §22-28.1 as addressing nonmotorized wheeled vehicles and Chapter 22, Article VI as addressing shared dockless electric low-speed scooters. Section 22-28.1 is outside the article expressly repealed by this draft. [Published City Code, Chapter 22](https://library.municode.com/MD/Frederick/codes/code_of_ordinances?nodeId=PTIITHCO_CH22STSI_ARTVIISICA)

Before adoption, request a complete comparison showing:

- Which existing duties §33.5(a) imports through §22-28.1.
- How overlapping park, sidewalk, and bicycle rules operate together.
- Which rental provisions are retained, expanded, or changed.
- The applicable parking fine under §13-31.
- The disposal procedures supplied by Chapter 17, Article III.
- Which provisions the general inconsistency/repealer clause would displace.

Those incorporated provisions were not all independently verified in full for this brief; no specific parking-fine amount or complete existing-law comparison is asserted here.

**10. Administration and implementation**

Section 33.4 permits Council, by resolution, to authorize police designations of specific travel ways for periods **under 30 days**. Separate event restrictions may be imposed by Police or Public Works for the event’s duration. Restrictions may require walking the device, reduced speed, or no use.

The text should clarify who may authorize permanent restrictions or permissions through signs, especially at Carroll Creek, and how those powers relate to the temporary authorization provision.

Section 33.11 makes the outreach strategy mandatory. Public Affairs must coordinate with Public Works and Police, provide printed and digital information, and review, update, and proactively distribute materials at least annually.

Public training and collaboration with FCPS are discretionary. General educational signage is also discretionary, while §33.4 contains mandatory sign language for its restrictions.

No appropriation, staffing estimate, sign inventory, enforcement protocol, or performance-reporting requirement appears in the draft.

The City-employee exemption applies broadly to Article II whenever employees perform official duties. Council should determine whether that breadth is intentional or whether exemptions should be limited to operationally necessary exceptions.

**11. Priority questions for the meeting**

1. **What devices are intended to be regulated, and can every common device be classified without conflicting definitions?**
2. **Does Council intend to prohibit riding along Market Street sidewalks, and does the adopted boundary actually accomplish that?**
3. **What evidence supports the Baker Park segment restriction and Carroll Creek default prohibition, and what alternative routes remain available?**
4. **Should the $1,000 offense require fault, unsafe conduct, or injury, or is any collision intentionally sufficient?**
5. **When may a personally owned device be impounded, and how can its owner promptly contest the seizure and obtain release?**
6. **Are disability protections consistent across definitions, exemptions, park restrictions, and event rules?**
7. **Is the commercial policy limited to shared dockless services, and is the narrower scope of the riding prohibition intentional?**
8. **What should a rider do when three feet of clearance is unavailable?**
9. **Who decides permanent sign-based restrictions, and what notice and public process apply?**
10. **Should implementation begin immediately, or after signs, outreach, staff training, and procedures are ready?**

**Recommended meeting position**

Request a corrected draft before final adoption, accompanied by a boundary map, a comparison with existing City law, an enforcement and impoundment protocol, and a short implementation cost estimate.

Council’s immediate policy decisions are the desired location restrictions, rental-service scope, collision standard, and implementation timing. The incorrect references, contradictory definitions, and unclear enforcement procedures should be corrected regardless of which policy choices Council makes.
