What groups benefit most from municipal ID legislation?

=== Answer ===

 Immigrants, particularly those who are undocumented or under-documented, benefit most from municipal ID legislation as they often lack valid identification to present to local authorities, which can prevent them from accessing various services and living a normal life. Other key constituencies that can benefit include unbanked residents, transgender residents (for correct gender identity representation), homeless residents (for government-issued identification without a stable address), and communities seeking improved relations between police and immigrant residents.

 What precautions must be taken to protect the data of applicants for municipal ID?

=== Answer ===

 To protect the data of applicants for municipal ID, the following precautions should be taken as outlined in the context:

1. Prohibit retention of underlying application documents (or copies thereof) altogether, unless necessary. If a city feels it must keep copies, they can limit the types of documents that may be retained, redact non-essential information, require a judicial warrant for disclosure of retained documents, and destroy retained documents after a period of time.

2. Limit access to the data by keeping the number of staff who have access to it minimal. The database in which the data is stored should not be linked to any other databases.

3. Issuing a public report every quarter listing all requests for access to the data that it has received from any third party and an account of how each request was handled.

4. The law should be clear that this protection includes not only data contained on application forms, but also the fact of any individual having submitted an application at all.

5. To make the ID card as accessible as possible to community members who have difficulty obtaining other forms of government-issued ID, cities should accept as many different kinds of documentation in as many different combinations as possible for both identity and residency verification. They should also be flexible in how applicants may combine these documents.

6. Special steps should be taken to address the burdens faced by homeless individuals and victims of domestic violence in proving residency, allowing them to use alternative addresses such as a local family member's home, a shelter, social service agency, or legal service provider's address.

How do municipal IDs interact with state and federal laws?

=== Answer ===

 Municipal IDs interact with state and federal laws in the following ways:

1. State Laws: In home-rule states like New York, local governments have broad authority to govern matters of local interest. However, states can limit local power where an issue involves a matter of "substantial state interest." In New York, there is no state law that preempts municipal IDs. Enacting identity documents is one of the many enumerated rights in a state statute governing cities.

2. Federal Laws: There is no federal law prohibiting a municipality from issuing its own identification cards. However, localities should be aware of limitations based on federal law. One significant limitation is preemption due to the federal government's exclusive authority over national immigration law and policy. Municipal ID programs should not attempt to regulate immigration or interfere with the execution of immigration policy.

   The federal REAL ID Act sets out certain requirements for identity documents to be accepted for federal purposes, but it does not prohibit states or localities from issuing IDs that do not comply with REAL ID. Municipal IDs are not compliant with the federal REAL ID Act and will not be accepted as valid identity documents for federal purposes such as entry into a federal building, military base, or for domestic or international travel.

   Municipal IDs do not conflict with the Personal Responsibility and Work Opportunity Reconciliation Act (PRWORA) of 1996 because they are not considered benefits under its definition. However, cardholders should be aware that the cards cannot extend benefits to otherwise ineligible people.

   It's also important for local governments to make it clear that municipal IDs are not intended for federal identification purposes, as their use could have serious consequences for undocumented cardholders in certain situations.

In summary, while municipal IDs can be issued by local governments without conflict with most state and federal laws, they should be clearly understood as being intended for local use only and not for federal purposes.
