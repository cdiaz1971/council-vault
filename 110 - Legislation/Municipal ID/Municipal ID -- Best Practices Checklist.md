# Municipal ID -- Best Practices Checklist

Distilled from national program implementations (New York City, Hartford, San Francisco) and the Government Law Center toolkit. These are the design decisions that separate durable, effective programs from ones that collapse under political pressure or fail the communities they're meant to serve.

## Program Structure

The program must be established by ordinance, not executive policy. This ensures council accountability, enforcement mechanisms, and protection against reversal by a future administration. All city agencies and offices -- including law enforcement -- must be required by ordinance to accept the card as proof of identity and residency.

## Eligibility and Access

Eligibility must be status-neutral: race, national origin, immigration status, gender, sexual orientation, housing status, conviction record, and income cannot be disqualifying factors. The only requirements are proof of identity and proof of Frederick residency.

The document list for both identity and residency should be broad and accept combinations. Foreign passports, consular IDs, ITINs, and EBT cards should be explicitly accepted. For residency, utility bills, lease agreements, employer pay stubs, and school enrollment verification all qualify. The administering agency should retain discretion to accept other documentation.

Special accommodation is required for homeless applicants and survivors of domestic violence. These populations cannot always produce a fixed address. The ordinance should allow shelter verification letters, social service agency addresses, and similar alternatives.

## Gender and Identity

Cardholders must be able to designate their own gender, including a non-binary option, regardless of what appears on other identity documents. This is both a policy commitment to transgender residents and a design requirement that must be built into the card system from the start, not retrofitted.

## Financial Access

Do not attach a pre-paid debit card function to the ID card itself. This approach has failed in multiple cities due to the complexity of banking regulation and the risk of card rejection. Instead, negotiate separately with local financial institutions to accept the municipal ID as sufficient identification to open accounts. The value is in bank acceptance of the card, not in the card being the account.

## Privacy and Data

Prohibit retention of original application documents or copies. No application data -- including the fact of participation -- should be shared with any government entity, federal or otherwise. The ordinance should require Frederick to defend against any compelled disclosure to the maximum extent permitted by law. Issue quarterly public reports on any third-party data access requests received and how each was handled.

## Community Infrastructure

Train Frederick Police Department to accept the card in the field and incorporate it into patrol manuals. Partner with local businesses, cultural venues, libraries, and health providers to create tangible cardholder benefits. Establish a fee waiver process for residents who cannot afford the application fee. Administer the program through an office with substantial public contact, with online application availability.

## Why This Matters for César

The feasibility study directed by your memo should use this checklist as its evaluation framework. Every item here has a documented failure mode in another city's implementation. The gender designation requirement and the financial institution partnership approach in particular need to be scoped before the program design is finalized.
