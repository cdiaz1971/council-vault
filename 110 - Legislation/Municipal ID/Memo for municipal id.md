**RE:** Exploration and Introduction of Municipal Identification (ID) Program Legislation

**Purpose of Legislation**

I am submitting legislation to explore and potentially establish a Municipal Identification (ID) Program for residents of the City of Frederick. The intent is to increase access to essential city services, improve public safety outcomes, and reduce administrative barriers faced by residents who currently lack government-issued identification.

**Background and Rationale**

Many residents in Frederick face challenges obtaining a Maryland state ID due to documentation requirements, cost, or transportation limitations. These barriers disproportionately affect undocumented residents, seniors, people experiencing homelessness, domestic violence survivors, and formerly incarcerated individuals.

Lack of identification limits the ability to:

- Access city services and facilities
    
- Interact confidently with law enforcement
    
- Open bank accounts or cash checks
    
- Sign leases or verify identity with landlords
    
- Participate fully in civic and community activities
    

Other municipalities nationwide have successfully implemented such programs and report improvements in community trust, financial inclusion, and service delivery.

**Relationship to Existing State Programs**

This legislation does not duplicate state functions nor replace state-issued identification.

Maryland already offers:

- State non-driver identification cards through the MVA
    
- REAL ID–compliant credentials
    
- Mobile digital ID options
    

However, these programs still require underlying documentation that many residents do not possess. A municipal ID would fill an access gap by providing a locally recognized credential for city services, while encouraging — not replacing — participation in state programs.

**Intended Uses**

A Frederick Municipal ID could be used for:

- Identification when interacting with city departments
    
- Library access and recreation programs
    
- Interactions with law enforcement
    
- Access to city facilities
    
- Verification for certain nonprofit and partner services
    

Additional uses could be explored through partnerships with local banks, health providers, and community organizations.

**Next Steps Proposed in Legislation**

The legislation will:

1. Direct staff to conduct a feasibility study and fiscal impact analysis
    
2. Evaluate legal and privacy considerations
    
3. Conduct stakeholder engagement with:
    
    - Frederick Police Department
        
    - Frederick County agencies
        
    - Local nonprofits and immigrant advocacy groups
        
    - Financial institutions and business community
        
4. Report findings and recommendations back to the Council
    

**Conclusion**

A Municipal ID Program presents a practical tool to strengthen inclusion, public safety, and access to services in Frederick. Advancing this legislation allows us to evaluate the opportunity responsibly and transparently with full public input.

I look forward to working with each of you as we explore this initiative.
