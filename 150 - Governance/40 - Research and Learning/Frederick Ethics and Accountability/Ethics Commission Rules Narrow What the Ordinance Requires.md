# Ethics Commission Rules Narrow What the Ordinance Requires

## Summary
Cathy Anderson documents that Ethics Commission Rules of Procedure narrow Chapter 21, that 2026 advisory opinions 26-4 and 26-5 were signed five months after being heard, and that the attorney hired to investigate the complaint against the Mayor is listed as advising on the Mayor's own opinion request.

## Key Points
Rules narrower than the ordinance. Section 21-3(b) bases advisory opinions on the facts "provided or reasonably available" to the Commission. Rule 4.3 limits it to facts provided by the requester, and opinion 26-5 follows the rule. Rule 4.4 protects only the requester's name, leaving everything else to case-by-case discretion: 26-3 named the requester's employer, 26-4 left a profile matching only one Council member, and 26-5 hid the name but kept enough to identify the Mayor.

Opinion timing. Between September 18 and 23 the Commission's opinions page went from three 2026 opinions to five. They are numbered by signing date, not meeting date. The two heard first, 26-4 (met March 18 and May 11) and 26-5 (met April 1), were signed last, on August 21 and September 22. The other three were signed within 12 days to six weeks. Section 21-3(b) requires the Commission to respond promptly.

Counsel changes by opinion. The City Attorney advised on 26-1, 26-3, and 26-4. Special counsel Leslie Powell advised on 26-2. Janice Rockwell, listed as "Independent Attorney," advised on 26-5. Rockwell's engagement letter, accepted March 17, hired her to investigate Nash's complaint against the Mayor. If the April 1 meeting occurred as the opinion states, the investigator heard the Mayor's account in an advisory setting before interviewing the complainant on May 15. No notice, agenda, or minutes for April 1 have been found.

Overlapping matters. 26-5 and Nash's July 22 complaint both concern the Mayor's spouse's nonprofit. 26-5 was signed two months after that complaint was filed, and the safeguards it approves (recusal, declining to sign support letters) could be cited in defense, even though the Frederick News-Post reported on September 17 that the Mayor did not issue a formal written recusal and "might have signed a draft" of support letters.

## Relevance
Rules of procedure that narrow an ordinance are squarely a Council fix: the ordinance can specify what the rules may not change, set a deadline for advisory opinions, set a consistent anonymization standard, and bar one attorney from serving as investigator on a complaint and adviser on a related opinion. These are Anderson's characterizations of records she obtained; the underlying opinions are public and can be checked directly before any of this is used on the record.
