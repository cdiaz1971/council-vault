# Frederick Ethics Process Concentrates Every Function in the Legal Department

## Summary
Cathy Anderson's 36-page report maps every step of Frederick's ethics complaint process and finds that intake, investigation, legal advice, agendas, minutes, notices, and records all sit with the Legal Department, which answers to the Mayor, with no recusal rule when the Mayor or City Attorney is the subject.

## Key Points
The architecture starts with Section 21-2(a)(5): the Ethics Commission is advised by the City Attorney unless the Commission or the Mayor decides otherwise. The Mayor also appoints every commissioner and directs the Legal Department under Charter Article VI, Section 3a, and may be the subject of a complaint.

Anderson walks through each function. Intake goes to the City Attorney under Rule 5.1. An informal threshold review, described by the Commission chair at a March 2023 workshop as a private conversation between him and the City Attorney, can end a complaint with no authorizing rule, no criteria, no record, and no notice, even though the ordinance assigns the frivolousness determination to the full Commission after a preliminary investigation. The City Attorney is the default preliminary and primary investigator and then advises the Commission on its own findings. The primary investigation has no deadline, unlike every other step. The report need not tie back to the provision the complaint cited. Confidentiality under Section 21-3.1(i) does not bar the City Attorney from briefing the Mayor during an active case. The Legal Department sends all notices, which need not explain which stage or criteria produced an outcome, prepares agendas, keeps minutes that define the "official record," and holds case files with no retention rule and no disclosure standard. There is no appeal.

Chapter 21's conflict-of-interest definition covers financial interests and relatives but not the one relationship this process depends on: staff whose position answers to a possible subject. Anderson is explicit that she is not alleging bad faith; the claim is that the design gives no one a way to find out.

Her recommendation: Council should commission an independent review of the ethics complaint process, run by someone with no reporting line to the Mayor and no prior role in any complaint, with recommendations returned to Council for a public vote.

## Relevance
The ordinance was drafted by the Legal Department and passed by the prior Board of Aldermen in December 2023, so this Council inherited it and has clear authority to amend it. The structural critique holds regardless of anyone's view of the Nash matter, and it applies equally to the pending complaint about the Mayor. It is also the necessary precondition to any removal-from-office charter change: a removal process built on this complaint process inherits all of its gaps.
