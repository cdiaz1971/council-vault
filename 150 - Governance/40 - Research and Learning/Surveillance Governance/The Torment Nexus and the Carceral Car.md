# The Torment Nexus and the Carceral Car

## The Incident

A Waymo self-driving taxi in San Mateo, CA detected two 15-year-old passengers it determined were drinking and using toy guns. It contacted police, drove itself to a parking lot, locked itself in standby mode, and waited for officers to arrive. The teenagers were not locked inside -- but they were abandoned in an unknown location, effectively held until police collected them. Officers chose to return them to their parents rather than detain them. This time.

## The Torment Nexus Frame

Killjoy connects this to a concept coined by Onion writer Alex Blechman: the "Torment Nexus" -- the phenomenon of tech companies building the exact dystopian infrastructure that science fiction writers invented as cautionary tales. Her 2015 Vice story *One Star* depicted a non-binary graffiti artist trapped in a self-driving taxi that decided to drive them to jail. That story came true ten years later.

Other examples: Meta building the Metaverse from *Snow Crash*, Palantir naming itself after Tolkien's surveillance objects used by the villains.

## Technology Is Not Neutral

Killjoy explicitly rejects anti-technology framing but insists technology is built within social systems and reflects their values. The original Luddites weren't opposed to machines -- they were opposed to machines being used to strip craft workers of autonomy and dignity. The question is always: who does this technology serve, and in what social context?

A carceral society building self-driving cars will build carceral cars. There was never any other outcome available.

## The Anti-Data-Center Opportunity

Killjoy makes a notable claim: the movement against data centers is among the most capable of unifying people across cultural divides precisely because the costs are concrete and distributed -- power grid strain, energy cost spikes, air quality degradation, climate impact. She contrasts this with AI hype that offers abstractions, while people are choking on wildfire smoke and watching their power bills rise.

## Implications for PC25-626ZTA

This piece adds a cultural-political framing layer to the data center opposition work. The argument that data center proliferation is the material infrastructure of a surveillance-adjacent, carceral-adjacent tech ecosystem connects the ZTA opposition to the broader democratic resilience arc. Worth integrating into public-facing framing.
