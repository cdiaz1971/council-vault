# Council Voting Seat on Planning Commission as Alternative to Dissolution

## Summary
César's reframe of the Planning Commission accountability problem away from dissolution and toward structural oversight, either a council member as a voting member of the commission or a requirement that the commission present directly to council rather than routing solely through the Planning Department.

## Key Points
Dissolving the Planning Commission is not the right lever. The actual problem is that council currently has no built-in mechanism to weigh in on planning decisions before they reach a vote. Two possible fixes:

1. A council member sits as a voting member of the Planning Commission itself.
2. The commission is required to present directly to council on items coming before it, rather than that information flowing through the Planning Department only.

Either approach keeps the commission's technical review function intact while closing the gap between what the commission recommends and what actually gets introduced to council.

## Relevance
This lands close to Brehm's announced but still undrafted zoning text amendment giving council review authority over site plans. The two ideas are not identical, Brehm's is about site plan review specifically, this is about planning decisions more broadly, but they come from the same diagnosis. Worth comparing notes with Brehm once his draft lands rather than developing this in parallel.

It also reopens the question the stalled [[LMC Review Commission -- Draft Resolution Analysis -- 2026-04-12]] never fully answered. That draft gives César direct appointment authority over a temporary advisory LMC review body, but says nothing about ongoing accountability for the standing Planning Commission.

The disconnect this note is reacting to has shown up directly this cycle. The ADU Ordinance draft did not adopt the conditions the Planning Commission attached to its 4-0 vote. The Mixed Use Vertical Amendment moved from a Planning Commission 4-0 vote straight to introduction by the Planning Director. Both are live examples of exactly the gap a voting seat or mandatory presentation requirement would address.
