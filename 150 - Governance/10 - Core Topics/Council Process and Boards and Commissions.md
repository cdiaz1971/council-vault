# Council Process and Boards and Commissions

## Purpose

This page supports Council decisions about how its own business moves and how the City's appointed boards and commissions relate to Council. It covers the Charter framework, the legislative sequence from idea to final vote, the Mayor's veto, appointments, and options for strengthening Council oversight of commissions such as the Planning Commission.

## Charter framework

All Charter citations are to the City Charter as amended through 2025 (Res. 24-18 through 24-25 and 25-08), as published in the City Code.

- **Composition.** Seven members: five elected by district and two at large, for four-year terms beginning the second Thursday in December of the election year (Art. II, § 1.1). Term limit of three consecutive terms in the same office, counted from the 2025 election (§ 1.2). District members must live in their district (§ 2.1(e)).
- **Leadership.** Council elects a President and Vice President from its members for the full term. The President presides and votes on all questions (§ 9.1). The Mayor is not a member of Council.
- **Meetings.** Regular sessions on the first and third Thursdays; a majority may call special meetings. Meetings are open as the Open Meetings Act requires, and "the public normally will be given a reasonable opportunity to be heard" (§ 7).
- **Quorum and votes.** A quorum is a majority of members. Ordinances, resolutions, and official actions pass by a majority of members present and voting (§ 10).
- **Rules.** Council sets its own rules of procedure and keeps minutes (§ 11). The ordinance rules must cover public notice, approval, rejection, and amendment (§ 15.1(a)).
- **Introduction and agenda.** One or more members may introduce an ordinance, which bears their names and the date (§ 15.1(a)). Council may compel inclusion of proposed legislation on the City's agenda (§ 15(a)).
- **Passage.** An ordinance or resolution may pass at the meeting where it is introduced (§ 15.1(b), § 16.1(c)). The multi-reading sequence below comes from Council's rules and practice, not the Charter.
- **Veto.** The Mayor must sign or veto each ordinance, resolution, and official action within two weeks, with written reasons for a veto. Council can override within 30 days by two-thirds of its total membership, which is five of seven votes (§ 15.1(b)-(c), § 16.1(c)-(d), § 17.1(c)-(d)). Charter amendment resolutions are not subject to the veto (§ 16.1(b)).
- **Members.** Council judges its members' qualifications; removal requires a unanimous vote of the other members. Council may expel a member from a meeting for disorder by unanimous vote of the others present (§§ 8, 12).
- **Ethics.** Every Council member and the Mayor is subject to the Ethics Ordinance (Art. II, § 5.1; Art. III, § 6(d)). See [[Ethics, Transparency, and Accountability]].
- **Charter review.** A Charter Review Commission is to be appointed by the Mayor, with Council consent, within six months after the 2033 election seats Council (Charter, as published).

## How legislation moves

This is the sequence as Council runs it, confirmed in August 2026.

1. **Origination.** Four routes: Council committees of three or four members organized by policy area (the most common route); City staff; individual members, alone or with staff or outside groups; and, for zoning and land use (text amendments, map amendments, annexations, LMC amendments), the Planning Commission, outside the committee structure.
2. **Routing.** Council Leadership decides whether an item goes to a full-Council workshop or straight to a hearing. For Planning Commission items, Leadership decides jointly with the Planning Department.
3. **Workshop (optional).** The whole Council meets as a committee. No votes; discussion and direction only.
4. **Hearings.** Votes happen only at hearings, held at the regular first- and third-Thursday sessions or at a special session.
   - **First Read (introduction):** required for every ordinance and resolution. No vote. Text is locked; later changes come as formal amendments.
   - **Second Read:** Council may pass, amend, or reject.
   - **Third Read:** an item held from Second Read must receive a final vote; it cannot be deferred again.
   - An item may be returned to committee after First Read.
   - Resolutions and Planning Commission zoning ordinances follow the same three-read sequence.
5. **Legal review.** The City Attorney's office may review legislation at any point. Review is advisory, not a required step.
6. **Consent agenda.** Routine budget items, recurring costs, and grant acceptances pass as a bundle in one vote. It is not part of the legislative sequence.
7. **Mayor's action and override,** per the Charter.

Resolutions create public record and political baselines but cannot by themselves produce housing units, reduce rents, or compel action; they are most useful as the first step in a defined legislative sequence.

Rules of Procedure details (agenda posting deadlines, public comment sign-up and time limits, remote participation): verify against the current Rules, which Council amended to require earlier agenda posting and to change the text amendment process.

## Boards and commissions

### Appointment and removal

- The Mayor creates and appoints committees and advisory groups "to advise in the administration of the City's affairs" with Council's advice and consent, and may create ad hoc advisory committees (Art. III, § 6(c)).
- Council consents to department heads, including the City Attorney and Chief of Police (Art. VI).
- Individual bodies set their own terms. Examples: the Ethics Commission has five resident members appointed by the Mayor with Council consent, removable by Council after a hearing (City Code § 21-2); the Board of Supervisors of Elections has three members appointed by the Mayor with Council consent, no more than two from one party (Charter Art. V, § 2).
- Appointments come to Council as hearing agenda items. Recent agendas show vacancies that limit what some bodies can do: in October 2026 the Fair Housing Commission had four of five full seats vacant, leaving it unable to hear complaints, and the Board of Zoning Appeals had a second full-member vacancy ([[Hearing Brief 2026-10-01]]).

### The Planning Commission

- The Planning Commission reviews zoning and land-use items before they reach Council, outside the committee structure.
- Under State law, a planning commission has three, five, or seven members serving staggered five-year terms; one member may be a member of the legislative body serving ex officio for their legislative term and may not vote on any question in which they have a personal or financial interest; the legislative body may remove a member for cause after written charges and a public hearing ([Land Use § 2-102](https://mgaleg.maryland.gov/mgawebsite/Laws/StatuteText?article=glu&section=2-102&enactments=false)). Frederick's current Planning Commission composition is set in the Land Management Code: verify whether it includes a Council seat.
- In the 2026 cycle, the ADU ordinance draft did not carry the conditions the Planning Commission attached to its 4-0 recommendation, and the Mixed Use Vertical amendment went from a 4-0 Commission vote to introduction by the Planning Director, without Council weighing in first ([[Council Voting Seat on Planning Commission as Alternative to Dissolution]]).
- A proposal to give Council review authority over site plans has been announced by Council Member Brehm but not drafted (same source).

### Temporary and proposed bodies

- **LMC Review Commission.** A draft resolution would create an advisory commission of 11 to 15 members to review the Land Management Code, with the Council President appointing members subject to Council confirmation, nonvoting Council liaisons, and dissolution three months after its final report. The draft is incomplete ([[LMC Review Commission -- Draft Resolution Analysis -- 2026-04-12]]; open questions in [[Land Management Code Review Commission Council Decision Points & Open Question]]). Whether Council can vest appointment power in its President for an advisory body, given Art. III, § 6(c), needs counsel's view.
- **Special Events Commission.** A July 2026 draft ordinance would create a commission with permitting authority over special events and transfer certain alcohol approvals from Council to it ([[Events Commission Ordinance Brief]]).
- **Affordable Housing Commission.** Named in the draft Housing Production Target Resolution; a planning-and-zoning policy analyst position is under consideration instead ([[Policy Analyst Position as Alternative to Affordable Housing Commission]]).

## Authority map

| Lever | Who controls it | Council role |
|---|---|---|
| Council rules, committees, workshops, agendas | Council (Charter Art. II, § 11) | Direct |
| Placing legislation on the agenda | Any member may introduce; Council may compel inclusion | Direct |
| Final passage | Council majority | Direct |
| Veto | Mayor | Override with five of seven votes within 30 days |
| Creating advisory bodies for administration | Mayor with Council consent | Consent |
| Creating bodies with regulatory or permitting power | Council by ordinance | Direct |
| Appointments to most boards | Mayor with Council consent | Consent; removal where the enabling law allows |
| Planning Commission composition | Land Management Code within State Land Use law | Direct, by LMC amendment |
| Charter changes | Council by resolution, not subject to veto; referendum possible | Direct |

## Policy options

| Option | Addresses | Cost and effort | Risks |
|---|---|---|---|
| Council ex officio seat on the Planning Commission, as State law permits | No Council voice before land-use items arrive | LMC amendment | Conflicts on items the member later votes on at Council; ex officio status may limit voting |
| Require the Planning Commission to present its recommendation and conditions directly to Council | Conditions dropped between recommendation and introduction | Rules or LMC amendment | Longer timelines |
| Council review of site plans (Brehm proposal) | Site plans approved without Council input | ZTA | Moves quasi-judicial decisions to an elected body |
| Track and report board vacancies with a recruitment plan | Bodies unable to act | Low | Requires administration cooperation |
| Codify the three-read sequence and committee structure in the Rules | Process depends on current leadership | Low | Less flexibility |
| Publish a legislative tracker showing each item's stage | Public and member visibility | Low | Staff time |

## Decision framework

- Does the proposal change who decides, or only who advises?
- Is the body created by the Mayor for administration, or by Council by ordinance?
- Who appoints, who confirms, and who can remove?
- Will the body have the members it needs to act?
- How do its recommendations reach Council, and are its conditions preserved?

## Metrics

- Days from introduction to final vote, by item type.
- Items returned to committee or held to Third Read.
- Vetoes issued and overrides attempted.
- Vacancies by board and how long each has been open.
- Planning Commission conditions carried into introduced legislation.

## Council checklist

- [ ] Has the item been introduced at First Read with final text?
- [ ] Has legal review been requested, if wanted?
- [ ] Are there five votes if a veto is likely?
- [ ] For a new body: who appoints, under what Charter authority, and is a sunset set?
- [ ] For an appointment: residency, conflicts, and quorum effect on the body?
