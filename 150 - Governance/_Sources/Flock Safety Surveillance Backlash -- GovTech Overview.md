# Flock Safety Surveillance Backlash -- GovTech Overview

## Summary

GovTech overview of the national backlash against Flock Safety ALPR systems, covering crime-reduction claims alongside privacy concerns, ICE access controversies, and the emerging municipal surveillance governance debate.

## Key Points

- Flock Safety's automatic license plate readers have documented crime-reduction outcomes but have simultaneously triggered a significant national privacy backlash
- The company markets to municipalities citing public safety gains, but critics and advocacy groups have raised concerns about a "surveillance industry" being embedded in American cities without adequate democratic oversight
- A central controversy is ICE access: Flock data has been accessible to immigration enforcement through state and wildlife agency channels, bypassing local sanctuary policies
- Illinois named its first chief AI officer during the same news cycle -- a sign that state-level AI governance is accelerating
- Tyler Technologies, a major govtech vendor, added a Chief AI and Chief Transactions Officer, reflecting broader industry institutionalization of AI roles
- Ohio Secretary of State is offering free cybersecurity training to elections boards -- another signal of govtech maturing beyond the technology-first frame
