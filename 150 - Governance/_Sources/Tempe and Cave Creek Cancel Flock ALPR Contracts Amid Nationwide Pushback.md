# Tempe and Cave Creek Cancel Flock ALPR Contracts Amid Nationwide Pushback

## Summary

Tempe, Arizona is turning off its Flock cameras despite an existing $80,000 contract, and Cave Creek is ending its contract too, joining more than 140 cities that have rejected automated license plate readers per DeFlock, as a Washington Post investigation finds at least 50 U.S. law enforcement officials have been accused of misusing Flock or similar ALPR systems, including to stalk partners.

## Key Points

The City of Tempe, Arizona announced it will turn off all of its Flock cameras despite an existing $80,000 contract with the company. Nearby Cave Creek separately announced it will end its own Flock contract. The anti-surveillance tracking site DeFlock counts more than 140 cities that have now rejected automated license plate readers outright.

The misuse pattern driving this pushback keeps compounding: as of early August 2026, a Washington Post investigation found at least 50 U.S. law enforcement officials accused of misusing Flock or other license plate reader systems, including officers using the technology to stalk current or former partners.

## Relevance

Direct addition to the Flock ALPR misuse cluster already substantial enough in `150 - Governance` to warrant a full opposition brief pass. Tempe and Cave Creek are new contract-cancellation data points alongside Bandera, TX, and the 140+ city DeFlock count and the 50-official misuse figure are strong aggregate numbers for that brief.
