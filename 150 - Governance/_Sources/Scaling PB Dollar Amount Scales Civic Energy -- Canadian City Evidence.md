# Scaling PB Dollar Amount Scales Civic Energy -- Canadian City Evidence

## Summary

Analysis of PB cycles in Vancouver, Victoria, and Montreal finds that civic energy scales proportionally with the budget pool. The recommendation is to commit at least one percent of capital budget with a multi-year runway so completed projects can be celebrated before the next cycle.

## Key Points

Published January 2026 by the Institute for Research on Public Policy in Policy Options. Draws on recent PB cycles in three Canadian cities.

Vancouver demonstrated fast resident engagement even in modest initial rounds. Victoria showed that small rounds build community capacity and habits of civic participation that persist beyond the first cycle. Montreal's third year showed what happens when the pool grows: more residents engaged, more diverse projects proposed, better deliberation quality. The dollar amount is not just a fiscal variable -- it is a signal of how seriously the government takes the process.

The key design recommendations:
- Target at least 1 percent of the city's capital budget for PB
- Commit to a minimum multi-year runway so projects can be scoped, built, and publicly celebrated before the next cycle begins
- Celebrate completed projects explicitly -- residents pointing to a park bench or crosswalk and saying "we voted for that" is what sustains participation across cycles

The authors frame PB explicitly as a democracy-repair tool. In an environment where people across the political spectrum express frustration with systems that feel unresponsive, giving residents a meaningful, tangible say over public spending is one of the few interventions that rebuilds institutional trust from the ground up.

On digital tools: platforms like Consul (developed for Madrid) and Decidim manage the full PB lifecycle -- idea submission, feasibility review, proposal refinement, and secure digital voting -- in multiple languages. Both are open-source and used by municipalities internationally.

## Relevance

Frederick's capital budget provides the baseline for sizing a pilot. Even a small percentage directed toward neighborhood-scale infrastructure priorities in District 2 would be enough for a meaningful first round with low fiscal risk.

The multi-year runway recommendation is the design imperative for Frederick specifically. A one-shot pilot that produces no visible output before the end of the current council term is a missed opportunity. Structuring a PB process that completes its first project cycle within the current administration creates a tangible legacy of participatory governance -- something César can point to in 2027.

The Consul or Decidim platforms could address the multilingual ballot challenge at Frederick's scale without requiring a custom build.
