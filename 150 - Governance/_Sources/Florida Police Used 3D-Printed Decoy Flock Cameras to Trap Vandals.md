# Florida Police Used 3D-Printed Decoy Flock Cameras to Trap Vandals

## Summary
404 Media reports a Florida police department used officer-3D-printed decoy Flock cameras to bait and catch vandals, charging a man with three felonies for destroying one, while refusing to disclose any details about how the operation was authorized.

## Key Points
An officer 3D-printed plastic decoy Flock cameras at home. The department monitored the fake cameras for days, then charged a man with three felonies after he cut down and destroyed one of them. The department refuses to release any information about how the decoy operation was devised or carried out, and claims there is no paper trail for it whatsoever.

## Relevance
A different governance failure mode than the usual misuse-by-individual-officer story: an entire department-run entrapment operation using surveillance-branded objects with zero documented authorization. Worth naming specifically when arguing for audit trail and disclosure requirements in any Frederick surveillance technology policy, since "there is no paper trail" is exactly the answer such requirements are meant to prevent. Also a strange point of overlap with César's own 3D printing hobby, an officer using a Bambu-style printer for a decoy device rather than the usual props.
