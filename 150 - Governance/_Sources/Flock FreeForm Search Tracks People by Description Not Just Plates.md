## What This Reveals

Flock Safety's network, marketed as a license plate reader system, has a FreeForm search capability that allows officers to query for people by physical description -- sex, clothing, tattoos, and reportedly race and political affiliation. This is person-tracking infrastructure built on top of (and obscured by) the plate-reader brand.

This is a significant escalation beyond what Flock presents publicly. The surveillance creep pattern documented in prior vault notes -- from plate reading to Bluetooth tracking to now physical-description search across a camera network -- confirms that "ALPR" is a narrower label than the actual capability.

## Immigration Enforcement Relevance

The FreeForm capability is directly relevant to immigrant protection work. Flock's ICE access via state and wildlife agencies (already documented in vault) combined with person-level search by physical description creates a mechanism for targeted enforcement well beyond any stated traffic or public safety purpose. A network that can search "Latino male, 40s, blue jacket" across camera coverage in a jurisdiction is immigration enforcement infrastructure regardless of how it is procured or described.

## Connection to Frederick

Frederick does not currently contract with Flock, but the Surveillance Technology Governance brief and the broader GovTech procurement governance thread make this directly relevant. Any future vendor presenting ALPR-adjacent capability should be evaluated against the full FreeForm capability, not just the plate-reader use case.

## Synthesis Note

This reporting is the third major 404 Media piece on Flock capability expansion in the vault (ALPR to Bluetooth to person-search). Together they form a complete picture of surveillance creep: each feature addition is incremental, each is technically legal under existing procurement contracts, and each substantially changes what the system does. The pattern supports the case for use-limitation policy in any future Frederick surveillance technology governance ordinance.
