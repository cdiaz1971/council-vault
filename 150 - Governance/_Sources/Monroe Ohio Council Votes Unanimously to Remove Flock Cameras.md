# Monroe Ohio Council Votes Unanimously to Remove Flock Cameras

## Summary
Monroe, Ohio's five-member council voted unanimously to terminate its Flock Safety contract and shut off all its cameras immediately, after discovering a recurring invoice had been paid for two years without council review.

## Key Points
- The vote (September 22, 2026) was 5-0 to end the contract and deactivate all cameras (reported as 23 or 24) within city limits. The ordinance took effect immediately. Hardware may stay visible until Flock physically removes it.
- Monroe signed a two-year Flock contract in 2024 for $149,950. The trigger was procedural: Council Member Todd Hickman found the city was paying a recurring invoice of about $73,000 a year that never came before council. A week earlier, council capped cameras at the 23 authorized in the 2024 resolution and required council sign-off on every Flock invoice.
- Public comment split evenly. Supporters cited child abduction and general public safety. Opponents described the cameras as watching every road into the city and expanding beyond plate reading.
- Police Chief Bob Buchanan called the cameras very effective. Flock argued the technology works responsibly with oversight and pointed to cities that returned after adding safeguards.
- Council reminded residents of their right to a referendum. Regional context: Oxford, Dayton (which bagged its cameras after data was searched for immigration purposes against city policy), and five Northern Kentucky jurisdictions have announced removals. Ohio legislators have introduced bills to restrict, pause, or ban Flock.

## Relevance
Monroe is the cleanest example of a council reclaiming authority over surveillance spending through ordinary budget oversight: the opening was an invoice nobody had approved. That is a low-conflict entry point for Frederick, a question to staff about which surveillance technology contracts and renewals have been approved administratively, and on what authority. Dayton's experience with immigration-related searches is directly relevant to Frederick's City Values Act commitments.
