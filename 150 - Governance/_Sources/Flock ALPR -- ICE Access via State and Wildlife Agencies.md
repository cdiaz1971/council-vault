# Flock ALPR -- ICE Access via State and Wildlife Agencies

## Summary
Florida's Fish and Wildlife Conservation Commission has been running Flock ALPR license plate lookups on behalf of ICE, documented through public records. ICE has no direct contract with Flock, but continues accessing the system indirectly through state and local agencies in ways that are difficult to track or hold accountable. Wildlife enforcement is an unexpected vector that illustrates how broadly the indirect access problem extends.

## Key Points
Flock Safety sells its AI-powered license plate reader network to local and state law enforcement. ICE does not hold a contract with Flock directly. Instead, ICE agents access Flock data by routing requests through partner agencies that do have contracts -- in this case, Florida's fish and wildlife cops. Public records obtained by 404 Media show dozens of such lookups were performed.

This pattern is significant for several reasons. First, it means the list of agencies that can serve as ICE access points is not limited to police departments or sheriff's offices -- it extends to any state or local agency with a Flock contract, regardless of mission. Second, because these lookups happen through intermediary agencies, they are harder for the public to track and harder for local governments to constrain through policy. Third, it illustrates how a technology governance framework that focuses only on direct contracts will miss indirect access pipelines entirely.

The same 404 Media issue also notes that the FBI was able to extract Signal messages from a deleted app via push notification cache -- relevant for anyone advising on digital security or opsec.

## Relevance
This is directly relevant to the Flock deployment in Frederick and any surveillance governance ordinance under consideration. The Frederick County Sheriff's 287(g) agreement is scheduled for termination around mid-May 2026 under SB 245/HB 444, but that termination does not sever data-sharing pipelines that run through Flock or Thomson Reuters/CLEAR. The wildlife agency vector raises the question: which other non-obvious agencies in Maryland or Frederick County hold Flock contracts? A governance ordinance focused only on FCSO or FPD contracts would leave this gap open.
