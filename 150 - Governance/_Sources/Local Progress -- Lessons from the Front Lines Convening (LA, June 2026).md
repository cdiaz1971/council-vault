# Local Progress -- Lessons from the Front Lines Convening (LA, June 2026)

## Summary

Local Progress Impact Lab, Public Rights Project, and Mayors for America convened nearly 100 local elected officials and partners in Los Angeles for a two-day strategy meeting titled "Organizing with Local Power -- Resisting Authoritarianism." The convening focused on sharing strategies from localities that have experienced direct federal ICE deployments and building a shared playbook for resisting federal overreach.

## Key Points

The convening drew local leaders from major metropolitan areas that have experienced direct federal deployments -- National Guard and ICE operations -- as well as leaders preparing their communities for future incursions. Day one focused on lessons from cities that have already faced occupations. Day two expanded to include labor partners and allies to build out the new playbook.

The framing from Local Progress: communities that have experienced direct federal violence have developed models of care and mutual defense that smaller and less-affected cities need to learn from before they face the same conditions. The convening was explicitly positioned as a peer-learning and strategy-sharing event, not a protest or lobbying action.

The writeup characterizes the convening as "the first of its kind" and describes it as creating "a new ecosystem of learning and connection" standing in opposition to executive centralization of power.

Partners: Public Rights Project (legal strategy and litigation support for local governments), Mayors for America (municipal executive network).
