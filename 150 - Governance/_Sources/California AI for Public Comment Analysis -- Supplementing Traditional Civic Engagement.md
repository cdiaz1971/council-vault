## Summary

California's digital conversation platform is supplementing its traditional public comment process with AI to gather more actionable insight from large volumes of citizen input. The system helps officials understand how AI is affecting work -- both within government and in the communities they serve -- by processing and synthesizing public comment at a scale humans cannot.

The model is additive rather than replacement: AI helps surface patterns across thousands of comments that a staff reader would miss, while human officials still make judgments and policy decisions. This is distinct from using AI to *respond to* public comment, which carries much higher democratic legitimacy concerns.

## Why This Matters for César

Frederick's public engagement infrastructure is thin relative to the volume of input that major decisions (LMC reform, data center zoning, housing production targets) can generate. An AI-assisted comment analysis tool could help the council surface constituent themes without requiring additional staff capacity. This is a low-controversy AI use case worth tracking as a potential Frederick application.
