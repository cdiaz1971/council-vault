# Flock Safety Automatic Auditing Tool

## Summary

Flock Safety, a major supplier of license plate readers and public safety tech, has released an automatic auditing tool in response to sustained criticism over privacy, immigration enforcement, and data-sharing practices. Directly relevant to Frederick's data governance and ALPR policy work.

## Key Points

Flock Safety has come under significant scrutiny for its role in immigration enforcement, data sharing with federal agencies, and privacy concerns related to its ALPR (automated license plate reader) networks. In response, the company has released an auditing tool designed to give agencies visibility into how data is being accessed and shared.

The tool is framed as a transparency measure, allowing law enforcement agencies to audit queries and data requests within their Flock networks. Critics have pushed for this kind of accountability mechanism for years, arguing that without it, agencies cannot credibly claim to control how their data is used downstream.

GovTech notes the tool debuts specifically "under scrutiny" -- the framing matters. This is a reactive move in response to advocacy pressure, not a proactive transparency initiative. That distinction is relevant for evaluating its adequacy.

The same GovTech edition also flagged Minneapolis considering a data center moratorium and a Maryland pilot using wearable tech to protect highway work crews -- both worth noting as context.

## Relevance

Flock Safety operates in Frederick and has been a recurring concern in governance conversations nationally around immigration enforcement and surveillance accountability. The release of an auditing tool is directly relevant to [[ALPR Policy and Surveillance Governance]] work -- specifically the question of whether cities can meaningfully audit and constrain how ALPR data flows to third parties, including ICE and other federal agencies.

If Frederick has or is considering a Flock contract, this auditing capability should be evaluated as part of any community benefit or data governance agreement. The adequacy of the tool itself -- what it audits, who can access the logs, whether it is independent -- remains an open question worth tracking.
