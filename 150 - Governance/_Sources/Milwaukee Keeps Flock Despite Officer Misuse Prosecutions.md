# Milwaukee Keeps Flock Despite Officer Misuse Prosecutions

## Summary
Milwaukee PD kept its Flock contract even as a second officer, an internal affairs detective assigned to investigate the first officer's Flock abuse, faces felony charges for using the system to track people, while neighboring Wisconsin jurisdictions cancel their contracts.

## Key Points
- **The cases.** Former officer Josue Ayala was convicted of using Flock to track an ex-partner. Detective Tehrangi Chapman, a 22-year veteran who was one of two detectives assigned to the Ayala internal investigation in December 2025, was then charged with felony misconduct in public office and misuse of a GPS device. Court filings say he ran the victim's plate 17 times between January 2024 and January 2025, logging "test" and "training" as the search justification, and also placed a GPS tracker on a vehicle. MPD learned of it only when a victim called internal affairs anonymously in February 2026. Chapman was discharged in August, pleaded not guilty, and was due back in court October 7.
- **Milwaukee's response.** Rather than end the contract, MPD tightened auditing to flag suspicious searches and cut the number of officers with access (previously around 370). Mayor Cavalier Johnson and Chief Jeffrey Norman later announced further restrictions. Common Council members say that does not settle it: the Council can modify a chief's policy only with a two-thirds vote, and recent Flock agreements were approved through a commodity-product waiver that bypassed Council review.
- **Regional contrast.** Milwaukee County's executive will not renew the county's Flock contract at the end of 2026, and a county committee voted to pull cameras from 11 parks. Racine (mayor breaking a 7-7 tie), Oconomowoc, Waukesha, Sheboygan, Verona, Kaukauna, Oshkosh, Stoughton, Fitchburg, and UW-Madison police have cancelled, declined to renew, or defunded. At least 221 Wisconsin agencies were using Flock as of 2025.

## Relevance
The Chapman case is the sharpest illustration yet that audit controls do not catch insider misuse: the person assigned to audit misuse was himself misusing the system, and it surfaced only through a victim complaint. That undercuts the standard vendor and police answer that better auditing fixes the problem. The procurement detail matters for Frederick too. Milwaukee's Council lost leverage because the contracts went through a commodity waiver, which is a concrete argument for requiring Council approval of any surveillance technology contract regardless of dollar threshold.
