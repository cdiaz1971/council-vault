# Federal Lawmakers Introduce Bills to Curb Flock ALPR Access

## Summary
Lawmakers across the political spectrum introduced federal bills in late 2026 to restrict Flock and other automatic license plate reader (ALPR) networks, citing 404 Media reporting on ICE lookups, protest surveillance, and an abortion-related search.

## Key Points
- **Ban Flock Act** -- Sen. Bernie Sanders (I-VT), Rep. Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez (D-NY), and Sen. Jeff Merkley (D-OR) announced this bill, which would bar federal agencies from using ALPRs, cut federal funding to state/local governments that use the cameras, and let individuals sue the federal government for ALPR-related rights violations.
- **Stop Flock Abuse Act** -- Sen. Josh Hawley (R-MO) introduced a separate bill requiring written approval for each search, mandating deletion of driver data after 10 days (with exceptions for active investigations), and banning facial recognition integration in ALPR networks.
- Reps. Greg Casar and Shontel Brown sent a letter to FBI Director Kash Patel asking how the agency plans to acquire nationwide ALPR access, after 404 Media first reported the FBI's plan in May 2025.
- Cited justifications for the bills: local police performed ALPR lookups for ICE (first reported May 2025, prompting Flock to block cross-state searches in Illinois, California, and Virginia and add a real-time search-blocking tool); California police investigated an "immigration protest" using Flock; police nationwide used Flock to monitor "No Kings" demonstrations, and Flock itself taught police how to surveil those protesters in a company webinar; a Texas officer searched ALPRs nationwide for a woman who self-administered an abortion.
- Demand Progress called the Ban Flock Act "the gold standard" for federal ALPR legislation.

## Relevance
Direct update to the [[Police Surveillance Technology]] thread: federal legislative response is accelerating, with bipartisan bills (Sanders/AOC/Merkley on the left, Hawley on the right) both responding to the same underlying ALPR/Flock concerns already tracked on that page, including ICE data access. Frederick's own use of ALPR technology and any data-sharing posture with federal immigration authorities should be checked against whichever of these federal approaches gains traction, particularly the Stop Flock Abuse Act's data-retention and facial-recognition provisions, which could become a model for state or local ordinances even if the federal bills stall.
