# Cities Struggle to Hire Despite More Applicants -- AI Application Volume as the New Bottleneck

## Summary
Local government vacancies persist even as applications surge, because AI tools let applicants flood postings faster than HR can review them. Pew recommends mapping the hiring process end to end, measuring it, and fixing cheap bottlenecks, citing Baltimore's weekly interview days and Columbus's trades pipeline.

## Key Points
- Post-pandemic vacancies raised workloads, overtime, and retirements, slowing services. Philadelphia's workforce is about pre-COVID size but vacancies rose from 6.6% to 16.5%. San Diego, Phoenix, and Dallas grew their workforces. Over 300,000 federal job losses in 2025 are spilling into state and local labor markets.
- **The shift:** two years ago the problem was too few applicants. Now agencies are overwhelmed, because AI tools make it trivial to submit dozens of applications, and review capacity has not grown. Time-to-hire stretches.
- **Service effects:** delayed permit reviews for small businesses and affordable housing, slowed or skipped inspections, unanswered 911 calls, burnout that drives more exits, eroding public trust.
- **Recommendations:** map hiring end to end and find where candidates drop out; measure progress with existing data; evaluate the government's employer "brand"; build shared definitions of excellence through peer learning, since no city is a gold standard.
- **Examples:** Baltimore rebuilt skilled-trades hiring with low-cost fixes such as fixed weekly interview days, replacing interviews that took about 60 days to schedule. Columbus partnered with the Ohio Reformatory for Women to train incarcerated women in trades for jobs on release.
- **Research gaps** (Caitlin Lewis, Work for America, which serves 300+ governments): workforce demographics, how many true entry-level jobs exist, which roles offer upward mobility.

## Relevance
Permit-review delay is a direct housing-production cost, and Resolution 26-15 commits Frederick to 8 to 10 month expedited review. That target depends on staffed review positions, so a vacancy and time-to-hire question to HR belongs in the FY2028 budget conversation and the SPARK follow-up. The AI-application flood is also a GovTech governance question: whether the City is screening applications with AI tools, and under what rules.
