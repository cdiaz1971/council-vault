# AI Surveillance Towers Deployed Near San Diego

## Summary

Authorities have deployed AI-powered surveillance towers near San Diego, adding another layer to the expanding ecosystem of automated surveillance infrastructure at the local government level. The deployment signals continued growth of AI-driven monitoring tools in public spaces, with limited public deliberation in most jurisdictions.

## Key Points

The GovTech item covers the deployment of AI surveillance towers -- distinct from ALPR cameras -- near San Diego. These towers likely incorporate multiple sensor types (cameras, possibly audio) with AI-driven analysis running in real time. The specific vendor, contracting agency, and public notice process are not detailed in the newsletter digest.

The broader pattern this fits into: automated surveillance infrastructure is being layered into communities through procurement decisions made without dedicated governance frameworks. ALPR, AI towers, and connected databases like Thomson Reuters CLEAR create an interconnected data pipeline that no single procurement decision fully accounts for.

The San Diego case illustrates that the ALPR conversation is just one node in a larger surveillance technology governance problem. A Frederick surveillance technology ordinance would need to address the full category -- not just Flock cameras -- to avoid whack-a-mole governance.
