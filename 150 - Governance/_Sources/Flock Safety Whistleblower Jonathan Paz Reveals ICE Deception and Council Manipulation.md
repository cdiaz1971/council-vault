# Flock Safety Whistleblower Jonathan Paz Reveals ICE Deception and Council Manipulation

## Summary

On-the-record interview with Jonathan Paz, former Flock government affairs manager, who reveals that his job was explicitly to suppress community opposition at council hearings, and that Flock management lied to employees about ICE access while secretly building federal agency pilot programs.

## Key Points

**Who Paz is:** Former activist, union organizer, and elected city councilor in Waltham, Massachusetts. Recruited into Flock at the 2023 National League of Cities conference in Atlanta. Background included municipal broadband work and "techno optimism." Joined believing in Flock's stated mission of finding missing people.

**What his job actually was:** As government affairs manager, Paz traveled to cities facing community opposition to Flock cameras. His explicit role was to ensure that community resistance did not translate into a council vote against adoption. His words: "make sure that this resistance doesn't become a vote reflected in the vote of the council." He came to see this as a "facade that we were helping these democratically elected boards" represent the people who voted for them.

**The ICE deception:** Paz repeatedly asked company leadership whether Flock worked with ICE. He was consistently told no. 404 Media's prior reporting revealed that local police were performing Flock searches for ICE. 9NEWS Denver found CBP had direct camera access. Flock initially denied this. Eventually Flock admitted CBP had direct access via a pilot program -- and so did ICE. Secret Service and the Navy's criminal investigation division also had access.

**The resignation:** Paz quit in July 2025, forfeiting tens of thousands in company equity and a severance package. After leaving, he founded an organization to pay rent, food, and legal fees for people impacted by ICE's mass deportation effort. He is now running for Congress in Massachusetts' 5th District.

**Flock's response:** A spokesperson told 404 Media that community conversations "give us direct feedback from the people we serve" and that "the decision to adopt Flock technology rests solely with each community."

## Relevance

This is the most significant Flock accountability piece to date -- an on-the-record admission from inside the company that suppressing democratic process at council hearings is a core business function, not an incidental practice. It is directly applicable to the Surveillance Technology Procurement Governance thread and to the data center ZTA opposition work (same pattern: industry representatives appearing at hearings with a specific mandate to overcome community resistance). The ICE deception confirms the pattern documented in earlier vault notes: Flock's public-facing commitments about civil liberties and local control are systematically contradicted by internal operations. Worth citing in any Frederick procurement governance framework or contract language discussion. The fact that Paz was himself a former elected councilor and union organizer -- not an ideological critic -- makes his account harder to dismiss.
