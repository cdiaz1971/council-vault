# Internal Friction Is the Real Climate Bottleneck in City Government -- Actionable Consensus Framework

## Summary

Cities globally have adopted ambitious climate targets but consistently fail to translate them into visible street-level results. Per Grankvist, drawing on six years of work with Swedish cities, argues the problem has been misdiagnosed: the real bottleneck is internal friction -- procedural, cultural, and relational barriers inside city hall -- not the more commonly cited culprits of funding, political polarization, or technical knowledge gaps. The Actionable Consensus Framework is a structured, free, self-administered tool for city leaders to identify and address these internal blockages.

## Key Points

**The misdiagnosis.** Cities invest in climate action plans, set science-based targets, and generate political intent. Then implementation stalls. The friction is especially acute in mid-sized cities: too large for informal corridor chats, too small for the integration machinery of a global metropolis. Frederick fits this description precisely.

**Three framework shifts:**

First, quality of life over carbon. Climate discussions that start with spreadsheets lose people. The framework reframes every climate project in terms of how it improves residents' daily lives and dignity. This makes the work concrete and connects it to justice: overturning systems that make life harder for marginalized communities rather than pursuing an abstract environmental goal.

Second, openness, not vague visions. Instead of open-ended visioning, the framework uses science-based, locally-grounded story templates depicting a day in a climate-neutral city. This gives all participants the same specific mental picture and moves directly to "how" rather than "what."

Third, real people, not roles. The framework invites participants to bring their professional worries and personal hopes into the room. Acknowledging what people fear losing in a transition builds trust and creates authentic connection across departments and agencies.

**The structural insight.** The framework is not designed to replace city machinery -- it is designed to lubricate it. The metaphor: cities don't need a new engine, they need to "grease the wheels of the one they have." Moving from informal favors between departments to a structured consensus-building process ensures that implementation is democratic, not dependent on who knows whom.

**Availability.** The Actionable Consensus Framework is free and does not require consultants. It can be applied across an entire department or used by an individual lead to unstick a single project. Website: actionableconsensus.org

## Relevance

The internal friction diagnosis applies directly to Frederick beyond climate: the same structural problem -- political intent outpacing implementation capacity -- shows up in LMC modernization, housing production, and any cross-departmental initiative. The quality-of-life reframing is also applicable to César's communication strategy: framing policy in terms of how it improves daily life for District 2 residents is more persuasive than framing it in terms of abstract policy goals.

The framework's emphasis on small groups with actual decision-making authority is worth noting for the LMC Review Commission and the Housing Production Target Resolution -- implementation stalls when the people in the room can't actually commit to anything.
