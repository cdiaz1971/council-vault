# Fusion Center Surveillance of Anti-Flock Activism and the DeFlock Movement

## Summary
404 Media obtained fusion center intelligence bulletins showing law enforcement is monitoring anti-Flock social media accounts and warning police departments about the DeFlock movement's National Week of Action Against ALPRs, August 16-22, while trying to tie political opposition to camera vandalism.

## Key Points
404 Media obtained intelligence bulletins from six fusion centers (Colorado, Wisconsin, New Jersey, Central Florida, North Florida, and New Mexico) plus a summary circulated to FBI headquarters staff. The bulletins document law enforcement tracking DeFlock, a crowdsourced open source map of ALPR camera locations created by Will Freeman in 2024, and warning about an upcoming coordinated week of public meetings and townhalls opposing ALPR contracts.

The documents consistently blur the line between people who politically oppose Flock through legitimate channels (contacting representatives, public education, contract cancellation campaigns) and a smaller number of individuals who have vandalized or disabled cameras. One bulletin flags a specific Instagram account posting videos of camera removal; another notes a brief, apparently short-lived attempt by a neo-Nazi accelerationist group to co-opt anti-Flock sabotage rhetoric, with no evidence they acted on it.

Freeman told 404 Media that DeFlock has never called for vandalism and that the project's actual track record, over 100 Flock contracts canceled through public engagement over two years, gets erased when law enforcement bulletins frame the whole movement around the vandalism minority. A Flock Safety spokesperson said the company has seen few actual incidents but condemned the property damage.
