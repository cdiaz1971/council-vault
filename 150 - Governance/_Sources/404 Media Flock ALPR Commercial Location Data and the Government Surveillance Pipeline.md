## Summary

The July 31 404 Media weekly roundup contains two distinct surveillance threads that together document the full commercial-to-government surveillance pipeline. The podcast episode covers what happens when Flock comes to a community and the rise of no-phone Luddite events as a response. The interview episode features Mike Yeagley, who spent hundreds of thousands of dollars buying commercially sourced location data and demonstrating its capabilities to government agencies -- in effect bootstrapping the government's appetite for commercial location tracking.

## Key Points

**Flock thread:**
- Podcast covers community response to Flock deployment -- what happens when it arrives, how communities resist.
- A subscriber-only segment addresses anti-Flock influencers who are fabricating claims, noting that misinformation undermines legitimate opposition.
- Flock coverage in this issue lands alongside the Emporia arrest story, reinforcing a civil liberties frame connecting data center opposition and surveillance resistance.

**Mike Yeagley / commercial location data thread:**
- Yeagley is described as the person who "introduced parts of the government to the idea of commercially sourced location data."
- He spent hundreds of thousands of dollars purchasing data himself and demonstrating what could be done with it to government audiences.
- This is a significant disclosure: it names an individual as the origin point for federal agency adoption of commercial location tracking -- a practice that now feeds ICE enforcement, Flock network queries, and other surveillance programs documented elsewhere in the vault.
- The Yeagley interview establishes the supply-side origin of the government's location data dependency -- a complement to the demand-side Flock notes.
