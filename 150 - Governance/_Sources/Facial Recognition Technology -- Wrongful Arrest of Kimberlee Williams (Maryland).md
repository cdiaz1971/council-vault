# Facial Recognition Technology -- Wrongful Arrest of Kimberlee Williams (Maryland)

## Summary

In 2021, Kimberlee Williams, an Oklahoma woman with no connection to Maryland, was wrongfully arrested and jailed for six months after Maryland police relied on a facial recognition match generated by an unknown third party via a private investigator listserv. She is the fourteenth person publicly known to have been wrongfully arrested in the United States due to a false FRT result. In April 2026, the ACLU filed administrative complaints against three Maryland police departments and demanded a public apology and policy reform.

## Key Points

**How the identification happened:** After a suspect made fraudulent bank withdrawals in Maryland, a bank investigator submitted a photo to Crimedex, a national listserv of police and private investigators. An unknown person on the listserv ran the image through facial recognition technology and flagged Williams as a match. The bank shared this result with detectives in Montgomery County, Anne Arundel County, and Prince George's County, providing little explanation of how the identification was made.

**Concealment from the court:** When Montgomery County detectives applied for an arrest warrant, they did not disclose to the magistrate judge that the identification was based on facial recognition technology, nor that the scan had been conducted by an unknown third party. They described the identification as visual confirmation only. The ACLU argues that had the court known the true basis for identification, the warrant would not have been issued.

**Williams' arrest and detention:** Williams was arrested on June 23, 2021 in Lawton, Oklahoma while accompanying her daughter on a DoorDash delivery to a military base. She was held in Oklahoma for 23 days before being transported to Maryland, where she spent additional months incarcerated. She had never been to Maryland before. Charges were eventually dropped after the error became apparent.

**ACLU's demands (April 2026):** The ACLU and ACLU of Maryland sent letters to Montgomery County PD, Anne Arundel County PD, and Prince George's County PD demanding a public apology, internal investigation into the failures, and adoption of stricter safeguards. Williams is seeking both accountability and systemic policy change.
