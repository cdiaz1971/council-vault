## Summary

404 Media's June 2026 roundup ("New Torment Nexus just dropped") includes two items directly relevant to the Flock/ALPR procurement governance thread:

**1. Police stalking via Flock:** More than a dozen cases around the country where officers have been arrested for using Flock's ALPR system to obsessively and illegally stalk people. This is not edge-case misuse -- it is a documented pattern of insider threat enabled by the system's architecture, which allows any authorized user to run location queries without audit friction sufficient to deter abuse.

**2. ALPRs that track Bluetooth devices:** New reporting documents that modern ALPR systems can now passively capture Bluetooth device identifiers broadcast by phones and vehicles near a camera. This extends the surveillance footprint far beyond the stated purpose of license plate capture -- a vehicle with a Bluetooth-enabled phone inside can be tracked even when the plate is obscured, not captured, or traveling in a non-vehicle mode.

## Key Points

- Flock's insider threat problem is structural, not incidental: the system's query accessibility enables stalking; a dozen-plus arrests nationally is a documented pattern
- Bluetooth tracking capability represents a significant undisclosed expansion of what ALPR procurement actually purchases -- Frederick's contract discussions should explicitly address whether Flock systems capture or store Bluetooth device identifiers
- The combination of these two items (easy insider access + broader data capture than disclosed) strengthens the case for mandatory audit logging, independent access review, and explicit contractual prohibition on non-plate data retention

## Relevance for Frederick

This is directly actionable for the Flock/ALPR contract language development. The NHI procurement gap note should explicitly address Bluetooth tracking as a non-obvious capability that vendors may not disclose unless contractually required to. The stalking arrest pattern provides concrete evidence for why access audit requirements and use limitation clauses are not hypothetical precautions -- they are responses to documented harms already occurring in peer jurisdictions. Useful for record-building on the Flock ZTA worksession.
