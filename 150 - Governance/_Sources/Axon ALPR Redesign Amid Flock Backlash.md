# Axon ALPR Redesign Amid Flock Backlash

## Summary
Axon is exploring redesigning its Outpost ALPR camera so it looks less like a Flock camera, after leaked comments show law enforcement customers asking for a different look to reduce vandalism amid the ongoing Flock backlash.

## Key Points
404 Media obtained a copy of a now-deleted Axon webinar in which CEO Rick Smith and Chief Customer Officer Mike Wagers discussed changing the shape of Axon's Outpost ALPR camera specifically because it currently resembles a Flock camera closely enough to draw the same vandalism. Smith also referenced Axon's Lightpost product, an ALPR and AI camera mounted on existing streetlights, as one option customers have asked for because it sits higher and is harder to damage. A competing idea raised internally is to go the opposite direction and make ALPR cameras more visibly labeled, with signage describing the camera and the city's ALPR policy.

The broader context is that some cities have already been switching from Flock to Axon following reporting that police used Flock lookups for ICE, tracked a woman who self-administered an abortion, and misused access to stalk former partners. Flock's national network, which lets an officer in one jurisdiction search cameras in another state entirely, has been a specific point of concern. Around the country, people have vandalized Flock cameras by tearing them down or spray-painting the lenses, and at least one person has been criminally charged in connection with destroying a fake camera set up by police as bait.

## Relevance
This is directly useful for the Flock ALPR opposition brief: it shows the surveillance vendor market is already reacting defensively to public pressure, which is evidence the organizing and policy pushback is working.
