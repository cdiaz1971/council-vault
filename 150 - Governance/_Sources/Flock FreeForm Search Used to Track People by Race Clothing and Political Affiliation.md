# Flock FreeForm Search Used to Track People by Race, Clothing, and Political Affiliation

## Summary

404 Media documents how Flock's FreeForm AI search feature allows police to query hundreds of cameras simultaneously using natural-language descriptions of people -- including racial descriptors and political affiliation markers -- far beyond the license-plate-reader use case Flock publicly advertises.

## Key Points

Data reviewed from HaveIBeenFlocked.com (a public-records aggregator) shows police departments across the country have used Flock cameras to search for specific people -- not vehicles -- using searches such as "heavy-set male with a black and white hat," "person on skateboard," "person wearing orange vest and construction hat," and in some cases descriptors of race or presumed political affiliation.

**Scale:** The California Highway Patrol ran one search for a person across 274 cameras simultaneously. Anne Arundel County MD PD (directly relevant to Maryland context) searched 198 camera networks for "white jeep with trump flag." Searches involving racial descriptors appeared despite Flock's claim of guardrails against race-based queries.

**FreeForm as a feature:** Flock launched FreeForm in February 2025, originally marketing it for vehicle descriptions. The system uses AI and image recognition to interpret natural-language queries and find matching footage across integrated camera networks. Flock's Condor video cameras (separate from ALPR cameras) have "people detection alerts" and a "Guardian Mode" that automatically zooms in on people and vehicles. The two systems are designed to work in tandem.

**Civil liberties response:** The ACLU's Jay Stanley called it "a significant increase in surveillance capability" even without facial recognition. CDT's Tom Bowman used the bait-and-switch framing: towns were sold stolen-car and missing-child tools; they now have cross-network person-tracking infrastructure. EFF's Beryl Lipton flagged the Trayvon Martin parallel -- that searching for someone "wearing a hoodie" or "walking on foot" sweeps in law-abiding people based on appearance.

**Flock's position:** The company says FreeForm is designed for investigators working with limited information (witness descriptions), denies it constitutes facial recognition, and claims guardrails prevent race-based queries. The data reviewed suggests the guardrails are imperfect.

**Prior Flock reporting:** 404 Media has previously reported that Flock planned to use hacked data to "jump from LPR to person" (scrapped after coverage), and that at least 60 Condor cameras were left exposed to the public internet in December 2025.

## Relevance

This is directly material to the active Flock ALPR governance track. Frederick is being asked to consider or extend Flock contracts, and the standard pitch is license plate reading for public safety. This reporting documents that Flock's capabilities extend well beyond plates -- the FreeForm feature enables population-level person surveillance using AI-interpreted natural language. For a city with significant immigrant communities along the Golden Mile corridor, this is not an abstract civil liberties concern.

The Anne Arundel County MD angle is useful for council record: a Maryland jurisdiction is already running political-affiliation-adjacent searches ("trump flag") across nearly 200 camera networks. That's a concrete local example, not a distant hypothetical.

This connects directly to [[Municipal Sovereignty Against Federal Enforcement Infrastructure]] -- Flock's ICE data-sharing pipeline and this FreeForm surveillance expansion are the same system, just different query types. Any procurement governance policy for Frederick needs to address both.
