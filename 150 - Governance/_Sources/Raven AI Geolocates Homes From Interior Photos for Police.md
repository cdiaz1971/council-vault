# Raven AI Geolocates Homes From Interior Photos for Police

## Summary
Graylark's Raven tool, formerly GeoSpy, now lets police identify a specific home from one interior photo by matching it against Airbnb and Zillow listing images. It extends police surveillance into private interiors without a warrant or public debate.

## Key Points
Raven is made by Graylark Technologies. The Los Angeles Police Department and the Miami-Dade Sheriff's Office have bought access. CEO Daniel Heinen announced the ability to "geolocate interior images across millions of short-term rental units worldwide." A demo shows a photo of a washer and dryer taken from X, uploaded to Raven, and matched within seconds to a specific Airbnb listing in Charlotte, with the listing's own text in the result.

The data source is the mass of interior photos published on real estate and rental sites. Those images were posted for commerce, not policing, and their owners never consented to law enforcement use.

The same issue reports that Senator Ron Wyden is demanding a privacy review of the High Intensity Drug Trafficking Area program, which mirrors city license plate reader data from Flock, Axon, and Motorola into federal databases through agreements with local governments. HIDTA sits under the White House drug policy office, which ran the Hemisphere phone surveillance program.

## Relevance
Two lessons for Frederick. First, police surveillance capability is now bought as software features that appear without any procurement event, so a surveillance ordinance needs to cover feature additions to existing contracts, not just new purchases. Second, the HIDTA story shows local license plate data flowing to federal databases through intergovernmental agreements, which matters for immigrant residents and for any data-sharing terms in a city ALPR contract.
