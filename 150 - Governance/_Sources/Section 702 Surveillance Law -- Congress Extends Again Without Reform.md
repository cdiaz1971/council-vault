# Section 702 Surveillance Law -- Congress Extends Again Without Reform

## Summary

Section 702 of the Foreign Intelligence Surveillance Act, which authorizes warrantless surveillance of foreign targets (with American communications swept up incidentally), received another short-term extension -- 45 days -- rather than a lasting reauthorization. The extension was linked to a commitment to declassify a Foreign Intelligence Surveillance Court opinion finding significant compliance problems with the program.

## Key Points

Section 702 is a perennial congressional football. The law authorizes intelligence agencies to surveil foreign targets without warrants, but because those targets communicate with Americans, the resulting database contains American communications that can be searched using American identifiers -- a practice that has drawn sustained civil liberties criticism.

The latest episode: the House passed a 3-year reauthorization with some changes, but that bill was dead on arrival in the Senate because it was paired with a ban on central bank digital currency. The Senate instead passed a 45-day extension hours before expiration.

The extension followed Sen. Ron Wyden's (D-OR) insistence that a classified FISA court ruling from March 2026 be declassified. That ruling -- obtained during routine annual recertification -- reportedly found major compliance problems with the program and blocked certain analytical tools, a decision the Justice Department is appealing. Senate Intelligence Committee leaders Cotton and Warner committed to seeking declassification within 15 days.

President Trump has pushed for a clean 18-month reauthorization. The 45-day window gives Congress more room to negotiate a durable deal.
