# Voice to Vision -- AI for Participatory Planning Public Comment Analysis

## Summary

MIT's Center for Constructive Communication used GPT models and Claude to geolocate, group, and summarize public comments from two NYC neighborhood planning processes, making the community input legible and searchable on public-facing websites -- an example of AI augmenting participatory planning without substituting for it.

## Key Points

Voice to Vision was a research project between MIT CCC and NYC's Department of City Planning, applied to two neighborhood planning processes: the Jamaica Neighborhood Plan and the White Plains Road Neighborhood Plan.

The AI layer did three things: geotagged comments to the locations they referenced, grouped related comments by theme, and summarized clusters of input for display on public-facing websites. The output is browsable maps of public feedback organized by theme and geography.

Critically, the AI did not make planning decisions. It made community input more usable for human planners. Hundreds or thousands of public comments are difficult to synthesize by hand, and they tend to get summarized poorly or selectively when staff do it manually under time pressure. AI-assisted synthesis that is then published publicly -- so the community can verify their input was captured accurately -- is a governance model worth examining.

The tool used both GPT models and Claude. Its first reported use was 2025.

The two neighborhood plans it supported -- Jamaica and White Plains Road -- are transit-oriented, mixed-use planning contexts not entirely unlike the land use and community engagement questions Frederick faces in areas like the Golden Mile corridor and East Frederick.

## Relevance

This is a concrete, near-term applicable model for Frederick. When César is preparing for community engagement on housing production, the LMC review, or any neighborhood-scale planning process, the challenge of synthesizing and representing community input is real. Traditional public comment processes produce a record that is technically available but practically inaccessible -- long PDFs that planners and council members rarely read in full.

A Voice to Vision-style approach would let community members see that their input was captured and how it relates to others, and would let planners and council members engage with synthesized themes rather than raw comment volumes. The public-facing publication is also an accountability mechanism: if the AI misrepresents the community input, someone can flag it.

This also sits cleanly in the assistive category from [[When AI Governs vs When AI Assists Procurement Distinctions for Local Government]]: AI is doing pattern recognition and presentation; humans are making the planning decisions.
