# Flock ALPRs Draw Bipartisan Senate Scrutiny

## Summary
A September 23 Senate Judiciary subcommittee hearing brought Republicans and Democrats together against unregulated license plate readers, with the ACLU arguing that ICE can reach local ALPR data through a single cooperating officer even without a 287(g) agreement.

## Key Points
The Subcommittee on Crime and Counterterrorism hearing, titled around Flock's nationwide AI surveillance network, followed Washington Post reporting on officers misusing Flock searches without their departments knowing. Flock, Axon, Motorola, and Verkada CEOs were invited and declined. Hawley led the questioning from the right, challenging Flock's claims that it does not use facial recognition and has never been hacked; a cybersecurity engineer testified that by any ordinary definition of unauthorized access, it has been.

The immigration angle came from Democrats. ACLU counsel Chad Marlow said formal 287(g) agreements are only one route; informal help from one officer is another. Asked by Padilla how to close that loophole, his answer was to ban the systems. Senators on both sides also raised use against people traveling for reproductive care, gun store customers, and pregnancy centers.

Proposed fixes: independent security testing of vendors, a GDPR-style federal privacy law, warrants for historical location searches, and short retention limits. Britt and Warnock asked DOJ for misuse-prevention guidance for state and local agencies. Flock responded by adding a required case number and a search-justification dropdown.

## Relevance
Maryland's 287(g) ban does not close the informal-access loophole Marlow described, and that is the scenario the City Values Act and the Community Trust Act are meant to address. If FPD or the county uses Flock or a similar system, the questions to ask are who can search, whether searches require a case number, how long data is kept, and whether any out-of-state or federal sharing is enabled by default. Vendor-side guardrails added under congressional pressure are not the same as city-side policy.
