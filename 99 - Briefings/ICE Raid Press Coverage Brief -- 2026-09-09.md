# ICE Raid Press Coverage Brief -- 2026-09-09
**Prepared for:** Council President César Díaz
**Date:** September 9, 2026
**Scope:** Press and video coverage of the ICE operation in the Taskers Chance neighborhood, September 3-7, 2026, and the immediate aftermath through September 9

---

## Executive Summary

ICE agents staked out the Taskers Chance neighborhood (Stoneybrook Court and Schaffer Drive) for roughly 40 hours starting Thursday, September 3, after a vehicle stop targeting a man described as a "criminal illegal alien" who fled the scene. Community members gathered to observe over two days, and Friday night, September 5, the confrontation turned physical: agents drove vehicles onto sidewalks toward observers, used batons against people forming a line at the targeted home's entrance, and dragged at least one protester from a porch. No arrests were made. You are quoted on the record in at least six outlets -- Frederick News-Post, The Banner (twice), CBS Baltimore, NBC4 Washington, WTOP, and The New Republic -- describing the vehicle incident, the baton use, and threatening statements agents allegedly made to residents ("we know where you live, we have your license plate"). The Frederick City Council has announced it will review the incident. Governor Moore, Rep. McClain Delaney, and Del. Kris Fair have all called for accountability or investigation; national outlets (The New Republic, CBS News) picked the story up by September 9.

This sits directly on top of the City Values Act track already documented in the March 22 immigration brief -- the ordinance you introduced restricting city cooperation with ICE. This incident is likely to become a talking point for that legislation and for any cost-recovery or data-governance follow-on.

---

## I. Chronology

**Thursday, September 3, ~6:00 a.m.** ICE vehicles arrive near the intersection of Schaffer Drive and Stoneybrook Court in Taskers Chance. The operation follows a vehicle stop targeting a man described by agents as a "criminal illegal alien," who fled.

**Thursday evening.** An ICE agent approaches Vice President Katie Nash directly and tells her about the man being sought.

**Thursday, 5:30 p.m.** (Unrelated, noted for the record.) The council held a previously scheduled closed session that evening on a personnel/promotion matter -- confirmed via the city's official notice, no connection to the ICE operation.

**Friday, September 4.** Roughly 100 community members, residents, and supporters gather to observe the continuing ICE presence. Frederick Police are called to help de-escalate tensions between agents and residents but take no enforcement action related to the ICE operation.

**Friday, September 4, evening.** Mayor O'Connor and the Council President's offices are reported to have objected publicly to the tactics as the standoff nears 30-36 hours (see Section III on the joint-statement question below).

**Friday night, ~9:30 p.m.** The confrontation escalates. Agents drive vehicles onto sidewalks where observers are standing, in what you describe as a deliberate move to clear the area. Agents push through a line of protesters at the targeted home's entrance, strike at least one person with a baton, and drag a demonstrator from the porch. Witnesses report injuries -- scrapes, a possible broken hand, back scratches; some accounts describe hospitalizations. No arrests are made; agents do conduct a search of the property.

**Total duration:** Approximately 40 hours of continuous ICE presence in the neighborhood.

**Weekend, September 6-7.** Coverage widens. Rep. April McClain Delaney and other Maryland officials publicly weigh in. WYPR, WUSA9, and Maryland Reporter's state roundup pick up the story.

**Tuesday, September 8.** Governor Wes Moore, Rep. McClain Delaney, and Del. Kris Fair publicly demand answers and call for investigation and congressional oversight (Maryland Matters). NBC4 Washington reports the Frederick City Council will formally review the incident.

**Wednesday, September 9.** The New Republic runs a national piece; the story has now moved from local to state to national coverage in under a week.

## III. The Joint Statement (Confirmed Full Text)

You provided the source graphic for this directly, so the text below is confirmed rather than reconstructed from press paraphrase. It was issued jointly by you and Mayor O'Connor, referencing "the last two days" of ICE activity, which places it Friday, September 4 or Saturday, September 5, 2026 -- consistent with The Banner's citation of it on September 4. The graphic carries a "CN" mark in the corner, consistent with distribution through a local news/community channel rather than a direct posting on the city's own News Flash page; I was not able to locate a separately hosted URL for it, so cite it as a direct city/office statement rather than a specific web link until your comms staff can point to where it's archived.

Full text:

> The City of Frederick is aware of the federal immigration enforcement activity in our community over the last two days. We understand that this activity creates fear, uncertainty, and disruption for residents and families.
>
> Immigration enforcement is a federal responsibility, and the City does not direct or control federal operations. At the same time, the City strongly objects to federal enforcement tactics that unnecessarily intimidate residents or disrupt an entire neighborhood. No federal operation should leave families feeling confined in their homes or unsafe in their own community. Such tactics damage public trust and undermine the sense of safety that law enforcement should protect.
>
> Our responsibility is to keep Frederick safe, support our residents, preserve the peace, and ensure that everyone in our community is treated with dignity and respect.
>
> The Frederick Police Department is staffed by trusted, trained law-enforcement professionals committed to constitutional policing, sound judgment, and de-escalation. Our officers remain focused on protecting and serving the entire community. Residents should not hesitate to contact FPD or call 911 when they need help.
>
> We also recognize that members of the public have a First Amendment right to observe and record law-enforcement activity from a lawful location. Anyone choosing to do so should maintain a safe distance, avoid interfering with officers, and comply with lawful safety directions.
>
> Earlier this year, the City adopted the City Values Act to uphold Frederick's values of "equity, inclusiveness, dignity, and justice for all" and to preserve trust between residents and their local government. The law is clear: "A City employee will make an inquiry only if required by law to do so." Accordingly, City employees and police officers will not ask residents about their immigration status, citizenship, country of origin, place of birth, or nationality unless legally required. City officials and the Frederick Police Department will continue monitoring conditions in the community, responding to public-safety needs, and sharing verified information when appropriate.
>
> -- Mayor Michael O'Connor & City Council President Cesar Diaz

Note the statement explicitly ties back to the City Values Act's inquiry restriction as the operative city policy during this incident -- worth citing directly if the council review in Section IV touches on what protections were actually in force during the stakeout.

## V. What Comes Next

NBC4 Washington reports the Frederick City Council will formally review the incident -- specifically, agents' use of force against protesters and the alleged intimidation tactics used during the stakeout. Maryland Matters reports officials are demanding an investigation into potential civil rights violations, a transparent inquiry by state and federal authorities, and congressional oversight.

This is separate from, but will likely get pulled into discussion of, the City Values Act, which you introduced in March 2026 to restrict city employee cooperation with ICE absent a judicial warrant. The Frederick News-Post's March 13 piece on that ordinance referenced a prior incident -- ICE agents near Hillcrest Elementary School -- as context; expect this new incident to be cited the same way going forward, and expect renewed interest in the cost-recovery amendment and data-governance policy flagged as open items in the March 22 immigration brief.
