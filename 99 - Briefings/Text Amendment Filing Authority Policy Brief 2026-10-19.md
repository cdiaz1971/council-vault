# Text Amendment Filing Authority Policy Brief 2026-10-19

## Executive Summary

The draft rewrites LMC § 306 so that only an elected City official or the Planning Division can file a text amendment, ending the right of City residents and property owners to file. It adds a Council review at a public meeting, scheduled by the Council President, before any official-filed amendment goes to the Planning Commission. The policy goal is sound and has regional precedent (Montgomery County and Frederick County both route text amendments through their councils), and it puts responsibility for citywide code changes on accountable officials. The weak points are drafting, not concept. The justification promises Council *approval* while the text says *no vote*, there is no deadline for scheduling the review, there is no rule for pending applications like the Trammell Crow data center ZTA, and nothing governs how a developer asks a Council member to sponsor. Fix those before introduction and the opposition loses its best arguments.

## What the Draft Changes

| Provision | Current § 306 | Draft |
|---|---|---|
| Who may file | City resident, City property owner, plus officials and the Division | An elected City official, or the Division |
| Pre-review | None in code. In practice, private ZTAs have been announced at a public meeting with no vote (Wormald and GG Frederick, April 2, 2026) | Official-filed: Council review at a public meeting scheduled by the Council President, sponsor presents concept and Comprehensive Plan fit, members comment, no vote. Division-filed: notice and overview to Council only |
| Planning Commission hearing | Scheduled on receipt of a complete application, "as soon as practicable" | Scheduled on completion of the review step |
| Recommendation and final action | PC recommends, Mayor schedules Council hearing, Council decides by ordinance | Unchanged, with "Board of Aldermen" updated to "City Council" |

Zoning **map** amendments are untouched. A property owner can still seek a rezoning of their own parcel. That is the single most useful rebuttal to "you're taking away property rights."

## Drafting Problems to Fix Before Introduction

1. **Approval vs. no vote.** The justification says "Requiring Council approval before a proposed text amendment proceeds" and refers to "demonstrated viability." The text says members "will not take a formal vote." As written, the screen is discussion only, so the real gate is the filing restriction. Pick one. A non-binding review is easier to defend and keeps the Council out of a pre-hearing up-or-down on the merits. A vote gives real filtering but invites the charge that a majority can kill an idea before any public hearing.
2. **No scheduling deadline.** The Council President schedules the review "at a public meeting" with no time limit. Opponents will call this a pocket veto in one person's hands. Add a clock, for example the next available workshop or within 45 days of a complete application.
3. **"Elected City official" includes the Mayor.** The justification speaks of "City Council members." If the Mayor is meant to keep filing authority, say so in the justification. If not, narrow the text.
4. **Asymmetry with staff.** Division filings get notice only, while Council member filings go through a review meeting. That inverts the accountability logic in the justification. Either put Division filings through the same review or explain why staff work needs less scrutiny.
5. **No transition clause.** The draft is silent on applications already filed by private parties, including PC25-626ZTA (Trammell Crow, data centers in M1 and M2), the Wormald § 816 amendment, and the GG Frederick first-floor residential amendment. See the legal section.
6. **No sponsorship process.** Developers will now ask Council members to file on their behalf. Without rules, influence moves from a public application to private conversations. Add a requirement that any official-filed amendment disclose who requested or drafted it, as Montgomery County ZTAs often note introduction "at the request of" a party.
7. **Fees.** Private applicants pay filing fees. If a Council member files a developer's amendment, the City may end up processing it for free. Address fee recovery for sponsored-on-request amendments.
8. **Cleanup.** Garbled sentence in the Commission hearing subsection ("receipt of a complete of a text amendment application"), unlettered new list items, and the document header says 10/19 while the filename says 20261008.

These need drafting work in chat, not here. A task has been flagged.

## Legal and Authority Considerations

**Authority to limit initiation: low risk.** Text amendments are legislative acts. Maryland's Land Use Article leaves the method of initiating text amendments to the local legislative body, and this brief found nothing in State law guaranteeing a private right to file one (outside César's notes, verify against Land Use §§ 4-202 to 4-204). Peer practice supports it. Frederick County Code § 1-19-3.100.2 says "The County Council may initiate a zoning text amendment," and in Montgomery County the District Council introduces ZTAs, with the Planning Board and private parties requesting introduction ([Montgomery Planning ZTA guide](https://montgomeryplanning.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/06/MP_ZTAOnePager_061020_v2.pdf); [Frederick County Code](https://codelibrary.amlegal.com/codes/frederickcounty/latest/frederickco_md/0-0-0-33944)).

**Council commentary before the hearing: low risk.** Because text amendments are legislative rather than quasi-judicial, members discussing a concept before the Planning Commission hearing does not raise the prejudgment problem it would in a map amendment or site plan case. Keep the review limited to text amendments so this stays true.

**Pending applications: moderate risk, highest political stakes.** Applying the new rule to applications already on file would end PC25-626ZTA and the other private ZTAs. Applicants generally have no vested right in a pending legislative change, but retroactive application invites a fairness and due process claim, and Quantum Maryland's suit against the County moratorium shows data center interests will litigate. A prospective-only rule is cleaner but lets the data center ZTA continue. This is a choice to make deliberately, not by silence. One middle option is to apply the new filing rule prospectively while requiring pending private applications that have not yet had a Planning Commission hearing to go through the new Council review step.

**Mayor's veto.** This is an ordinance amending the LMC, so it is subject to veto. Count to five before introduction if the administration objects.

## Who Benefits, Who Bears Costs

- **Gains:** Residents generally, through earlier public visibility of code changes and fewer site-specific rewrites of citywide rules (the justification's core point). Planning staff time shifts toward public priorities, including César's missing middle, parking, and ADU package.
- **Losses:** Developers and their counsel lose direct access. Residents and neighborhood groups also lose a formal tool, though in practice private filings come almost entirely from represented developers because a text amendment application takes money and expertise.
- **Equity risk:** If sponsorship becomes an informal favor, well-connected developers keep their access through Council members while residents without those relationships lose theirs. The fix is a public, documented request process open to anyone, with a response deadline.
- **Housing production risk:** Some private ZTAs have been pro-housing. GG Frederick's first-floor residential amendment (PC25-249ZTA, PC recommended 4-0) removed a real barrier to mixed-use infill. Under the draft that change would have needed a Council sponsor. The answer is that the Council did carry it (César and Nash both had drafts in July), which shows the sponsorship path works, but be ready for the argument.

## Likely Opposition

Positions below are projections from vault records and public reporting, not stated positions, unless noted.

**Development community** (Frederick County Building Industry Association, Chamber, land use counsel such as McCurdy Dean and Graditor, active applicants like Wormald, GG Frederick Holdings, Trammell Crow). Expect the strongest and most organized opposition. Arguments: it politicizes code changes, adds months to timelines, chills investment, and hands code reform to whoever has a Council member's ear. Response: map amendments and project approvals are untouched, both neighboring councils already work this way, and a transparent sponsorship process gives every applicant the same door.

**Mayor O'Connor and the administration.** Mixed. Planning staff may welcome the workload relief the justification describes. The Mayor may read the Council President's scheduling authority and the pre-Commission Council review as a move into the Division's lane, and could veto. The draft still has the Mayor scheduling the final Council hearing, which leaves a split of scheduling power that is worth deciding on purpose.

**CM Adkins.** His § 9.1 charter amendment on removing Council leadership is pending and he wants to discuss scheduling it with César. A provision that gives the Council President open-ended control over when text amendments get heard fits neatly into a "concentration of power" narrative. His October 5 memo also asked whether the LMC rewrite and housing-study text amendments should merge into one track, so he may argue process changes belong in the rewrite. A scheduling deadline takes most of the force out of the first argument.

**CM Taylor.** Her LMC modernization proposal included a 270-day pause on private text amendments once a draft code reaches Council, so she shares the diagnosis. She may still argue this belongs inside modernization rather than ahead of it. Likely gettable.

**Planning Commission.** Could see a Council review before its own hearing as preempting its role, given existing friction over how its recommendations reach Council (the ADU conditions and the Mixed Use Vertical introduction). The non-binding format helps. So does framing the review as giving the Commission clearer Council direction, which is what César's Planning Commission accountability note was after.

**Housing and YIMBY advocates.** May worry that pro-housing code fixes from builders get slower. Point to the Council-led housing package and the GG Frederick precedent.

**Good-government and civic voices.** Removing "City resident" from the list of filers is the line most likely to read badly in a news story ("Council strips residents' right to propose zoning changes"). A resident request process with a guaranteed hearing answers this directly.

**Possible allies.** Data center opponents and neighborhood groups who have fought developer-driven ZTAs, including residents who pushed back on the 2025 MU phasing amendment that the Planning Commission unanimously recommended denying ([CitizenPortal](https://citizenportal.ai/articles/6475382/maryland/frederick-county/frederick-city/planning-commission-recommends-denial-of-proposed-mu-phasing-text-amendment-asks-council-for-broader-study)). Watch that their support doesn't turn the bill into a proxy fight over PC25-626ZTA unless that is the intent.

## Options

| Option | What it does | Strength | Weakness |
|---|---|---|---|
| A. Introduce as drafted, after cleanup | Officials and Division only, non-binding review | Simple, clear accountability | Leaves the scheduling, pending-application, and sponsorship gaps open |
| B. Add guardrails (recommended) | A plus scheduling deadline, transition clause, sponsorship disclosure, fee recovery, and a resident request path | Removes the best opposition arguments, keeps the core policy | Longer text, more for staff to administer |
| C. Keep private filing, add mandatory Council review | Anyone may file, but every application gets the public Council review before the Commission | Least resistance, codifies current announcement practice | Does not reduce staff time on non-viable proposals, the main stated goal |
| D. Fold into LMC modernization | Hold until the comprehensive review | Avoids a standalone fight | Delays a fix for an ongoing problem; the rewrite has no timeline |

## Recommended Action

Option B. Before introduction: resolve approval vs. no vote, add a scheduling clock, decide the pending-application rule on purpose, add a public sponsorship request process with disclosure, and confirm whether the Mayor keeps filing authority. Then count votes with the veto in mind, and talk to Taylor and the Planning Commission chair early.
