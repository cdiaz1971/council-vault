# Weekly Council Briefing 2026-10-07

## Executive Summary

Immigration pages absorbed new federal data-sharing and litigation developments (TANF benefit data, the mask-ban suit, H.R. 1's Medicaid and SNAP effects) that sharpen the case against cooperating with federal enforcement. Data center regulation moved fast nationally, with Charlotte's moratorium now enacted, new state moratorium waves, and a federal moratorium bill introduced, while the resource-impacts page picked up hard evidence tying data centers to Maryland grid strain. Police surveillance technology got the week's heaviest sourcing, including a documented multi-case officer-stalking pattern and an accelerating wave of agency contract cancellations. Housing pages added new poverty-reduction evidence for rent interventions and new public-land production models. Parking Reform's refresh found no change to Frederick's or Maryland's governing facts.

## What changed, by topic

### [[Data Center Regulation and Local Authority]]
- *New:* Added the APWA Data Center Development Toolbox (early public-works review on water, power, stormwater, decommissioning) as a new LMC policy option; added Maine's LD 307 veto-override outcome and LD 713 signature, new moratorium waves in New Jersey and Michigan, pending-bill rundowns in five more states, a negotiating-leverage section, and six new process-and-public-trust examples.
- *Corrects earlier notes:* The Charlotte moratorium "verify" flag is resolved: enacted 11-0, in effect through November 5, 2026.
- *New avenue:* The Sanders-AOC federal data center moratorium bill is now on record as a federal-level option, though only introduced, not advanced.

### [[Data Center Resource and Community Impacts]]
- *New:* Added PJM's FERC affidavit and the Maryland People's Counsel ratepayer-cost complaint naming data centers as a cause of recent Maryland grid failures, the Warrior Run coal-plant reopening, and the Utah Stratos off-grid case.
- *Needs verification:* Maryland's proposed building energy code rollback is flagged "verify."

### [[Police Surveillance Technology]]
- *New:* Added Bluetooth-identifier capture and FreeForm by-description search as new ALPR capabilities tied to the ICE access review; the Dunwoody sales-demo-camera failure case; drone contracts tied to the NLC/BRINC partnership; the Council on Criminal Justice's four-part AI accountability framework measured against Frederick's current procurement language; ALPR network scale and the Washington Post's 50-officer misuse finding; a named wrongful-arrest case; vendor-credibility problems (a whistleblower account and the vendor CEO's false denial to Ohio officials); the new vendor-controlled audit tool; enforcement-creep and decoy-camera examples; the vendor's grant-subsidy playbook; fusion-center monitoring of anti-Flock organizing; Axon's defensive redesign; and fuller detail on the September 23, 2026 Senate hearing.
- *Corrects earlier notes:* The officer-stalking pattern is upgraded from a single case to a documented multi-case pattern.
- *Sources disagree:* The contract-cancellation wave is accelerating, but counts are flagged provisional; trackers disagree on the pace.
