# Data Center Griffin Presentation -- Questions 2026-06-08

## Summary

Questions prepared for Richard Griffin, Director of Economic Development, presenting on the economic impact of data centers at the June 12 work session. Questions are designed to surface gaps in the fiscal argument on the record without being adversarial.

## Questions

**1. On the revenue baseline**

The fiscal projections we have seen compare data centers to warehouses. What does the city currently collect in real property tax from M1 and M2 zoned properties combined? Without that baseline it is hard to put the projected revenue in context.

This is a reasonable, factual ask. It surfaces whether Griffin has done independent analysis or is relying on the applicant's numbers.

**2. On personal property**

The fiscal projections capture real property tax on the building. Data center equipment -- servers, cooling systems -- is personal property and currently exempt from the city's personal property tax. Has the department modeled what the revenue picture would look like if that exemption were addressed? Or is the $13.7 million figure the ceiling under current law?

This is the single most important gap in the fiscal case. Asking it on the record establishes the council's awareness of the issue.

**3. On phasing and timeline**

The projections assume full buildout. What is the realistic timeline for that buildout, and is it contingent on power infrastructure that does not yet exist? What does the revenue picture look like at 25%, 50%, and 75% buildout?

This tests whether the administration has stress-tested the projections or is presenting the best-case scenario as the baseline.

**4. On jobs and workforce**

The data center scenario supports fewer total jobs than a warehouse scenario, though at higher wages. What is the city's strategy for ensuring Frederick residents -- including residents without four-year degrees -- actually fill those jobs rather than commuters from outside the city?

This surfaces the equity dimension of the workforce argument without being oppositional to the project.

## Notes on Use

Ask question 2 even if Griffin does not have the answer Thursday. The point is to establish on the record that the council is aware of the personal property exemption gap and expects it to be addressed before a vote in August. Questions 1 and 3 test the independence and rigor of the administration's analysis. Question 4 connects the economic development frame to District 2 constituents and the city's ALICE population.
