## Why this matters for César

This complements the Demos piece on civic disconnection — Nakagawa's lens is specifically how **opacity and illegibility** of economic systems prevent democratic action. When nobody can see *where* wealth is going or *how* extraction happens, collective response becomes impossible. This directly informs your work on:

- Municipal transparency and accountability
- Data access as a civic good
- The relationship between information control and power
- Why GovTech governance matters (who controls the data, who sees it)

## Core idea

Wealth extraction used to be legible — workers could see wages, union organizers could count, movements could mount coordinated response. Modern extraction hides itself through complexity: financialization, offshore accounts, algorithmic pricing, nested corporate structures. When the mechanism is invisible, the politics of resistance becomes impossible. You can't organize against what you can't see.

## Connection to GovTech

This is why municipal data governance is political work, not just technical work. Who controls the data about your city's budget, housing stock, development patterns? That control is power. Making it legible is a precondition for democratic action.
