# Weekly Council Briefing 2026-10-18

## Executive Summary

This was a light week for new source material (27 new or triaged source notes against last week's much larger volume), but two items matter directly for Frederick: the October 1 Council meeting minutes added two new annexation petitions and a Díaz on-the-record comment on the City/County ALICE divergence, and a Frederick County Sheriff story gave the data center pages their first named, local construction-phase impact. A research refresh also surfaced a real gap: no public record shows Frederick adopted an ADU ordinance by Maryland's October 1, 2026 compliance deadline.

## What changed, by topic

### [[Zoning Reform]]
- *Needs verification:* Maryland's HB1466 required local ADU laws by October 1, 2026. The City's own pending/adopted-legislation tracker shows no ADU ordinance or case PC25-614ZTA action at all. Whether Frederick is currently in compliance is unconfirmed and worth a direct check with Planning before this comes up publicly.

### [[Annexation and Rezoning]]
- *New:* Two annexation petitions were announced at the October 1 meeting: about 156 acres on US Route 15 north of Sundays Lane (requesting R8 Medium Density Residential) and about 200 acres north of Winpenny Drive and Yellow Springs Road (requesting R4 Low Density Residential). Both are announcements only, not yet at hearing.

### [[Housing Affordability and Inequality]]
- *New:* At the October 1 meeting, Díaz cited the 2026 ALICE report to note that struggling households rose in the City while falling in the County, and said those families should be Council's focus. This is a council-record characterization, not a recalculated figure.

### [[Data Center Resource and Community Impacts]]
- *New:* Frederick County Sheriff Jenkins rerouted Adamstown-area data center construction traffic through New Design Road after a Buckeystown tie-up, citing "hundreds, even maybe thousands" of daily truck trips — the page's first named, local construction-phase impact, distinct from the water/power/ratepayer effects already documented.
- *New:* A DOE emergency order let PJM delay maintenance and order data centers onto backup power during an October heat stretch, naming data center growth as a stress factor.

### [[Data Center Regulation and Local Authority]]
- *New:* Opposition has moved into this November's statewide races: 39 ballot measures across eight states, an 11%-support national poll, and a Columbia/Census finding that data center construction value grew $48 billion since 2024 while housing construction value fell $29 billion nationally — concrete support for the "crowds out housing investment" argument.

### [[ICE Detention Infrastructure]]
- *New:* Two Delaney Hall (NJ) detainees died within two weeks of a House hearing that dismissed conditions complaints as exaggerated; one death went uncounted in official totals because the detainee was technically released first, a counting gap worth flagging whenever official tallies are cited.

### [[ICE Enforcement in Maryland and Frederick]]
- *New avenue:* A new section ties Frederick's pending AG guidance request on officer intervention to the national accountability gap, using the Renee Good (Minneapolis) and Marble Hill (NYC) ICE shootings as context for why that guidance matters.

### [[Local Authority and Federal Preemption]]
- *New:* A fresh real-world example (the Mullin/Hochul detainer dispute after an NYC ICE shooting) reinforces the page's existing point that detainers are voluntary requests, not mandates.

### [[Police Surveillance Technology]]
- *New:* Added body-worn cameras as a tracked technology (previously missing from the baseline table) and a new potential ICE-access channel for ALPR data through federal HIDTA drug-enforcement databases, independent of any direct vendor contract.

### [[Housing Production Strategies]], [[Homelessness]], [[Housing Politics and Advocacy]]
- *New:* A vacant-historic-school adaptive-reuse model (HUD Section 202 plus historic tax credits) was added as a production channel; a national camping-ban criminalization trend was added as enforcement context; and a contested "mobility as opportunity" advocacy frame was added and flagged against two already-cited vault sources it directly conflicts with.

### [[Frederick Streets, Traffic Safety, and Transit]]
- *New avenue:* San Francisco's Great Highway/Prop G backlash is a concrete cautionary case for pairing any Frederick street reallocation with a diversion plan for receiving streets.

### [[Voting Rights and Elections]], [[Digital Services and Accessibility]]
- *New:* National RCV polling (66% bipartisan support) and plurality-winner counts; a Georgetown report on AI-readable government websites, with a cautionary case about JavaScript-heavy dashboards misleading AI assistants.
