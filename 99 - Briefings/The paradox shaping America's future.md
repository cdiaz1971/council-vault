## Why this matters for César

This piece synthesizes two of your core policy concerns — housing/economic affordability AND democratic resilience/civic organizing. The thesis that these trends are **connected** (not separate silos) maps directly onto your council work on housing production, immigration protection, and community participation infrastructure.

## Core idea

Two trends are reshaping America: widening wealth inequality and the collapse of spaces where citizens collectively solve problems. These aren't independent crises. Economic precarity fragments civic life; fragmented civic life means less power to address inequality. The feedback loop creates a "paradox" — we need collective action to solve systemic problems, but the systems that built collective action are failing.

## Framing

The piece closes Demos' Presidents Series, positioning this paradox as **the** defining challenge of our moment. Not one policy problem, but a structural dynamic that makes policy-making itself harder. This connects to why municipal-level organizing (your focus) matters — cities are one of the few scales where collective action is still legible and possible.
