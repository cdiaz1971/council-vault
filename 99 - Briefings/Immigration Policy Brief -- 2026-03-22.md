# Immigration Policy Brief -- City of Frederick
**Prepared for:** Council President César Díaz
**Date:** March 22, 2026
**Sources:** 17 vault notes across `10 - Council/`, `10 - Council/120 - Immigration/`, and `10 - Council/190 - Organizations/`, plus daily notes March 16-22
**Scope:** Active legislation, community infrastructure, rapid response networks, state legislative environment, open policy questions

---

## Executive Summary

Frederick's immigration policy work is the most politically active thread in the current council term. Two distinct legislative tracks are running in parallel -- the City Values Act (CVA), which was introduced March 19 and governs how City employees interact with federal immigration enforcement, and the Municipal ID program, which remains in feasibility study mode. A proposed cost recovery amendment to the CVA has been drafted but not yet introduced. Beneath the legislation, a community infrastructure of three well-documented immigrant-serving organizations and a rapid response network provides the civic foundation. The national enforcement climate -- documented ICE arrests, data sharing requests, and the Renee Good shooting -- is accelerating urgency around all of it.

The central challenge is sequencing. The CVA is moving through the legislative process now. The Municipal ID feasibility study has not reported back. The cost recovery amendment and a data governance policy addressing federal data requests are on the horizon but not yet formally introduced. Frederick risks getting ahead of its implementation capacity if these tracks advance faster than the community infrastructure can absorb them.

## I. The City Values Act -- Legislative Status and Substance

### What It Does

The CVA (Ordinance G-26-1) was introduced at the March 19 regular public meeting. No vote on the merits was taken that night -- introduction only. The ordinance adds Chapter 33, Article II to the City Code and establishes three core prohibitions: City employees cannot assist ICE in accessing non-public City facilities without a judicial warrant or exigent circumstances; City property cannot be used for ICE enforcement operations; and City employees cannot inquire about immigration status except when legally required.

The [[City Values Act]] note documents the full legislative development strategy, and [[topic_city_values_act]] provides the current status framing and Bluesky communication angles. The implementation framework assigns responsibility to the Legal Department (staff guidance), the Mayor (departmental protocols and signage), and Department Directors (designee training and supervision).

### What Makes It Defensible

The ordinance is deliberately framed as administrative and procedural rather than ideological. It avoids sanctuary city language entirely. It includes a federal law compliance clause (§33-9) as a preemption shield. It creates no private right of action. Employee violations trigger internal discipline only. The FPD carve-out -- which exempts Frederick Police Department criminal law enforcement activities -- is the provision most likely to draw public questions and needs a prepared answer before any public hearing. The correct framing: the ordinance governs civil immigration enforcement by civilian employees; FPD operates under separate legal authorities for criminal enforcement and retains the ability to share immigration status information as required by federal or constitutional law.

### The Work Session and Where Things Stand

A work session was held March 12, 2026. The daily notes reference a "CVA PP fix" deferred across March 18, 19, and 20 -- indicating a specific technical or drafting issue needing correction before the ordinance can fully advance. No note in the vault documents what the PP fix specifically involves. As of March 20, it remained unresolved.

## II. The Proposed Cost Recovery Amendment

The [[Proposed City Values Act Amendment]] was drafted by city staff (dated March 20, 2026) and would authorize the Mayor to identify, document, and pursue reimbursement from DHS, ICE, and CBP for costs incurred by Frederick as a result of federal immigration enforcement activities. Recoverable costs are defined broadly: personnel overtime, public safety response, administrative and legal costs, community impact mitigation, and any other direct or indirect costs reasonably attributable to federal enforcement activities within the City.

This amendment has not been introduced as legislation. It exists in the vault as a staff draft. Sending an invoice to ICE is a posture statement as much as a fiscal recovery mechanism -- it creates a paper trail of every federal enforcement incursion into Frederick, makes cost visible, and signals that Frederick is not a passive recipient of federal activity. The legal enforceability of cost recovery against a federal agency is uncertain, but the documentation and billing infrastructure would have independent value as a public accountability record.

The [[Federal Agencies and Local Government Data -- ICE Data Requests]] note raises a related issue: federal agencies are actively requesting access to local government data -- driver records, utility accounts, benefits enrollment, court records -- for immigration enforcement purposes. A city data governance policy governing those requests would complement the cost recovery amendment and form a coherent policy posture, but no such policy has been drafted or proposed.

## III. The Municipal ID Program

### Where It Stands

The [[Memo for municipal id]] directed staff to conduct a feasibility study and fiscal impact analysis before legislation is introduced. As of the current vault snapshot, there is no note documenting that the feasibility study has been completed, that findings have been reported back to Council, or that a draft ordinance is in preparation.

### The Policy Design Framework

The vault has exceptionally deep research on Municipal ID design. [[Municipal ID -- Best Practices Checklist]] synthesizes national program implementation lessons. [[Municipal ID -- Legal Risks and Federal Preemption]] covers home-rule authority, field preemption risk, and data retention strategy. [[Municipal ID -- Equity and Inclusion Rationale]] provides the public-facing argument. [[Sample municipal id legislation]] includes a full model ordinance.

The design decisions requiring local resolution before drafting begins: the document list for identity and residency verification, the gender designation protocol, the financial institution partnership approach, and the data retention prohibition structure. These are not open legal questions -- they are documented design commitments that need to be embedded in the ordinance from the start.

### The Population Being Served

The organization notes make the served population concrete. Centro Hispano serves nearly 2,000 clients annually. AACF enrolled over 800 undocumented or underdocumented individuals in Kaiser CHAP health coverage in 2023 alone -- residents actively using city-adjacent services who lack standard identification. SSCM provides DOJ-accredited immigration legal services and tenant-landlord intervention from its Frederick office on South Jefferson Street. All three organizations are direct partners for outreach, application support, and trusted community access points in any Municipal ID rollout.

## IV. The Community Infrastructure

### The Three Primary Organizations

**[[Centro Hispano de Frederick]]** has grown from under $70k in annual revenue in 2019 to over $860k in 2024 under Executive Director Suzanne Salichs, Ph.D. It is fully grant-dependent with zero program service revenue. The board includes immigration attorney Lucelia Rodriguez Justiniano -- a natural partner for Municipal ID design and CVA implementation. The 2023 deficit year (revenue fell to $168k while expenses held at $337k) warrants a question about structural stability before the city deepens any formal funding relationship.

**[[Spanish Speaking Community of Maryland]]** has 50 years of institutional history, DOJ-accredited immigration legal services, and a $650k annual budget. Its Frederick office at 329 S. Jefferson Street serves overlapping populations with Centro Hispano. SSCM's domestic violence work -- over 224 clients served in the most recent reporting period -- is a dimension of immigrant services the other notes don't capture, intersecting with housing and safety policy.

**[[Asian American Center of Frederick]]** has 30+ staff, three locations including a west-side Old National Pike facility on the Golden Mile, and the broadest program portfolio of the three: early childhood education, community health, workforce development, refugee resettlement, and senior services. Primary council contact: Elizabeth Chung, Executive Director. Board member Dr. William Reid is Chair of Frederick County Progressives and VP of Frederick County NAACP -- a politically connected figure worth knowing.

### The Rapid Response Network

The [[Rapid Response meeting agenda]] from August 2025 documents the Frederick Rapid Response Network -- a community-organized ICE response infrastructure with a leadership team, tiered notification protocols, and canvassing capacity. The network developed from actual response experience (the Katherine Drexel response is cited as a positive case study) and is building toward accompaniment, mutual aid, and training tracks. The March 17 and 18 daily notes show César integrated into these operations: attending Centro Hispano class, navigating the New American Taskforce coordination, and being plugged into RISE/IRC planning.

### Political and Rhetorical Foundation

The [[March Speech]] and [[287 speech]] establish the ideological grounding from which the CVA and Municipal ID work flows. The March Speech explicitly names ICE as a gang of thugs, names the Renee Good killing as state-sponsored violence, and calls for building the ICE response team and Western Maryland Care Network. The 287(g) speech builds the case against local law enforcement participation in federal immigration enforcement on due process and public safety grounds. These speeches are the public register; the ordinances are the institutional translation.

## V. The State and Federal Context

### 287(g) Ban

Active advocacy as of January 2026. No vault note documents current status in the Maryland General Assembly's 2026 session.

### ICE Data Requests

Federal agencies are requesting access to local government administrative data for immigration enforcement. Frederick has no formal data governance policy addressing these requests. The [[Federal Agencies and Local Government Data -- ICE Data Requests]] note recommends proactive policy development and a conversation with the City Attorney. Cities with clear advance policies are better positioned than those responding ad hoc.

### Enforcement Climate

The [[percent of Maryland deported]] note records that 55% of those arrested by ICE in Maryland in June had no criminal record -- a sharp increase from the prior month. The Hillcrest ICE incident -- 200 yards from a school during school hours -- is in the official March 5 meeting minutes approved at the March 19 council meeting and serves as direct contextual grounding for the CVA introduction.
