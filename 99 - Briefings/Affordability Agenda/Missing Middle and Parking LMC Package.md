# Missing Middle and Parking LMC Package

**Agenda Item 5 of 6.** Part of [[Frederick Affordability Agenda 2026-2029]]. Vehicle: **ordinance** (LMC text amendment), Planning Commission review required. Sponsor: Díaz, with Taylor as the natural co-sponsor given her LMC modernization proposal.

## Summary

This is the only item in the agenda that changes what can be built, and it is the slowest because the LMC runs through the Planning Commission first. It is also the item most exposed to the Housing Certainty Act clock: every complete application filed before adoption vests under today's rules for five years. The package is César's four-amendment plan from May, narrowed by what has happened since. Nash's ADU ordinance is already carrying the owner-occupancy repeal, so this package supports it rather than competing with it, and carries the three pieces nobody else is: R4 and R6 missing middle, parking reform, and faith-owned land.

## What the LMC says today

- **R4 and R6** are single-family detached zones, minimum lots of 8,000 and 7,000 square feet ([[Frederick Housing Policy Baseline]]). Duplexes, two-family, and townhouses are permitted by right starting at R8 (Table 404-1).
- **Parking minimums (Table 607-1):** single-family 2 per unit; townhouse 2; two-family 2; duplex 1.5; multifamily 1.5. The downtown overlay already relaxes these. The vault's cost figures: $5,000 to $10,000 per surface space, $25,000 to $50,000 structured, passed through to rent.
- **ADUs (§ 802(c)):** permitted by right on single-family, duplex, and townhouse lots in RO, R4, R8, R12, R16, R20, DBO, and DR, with one additional parking space where the main house is at the minimum, owner occupancy of one of the two units, a 90-day minimum lease, and a recorded declaration. **Drafting check:** § 802(c)(1)'s district list omits R6. Confirm with Planning whether ADUs are actually permitted in R6.
- **Zero detached ADUs** permitted since the 2021 amendment ([[ADU Ordinance Brief 2026-08-13]]).
- **State parking relief will not come.** HB 894 (Transit and Housing Opportunity Act) covers WMATA, Purple Line, Baltimore rail, and MARC Penn Line stations. The Brunswick Line serving Frederick is not on the list ([[Maryland Transit and Housing Opportunity Act -- Parking Minimums Near Transit]]). Parking is a local decision.

## The package

**1. Missing middle by right in R4 and R6.** Permit duplexes and townhouses using R8's dimensional standards (4,000 square feet per duplex, 1,400 per townhouse unit). Layer Denver's size-for-units design: cap the size of a single dwelling on a lot and allow more total floor area only when it is divided into more, smaller units. This answers the "luxury teardown" objection with a code provision ([[Zoning Math as Gentrification Intervention -- Denver Unlocking Housing Choices]]).

**2. Parking reform.** Reduce the minimum for duplex, townhouse, two-family, and multifamily outside downtown (for example to 1 per unit, or eliminate minimums within a set distance of fixed-route TransIT service), keep maximums, keep single-family minimums unchanged. Parking is still allowed, just not mandated ([[Parking Reform]], [[Dallas Parking Reform Produced Concrete Results -- Peer City Evidence]]). This is also the agenda's transportation-cost item: the ALICE transportation line is driven by car ownership, and the Council does not run transit.

**3. Faith-owned and nonprofit land by right.** A targeted amendment allowing residential development on land owned by religious institutions and nonprofits without rezoning, with a four-year sunset on the Virginia HB 1279 model so reauthorization is voted on against real production data ([[Housing -- Faith-Owned Land as Infill Housing Opportunity]]).

**4. ADUs: back the Nash ordinance.** PC25-614ZTA (Nash) eliminates owner occupancy, reduces ADU parking, and raises the detached cap to 1,200 square feet. It is in workshop, with the Planning Commission's 4-0 recommendation diverging on owner occupancy and a downtown parking exemption. Supporting it is faster than carrying a duplicate. Confirm with Rachel Gray whether Maryland's ADU law (HB 1466, local compliance deadline October 1, 2026, per the Council Tracker) independently requires removing the deed-restriction requirement, since that deadline is this week.

**Pairing.** Every unit this package unlocks in the 7 to 14 unit range contributes nothing to affordability unless the MPDU threshold changes. That is why the tiered threshold in [[MPDU Modernization and Housing Fund Capitalization]] should be adopted first.

### Option to consider

- **Frame duplexes, townhouses, and ADUs as aging-in-place housing.** 44% of senior households in Frederick County are below the ALICE threshold, and seniors are now the largest age group below it. Presenting the package partly as a way for seniors to downsize or add a unit and stay in their neighborhoods broadens the coalition for the slowest and most contested item on the agenda.

## Obstacles

- **Housing Certainty Act vesting.** Five years from complete application (G-26-11, September 17, 2026). A proposed LMC review moratorium does not stop vested projects and may accelerate filings under current rules ([[Maryland Housing Certainty Act ↔ Frederick LMC Reform Synthesis]]).
- **Competing LMC vehicles.** Taylor's LMC Review Commission resolution and moratorium proposal, and Brehm's major site plan amendment (Council option to take final site plan authority for 5+ unit residential, adding up to about 90 days), which lands squarely on missing-middle projects. Raise the overlap before both move.
- **Planning Commission.** Its chair opposed the ADU draft as written. Engage the Commission early, not at the hearing.
- **Neighborhood opposition.** Citizens for Responsible Growth has organized on the ADU ordinance. Size-for-units and the sunset on faith land are the design answers. The political answer is the Housing RFI data and the annual production report.
- **Schools.** Any R4 and R6 change runs into the APFO school test. The Chapter 4 companion in Item 3 addresses deed-restricted units only.
- **Supply without protection.** Upzoning raises land values before it delivers units. Items 1 and 4 are the protection track and should be enacted first.

## Votes

Taylor authored LMC modernization; Nash carries ADUs; Hempel Irani and Shackelford were rated favorable to task force zoning recommendations; the MAHTF rated missing middle, ADU reform, parking reduction, and faith-based by-right zoning high urgency. Adkins's planning background makes him the key technical voice. Five votes is plausible, but this is the item most likely to draw a veto if neighborhood opposition is loud.
