# Housing Production Accountability Ordinance

**Agenda Item 2 of 6.** Part of [[Frederick Affordability Agenda 2026-2029]]. Vehicle: **ordinance** (new Article in City Code Chapter 19). Sponsor: Díaz.

## Summary

Resolution 26-15, "Declaring the City of Frederick's Commitment to Advancing Affordable Housing Production," passed 6-0 on October 1, 2026, as written. It is settled and is not being amended. It is the basis for action, not a list of actions. It adopts the task force's goal of 4,000 additional units over ten years, names nine priority recommendations, and requires an HHS annual Housing Production Report starting in FY2028 ([[Hearing Brief 2026-10-01]]).

César's own rule applies: a resolution creates a public record but cannot compel anything, so it is worth passing only as the first step in a defined sequence. This item is the next step. Put the production goal and the annual reporting into the City Code by ordinance, where the Code already has three working precedents for mandatory department reports. The ordinance is cheap, hard to oppose, and turns the goal into a scoreboard.

## The ordinance

### Why an ordinance

Charter Art. VI(A), § 2 places all departments under the Mayor. A resolution "directing" HHS to publish a report is a request. A Code section requiring the Director to deliver a report by a date is law. The Code already does this three times: § 12.5-88 (annual rental licensing report to Council by March 1), § 19-6.1(g) (annual Housing Fund fee report), and § 33-11(b) (quarterly City Values Act report under G-26-1). The Council is not inventing a power, it is using one it has used before.

### What it would contain

A new Article in Chapter 19, "Housing Production and Affordability Reporting."

1. **Benchmark.** Codify Resolution 26-15's goal of 4,000 additional units over ten years as the measure the report is scored against. Framed as a benchmark, not a mandate on private builders, which addresses the Adkins committee objection that shaped the May redraft.
2. **Annual Housing Production and Affordability Report**, due to Council by October 1 for the prior fiscal year and presented at a public meeting. Contents:
   - Units permitted, started, and completed, by type (single-family, townhouse, 2 to 8 units, 9+ units, ADU) and by tenure.
   - Deed-restricted units by AMI band (30, 60, 80, 90%), and progress against each benchmark.
   - MPDU inventory: units produced, units paying in lieu, **covenants expiring in the next ten years** under the 40-year control period (§ 19-2(g)), and resales released under § 19-12(d).
   - City Housing Fund and any successor fund: receipts by source, commitments, units supported ([[MPDU Modernization and Housing Fund Capitalization]]).
   - Displacement indicators: rental license revocations and notices to vacate (already counted under § 12.5-88), failure-to-pay-rent filings in District Court for City addresses, and residential water shutoffs (from [[Water and Sewer Affordability Ordinance]]).
   - Recommendations when any benchmark is missed.
3. **Quarterly permit timeline report.** Median and 90th-percentile review time from complete application to approval, by project type, with deed-restricted affordable projects broken out. The MAHTF's target is 8 to 10 months against 18 to 24 today. A target without a measurement is not a target.
4. **Data portal.** Publish the underlying tables on the City website, which answers the task force's dashboard recommendation without a separate procurement.
5. **Sunset review**, not a sunset: the Council reviews the benchmark after five annual reports.

### Cost and burden

Staff time in HHS, Planning, and Permitting. Most data already exists in permitting systems, the MPDU agreements recorded under § 19-6(d), and the licensing annual report. No appropriation is required to pass it. If staff say otherwise, that is useful information about the City's capacity to hit any target at all.

## Obstacles

- **Administration resistance to reporting mandates.** Expect the argument that the Council is managing staff. Answer with the three Code precedents.
- **Legal review.** Low risk. Advisory only.
- **Coalition.** Resolution 26-15 passed 6-0, so the ordinance codifies something the Council already voted for. Adkins's objection in committee was to the methodology behind the unit numbers, which the "benchmark" framing addresses. Expect his October 5 Government Operations memo on the TPMA projection to resurface.
