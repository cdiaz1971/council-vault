# Water and Sewer Affordability Ordinance

**Agenda Item 4 of 6.** Part of [[Frederick Affordability Agenda 2026-2029]]. Vehicle: **ordinance** amending City Code Chapter 25, plus an FY2028 appropriation. Sponsors: Díaz and Nash (Nash originated the Government Operations workplan items). Committee: Government Operations.

## Summary

Water is the one household bill the City sets directly. The Council fixes the rate, writes the shutoff rules, and runs the utility, so this is the cleanest affordability authority it has outside housing. Four separate water items sit on the roadmap in different stages (interest waiver, fee waiver, program codification, assistance fund, plus a check-off study). Moving them one at a time means four trips through committee for what is one policy. This item packages them into a single ordinance, adds the tenant-notice protection that nothing in state or City law currently provides, and funds a low-income credit in a way that respects the utility fund's legal limits.

## What exists today

- **Rates.** Council sets water rents under Charter Art. XI, § 2(d) and Code § 25-1. Rates were proposed at 3% a year for FY2026 through FY2030. A typical household using 10,000 gallons a quarter was projected at about $115 each for water and sewer per quarter in 2025, roughly $230 a quarter combined (WFMD, February 2025; Frederick News-Post via Yahoo). Confirm the adopted schedule with Finance.
- **Billing.** Quarterly, to "the property, the property owner, or the property owner's designee" (§ 25-1(c)). Late payments carry 1% a month (§ 25-1(d)).
- **Shutoff.** Ten days' mailed notice **to the owner only**, then discontinuance. After 60 days unpaid, charges become a first lien collectible like taxes. Service returns only after the full balance, interest, penalties, and reconnection fee are paid (§ 25-2).
- **Administrative practice (not in Code).** No residential shutoff below a $350 balance; payment plans on request; one-time leak forgiveness (55 accounts, $68,742 in FY2025; 26 accounts, $48,038 in FY2026 through February 17); InvoiceCloud notifications ([[Water Shutoff Prevention -- Frederick Existing Programs and Proposed Legislative Actions]]).
- **Round-up fund.** § 25-12 lets customers round up bills into a fund "to assist residential customers facing financial hardship," administered under rules the Mayor sets. Authority: Env. § 9-202, which HB 923 (2018) amended to allow affordability programs "to assist homeowners."
- **Utility fund rule.** § 8-11 keeps water and sewer revenue in a separate fund for operations, replacement, and debt. Deficits are made up from the General Fund.

## The gaps

1. **Tenants get no notice.** A renter whose landlord stops paying the water bill learns about it when the tap runs dry. Maryland's occupant-notice statute, PU § 7-309, requires 14 days' "IMPORTANT NOTICE TO ALL OCCUPANTS" and lets tenants pay and deduct, but it applies only to PSC-regulated gas and electric companies, not to a municipal water system. Frederick's § 25-2(a) notifies only the owner. This is a gap only the City can close, and it matters most in District 2's rental stock.
2. **Protections are policy, not law.** The $350 threshold and payment plans can change without a vote.
3. **The penalty stack compounds.** 1% a month plus reconnection fees plus full-balance-before-restoration turns a missed quarter into a lien on a home.
4. **No low-income rate or credit.** Assistance is one-time (leak forgiveness) or donation-based (round-up).

## The ordinance

**A. Codify the floor.** Put the $350 no-shutoff threshold (indexed to the rate schedule) and the right to a payment plan into § 25-2.

**B. Interest and fee relief.** Adopt the June 22 interest waiver if it has not passed, with two changes flagged at committee: codified standards for the CAO's discretion, and Council review of implementing regulations before they take effect. Add waiver of reconnection and turn-on/turn-off fees for enrolled households.

**C. All-occupants notice.** When the service address differs from the billing address, or the property is a multi-unit building, the City must mail and post a notice to "All Occupants" at least 14 days before discontinuance, in English and Spanish, modeled on PU § 7-309. § 25-1(e) already has the City posting default notices in condominium common areas, so the mechanics exist.

**D. Tenant continuity.** Occupants may keep service by paying current charges going forward in their own name, without assuming the owner's arrears. The arrears stay where § 25-2(c) already puts them: a lien on the owner's property. Pair with the Real Rent rule that a landlord may not charge a tenant for a shutoff caused by the landlord's nonpayment ([[Real Rent Ordinance Revisions]], Revision 5). **Needs a City Attorney opinion** on account-holder mechanics.

**E. Protective periods.** No residential shutoff on a day before a weekend or City holiday, when the forecast low is at or below freezing or a heat advisory is in effect, or for 30 days (renewable once) on certification from a medical provider that a household member's health would be endangered.

**F. Low-income water credit.** A quarterly bill credit for households enrolled in SNAP, Medicaid, or Maryland energy assistance, or at or below 200% of the federal poverty level, the eligibility line Baltimore uses for its Water4All income-based program. Funding: the § 25-12 round-up fund, a General Fund transfer, grants, and any State or federal water assistance. **Not from the rate base.** § 8-11's structure and ordinary cost-of-service principles make a rate-funded discount legally riskier than a General Fund subsidy, and Env. § 9-202 speaks only of homeowners. Enroll through the existing HHS and Frederick Community Action Agency intake so there is one application.

**G. Reporting.** Annual count of shutoff notices, shutoffs, restorations, payment plans, waivers, and credits, by census tract, delivered into the [[Housing Production Accountability Ordinance]] report.

### Options to consider

- **ALICE-threshold tier for the credit.** As drafted, the credit reaches households at or below 200% of the federal poverty level, about $62,400 for a family of four. The Frederick County ALICE survival budget for that family is $121,080, so most of the City's roughly 11,100 ALICE households would not qualify. Option: add a second, smaller credit for households above 200% FPL and up to the Frederick County ALICE Threshold, enrolled through the same single application. Cost scales with enrollment, so it belongs in the FY2028 budget decision alongside the base credit.
- **Seniors as a priority group.** 44% of senior households in Frederick County are below the ALICE threshold, and seniors are now the largest age group below it. Env. § 9-202 speaks only of homeowners, which limits the credit for tenants but fits senior homeowners on fixed incomes. Option: name 65+ homeowners as a priority enrollment group.

### What it costs

Parts A through E cost the utility some interest and fee revenue and some collection delay. The leak forgiveness program is the scale reference: about $50,000 to $70,000 a year. Part F is the real appropriation. **Illustrative only:** 1,000 enrolled households at a $30 quarterly credit is $120,000 a year. Finance should replace this with actual residential account counts and SNAP and Medicaid enrollment for City addresses. Note what the credit cannot do: tenants in master-metered buildings do not have their own account, so they are reached through the Real Rent utility-billing rules rather than the credit.

## Equity read

Past-due water debt becomes a first lien collectible like taxes (§ 25-2(c)), which puts a household's home at risk over a utility bill, and it falls hardest on the lower-income homeowners in District 2. Tenants carry the other risk: losing service for a debt that is not theirs. Both are addressed here without asking anyone to stop paying.

## Obstacles

- **Shackelford.** Opposed shutoff prevention at the April 8 budget session on non-payment abuse grounds ([[Budget Notes 2026-04-08]]). The design answers him: relief requires enrollment, a payment plan, and continued engagement, and nobody's balance is forgiven by this ordinance.
- **Utility fund integrity.** Finance will defend the enterprise fund. Keeping Part F off the rate base is the concession that makes the rest passable.
- **Tenant continuity mechanics** (Part D) are new for Frederick and need legal and Finance design time.
- **Budget veto.** Part F is an FY2028 appropriation, exposed to the Mayor's veto and the five-vote override. The Finance Department floated shutoff prevention itself in April, which suggests the administration is not opposed in principle.
