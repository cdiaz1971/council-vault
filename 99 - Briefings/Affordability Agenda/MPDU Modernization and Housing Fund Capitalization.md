# MPDU Modernization and Housing Fund Capitalization

**Agenda Item 3 of 6.** Part of [[Frederick Affordability Agenda 2026-2029]]. Vehicle: **ordinance** amending City Code Chapter 19 (plus a companion Chapter 4 APFO amendment). Sponsor: Díaz, coordinated with Brehm's Public Works Committee.

## Summary

Frederick's inclusionary program has good bones (12.5% set-aside, 40-year control period, covenants senior to financing) and two broken dials: a $2.00 per square foot payment in lieu and a 25-unit threshold that exempts all missing-middle development. The Code already contains the fix for the first dial. Section 19-6.1(b)(3) says that after three years of square-footage data the Council "will establish a formula" for the fee based on the actual affordability gap. That three-year mark arrives around November 2026. The strongest version of this item is to make the City run its own formula, adopt the result with a floor, restructure the threshold, and route the money into a fund with more than one revenue stream.

## What the Code says today

- **Threshold.** Applies to residential projects of 25 or more units at one location (§ 19-3(a)).
- **Set-aside.** 12.5% of units, matching bedroom mix (§ 19-6(b), (c)).
- **Affordability.** Rental MPDUs in thirds at 60, 70, and 80% FCAMI; for-sale in thirds at 70, 80, and 90% (§§ 19-8, 19-9). Control period 40 years (§ 19-2(g)).
- **Payment in lieu.** Developer may pay instead of building, at a per-square-foot rate on every unit in the project, **$2.00 "as of November 2, 2023"** (§ 19-6.1(b)(2)). Paid before the first building permit (§ 19-6.1(d)). Using the fee forfeits the density bonus (§ 19-6.1(f)).
- **Recalculation.** DPW collects square-footage data "as soon as practicable." After three years of data, the Council "by ordinance or resolution will establish a formula": affordability gap (median attached home price over three years vs. what a 70% FCAMI buyer can finance at FHA rates) × 12.5% ÷ average new-unit square footage (§ 19-6.1(b)(3)).
- **Fund.** Fees go to the City Housing Fund (§ 19-18), with ten authorized uses including LIHTC financing and assistance to households below MPDU incomes. Annual fee report required (§ 19-6.1(g)).
- **Vehicle correction.** Chapter 19 is City Code, not the LMC. The Council Tracker lists the vehicle as "Ordinance (LMC amendment)." Only the density bonus reference (LMC § 405(d)) touches the LMC. Fee, threshold, and fund changes do not need Planning Commission referral, which saves roughly three months.

## The numbers that frame the fight

- $2.00 per square foot on a 1,000 square foot unit is $2,000 against a capitalized affordability gap César's brief estimates at $60,000 to $80,000 ([[MPDU Reform -- Personal Strategy Brief (2026-05-13)]]).
- City Housing Fund balance about $4.3 million. Task force projects in-lieu revenue of $1.83 million by 2030 and $4.58 million by 2035, and calls that insufficient ([[MAHTF Final Report Policy Brief -- 2026-07-16]]).
- Beyond Shelter projected $2.5 million in MPDU fee revenue over five years. FCBIA cited $18.2 million in anticipated fees from 9,200 approved units against 1,300 on-site MPDUs. Those two figures cannot both be right, and staff were asked to confirm them ([[Public Works Committee Brief 2026-07-28]]).

### What the Code's own formula might produce

The formula is the legally anchored number, not the $12 in César's strategy brief. Its output depends on two inputs only staff can supply. The table below is illustrative only, to show the range, and must be replaced with HHS and DPW data.

| Affordability gap per unit (assumed) | × 12.5% | ÷ 1,400 sq ft avg | ÷ 1,800 sq ft avg |
|---|---|---|---|
| $80,000 | $10,000 | $7.14 / sq ft | $5.56 / sq ft |
| $120,000 | $15,000 | $10.71 / sq ft | $8.33 / sq ft |
| $160,000 | $20,000 | $14.29 / sq ft | $11.11 / sq ft |

Two implications. First, the formula probably lands well above $2 and plausibly near $12 only if the ownership gap is large, so the Council should see the formula result before committing to a number in public. Second, the formula measures the **for-sale** gap even though most new MPDUs in Frederick are rental. The ordinance should add a rental-gap calculation and use the higher of the two.

## The ordinance

**A. Adopt the formula, with a floor and an index.** Establish the § 19-6.1(b)(3) formula as required, set the fee at the formula result or a statutory floor, whichever is higher, and index it annually to CPI between three-year recalculations (§ 19-2(e) already defines the CPI to use). Add the rental-gap calculation.

**B. Tiered threshold.** Full MPDU requirement at 15 or more units; payment in lieu only at 7 to 14 units; exempt below 7. The MAHTF recommends 10 units with no fee or depth change, which would put more projects under a weak requirement. The tiered version captures missing-middle production (the units the LMC package in [[Missing Middle and Parking LMC Package]] is designed to unlock) without forcing a 3-unit builder to administer resale covenants. Habitat's testimony supports a graduated fee tied to project value, which is compatible.

**C. Deeper-affordability option.** Let a developer satisfy the requirement with fewer units at deeper affordability (for example, 10% at 60% AMI or below instead of 12.5% in the standard thirds), with the equivalence set by the same gap math.

**D. CLT compliance option.** Allow the obligation to be met by conveying land or capital to a designated community land trust or nonprofit housing agency (§ 19-11 already lets the Council designate housing agencies by resolution). Converts 40-year affordability into permanent affordability. See [[Frederick Community Land Trust -- Policy Brief]].

**E. Vesting question.** HB 548 (Housing Certainty Act), implemented by G-26-11, vests projects for five years from complete application. Ask the City Attorney whether the MPDU fee and threshold are "standards" that vest. If they do, every month between now and adoption locks more projects at $2.00.

**F. Companion Chapter 4 amendment (APFO).** The task force's highest-urgency council-actionable item: deed-restricted units at or below 40% AMI do not count toward the school adequacy test, 40 to 60% count as half, 60 to 80% as three quarters, for developments entirely affordable at or below 80% AMI. The Code already exempts age-restricted "housing for older persons" from the school test (§ 4-15(b)(1)(C)), so the Council is extending an existing category, not inventing an exemption. Be direct about the tension: three City schools are above 120% of state-rated capacity and six above 100%. The equity argument is that exempting senior housing while counting affordable family housing at full weight is itself a distributional choice.

## Capitalizing the fund

Council Member Brehm's Housing Solutions Fund Ordinance, workshopped in Public Works on July 28, 2026, would repeal § 19-18 and create Chapter 19.5 with a spending hierarchy and an annual report. Its fiscal impact field reads N/A and it names no revenue beyond MPDU receipts. Rather than a competing bill, this item proposes a **capitalization amendment** to Brehm's ordinance, which respects his committee and gives him a better bill.

| Stream | Authority | Status |
|---|---|---|
| MPDU payments in lieu at the recalculated rate | § 19-6.1(g) | Grows with Part A. |
| MPDU foreclosure and resale excess | § 19-16(a) | Existing, small. |
| Net rental licensing revenue | § 12.5-87 | Already split 50% rental assistance, 50% affordable housing conservation. FY2026 licensing revenue ran **$1.73 million against a $300,000 projection** through Q3 ([[FY2026 Q3 Budget Update Analysis 2026-08-10]]). Ask Finance how much reached the two special funds and whether HHS adopted the § 12.5-87(e) regulations. The size of this stream depends on the fee level set in the separate licensing rewrite. |
| Habitually vacant property surtax | § 8-14(b) | Revenue above the normal rate could be dedicated by ordinance. Ask Finance for annual yield. |
| General Fund appropriation | Charter Art. VII, § 5(c) | The City Housing Fund is a governmental fund, fully within the Council's appropriation power ([[FY 2027 Budget -- City Fund Types Reference]]). A standing target (for example, a share of prior-year unassigned surplus) is a budget decision each year, subject to the Mayor's veto and the five-vote override. |
| Data center community benefit contributions | Negotiated | A formula-based housing contribution as a standard approval condition ([[Housing Affordability Policy Brief -- 2026-09-08]]). |

Two governance amendments to Brehm's draft: require Council approval for any spending under the Director's catch-all category (§ 19.5-02(b)(6) in his draft), and fold the fund's annual report into the [[Housing Production Accountability Ordinance]] so there is one scoreboard.

### Longer pole: equity-replacement production fund

The Montgomery County HOC model (public equity at about 5% replacing private equity at 15 to 20%) and Ryan Trout's $15 million Housing Accelerator Fund proposal to Government Operations on September 14 both point to HACF as the institutional home ([[Montgomery County Housing Production Fund as State Model]], [[A.1 Frederick Housing Fund Memo]]). Nash is drafting a policy. That work is a 2027 to 2028 build. This item's role is to make sure the fund those proposals would draw on has recurring revenue.

## Obstacles

- **Developer opposition.** FCBIA and FCAR will argue feasibility. Answer with the Housing RFI (already on the roadmap as a president letter) and the Code's own formula. "We are running the formula the 2023 ordinance required" is a stronger public position than "we picked $12."
- **Venue.** Brehm chairs Public Works and authored both the fund ordinance and an MPDU reform agenda. He controls pace. Co-sponsorship is the realistic path.
- **Staff capacity.** HHS spending and staffing were flagged as declining in the Q3 analysis. The formula analysis is the long pole. Request it now.
- **Veto.** Moderate. The Mayor's own task force recommended MPDU modernization and more fund capitalization.
- **Vesting.** See Part E.
