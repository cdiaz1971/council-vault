# Weekly Council Briefing 2026-10-25

## Executive Summary

The October 8 work session minutes supplied two concrete, Frederick-specific facts: County Transit's shelter count and 2030 census funding-tier risk, and CAO Barkdoll's bond-financing briefing ahead of a December bond sale. The Díaz/Brehm Section 306 text-amendment-filing-authority amendment is now tracked on the Land Management Code page, with its two competing draft versions and open scheduling gaps spelled out. AI governance and procurement picked up the heaviest load of new material this week, mostly peer-city adoption data and a Council-oversight framework. Both Immigration research refreshes (City Values Act, Maryland Community Trust Act) found no material change: all three live court challenges remain pending.

## What changed, by topic

### [[Tenant Protections]]
- *New:* Baltimore County eviction data (4,300+ FY2025 evictions, a 14-day pre-eviction notice extension, and repeal of its abandonment ordinance) as a near-neighbor comparison case.
- *Sources disagree:* The Baltimore Banner reports the county council passed the notice-extension/abandonment-repeal bill; a same-month Baltimore Brew report describes it as still pending council action. Flagged verify.
- *New avenue:* Baltimore City's abandonment ordinance was struck down as unconstitutional in 2022 and again in 2024, giving Frederick a concrete legal-exposure argument for auditing its own practice now rather than reacting to a future lawsuit.

### [[Building Types and Codes]] / [[Housing Production Strategies]]
- *New:* CASA's Method Road triplex-to-nine-unit conversion in Raleigh, built for youth aging out of foster care, using a transit-density allowance, parking-minimum elimination, and a pre-2001 stormwater exemption to avoid rezoning. The $2.7M financing stack (city bond, county HOME CHDO funds, federal CPF earmark, other grants) is cross-linked between the two pages.
- *New avenue:* A replicable by-right template for small nonprofit-led supportive housing, with a caution that discretionary-style review (four rounds in this case) can blunt a by-right reform's speed advantage even when no variance is needed.

### [[Childcare and the Care Economy]]
- *New:* Maryland's HB 849 / Chapter 594 (2026) exempts families experiencing homelessness from the Child Care Scholarship Program waitlist, effective July 1, 2026 (about 3,000 children exempted to date per MSDE). A separate 2025 county feasibility study names Frederick's own Golden Mile/Route 40 corridor as a childcare hotspot and documents a capacity decline from 10,352 to 9,485 slots (2020-2024), plus a county pilot scholarship for 4-year-olds.

### [[Federal Immigration Policy]]
- *New:* The Migration Policy Institute's October 2026 estimate of roughly 980,000 ICE/CBP deportations (January 2025-July 2026), presented as a more credible count than DHS's public claim of 3 million.

### [[City Values Act]] / [[Maryland Community Trust Act]]
- *No change this week:* Both research refreshes found nothing new. The Q3 2026 City Values Act compliance report isn't due until November 14. All three live court challenges (Gahler v. Moore, United States v. Maryland, and DOJ v. Maryland over the police mask ban) remain pending with no ruling found.

### [[Frederick Streets, Traffic Safety, and Transit]]
- *New:* Montgomery County's bill lowering its sidewalk-hearing-waiver threshold to a 25 percent petition (instead of a majority) as a procedural model, noting Frederick's own waiver/hearing rules aren't documented in the vault. A multi-state 2026 trend toward speed/power-tiered e-bike rules as context for Brehm's Class 1 sidewalk-access compromise. From the October 8 work session minutes: 34 bus shelters system-wide, 25 in the City, and the 2030 census could move Frederick County into the large-urban-transit funding category.

### [[Land Management Code]]
- *New:* The Díaz/Brehm Section 306 text-amendment-filing-authority amendment, presented at the October 22 workshop, is now tracked as a pipeline item, distinguishing the two drafted versions (a Council vote versus non-voting Council review before Planning Commission) and flagging the missing scheduling deadline and transition rule.

### [[Data Center Regulation and Local Authority]]
- *New:* Anne Arundel County's moratorium detail: confirmed 7-0 vote, a hard January 31, 2028 sunset, adjacent-phase grandfathering that let Microsoft keep building at Annapolis Junction, and repeal of a personal-property tax assessment reduction.
- *New avenue:* These give Frederick two concrete drafting precedents for its own PC25-626ZTA moratorium response: deciding the adjacent-phase question deliberately, and pairing a moratorium with incentive repeal as a fiscal lever that doesn't conflict with the Housing Certainty Act.

### [[AI Governance and Procurement]] / [[Digital Services and Accessibility]]
- *New:* San Diego's 98 percent staff AI-approval figure, Utah's Claude Code productivity pilot, the Code for America/Anthropic caseworker pilot, California's AI public-comment analysis, and a "three modernization myths" framework usable as a Council oversight diagnostic. Separately, a dedicated-digital-service-team framing noting Frederick has no such team.

### [[Public Sector Labor in Maryland]]
- *New:* Maryland PERB ruled the state violated its bargaining agreement on telework records, a live precedent relevant to César's binding-arbitration bill.

### [[Maryland Legislature and State Landscape]]
- *New:* The Latino Legislative Caucus has grown to 100 members with a 21-candidate pipeline for 2026, flagging an open research gap: Frederick County's own nominee in that pipeline hasn't been identified.

### [[Voting Rights and Elections]]
- *New:* RNC v. Mi Familia Vota, argued at the Supreme Court December 8, 2026, as the registration-side counterpart to the SAVE database purges and Callais. Decision expected by June 2027.

### [[Priority-Driven Budgeting]] / [[Revenue, Taxes, and Debt]]
- *New:* An SMU DataArts ten-city study using municipal arts funding as a PDB evidence case, tied to Frederick's Art in City Hall program. From the October 8 work session minutes: CAO Barkdoll's bond-financing briefing (bonds versus PayGo, the City's strong bond rating), with a bond ordinance expected the following week and a December market date targeted.
