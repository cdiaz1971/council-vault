# Water Shutoff Prevention -- Frederick Existing Programs and Proposed Legislative Actions

## Reading context

This April 2026 correspondence note preserves the administrative practices and proposals described then. The shutoff threshold, repayment arrangements, and leak credits are not permanent statutory rights or confirmation of current practice. The FY2026 credit figures cover only the period through February 17 and are not directly comparable with a full fiscal year. Verify current written rules and live legislative status before acting. Use [[Frederick Water, Sewer, and Stormwater]] for the core reference and [[Parks and Infrastructure - In-Progress Plans#Water and utility affordability|Utility affordability planning]] for recorded evidence needs.


## Summary

[[Katie Nash]] documented Frederick's existing water shutoff prevention programs and proposed three legislative actions for the Government Operations Committee. César's April 9 reply flags two of the three as near-term legislative targets -- interest waiver authority and turn-on/turn-off fee waiver -- and asks whether to move on them incrementally or wait for a complete package.

## Existing Programs

**InvoiceCloud implementation** -- online account access, multiple payment options (credit/debit/e-check), text/email notifications, paperless billing, QR codes on mailed bills.

**Disconnection threshold** -- City does not initiate residential shutoffs unless outstanding balance exceeds $350. Designed to give residents time to catch up on short-term shortfalls without service loss.

**Flexible payment plans** -- available to any residential customer on request. Goal is early engagement before balances compound.

**One-time leak forgiveness program** -- available to residential customers only; requires at least double normal usage compared to prior cycles; leak must be repaired and usage returned to normal before adjustment. FY2025: 55 accounts, $68,742 in credits. FY2026 through February 17: 26 accounts, $48,038.

## Proposed Program -- Engagement-Based Assistance

Finance/HHS are jointly developing a residential water/sewer utility assistance program. Core principle: if a resident is actively working with the City to resolve their balance, the City supports that effort rather than adding penalties.

Interest waiver for residential customers who enter a payment plan or assistance program, stay in communication, and make a good-faith effort to pay. Funding strategy includes non-rate revenues, grants, nonprofit/community partnerships, and options to maintain utility fund financial stability.

## Legislative Actions Needed

Three distinct legislative asks identified:

1. **Authorization to Waive Interest** -- allow the City to waive or reduce interest on residential accounts when customers are actively engaged in repayment or assistance efforts.

2. **Authority to Modify or Waive Turn-On/Turn-Off Fees** -- flexibility to reduce or eliminate disconnection and reconnection fees for qualifying residential customers.

3. **Codification of Residential Assistance Program** -- establish a formal program structure including eligibility criteria and administration.

## César's Position (April 9)

César replied to Nash flagging items 1 and 2 as the simpler near-term moves: item 1 (interest waiver) looks like an ordinance; item 2 (fee waiver) also relatively tractable. Item 3 (codification of full program) is more complex -- eligibility determination requires infrastructure not yet built.

César's stated preference: move fast on direct relief items 1 and 2 rather than waiting for a complete package. Asked Nash to advise if moving slower would be more effective.
