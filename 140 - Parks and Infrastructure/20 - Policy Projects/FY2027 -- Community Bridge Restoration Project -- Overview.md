# FY2027 -- Community Bridge Restoration Project -- Overview

## Summary

The City of Frederick has a $1.355M contract with artist William Cochran (Cochran Studio) to restore the Carroll Creek Community Bridge mural, executed November 2022 with a 42-month completion timeline. As of April 2026, Cochran has painted zero panels under that agreement, the project is now projected to finish no earlier than 2030, and the artist's attorney has threatened litigation claiming $692K in delay damages.

## Key Points

**History and contract structure.** The City originally commissioned Cochran to paint the Community Bridge mural in 1993 as part of the Carroll Creek Park project; the mural was completed in 1998. After deterioration, the Board of Aldermen recommissioned him for restoration. A 2021 Purchase Order ($48,200) covered 144 sq ft on eight panels. The 2022 Agreement covers the remaining 2,531 sq ft of the 2,675 sq ft total, with a fixed artist fee of $790,937.50 and total project budget of $1,355,000 (city funds $1,075,000; 2022 State Bond Initiative $250,000; 2024 MSAC Conservation Grant $30,000). The City is responsible for panels, materials, construction, and installation. The artist is responsible only for painting.

**Performance to date.** Cochran painted 8 of 140 panels under the 2021 Purchase Order, completing them in mid-2025. He has painted nothing under the 2022 Agreement. As of the April 7, 2026 budget update, $209,704.94 has been spent (15.48% of total budget); the artist has received $152,368.75 (19.26% of his contracted fee).

**The standoff.** Cochran claims the City failed to provide technical prerequisites before he could begin painting: stamped engineering drawings, finalized panel dimensions, resolved installation design, and approved materials. The City hired architect Tom King of Noelker and Hall in August 2024 — nearly two years into the agreement — who has since provided stamped drawings. Cochran now disputes the measurements, insisting on 1-2mm panel tolerances achievable only through a scribing company the City did not budget for. He is also refusing to use mineret panels selected by the City's architect, demanding gammastone for certain sections — a material substitution that would exhaust the remaining budget. He submitted a $17,280 invoice for "technical track" work (attending meetings, providing artistic input) that the City disputes as outside his painting-only scope under the 2022 Agreement.

**Legal exposure.** Cochran's attorney Dave Greber has threatened suit for $692,252 in delay damages calculated from the November 2022 contract date, plus an inflation adjustment pushing total compensation demand to $819,982. To forestall litigation, Greber proposed a tolling agreement suspending the statute of limitations while parties negotiate. The City intends to assert defenses and potential counterclaims. Staff notes that a significant portion of delays is attributable to the artist, including a period of indisposition, time committed to consultant collaboration, and work performed under the 2021 Purchase Order for which he submitted final invoice only in July 2025.

**Public-private partnership proposal.** Cochran's attorney has proposed that the City form public-private partnerships with interested institutions to help complete the project. Staff from the Mayor's office, public affairs, purchasing, and public works are working with the artist's attorney to develop this structure. Council has not been presented with terms.

**Decision points for closed session.** Staff has identified three: (1) whether to terminate the contract, which would void the tolling agreement and likely trigger the lawsuit; (2) whether to proceed to mediation on delay claims; and (3) whether to appropriate additional funds for unanticipated costs including scribing.

## Relevance

This is a CIP contract dispute with material litigation exposure and a $1.355M public investment at stake. The City's delay in hiring an architect is a genuine vulnerability. The pattern of payment without performance, unilateral material demands, and a pre-emptive damages claim suggests the artist is positioning for settlement leverage. Council oversight is warranted on both the legal strategy and the Mayor's office negotiations on the public-private partnership structure, which have proceeded without a Council-approved framework.
