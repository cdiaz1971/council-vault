# Lighting and Greening as Low-Cost Public Safety Infrastructure

## Summary

A growing body of research shows that targeted urban design interventions -- LED streetlighting, greening vacant lots, fixing derelict buildings -- produce significant and measurable crime reductions. This positions urban design as a more cost-effective and politically viable public safety tool than enforcement-based approaches, particularly for budget-constrained cities.

## Key Points

Research findings cited in The Atlantic, summarized by Elizabeth Glazer:

- Philadelphia LED streetlight installation: outdoor nighttime street crimes dropped 15%.
- Greening vacant lots in Philadelphia: gun violence reduced 29%, overall crime reduced 9%.
- Fixing derelict buildings in Philadelphia: gun violence reduced 39%.
- Six-city study (including Baltimore and Washington DC): tree planting associated with 9% reduction in gun violence.

The mechanism is twofold: deterrence (better lighting removes cover) and activation (more people on streets makes space safer through presence). Darkness is described as "a good cover for crime"; better lighting deters misdeeds and encourages activity that itself reduces crime.

Glazer's argument is that effective design can be more impactful and less politically fraught than policing-based strategies, and that any serious crime reduction effort needs to use all viable strategies simultaneously.

## Relevance

This is directly relevant to Frederick's Golden Mile corridor, which is District 2, where César's constituency is concentrated. The corridor has aging infrastructure, vacant and derelict properties, and limited street-level activation. The research supports making the case for targeted infrastructure investment as a public safety argument -- not just an equity or aesthetics argument.

It also connects to the LMC modernization thread: land use rules that permit vacant lot greening, allow adaptive reuse of derelict buildings, and require lighting standards are all design-layer interventions that could produce the crime reduction effects documented here.
