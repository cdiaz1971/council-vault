## Summary

The June 2026 Better Block Bulletin focuses on three interconnected themes: learning programs, temporary urbanism as proof-of-concept infrastructure, and community co-creation. The edition is particularly rich on the methodology of using short-term, low-cost street interventions to generate data, community buy-in, and political will for permanent change -- directly applicable to Frederick's LMC reform process and Golden Mile corridor work.

## Key Points

**Better Block University (BBU) -- Applications Open**
BBU is a four-month program training community members to execute tactical urbanism projects focused on public safety. The 2026 cohort application deadline is June 12. Full tuition scholarships are available for North Texas applicants via the Communities Foundation of Texas. The program is explicitly designed to transform residents from participants into project leaders -- "community as co-creators" is the core frame.

**Activate Main Street -- Downtown Dallas**
Better Block converted on-street parking into protected curb extensions and community parklets in partnership with Downtown Dallas, Inc. The installation went in ahead of World Cup activities to collect baseline data on public life, traffic speeds, and safety over 39 days. The explicit goal is using event conditions to validate design interventions before they become permanent capital investments. Key framing: "Temporary projects give communities permission to experiment, learn, and adapt."

**Via Futbol -- Downtown Houston**
A wayfinding and public space project for FIFA, using hexagonal ground markings, bold signage, and Oasis Zones (rest, water, amenities) to move pedestrians safely through high-traffic areas. Demonstrates how temporary lane closures and parking adjustments can create demonstrably safer pedestrian environments -- with political cover provided by the event.

**Better Intersections -- New Initiative**
A program allowing neighborhoods to pilot and evaluate safety improvements at specific intersections without waiting for permanent capital projects. Rooted in Vision Zero principles. Better Block is coordinating directly with Dallas Councilmember offices and city departments so pilot findings can inform long-term investment decisions. This is the most directly translatable model for Frederick: low-cost, community-informed interventions at dangerous intersections that generate data before we commit to infrastructure spending.

**Community as Co-Creators -- East Point, Georgia**
A case study in residents moving from meeting attendees to hands-on builders -- picking up paintbrushes and tools to build their vision for downtown. "Lasting change happens when residents move from being participants to partners."

**Wikiblock -- Everybody Table**
An AARP Texas partnership producing an accessible outdoor table designed for all ages and mobility needs. Part of Better Block's open-source design library.

**Placemaking Week -- June 22**
Executive Director Krista Nightengale presenting at the In Pursuit of Place Summit (Green Bay, June 23) and Project for Public Spaces Placemaking Week, including panels on building a national placemaking network and a 2024 G7 project in Detroit.

## Relevance to Frederick

The Better Intersections model is the highest-priority takeaway here. Frederick has dangerous intersections throughout the Golden Mile corridor with no near-term capital budget for improvements. A community-powered pilot program -- installing temporary treatments, collecting data, and feeding findings into the LMC reform process -- could do several things at once: generate safety data, build community ownership, demonstrate political will, and create a proof-of-concept for what the corridor could look like. This is exactly the "show, don't tell" methodology Better Block has proven works.

The BBU program itself is also worth flagging to Golden Mile Alliance and East Frederick Rising as a training pathway for community members who want to lead placemaking projects.
