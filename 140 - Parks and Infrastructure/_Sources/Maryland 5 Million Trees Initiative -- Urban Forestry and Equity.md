# Maryland 5 Million Trees Initiative -- Urban Forestry and Equity

## Summary

Maryland passed the Tree Solutions Now Act in 2021 committing the state to planting more than 5 million native trees by 2031. The program targets air quality improvement, urban heat island reduction, and climate resilience, with a specific equity mandate requiring that at least 10% of plantings go into underserved urban areas defined by income, unemployment, and redlining history.

## Key Points

As of the fiscal 2025 report, more than 1.5 million trees have been planted under the initiative. The Chesapeake Bay Trust administers the underserved urban area component through a grant program and has already made roughly 150 awards for more than 100,000 trees in those communities.

The equity dimension is explicit in statute: underserved areas are determined by income levels, unemployment rates, and redlining history. Organizations like the Baltimore Tree Trust do the planting on the ground.

A significant operational challenge is survival rate. About 30% of newly planted trees die within the first few years due to deer, invasive species, insufficient follow-through maintenance, and inadequate watering. The Maryland Forest Service acknowledges a pattern of "plant and leave" that undermines long-term canopy gains.

Maryland is still losing forest cover to development, though the rate has dropped from over 1,000 acres per month in the 1980s-90s to under 200 acres per month now. The 5 million tree target is partly compensatory for this continued loss.

## Why This Matters for César

Frederick sits within the Chesapeake Bay watershed and has active parks and environmental commitments. This state program is a potential funding source for Frederick tree canopy work, especially in lower-income neighborhoods like those along the Golden Mile corridor. The equity-by-design framing -- using redlining history as a targeting criterion -- is also a model for how Frederick could approach capital investment prioritization in the LMC or parks planning contexts.
