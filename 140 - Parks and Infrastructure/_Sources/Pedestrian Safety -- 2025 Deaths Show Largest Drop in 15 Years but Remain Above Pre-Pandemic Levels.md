# Pedestrian Safety -- 2025 Deaths Show Largest Drop in 15 Years but Remain Above Pre-Pandemic Levels

## Summary

Pedestrian deaths in the U.S. fell 11% in the first half of 2025 -- the largest single-period decline recorded by the Governors Highway Safety Association in 15 years. Despite this progress, deaths remain above 2019 levels, and the distribution across states is highly uneven.

## Key Points

The GHSA recorded 3,024 pedestrian fatalities in H1 2025, a reduction of 371 deaths compared to the same period in 2024. Deaths increased in 24 states and fell in 23, meaning the national improvement is not uniform.

States with the highest per capita rates: Hawaii, Louisiana, Florida, South Carolina, and Arizona. Lowest rates: Idaho, Rhode Island, Minnesota, South Dakota, and Wisconsin.

New Mexico, previously the worst-performing state in 2023, cut deaths from 53 to 27 in early 2025 after piloting crosswalk warning lights that flash when a pedestrian enters the roadway. This is a direct example of technology-enabled safety intervention working at scale.

The overall trend is encouraging but the baseline is elevated. The pre-pandemic (2019) levels were lower than the current trajectory, meaning the post-2020 spike in pedestrian deaths has not been fully reversed.

## Relevance

This is directly relevant to the FY2027 budget negotiation. Nash's capital requests include a Tactical Traffic Calming and Pedestrian Safety Program at $150k and solar-powered crosswalk signaling at $150k. The New Mexico crosswalk technology example supports the crosswalk signaling line specifically -- it provides evidence that relatively modest technology interventions can produce measurable safety outcomes.

For the April 15 budget session (Planning, Parks, Permits), this data could be useful framing if pedestrian safety investments are questioned.
