# Pay-Per-Use Park Registration as a Municipal Revenue and Access Tool

## Summary

Maryland's Board of Public Works approved expanding a park reservation and fee-collection system after a pilot showed a 352% revenue increase without changing fees -- but the contract raised procurement concerns over lack of competitive bidding.

## Key Points

The Maryland Park Service piloted an online pre-registration system at five state parks last summer to manage capacity at popular beach parks. The pilot produced a 352% revenue increase over honor-box collection without any fee changes -- a meaningful signal about compliance rates when friction is reduced. Based on that result, DNR contracted with Kaizen Laboratories to expand the system to additional parks and shooting ranges.

The contract structure: Kaizen takes a 23% fee tacked onto park entry fees (typically $3-$5/visitor, so up to $1.15 per person more). State estimates Kaizen earns ~$4M over the contract period through 2031 while the state takes in ~$18M. Net new revenue to the state vs. current system: ~$725,000.

Comptroller Brooke Lierman voted against the contract over competitive bidding concerns, noting she supports the goal but not the process: "I do believe that competitive contracts are how we get best value for the state." The other two BPW members (Moore and Davis) approved it.

## Relevance

Two angles worth tracking for Frederick. First, the revenue finding: honor-box or cash collection dramatically undercounts actual usage and revenue. If Frederick parks use any informal collection methods, a digital reservation or payment system could capture significantly more without raising fees. Second, the procurement lesson: the Lierman dissent is a model for how to raise competitive bidding concerns without blocking the policy goal -- the right frame is process integrity, not opposition to the program.

The QR code payment approach (for less-popular parks) is a lower-cost alternative to full reservation systems and could be relevant for Frederick facilities that don't need capacity management but do need better fee collection.
