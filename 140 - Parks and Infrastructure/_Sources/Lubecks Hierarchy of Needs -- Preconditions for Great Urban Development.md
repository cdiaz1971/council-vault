# Lubecks Hierarchy of Needs -- Preconditions for Great Urban Development

## Summary

Aaron Lubeck proposes a Maslow-style hierarchy arguing that great development requires sequential preconditions: cultural discontent must precede political will, which must precede legal reform, before skilled execution and beautiful cities become possible.

## Key Points

- Starting point: regulatory reform (middle housing, LMC-style) is necessary but insufficient -- society also has to relearn how to design the housing it's now allowed to build
- The process is "linear and sequential": cultural tidal wave demanding better places must come before political courage; political buy-in must precede legal reform; legal freedom must precede universities and trade groups relearning the craft
- Practical implication: as long as great urbanism is illegal in most places, schools and trades won't bother teaching it -- the reform sequence has to run first
- Lubeck's Hierarchy (Maslow adaptation): cultural discontent and institutional pressure at the base, then political will, then legal freedom, then skilled execution, and finally the actual development of beautiful places
- The paywalled portion contained a detailed breakdown of the full five-level structure
- The argument resonates with Frederick's LMC reform work: changing the code is step 3 or 4 in the sequence, not step 1

## Relevance

This is a useful mental model for calibrating expectations around LMC Reform Commission outcomes. The code change is not the end -- it's enabling conditions for a cultural and professional shift that takes longer. Useful framing for council communications: we're not just changing rules, we're trying to move a whole sequence forward. Also connects to Tactical Urbanism and the [[Imagination Barriers -- Why Show Don't Tell Works in Community Change]] thread -- demonstration projects matter precisely because they interrupt the "why bother learning if it's illegal" loop.
