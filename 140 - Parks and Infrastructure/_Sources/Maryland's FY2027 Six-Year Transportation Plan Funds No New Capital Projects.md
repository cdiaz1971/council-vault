# Maryland's FY2027 Six-Year Transportation Plan Funds No New Capital Projects

## Summary

Maryland's proposed $21.9 billion six-year Consolidated Transportation Plan (CTP) is $200 million less than last year's version and funds no new capital projects, directing money instead toward existing safety, maintenance, and economic-development priorities.

## Key Points

- $21.9B six-year plan, down about 1% ($200M) from last year, amid rising costs (asphalt and diesel prices up as much as 38% in the last six months) and a growing maintenance backlog. Transportation Secretary Katie Thomson: "It is essentially flat-funded... We're moving some of the dollars around where they're most needed for safety or state of good repair."
- No new capital dollars in this CTP. Funding covers "must-fund" projects: Purple Line operational systems testing, Cox Creek expansion, and enterprise IT/cybersecurity investments.
- Baltimore's Red Line remains stalled. Moore promised to restart the project (canceled by Hogan in 2015) and revived it in 2023 with construction slated for 2026, but funding never materialized -- $150M earmarked "will get us fairly along in planning and design but would not be sufficient for long-term construction." The state is now studying bus rapid transit as an alternative, with a stakeholder trip to Cleveland's BRT system planned for October 2026 and a decision expected "with the governor" in early 2027.
- The Transportation Trust Fund faces a nearly $1.2 billion gap in road-maintenance spending over 10 years (Pew Center, July 2026); Maryland is spending about 25% less than the recommended level.
- Motor fuel tax revenue is in structural decline; a February 2026 Department of Legislative Services analysis projects state titling taxes will overtake gas taxes as the largest state-sourced Trust Fund revenue source within the 2026-2031 plan window. Federal funding plus titling/fuel taxes and registration fees make up about 65% of Trust Fund revenue.
- A 31-member Transportation Revenue and Infrastructure Needs Commission, created by the General Assembly in 2023 to modernize how the state pays for transportation as gas tax revenue declines, made only modest interim recommendations before the legislature effectively dissolved it.
- Broader context: the state faces a projected $3 billion budget gap next year. Thomson: "all options that are viable should be on the table, but we need to be thoughtful about the short-term and long-term implications... for Maryland taxpayers."

## Relevance

Directly relevant to any Frederick ask for state transportation dollars -- the state is flat-funding the CTP with a hard "no new capital projects" posture and has its own maintenance backlog, so expectations for new state money toward local projects like the Golden Mile Multimodal Access Plan should be set accordingly. Also useful backdrop for the E-Bike (EPMD) Ordinance's return to committee: any council argument for state co-funding of corridor safety improvements is competing against a Trust Fund that is short on both new dollars and structural revenue.
