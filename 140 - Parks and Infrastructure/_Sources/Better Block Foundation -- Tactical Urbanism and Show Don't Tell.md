# Better Block Foundation: Tactical Urbanism and "Show, Don't Tell"

## Summary

The Better Block Foundation's March 2026 bulletin highlights their 10-Year Impact Report, organized around 8 pillars of Urban Transformation. The first pillar — "Show, Don't Tell" — is directly applicable to Frederick's housing, walkability, and community engagement work. The core insight: temporary activations let communities experience a better version of their neighborhood before committing to permanent change, lowering political resistance and building public will.

## Core Framework: Show, Don't Tell

The biggest barrier to urban change is not policy — it is imagination. People cannot advocate for what they have never experienced. Better Block's model uses temporary, low-cost pilots to let residents "test-drive" the future, which then creates the political conditions for permanent investment.

This framework maps directly onto Frederick challenges around housing density, road diets, parking reform, and walkability — areas where community opposition often stems from unfamiliarity rather than principled disagreement.

## Case Studies Worth Citing

**Toronto, Canada (2019) — 880 Streets Better Block**
City officials said reallocating street space on Danforth Avenue was impossible. A two-day pop-up with 100 volunteers installed protected bike lanes, expanded pedestrian space, and seating. When the pandemic hit, the city had a tested blueprint. The temporary pilot became a permanent separated cycle track, and the prototype café seating became the model for Toronto's citywide "cafeto" program.
*Lesson: A temporary pilot can produce permanent infrastructure and serve as a crisis-ready blueprint.*

**Akron, Ohio (2017) — Kenmore Better Block**
Residents said they would never visit Kenmore Boulevard. A temporary road diet, protected bike lane, pop-up coffee shop, and live music activation drew 3,000 people. The road diet became permanent, the pop-up became a real tea house, and a renewed arts identity catalyzed a $62 million school investment.
*Lesson: Civic pride and economic investment follow demonstrated possibility, not plans.*

**Dallas, Texas (2023) — Pleasant Grove Better Block**
A parking lot serving 2,000 families daily was stressful and undignified. A one-day activation with color, seating, shade, and a basketball court transformed the experience. The permanent environment became calmer and more dignified.
*Lesson: Even functional spaces can be humanized through low-cost design interventions.*

## Council Relevance

- **Road diets and parking reform:** The Toronto and Akron cases directly support arguments for temporary pilots before permanent changes — a useful frame for community pushback on road diet or parking minimum proposals in Frederick.
- **Housing and mixed-use activation:** The "test-drive" model could apply to pop-up commercial uses in underutilized spaces as a precursor to zoning changes.
- **Community engagement strategy:** The "Show, Don't Tell" pillar reframes how council communicates proposed changes — experience over explanation.
