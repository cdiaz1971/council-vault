# Autonomous Vehicles and City Planning -- Fleet vs Ownership Models

The autonomous vehicle market has not yet resolved whether it will develop primarily as shared fleet services (robotaxis) or individually owned vehicles. This distinction matters enormously for city planning: shared fleets reduce parking demand and can integrate with transit, while individual AV ownership could reproduce suburban sprawl patterns with AI-driven cars parked at homes rather than centrally.

Cities are advised to begin scenario planning now -- embedding AV considerations into long-range infrastructure, parking, and zoning decisions -- rather than waiting for the market to settle. The window for proactive policy is narrowing.

## Why This Matters for César

Frederick is not a near-term AV city, but infrastructure and zoning decisions made today will shape the built environment for decades. Parking minimums, street widths, and transit investments all have AV implications. This is directly relevant to the LMC reform discussion -- zoning codes written today should not over-specify for car storage if the ownership model is in flux.

The fleet vs. ownership framing is a useful analytical lens for any transportation or land use discussion. Connects to [[Mobility vs Accessibility -- The Urban Planning Distinction]] and the broader conversation about what Frederick's 15-year infrastructure vision should assume.
