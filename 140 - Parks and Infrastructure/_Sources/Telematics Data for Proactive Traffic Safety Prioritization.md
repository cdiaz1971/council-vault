# Telematics Data for Proactive Traffic Safety Prioritization

## Summary

The Missouri Department of Transportation (MODOT) is using telematics data from Cambridge Mobile Telematics (CMT) to identify high-risk intersections before crashes happen, rather than after. The platform aggregates hard braking, phone use, and speeding data from drivers' mobile devices and cross-references it with state crash records to rank intersections by risk. The key insight: telematics is proactive, not reactive -- it shows where risk is accumulating, not just where crashes already occurred.

## Key Points

The CMT StreetVision platform pulls data from drivers enrolled in safe driving programs. It then ranks intersections by risky behavior density and allows transportation staff to virtually inspect flagged locations using street view before physically visiting them -- saving significant staff time and enabling faster intervention.

Specific applications MODOT is using:
- Identifying "low-hanging fruit" intersections for quick safety improvements
- Coordinating with local police to deploy enforcement in high-risk areas
- Virtually assessing risk factors like vegetation obstructions without site visits
- Building a data-backed portfolio of safety investments to justify future funding

The framing from MODOT is explicitly about fiscal stewardship: data-backed prioritization helps agencies demonstrate they are "good stewards of the investment" entrusted to them, which builds the case for future funding.

Intersections account for roughly 25% of U.S. traffic fatalities and half of all injury crashes despite being a small fraction of total roadway mileage.

## Why This Matters for César

Frederick has active pedestrian safety concerns, particularly along high-traffic corridors. The Safe Streets and Roads for All (SS4A) federal grant program specifically funds this kind of data-driven safety planning. This model -- using telematics to prioritize where to act before fatalities occur -- is directly applicable to a Frederick transportation safety initiative and would strengthen any SS4A application. Worth flagging to Public Works or the city's transportation planner as a tool worth evaluating.
