# Better Block Bulletin -- July 2026

## Summary
July 2026 edition of the Better Block Bulletin, covering Pillar 4 of their 10-year impact report (data-driven advocacy for street redesigns) and the launch of the Better Intersections Cohort 1 in Dallas.

## Key Points
- Pillar 4 of Better Block's impact report: temporary, low-cost demonstrations generate real-world data (foot traffic, business activity, vehicle speeds) that converts skepticism into confidence for permanent street redesigns. The framing is "here's what worked" instead of "what if this works."
- Detroit G7 Better Block (2024): temporary traffic calming and a public plaza on a dangerous corridor. No vehicles exceeded 40 mph during the demonstration. The data helped secure a $24 million state grant for permanent safety improvements, and the temporary plaza is still serving the community two years later.
- Richardson, TX Innovation District (2019): temporary reconfiguration of a six-lane road to four lanes with protected bike lanes. Traffic studies proved the corridor functioned fine with fewer lanes, leading to permanent protected bike lanes and a $2.6 million federal transportation grant.
- Better Intersections Cohort 1 has launched in Dallas Districts 1, 3, and 9. Community meetings and walk audits are complete at each site; the work is now moving into quick-build design development.
  - District 1: West Clarendon Drive, senior-focused park and bus access
  - District 3: South Hampton Road / Perryton Drive, serving a school, library, and nature preserve
  - District 9: Lakewood Elementary area, child-focused school access safety

## Relevance
Direct precedent for Frederick's parks and infrastructure work. The core lesson, that temporary low-cost demonstrations produce the evidence needed for both public buy-in and outside grant funding, is a repeatable model for any Frederick corridor or intersection safety push.
