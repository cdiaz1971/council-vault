# Great Highway Car-Free Conversion Backlash and San Francisco Prop G

## Summary
San Francisco's two-mile car-free Upper Great Highway, now Sunset Dunes park, diverted up to 20,000 vehicles a day onto residential streets, fueling Prop G on the November 3 ballot to restore weekday car access. The No campaign outraised Yes about six to one.

## Key Points
- Quote: "I'm so angry about it. I mean, my street has become a major artery." Judi Gorski, 75, retired art business owner whose home overlooks Sunset Dunes.
- The closure converted roughly two miles of Upper Great Highway to a park for cyclists, skateboarders, and pedestrians, and pushed up to 20,000 vehicles a day onto alternate routes through neighborhoods.
- Prop G (November 3, 2026) would restore cars on weekdays. Yes on G raised about $130,000; No on G about $800,000, roughly half from Yelp CEO Jeremy Stoppelman.

## Relevance
A cautionary case for any Frederick street reallocation, including Golden Mile multimodal work and tactical traffic calming: the benefit is concentrated and visible, but diverted traffic lands on specific residential blocks whose residents become the opposition. Pairing any conversion with a diversion plan and neighborhood traffic calming on the receiving streets is the lesson. The lopsided funding is also a reminder that well-funded defenders can make a contested project look more popular than it is. See [[Frederick Streets, Traffic Safety, and Transit]].
