# Bike Safety Requires Infrastructure Not Behavior Change

## Summary

Transportation planner Chris Ridgeway argues that the "Dutch reach" and other behavioral interventions are insufficient substitutes for protected infrastructure, and that design -- not individual behavior -- is what makes the Netherlands safe for cyclists.

## Key Points

Written in response to the deaths of two transportation planners while biking, Ridgeway's argument is that admonishing drivers to use the Dutch reach (opening a car door with the far hand to force shoulder-checking) places the burden of safety on individual behavior rather than on the systems that create risk.

The core claim: road safety is a design problem. Ridgeway draws on design researcher Don Norman's concept of "affordances" -- physical environments that, when designed correctly, allow humans to understand how to use them without instruction. Protected bike lanes function as affordances: they make the safe behavior the obvious behavior, rather than requiring deliberate individual action.

The Netherlands is safe for cyclists not because of the Dutch reach but because of a comprehensive system: separated infrastructure, traffic calming, lower speed limits, education, and cultural norms that developed over decades. The Dutch reach is one small tool in a much larger toolbox.

The implication for American cities: behavioral campaigns and individual responsibility framing obscure the structural changes needed and let policymakers off the hook.

## Relevance

This framing is useful for Frederick infrastructure discussions, particularly around the E-Bike ordinance currently in Public Works Committee and any future bike infrastructure investments. When the argument arises that cyclists just need to be more careful, or that driver education is sufficient, the Ridgeway/Norman framework provides a clear counter: you cannot design safe behavior out of people; you have to design safe environments. Infrastructure is the intervention.

It also connects to how Frederick frames pedestrian and bike safety in its LMC updates and transportation planning -- design standards are more durable than behavioral campaigns.
