# Frederick Wastewater Plant -- Chronic Phosphorus Discharge Violations

Frederick's city-operated wastewater treatment plant has exceeded its annual phosphorus discharge limit for the fourth time in the last five years. In 2025, the plant discharged 10,827 pounds of phosphorus against an MDE annual limit of 5,720 pounds -- nearly double the permitted level. MDE described the violations as "significant in nature" and is evaluating penalties that could reach $10,000 per day.

The city has attributed the overages to increased phosphorus concentrations in the influent water that the plant struggles to process through its bioaugmentation reactors. The city's public affairs manager noted the city is working with Moonshot Missions technical consultants to evaluate plant operations and strengthen phosphorus removal. Phosphorus and nitrogen are both major Bay pollutants -- excess phosphorus drives algae blooms that deprive water of oxygen and harm aquatic life.

This is a recurring pattern, not a one-time event. Four violations in five years signals a systemic capacity or process problem, not an anomaly.
