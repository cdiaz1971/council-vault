# Better Block -- Proving Market Demand Through Tactical Urbanism

## Summary

Better Block's core methodology -- building temporary, beautiful, functional prototypes of a desired future space -- is about replacing speculation with experience. This issue documents three case studies and announces Better Block University (BBU) applications now open with a June 12 deadline.

## Key Points

**The methodology:**
Better Block argues that communities contain untapped economic and social capital that only needs a catalyst to surface. Temporary demonstrations create real data: they show city leaders what a redesigned street feels like, give local entrepreneurs a low-risk platform to test ideas, and give residents a reason to reinvest in their neighborhood. Belief follows experience.

**Case studies:**
- Frenchtown, Tallahassee (2016): Pop-up activation in a historically Black neighborhood led to permanent bike lane infrastructure, new businesses, and a $6.5M neighborhood improvement grant.
- Corpus Christi waterfront pier (2023): Transformed a "forgotten space" through a 14,400-sq-ft mural and pop-up activation; sparked a broader Marina Master Plan.
- Dallas Forest-Audelia Park (2021): Pop-up living lab with a $7.5M resulting city investment, including a basketball court shaped by what residents demonstrated they wanted.

**BBU applications open:**
4-month leadership program, 15 spots (5 reserved for North Texas, 10 worldwide), $5,000 tuition, closes June 12. Full scholarships available for North Texas participants via community foundation support.

**Paris Learning Journey:**
Better Block co-hosted a 3-day learning journey for 25 leaders from 10 U.S. cities, exploring how Paris used public realm redesign as a strategy for climate, mobility, and quality-of-life challenges -- from Seine riverbank transformation to cycling network expansion.

## Relevance

This connects directly to Frederick's LMC reform and housing production work. The "show don't tell" approach is a useful frame for community engagement on contested zoning or streetscape changes.
