# Private Parking Garages as an Untapped Mobility Data Layer

## Summary
Private parking garages sit outside the digital mobility infrastructure cities have built for transit and rideshare, and folding their monthly inventory into city dashboards could cut cruising-driven congestion and improve EV charging access.

## Key Points
Most city mobility strategies cover transit expansion, bike lanes, rideshare, and congestion pricing, but private parking garages remain almost entirely undigitized, still run on phone calls and paper waitlists even as everything else in the mobility stack reports live data. That gap is getting more consequential as EV adoption grows, since charging infrastructure lives in garages rather than on the street, so a monthly parker with a garage spot is a driver who can charge during the workday.

The congestion cost is real. Cited research puts cruising for parking, drivers circling in search of an open space, at 18 to 68 percent of downtown traffic in the studies referenced, averaging roughly eight minutes per search, and INRIX's 2024 Global Traffic Scorecard put the national cost of congestion above $74 billion. Underused garage capacity pushes more of that search traffic onto the street.

The proposed fix does not require new infrastructure or public capital. Cities already integrate private rideshare, scooter-share, and shuttle operators into public mobility systems through data-sharing agreements. The argument is that private garage operators, many of whom already run digital monthly-parking platforms, could be brought into the same kind of connected ecosystem, making existing unused garage inventory visible to both commuters and city planners. The practical starting point offered is an audit: identify the private garage operators downtown, check whether an aggregator platform already serves the local market, and ask whether the city's existing mobility apps and dashboards could ingest that data.

Worth flagging directly: the author is the Chief Revenue Officer of Spacer, a company that sells exactly this kind of monthly-parking data platform. The piece is industry-interested advocacy, not neutral analysis, though the underlying congestion and EV-charging access argument holds up on its own regardless of who is making it.

## Relevance
Frederick already has two live threads this connects to. The E-Bike (EPMD) Ordinance and the Golden Mile Small Area Plan's in-design Multimodal Access Plan are both working from corridor-level crash and mobility data (576 car accidents, 42 non-motorist accidents, 25 severe or fatal injuries on the Golden Mile corridor 2020-2024), and a parking-data integration layer is the kind of low-cost, no-capital lever that could reduce cruising congestion without competing for the same budget as physical infrastructure. It is also a natural extension of the GovTech dashboard work already underway with the Frederick City GIS Server, if that data layer ever expands from boundaries and zoning into live mobility data. Any future citation of this idea in council material should carry the vendor-interest flag noted above.
