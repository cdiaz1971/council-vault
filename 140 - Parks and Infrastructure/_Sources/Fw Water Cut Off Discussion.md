Government Operations Members -

Katie and I began this work last term and she has worked hard to identify actions for moving away from water shut-offs.

I am providing the notes from our last call so you have these and know where we are headed. Please see below for actions she has identified for our work. These will be within our Committee later this year.

This list does not include a budget line item that I will mention at this evening's budget meeting.


Current Strategies and Progress

Expanded Access to Payment Options (InvoiceCloud Implementation)
The City has implemented InvoiceCloud to make it easier for residents to manage and pay their utility bills. This includes:

  *   Online account access with real-time balances
  *   Multiple payment options (credit, debit, e-check)
  *   Text and email notifications
  *   Paperless billing
  *   QR codes on mailed bills for quick access

Increased Threshold for Service Disconnection
The City does not initiate residential water shutoffs unless outstanding balances exceed $350. This gives residents additional time to catch up and avoids shutoffs for smaller, short-term balances.

Flexible Payment Plans
Payment plans are available to any residential customer who requests one. The focus is on getting residents engaged early and setting up a plan that works, rather than allowing balances to continue to grow.

One-Time Leak Forgiveness Program

The City offers a one-time leak forgiveness program for residential customers who experience unusually high bills due to a leak.

Key elements of the program:

  *   Available to residential customers only
  *   Bills must reflect at least double normal usage based on prior cycles
  *   The leak must be repaired and usage must return to normal before an adjustment is made
  *   Limited to one occurrence per account

This program addresses one of the most common causes of large, unexpected bills and helps residents avoid falling behind due to circumstances outside of their control.

Program Impact:

  *   FY 2025: 55 accounts, totaling $68,742 in credits
  *   FY 2026 (as of February 17): 26 accounts, totaling $48,038

Proposed Program:

Engagement-Based Assistance

The goal of this program is simple: if a resident is actively working with the City to resolve their balance, we should support that effort—not add penalties.

Under this approach:

  *   Interest can be waived for residential customers who:
     *   Enter into a payment plan or assistance program
     *   Stay in communication with the City
     *   Make a good-faith effort to pay

This encourages residents to reach out early and stay engaged, which is the most effective way to prevent shutoffs.

Expanding Residential Assistance

Water Assistance Program Development
Finance is working with Housing and Human Services to develop a residential water/sewer utility assistance program that will:

  *   Build on existing local, state, and nonprofit resources
  *   Provide targeted support to residents in need
  *   Focus on preventing shutoffs before they occur

Long-Term Funding Strategies
To support this program over time, the City is exploring:

  *   Use of non-rate revenues where appropriate
  *   Grant opportunities and external funding
  *   Partnerships with nonprofit and community organizations
  *   Policy options that maintain the financial stability of the utility fund


Legislative Actions Needed

Authorization to Waive Interest
Allow the City to waive or reduce interest on residential accounts when customers are actively engaged in repayment or assistance efforts.

Authority to Modify or Waive Turn-On/Turn-Off Fees
Provide flexibility to reduce or eliminate disconnection and reconnection fees for qualifying residential customers.

Codification of Residential Assistance Program
Establish a formal structure for the program, including eligibility and administration.
