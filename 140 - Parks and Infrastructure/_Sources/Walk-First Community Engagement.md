# Walk-First Community Engagement -- Learning from Charlotte Urbanists

From Strong Towns, April 2025. The argument: meaningful urban planning knowledge starts with walking neighborhoods alongside residents, planners, and elected officials -- not in conference rooms reviewing reports.

## The Core Practice

The Charlotte Urbanists group in North Carolina developed a practice of inviting mixed groups -- residents, planners, and elected officials -- to walk through neighborhoods together. The act of walking the same streets surfaces observations that no report captures: where people feel unsafe crossing, where storefronts are dead, where density is working, where it isn't. Shared physical experience generates shared understanding faster than slide decks.

## Why This Matters as a Methodology

Community engagement in planning tends to default to public hearings and online comment forms, both of which systematically underrepresent the people most affected by land use decisions. Walking tours are lower-barrier, observational, and harder to dominate by organized voices. They also put elected officials and planners in the same perceptual frame as residents, rather than mediating that experience through staff summaries.

## Application for Frederick

This is relevant to the LMC review process and to any corridor or neighborhood-level planning work. A structured walk with council members, planning staff, and affected residents before a zoning decision is both a better engagement method and a politically useful record of direct constituent contact. It doesn't require a budget line -- it requires scheduling.
