# Dallas Activate Main Street Pedestrian Data Study

## Summary
A 56-day pedestrian-focused installation on Downtown Dallas' Main Street during the World Cup produced survey and observation data showing strong public support for a more walkable street and frequent non-parking use of the curb.

## Key Points
Activate Main Street gave more of Downtown Dallas' Main Street over to people for 56 days during the World Cup, in partnership with Downtown Dallas, Inc. Better Block collected 406 community survey responses and observed 586 pedestrian crossings. Of respondents, 95 percent supported a more pedestrian-friendly Main Street, 78 percent said the installation made the street feel more walkable, and 74 percent discovered a downtown business they had not visited before.

Observers also logged 137 instances of the curb being used for deliveries, ride-share, and pickups or drop-offs. The finding pushes back on the assumption that curb space is only parking and supports treating it as a flexible, multi-use asset.

The full study contains nine findings and is framed as a baseline for future decisions about the street rather than a one-time event report.

## Relevance
This is a concrete template for what a Frederick demonstration should measure if it is meant to inform policy: resident support, perceived walkability, business discovery, and actual curb behavior. The business discovery number is the most useful for downtown merchants who worry about losing parking, and it reinforces [[Tactical Urbanism Produces Measurable Economic Benefits]]. The curb data is relevant to any downtown discussion in [[Frederick Urbanism and Placemaking]] about loading, ride-share, and parking tradeoffs.
