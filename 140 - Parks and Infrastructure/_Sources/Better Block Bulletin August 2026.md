# Better Block Bulletin August 2026

## Summary

August 2026 bulletin covers BBU Dallas 2026 cohort kickoff and two case studies demonstrating how temporary, low-cost demonstrations produce permanent infrastructure investment: Charlotte's Norview Five Points ($3.5M) and Pittsburgh's Backyard riverfront park ($31M).

## Key Points

**Pillar 5: Low-Risk, High-Impact Testing** is the organizing frame this month -- the argument that temporary demonstrations replace uncertainty with experience, shifting from top-down planning to community-led discovery.

**Charlotte, NC -- Norview Five Points (2017):** A dangerous five-lane intersection that had stymied redesign efforts for over a decade. Better Block built a temporary people-first street with safer crossings, bike lanes, and pop-up businesses. Community and city leaders experienced the possibilities firsthand. Outcome: permanent transportation improvements, new economic investment, three new permanent businesses, and a $3.5M permanent infrastructure project.

**Pittsburgh, PA -- The Backyard (2024):** A four-acre riverfront site in Pittsburgh's Cultural District that sat as empty asphalt for 30+ years despite dozens of redevelopment plans. Better Block and the Pittsburgh Cultural Trust created a temporary public park -- the Backyard -- where residents could test amenities, gather, and shape the future of the site. A stopgap solution became the cornerstone of a new vision. Outcome: a $31M permanent urban park now open, with features directly inspired by what residents asked for in the temporary activation.

**Better Block University 2026** kicked off its Dallas cohort in-person. The program trains community leaders in the Better Block methodology.

## Relevance

Both case studies are directly applicable to Frederick's pedestrian safety and public space work. The five-lane intersection transformation in Charlotte is the exact model for the EPMD e-bike and pedestrian infrastructure thread -- Brehm's text amendment on site plan review authority and the ongoing Public Works Committee conversation both involve exactly this tension between community input and permanent commitment. The Pittsburgh riverfront case is relevant to any underutilized city-owned land RFP work under the Housing Production Target Resolution (one of the nine priority recommendations). The core argument -- test first, then invest -- is politically useful for reducing council resistance to infrastructure changes. Worth citing in any public testimony or council memo on pedestrian safety or placemaking.
