# Maryland State Parks -- Reservation System Expansion and QR Entry Gates

## Summary

Maryland DNR is expanding its online reservation system to at least 8 new state parks in summer 2026 and introducing QR code payment gates at unmanned entry points -- building on a successful 2025 debut that processed 67,000 reservations and eliminated early park closures.

## Key Points

Maryland piloted mandatory advance registration at five high-traffic parks in summer 2025 (Sandy Point, Newtowne Neck, and others) after cars were lining up at 3am and parks were closing by midmorning. The system processed 67,000 reservations and an associated call center handled 4,000 calls. DNR called it an "unqualified success."

**2026 expansion targets** include Rocky Gap State Park, Gunpowder Falls (Hammerman), Swallow Falls, Elk Neck, Rocks State Park, Seneca Creek, and Gambrill State Park in Frederick, among others.

**QR entry gates** are being deployed at formerly unmanned "honor box" locations to capture revenue that was routinely lost (DNR's assistant secretary jokingly calls the old boxes "dishonor boxes"). The gate system is identical in function to a ranger-staffed entry, but automated. It also creates a timestamped entry record per visitor, which DNR frames as a safety feature for locating lost or injured visitors.

**Procurement note:** The contract with vendor Kaizen Labs hit a snag at the Maryland Board of Public Works (which requires approval) -- DNR withdrew the agenda item after board pushback and was asked to provide additional documentation and national comparisons. The item could return as early as May 20.

**Revenue model:** Kaizen takes a 23% processing fee on each entry payment rather than a lump-sum contract. At $3-5 park entry fees, that adds roughly $0.70-$1.15 per visitor. DNR reports no pushback from visitors on the modest fee increase.

## Why This Matters

Gambrill State Park is specifically listed for the new system -- this is Frederick's backyard. The QR entry model is a real-world case study in low-friction civic technology: automated, revenue-recovering, and adding safety value. The procurement story (Board of Public Works pushback, documentation requirements) is a useful reminder that even successful pilot programs face institutional friction when scaling. Worth tracking as a GovTech model applicable to other municipal services.
