# Developer Sediment Enforcement -- Maryland $4.1M Gunpowder River Settlement

## Summary

Maryland secured a $4.1M settlement from D.R. Horton, Forestar, and Kinsley after repeated sediment violations from the Ridgely's Reserve development damaged the Gunpowder River -- a community-riverkeeper enforcement model that held the largest US homebuilder accountable.

## Key Points

The violations began in 2022 at the 121-acre Ridgely's Reserve development in Joppa, Harford County. Massive sediment plumes in the Foster Branch and Gunpowder River were visible from satellite imagery. Inspectors found repeated failures to manage construction site sediment as required. The Gunpowder Riverkeeper, represented by the Chesapeake Legal Alliance, signaled intent to sue in August 2024; the state filed suit first, and the riverkeeper joined as intervenor.

Settlement breakdown: Kinsley (contractor) pays $2.2M, Forestar (developer) ~$1.5M, D.R. Horton (homebuilder) $410,000. Total: $2M civil penalty plus $2.1M for environmental remediation projects in the affected watershed, administered by the Chesapeake Bay Trust through competitive bid. Future violations trigger per-day fees.

None of the companies admitted liability. Construction on Ridgely's Reserve is continuing under the settlement.

## Relevance

For Frederick, this is a model for what aggressive enforcement looks like when developers cut corners on stormwater and sediment management during construction -- and who has standing to bring it. The Riverkeeper-as-intervenor structure is worth noting: community organizations can join state enforcement actions rather than filing separately, which reduces cost and increases credibility.

Frederick is actively reviewing development standards as part of the LMC process. This case is a concrete example of what happens when those standards are weak or inadequately enforced. The reputational and financial cost to D.R. Horton -- the largest US homebuilder -- from one project is significant.

The environmental remediation fund model (competitive grant process through Chesapeake Bay Trust) is also a useful template if Frederick ever negotiates a community benefit or mitigation fund connected to a development approval.
