# Bogota -- Equity for Working Women as Urban Innovation

## Summary

Bogota is pursuing urban equity for working women as a major civic innovation, framing gender-responsive urban design -- transit access, safe public space, economic inclusion -- as infrastructure rather than social policy.

## Key Points

Bogota's approach positions women's economic mobility as an urban planning problem with urban planning solutions: better transit routes that align with care work schedules, safer public spaces that remove barriers to women's movement through the city, and economic infrastructure that supports informal and formal labor. The framing is significant -- treating gender equity not as a welfare intervention but as a design and infrastructure challenge puts it in the same category as roads and water systems. Cities that have adopted this frame tend to produce more durable policy because the investments are legible to conventional planning budgets.

The article is a longer read (9 minutes per the source) so the full analysis was not captured in the Inoreader clip. The URL contains the full piece.

## Why This Matters

Frederick's planning conversations tend to center housing production and traffic. A gender-equity lens applied to urban design -- who can safely move through the city, who can access employment, whose care burdens shape mobility patterns -- is largely absent from local discourse. This framing could inform future conversations around transit, safe streets, or economic development in a way that connects progressive values to concrete infrastructure decisions.
