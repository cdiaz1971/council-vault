# Masons Law -- Maryland Storm Drain Inspection Requirements

## Summary

Senate Bill 189, named for 13-year-old Mason Kearns of Mount Airy who died in July 2025 after being swept into an open storm drain, was signed into law May 13 2026. It creates a mandatory municipal compliance schedule for storm drain inspection, prioritization, and repair across Maryland.

## Key Points and Compliance Timeline

Sponsored by Sen. Karen Lewis Young (D-Frederick), the law establishes three mandatory deadlines for municipalities:

July 1, 2027 -- Municipalities must have completed an inspection and inventory of all open storm drains within their jurisdiction.

April 1, 2028 -- Municipalities must have developed a prioritization plan for improvements to drainage inlets that pose the greatest risk to public safety.

April 1, 2031 -- Municipalities must have completed repairs to both public and private inlets. Once a municipality installs an approved drainage system, private owners assume maintenance responsibility.

## Relevance

Frederick is directly subject to this law. The July 2027 inventory deadline is 14 months away. The relevant question for DPW is whether an existing storm drain inventory is in place or whether this triggers a new capital project. The 2028 prioritization plan deadline will require council action or at minimum staff coordination. This should be flagged to the Parks and Infrastructure committee and to DPW for FY2027 budget planning -- if the inventory does not exist, staff time and possibly contracted survey work will be needed before July 2027.

The law was championed by Sen. Lewis Young, Frederick's own senator. Worth acknowledging publicly and confirming Frederick's compliance path.
