## Purpose

## Reading context

This January 2026 internal analysis preserves its source-date assumptions. It is supporting research, not the adopted master-plan document, a current asset register, an independent audit, or confirmation of present appropriations and project completion. Use [[Frederick Parks and Recreation]] for the core decision reference and its distinctions between plan evidence, capital records, access, and operating capacity. The priority ordering and alignment ratings are analytical interpretations, not an adopted ranked priority table. Reconcile them to the adopted plan and current capital accounts before using them in a funding decision.

This document compares the capital priorities identified in the Parks & Recreation Master Plan with the City of Frederick’s current Capital Improvement Program (CIP) park projects to assess alignment, gaps, and strategic risks.

---

## Priority 1: Renovation & Modernization of Existing Parks
**Parks Plan Priority:** Highest emphasis on reinvestment in aging facilities.

**CIP Alignment:**
- Playground Replacement Program
- Sports Court Replacement & Renovation
- Hillstreet Park Renovation
- Multiple individual park renovations (Riverside, Avalon, Clover Ridge, Renn, Kellerton, Mullinix, McCurdy Field)

**Assessment:** ✅ Strong alignment  
CIP heavily reflects this priority with multiple funded renovation projects.

---

## Priority 2: Trails & Greenways Expansion
**Parks Plan Priority:** Major community-supported goal.

**CIP Alignment:**
- Rails-to-Trails Program
- Shared Use Path Improvements
- North Branch Trail
- Golden Mile multimodal access projects

**Assessment:** ✅ Strong alignment  
Trails are treated as essential infrastructure across departments.

---

## Priority 3: Equity-Focused Park Improvements
**Parks Plan Priority:** Close service gaps between neighborhoods.

**CIP Alignment:**
- Park Improvements – Equity Emphasis Areas
- Hillstreet Park Renovation (west-side investment)
- Playground replacement phased by need

**Assessment:** ⚠ Partial alignment  
Projects exist, but equity framing is not consistently explicit across all park investments.

---

## Priority 4: System-Wide Facility Improvements (Signage, Data, Standards)
**Parks Plan Priority:** Coordinated upgrades and asset management.

**CIP Alignment:**
- Limited evidence of system-wide projects (signage, wayfinding, usage tracking)
- Projects remain site-specific rather than system-based

**Assessment:** ❌ Weak alignment  
Master Plan calls for system upgrades, but CIP focuses on individual park projects.

---

## Priority 5: Strategic New Facility Development
**Parks Plan Priority:** Targeted new facilities based on growth and service gaps.

**CIP Alignment:**
- No major new park acquisitions or new facility builds evident
- Focus remains on renovation

**Assessment:** ⚠ Limited alignment  
Appropriate in near term, but long-term growth planning is not yet visible in CIP.

---

## Priority 6: Sustainability & Green Infrastructure
**Parks Plan Priority:** Eco-friendly maintenance and climate integration.

**CIP Alignment:**
- Some projects include lighting upgrades and stormwater improvements
- Sustainability is embedded but not highlighted as a capital category

**Assessment:** ⚠ Indirect alignment  
Sustainability exists but lacks a clear, visible capital strategy within parks.

---

## Priority 7: Programming-Supportive Infrastructure
**Parks Plan Priority:** Facilities must support expanded recreation programming.

**CIP Alignment:**
- Playgrounds, courts, skate park upgrades, and park renovations support programming
- No clear investments in indoor or flexible multi-use recreation facilities

**Assessment:** ⚠ Partial alignment  
Outdoor recreation is well supported; indoor/multi-use space less so.

---

## Key Gaps Identified
- System-wide signage and wayfinding program
- Park usage data and asset management technology
- Long-range land acquisition and new park development
- Explicit sustainability-focused park capital projects
- Indoor recreation and flexible programming spaces

---

## Strategic Risks
- Continued reactive, site-by-site investment rather than system planning
- Equity goals may be diluted without explicit tracking
- Future population growth may outpace park capacity
- Maintenance and staffing pressures increase without parallel operating funding

---

## Strategic Opportunities for Council
- Require CIP projects to reference Parks Plan priorities explicitly
- Add a system-wide signage/wayfinding CIP project
- Create a visible “Equity Parks Investment” category
- Integrate sustainability metrics into park capital scoring
- Begin planning for next-generation park acquisition or indoor recreation facility

---

## Bottom Line
The current CIP shows strong alignment with the Parks Plan in:
- Renovation and modernization
- Trails and connectivity

However, it underrepresents:
- System-wide improvements
- Sustainability-focused capital projects
- Long-term growth and new facilities
- Data and performance infrastructure

Council can strengthen alignment by using the Parks Plan as an explicit capital prioritization framework rather than an advisory document.
