## Reading context

This document extracts the major **capital investment priorities** implied by the Parks & Recreation Master Plan. These priorities should guide future Capital Improvement Program (CIP) decisions and sequencing of projects.

## 1. Renovation and Modernization of Existing Parks
Highest priority is placed on reinvesting in existing facilities rather than expanding footprint first.

Focus areas:
- Aging playgrounds and sports courts
- Restrooms, shelters, and basic amenities
- Accessibility (ADA upgrades)
- Safety and lighting improvements
- Deferred maintenance backlog

Rationale:
The plan identifies aging facilities and inconsistent maintenance as a system-wide risk to service quality and public trust.

## 2. Trails and Greenways Expansion
Trails are one of the most strongly supported community priorities.

Capital emphasis includes:
- Expansion of trail networks connecting neighborhoods
- Greenway corridors linking parks and open space
- Connections to regional trail systems
- Multi-use paths for walking and biking

Rationale:
Trails serve transportation, recreation, health, and equity goals simultaneously and show high return on public value.

## 3. Equity-Focused Park Improvements
Investment should prioritize underserved and high-need neighborhoods.

Capital actions include:
- Park upgrades in equity emphasis areas
- Improved access to recreation facilities
- Closing service gaps between neighborhoods
- Targeted facility improvements rather than cosmetic upgrades

Rationale:
The plan explicitly frames equitable access to parks as a planning principle and community expectation.

## 4. Facility System-Wide Improvements
Rather than isolated projects, the plan calls for coordinated system upgrades.

Includes:
- Standardized signage and wayfinding
- Consistent park design standards
- Technology for usage tracking and asset management
- Coordinated maintenance infrastructure

Rationale:
Inconsistent facilities and lack of data were identified as major operational weaknesses.

## 5. New Facility Development (Strategic, Not Broad Expansion)
New parks and facilities should be driven by:
- Population growth areas
- Documented service gaps
- Long-range land acquisition strategy
- Integration with other City plans

Priority is not expansion for its own sake, but targeted development aligned with demand.

## 6. Sustainability and Green Practices
Capital investments should incorporate environmental goals.

Includes:
- Eco-friendly maintenance infrastructure
- Green stormwater management
- Tree canopy and shade structures
- Energy-efficient lighting and facilities
- Alignment with Sustainability Plan

Rationale:
Parks are identified as part of the City’s climate and environmental strategy.

## 7. Programming-Supportive Infrastructure
Facilities must support modern recreation programming.

Capital needs include:
- Flexible multi-use spaces
- Indoor recreation capacity
- Event infrastructure
- Support facilities for youth and seniors

Rationale:
Programs cannot expand without adequate physical infrastructure.

## Capital Planning Principles (from the Plan)
Future capital decisions should be:
- Data-driven
- Equity-centered
- Integrated with Comprehensive Plan and CIP
- Phased and sustainable
- Focused on long-term lifecycle costs, not just construction

## Strategic Implications for CIP
- Shift funding toward renovation over new construction
- Make trails and greenways a recurring CIP category
- Create an explicit equity lens for park capital projects
- Tie staffing and maintenance funding to capital expansion
- Treat parks as essential infrastructure alongside roads and utilities

## Bottom Line
The Parks & Recreation Master Plan prioritizes capital investment in:
1. Existing park renovation and modernization  
2. Trail and greenway expansion  
3. Equity-based neighborhood improvements  
4. System-wide facility upgrades  
5. Sustainable and strategic new development  

Capital spending is framed not as optional amenities but as core quality-of-life and public health infrastructure.
