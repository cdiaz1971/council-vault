## Purpose

## Reading context

This January 2026 internal analysis preserves its source-date assumptions. It is supporting research, not the adopted master-plan document, a current asset register, an independent audit, or confirmation of present appropriations and project completion. Use [[Frederick Parks and Recreation]] for the core decision reference and its distinctions between plan evidence, capital records, access, and operating capacity.

The Parks & Recreation Master Plan establishes a 5–10 year roadmap to guide investment in parks, facilities, programs, trails, and operations. It is intended to be integrated into Citywide planning efforts including the Comprehensive Plan and Capital Improvement Program.

## Vision & Mission
**Vision:** “Essential Spaces … A vision for City Parks and Recreation.”  
**Mission:** Provide diverse recreational opportunities and amenities that enhance quality of life for residents and visitors through a comprehensive parks and recreation system.

## System Snapshot
- 73 developed parks (~950 acres)
- 12 undeveloped properties (~69 acres)
- Four divisions: Golf Course, Special Events, Parks Division, Recreation Division
- Staffing and maintenance capacity identified as constraints

## Community Engagement
Input was gathered through:
- Statistically valid community survey
- Public forums and focus groups
- Stakeholder and leadership interviews

Key themes from residents:
- Desire for better maintenance of existing parks
- Strong support for trails and greenways
- Need for clearer communication and signage
- Interest in youth engagement and equitable access

## Key Issues Identified
- Inconsistent signage and communication
- Confusion over park ownership and responsibilities
- Staffing shortages and pay inequities
- Aging facilities and deferred maintenance
- Limited ability to track park usage data

## Five Core Goals
1. Improve organizational efficiency  
2. Increase financial opportunities and sustainability  
3. Strengthen recreation programs and services  
4. Invest in existing and new facilities system-wide  
5. Expand trails and greenways  

## Strategic Direction
The plan emphasizes:
- System-wide planning rather than reactive maintenance
- Equity in access across neighborhoods
- Integration with sustainability, transportation, and public health goals
- Data-driven decision making and performance measures

## Policy Implications
- Parks are treated as essential quality-of-life infrastructure
- Future capital budgets should prioritize renovation and modernization
- Trail expansion is a major long-term priority
- Staffing and operational capacity must grow alongside facilities
- Equity and neighborhood access are explicit planning principles

## Bottom Line
The Parks Plan frames parks and recreation as a core public service that supports health, equity, and community cohesion. It calls for sustained investment in facilities, trails, staffing, and coordination with other City plans to meet Frederick’s future growth and demographic change.
