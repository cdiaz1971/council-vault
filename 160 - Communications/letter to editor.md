Letter to the Editor: Turning Vacant Storefronts into Opportunity

Dear Editor,

Vacant storefronts are scattered across our city—silent symbols of economic shifts and disinvestment. But they also represent an opportunity: to help disenfranchised residents become entrepreneurs and bring life back to our neighborhoods.

I propose a city-led initiative to support small businesses started by individuals from historically marginalized communities in these empty retail spaces. The city can play a critical role by lowering the barriers to entry and providing meaningful support for startup costs.

This could include offering grants to cover initial expenses such as equipment, signage, insurance, and licensing fees. The city can also provide no-interest microloans for working capital, subsidize commercial rent for the first year, and partner with local banks and community development financial institutions (CDFIs) to increase access to capital. In addition, technical assistance—such as help with business plans, permitting, digital marketing, and accounting—should be made readily available through a city-supported resource center or incubator program.

This approach would not only empower new business owners who have traditionally been excluded from economic opportunity, but also revitalize vacant commercial corridors, create local jobs, and build community wealth. We already have the space; with vision and political will, we can make it a springboard for equity and growth.

I urge city leaders and residents to support this effort and reimagine our empty storefronts as engines of opportunity.
