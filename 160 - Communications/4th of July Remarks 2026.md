# 4th of July Remarks 2026

## Summary
Prepared remarks for César's appearance at the City's 4th of July celebration, marking the 250th anniversary of the Declaration of Independence with values-only allusion to current civic pressures and a civic engagement call to action.

## Context
Event: City of Frederick 4th of July celebration, semiquincentennial of the Declaration of Independence
Length: base version runs 2 minutes 45 seconds, extended version with call to action runs approximately 4 minutes 15 seconds
Framing: values-only allusion to principles under strain, no reference to specific current events, agencies, or policies, per César's direction

## Remarks (base version, 2-3 minutes)

Good evening, Frederick.

Two hundred fifty years ago this week, a group of people sat down and wrote something remarkable. Not a law. Not a treaty. A declaration. A statement that governments exist to serve the people, not the other way around. That certain rights belong to us simply because we are human. Life. Liberty. The pursuit of happiness.

They didn't get everything right. We know that. The story of this country has always been the story of trying to close the gap between what we wrote down and how we actually live. Every generation gets a turn at that work.

Tonight we're not here to argue about that history. We're here to celebrate it. Fireworks over Frederick. Kids running around with sparklers. Neighbors on blankets in the grass. This is what the day is supposed to feel like.

But I'd ask you to hold onto one thing tonight, alongside the fun. Those words in that document weren't guarantees. They were a promise. And promises only hold if people keep showing up for them.

Equal protection. Due process. The idea that no one is above the law and everyone is entitled to its protection. These aren't abstract ideas from an old parchment. They are choices we make, over and over, in how we treat each other and how we govern ourselves. Right now, in this moment, those choices matter as much as they ever have.

That's not a burden. That's the inheritance. Two hundred fifty years of people deciding those words were worth fighting for, worth improving, worth handing to the next generation a little better than they received them.

So enjoy tonight. Eat the barbecue. Watch the fireworks. Be proud of this city and this country. And tomorrow, when the holiday's over, remember that the Declaration didn't finish the job. It started one. We're still on it.

Happy Fourth of July, Frederick.

## Extension Block (insert before "So enjoy tonight" to reach approximately 5 minutes)

And that inheritance isn't something you hand off to someone else to protect. It's not just for the people up here with microphones or the people in Annapolis or Washington. It's yours. It shows up when you go to a council meeting and say your piece. It shows up when you vote, not just in November but in the smaller elections nobody pays attention to. It shows up when you check on your neighbor, when you show up for a school board meeting, when you volunteer for something that has nothing to do with you personally except that you live here and you care.

Frederick runs on that kind of participation. This city doesn't get better because a handful of people in office decide it should. It gets better because thousands of people decide they're not going to sit this one out. That's true this year more than most.

## Risk Flags

The line "that's true this year more than most" in the extension block is the only spot that gestures at present circumstances. Mild and defensible as written. Cut it if full neutrality is wanted.

If pressed afterward on what the "principles under strain" language meant, the safe answer is that it was a general reflection on civic responsibility tied to the anniversary, not a comment on any specific pending matter.

## Relevance
Public-facing remarks tied to César's role as Council President. Connects to the ongoing CVA communications thread on framing discipline around civic and immigration language. The "inheritance" framing pairs well with the housing inheritance note filed this same session -- the word is doing double duty across democratic and economic registers.
