# State of the City Speech 2026

## Summary
This is Video 2 of the State of the City presentation, titled "The Ghost of Frederick Present OR A Safe and Accessible City." It was written by the City of Frederick Public Affairs Department for César to record as narrator. It is not a draft and is filed here as the final copy of record. The text below is unedited.

## Script

**Video 2: The Ghost of Frederick Present OR A Safe and Accessible City
Narrator: Council President César Díaz**

Frederick is steeped in history and charm, a town older than our country. We're also growing, changing, and becoming more diverse. More people are choosing to build their lives here, and it's our job to make sure that the City is here for every one of them.

We are only as strong as the people we include. Every person who lives in Frederick should be able to walk into a public building, use City services, and participate in civic life without fear, and without encountering barriers that others never had to think about. From policy, to access, to partnerships, to representation, we're building a Frederick where growth doesn't leave people behind, and differences aren't divisions. That's part of why the Council is taking a holistic look at our Land Management Code, modernizing the policies that shape how and where we grow.

And those principles guided the City Values Act, which I was proud to introduce and help pass this year. It keeps immigration enforcement out of the private areas of our buildings and off City property without a warrant signed by a judge. And City employees won't ask where you were born unless the law requires it.

I've taught beginner computer classes at Centro Hispano, and I've sat with people who were afraid to walk into a public building for services their own taxes pay for.

This ordinance is our City saying, "Come to the health clinic. Bring your kids to the pool. Come to a City Council meeting and tell us what you think!"

Being included means being able to use what this city offers. Accessibility is not a courtesy, and neither is safety on our streets... they are rights.

We're installing new production systems, including translation services, so residents who are Deaf or hard of hearing can follow a public meeting, and so residents whose first language isn't English can follow it in theirs. We are ensuring ADA compliance across our facilities and our website and providing plain language recaps of our public meetings. We're hiring a new Neighborhood Connections Ombudsperson, so residents have a direct connection to help solve problems.

Someone who can't cross the street safely can't get to any of it. That's why we are leading a Vision Zero initiative to ensure all pedestrians, cyclists, and motorists are safer. It's our commitment to zero traffic deaths on Frederick's streets by 2035.

We're using our resources to reach people where they already are, supporting the Asian American Center of Frederick, Family Connects, the Dolly Parton Imagination Library, and our Neighborhood Advisory Councils, among others. These organizations reach more people than the City could alone, which is why we made sure to support them in the FY27 Budget. Help is already in the community, on your block, and should be easy for everyone to access.

City Hall should feel like it belongs to the people who walk into it. Our Art in City Hall program is a rotating exhibition built on partnerships with nonprofits and neighborhood groups, that showcases the work of young people and artists from marginalized communities. This year it showed the Continuing Bonds Quilt, where family members and friends sewed a quilt square for a loved one they lost to overdose. It showed student work from the Academy for the Fine Arts at Governor Thomas Johnson, and pieces from our City's Parks and Rec art classes.

All this work is not finished, but everything we build says the same thing to the people who live here: you belong.
