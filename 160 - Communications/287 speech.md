My name is Cesar Diaz, I am a resident of the city of Frederick and the Council Member Elect for District 2 to the Frederick city council and a member of the RISE Coalition of Western Maryland.  I am here to talk to you about the 287(g) program.

This program was sold to the public as a tool for removing dangerous criminals. But the reality in Maryland and across the nation tells a very different story. The majority of people funneled into deportation through 287(g) are not violent offenders. Many, over 80%, have no criminal record at all. 


When a traffic stop becomes a deportation pipeline, that is not justice, that is a due process failure. We are empowering local law enforcement to detain people for the federal government without the legal protections, oversight, or accountability required in immigration proceedings. People are held longer than they should be. They are questioned without counsel. They are treated as though civil immigration violations place them outside the Constitution's reach.

Maryland should reject that.

287(g) also makes all of us less safe. When immigrant communities fear that any interaction with the sheriff's office could lead to separation from their families, they stop reporting crimes. They stop serving as witnesses. They stop trusting the very institutions sworn to protect them. Public safety is built on cooperation, not coercion. On trust, not terror.

Maryland is better than this.
We are a state that believes in fairness.
We are a state that believes in facts, not fear mongering.
We are a state that upholds constitutional principles not selectively, not conditionally, but for everyone.

By banning 287(g) agreements, we will stand on the side of due process, of fairness, of real justice. . Local police should be focused on local public safety, not federal immigration enforcement. And we send a clear message that Maryland will not participate in policies that divide families, silence communities, or undermine our justice system.

This is our moment to choose what kind of state we want to be, one that deputizes fear, or one that champions dignity, safety, and equal protection under the law.

I urge all our state representatives to follow the example of their fellow legislators who have stood up for these core principles and support a ban of 287(g) agreements in Maryland
