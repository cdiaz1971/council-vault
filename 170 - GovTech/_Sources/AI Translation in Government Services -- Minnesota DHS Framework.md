# AI Translation in Government Services -- Minnesota DHS Framework

## Summary

Minnesota's Department of Human Services built an interdisciplinary evaluation framework for deciding when and how to use LLMs for government translation services. Presented at Code for America's 2026 Summit in Chicago, the framework offers a replicable decision process for any local government considering AI-assisted language access.

## Key Points

The four questions the framework poses before adopting an LLM for translation:

1. **Does the content warrant it?** Consider the type, frequency, and volume of translation needed. Minnesota was translating Provider Hub content (licensing, training, alerts, newsletters) four to twelve times a year depending on product -- high enough frequency to justify LLM investment.

2. **What resources are available?** Assess speed, accuracy, and cost of AI vs. human translators vs. hybrid approaches against the organization's actual staffing and funding capacity. Minnesota ultimately created the Enterprise Translation Office in 2024 with six multilingual staff who use ChatGPT for generation and conduct human quality assurance.

3. **Which product to implement?** The choice should follow from the use case and resource assessment, not lead it.

4. **How to prepare the LLM and content?** This is described as the most important step. Key considerations: understand language-specific nuances that affect prompt design; distill English training content into plain language so translations are more direct; decide which content should not be translated (proper names, official program titles like "Provider Hub" to maintain recognizability across languages); build a QA plan with repeated human review.

Results: Spanish and Somali translations dropped from two hours to one; Hmong from four hours to two.

## Relevance

Frederick has a significant Spanish-speaking population served by Centro Hispano and SSCM. The city produces communications, permits, and public-facing documents that are not consistently available in Spanish. The Minnesota framework is the clearest model in the vault for how to think about AI-assisted translation without either over-relying on AI (quality risk) or dismissing it (efficiency loss). The key takeaway is the hybrid model: AI generates, humans review. The QA step is non-negotiable.

This also connects to the broader GovTech/AI civic services question. Language access is one of the clearest use cases where AI provides genuine public benefit without displacement risk -- it extends human capacity rather than replacing human judgment on consequential decisions.
