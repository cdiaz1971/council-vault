# FirstNet Reauthorization -- Public Safety Broadband at Risk

## Summary

FirstNet -- the dedicated public safety broadband network built on a public-private partnership with AT&T -- expires in February 2027 without congressional reauthorization. City leaders speaking at the National League of Cities Congressional City Conference warned in March 2026 that failure to reauthorize would leave first responders without coordinated communications during disasters. A reauthorization bill has passed subcommittee and is awaiting full committee action.

## Key Points

FirstNet was established to give first responders a dedicated, interoperable communications network that prioritizes public safety traffic over commercial users. AT&T won the build-out contract in 2017. As of early 2026, around 31,000 public safety agencies are subscribed with coverage across roughly 3 million square miles.

NLC CEO Clarence Anthony made the stakes plain at the conference: when disasters strike, coordinating across FEMA, state emergency management, airports, transit systems, and local departments requires interoperable communications. Without FirstNet, Anthony said, "we're going to be in real bad trouble."

The reauthorization bill (H.R. First Responder Network Authority Reauthorization Act) would extend the authority through September 2037 and strengthen NTIA oversight -- requiring annual reports to Congress on cybersecurity and adoption rates, and appointing an associate administrator to manage staff and liaise with NTIA. The House Energy and Commerce Subcommittee on Communications and Technology advanced it to the full committee, which has not yet acted.

Over 70 public safety associations representing more than 5 million first responders have called for reauthorization. A white paper from the Public Safety Broadband Technology Association argued the reauthorization is a "critical opportunity" to reaffirm board autonomy, strengthen leadership qualifications, and ensure spectrum revenues are reinvested in the network rather than diverted. The coming decade's plan includes $8 billion in reinvestment focused on 5G, rural coverage, Tribal areas, and US territories.

## Why This Matters for César

As Frederick's M365/Azure Administrator, César manages city IT infrastructure that interfaces with public safety systems. FirstNet reauthorization is a federal legislative issue, but its outcome affects local emergency communications capabilities directly. NLC is tracking this actively, which is relevant given César's council role and Frederick's NLC membership.

This is also a useful data point for conversations about municipal technology resilience -- the risks of public safety infrastructure depending on federal authorization timelines.
