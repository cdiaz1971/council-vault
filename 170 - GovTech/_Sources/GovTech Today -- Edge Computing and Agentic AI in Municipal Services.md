# GovTech Today -- Edge Computing and Agentic AI in Municipal Services

## Summary

Cities are beginning to pair edge computing with agentic AI to manage street-level maintenance issues faster and more accurately than traditional methods. The same GovTech Today issue covers a NASCIO analysis of how midterm elections are likely to reshape state CIO leadership in 2027, and LA Metro's new public-facing safety dashboard for transit operations.

## Key Points

Edge computing plus agentic AI for street maintenance is the lead story. The combination allows cities to spot problems like potholes in real time using AI running close to the data source rather than routing everything through centralized cloud infrastructure. GovTech frames this as an unprecedented opportunity for proactive street-level maintenance -- though the article also notes that cities must maintain traditional privacy standards as these systems collect physical environment data.

The NASCIO midterm analysis projects significant churn in state CIO rosters after 2026 elections. The framing is that gubernatorial and legislative changes predictably bring new technology leadership, and cities and counties should plan for disruption in state-level digital infrastructure partnerships. This is relevant context for Maryland-level IT relationships.

LA Metro's safety dashboard makes incident and crime data publicly available online as a response to rider safety concerns. The transparency angle -- publishing what was previously internal -- is a model for how transit and infrastructure agencies can rebuild public trust through data. Frederick doesn't have a metro system, but the dashboard-as-transparency-tool approach is applicable to public works, code enforcement, or ALPR data from the Flock system.

GovTech also flagged a story about a government website banning AI use in article writing, which signals ongoing uncertainty in public sector communications about AI authenticity and disclosure norms.
