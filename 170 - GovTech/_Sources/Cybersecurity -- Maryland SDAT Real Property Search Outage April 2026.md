# Maryland SDAT Real Property Search Outage -- April 2026 Cyber Incident

## Summary

Maryland's SDAT Real Property Search tool was taken offline April 14, 2026 after suspicious activity was detected on its servers. Early analysis suggests only publicly available property records were affected, and no broader state cybersecurity risk is anticipated.

## Key Points

The Maryland Department of Information Technology detected suspicious activity on April 14, 2026 on servers running the State Department of Assessments and Taxation's Real Property Search application and immediately took the site offline. As of April 20-21, the tool remained down with no public restoration timeline given.

The affected database covers roughly 2.5 million real property accounts statewide, providing ownership, valuation, and sales history information. DoIT's initial forensic analysis concluded that compromised systems contained only information already publicly accessible through the search tool -- no private data appears to have been exposed. Officials stated they do not anticipate broader risk to state systems.

The outage is disrupting real estate workflows statewide: title searches, appraisals, and property closings have been affected, particularly during the mid-April business filing season. State officials are directing affected residents and professionals to contact local Real Property Assessment County Offices for manual assistance, and are warning against third-party sites claiming to provide SDAT records.

DoIT is recommending standard cybersecurity hygiene in response: software updates, phishing awareness, strong passwords, and multi-factor authentication.

## Relevance

As Frederick's M365/Azure Administrator, incidents like this illustrate the vulnerability of state government systems and the operational disruption that follows even a contained breach. The manual fallback to county offices is notable -- Frederick staff may be fielding elevated call volume during the outage. The "only public data was exposed" framing is worth watching; this is a common initial characterization that sometimes changes as investigations mature. Relevant to ongoing thinking about municipal cybersecurity posture and incident response planning.
