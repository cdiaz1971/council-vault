# Government Tech Leadership Changes and AI Implementation Across Cities

## Summary

GovTech Today's March 16 newsletter covers major personnel changes in state and local government IT leadership, including Texas appointing Tony Sauerhoff as its new CIO and Baltimore naming T.J. Mayotte to lead IT operations. The newsletter highlights how cities are scaling artificial intelligence projects from pilots into operational systems, with Raleigh, North Carolina reporting success in deploying AI for help-desk automation and optimizing service delivery. Additionally, the San Diego County Sheriff's Office has implemented AI to handle non-emergency call routing, while New York has launched grants to help water systems improve cybersecurity defenses amid rising threats linked to foreign actors.

## Key Ideas

Raleigh's CIO Mark Wittenburg has moved beyond experimental AI pilots toward full technology adoption across city services. The city is deploying agentic AI systems for help-desk support and has implemented AI-driven optimization for trash collection routes, demonstrating how structured testing in pilot phases allows governments to validate solutions before wider rollout. This methodical approach transforms early experiments into sustainable technology strategy that benefits multiple departments.

The San Diego County Sheriff's Office has deployed an AI system specifically designed to answer non-emergency calls and triage requests that do not pose immediate life-threatening risks. This application reduces burden on human operators by using artificial intelligence to classify incoming calls and route them appropriately, freeing law enforcement resources for more urgent matters while maintaining service responsiveness to the public.

New York State has created a grant program targeting water and wastewater systems to upgrade their cybersecurity infrastructure. This initiative emerged from concerns about cyberattacks linked to Iran and reflects growing recognition among state and local governments that critical infrastructure utilities require direct investment in security improvements to defend against nation-state threats and other sophisticated actors.

Tony Sauerhoff's appointment as Texas CIO follows a comprehensive search process and represents institutional continuity since he served in an interim capacity beginning in January 2026. His confirmation by the Texas Department of Information Resources board signals confidence in leadership of statewide IT operations and policy across the state government enterprise.

T.J. Mayotte brings combined experience from both private sector and public sector roles in adjacent counties to Baltimore's new CIO position, appointed by the mayor to oversee IT leadership for the city government.

## Why this matters for César

As a city council president managing M365 and Azure infrastructure, César should note how Raleigh's methodical approach to AI implementation offers a replicable model for testing new technologies before full organizational deployment. The success stories around agentic AI systems and route optimization demonstrate concrete ROI that can justify technology investment at the municipal level. Understanding how peer cities are structuring pilots and scaling winners provides valuable reference points for local government technology strategy. The cybersecurity grants program in New York also signals that state-level funding opportunities exist to support infrastructure upgrades, which could inform César's own municipal IT security planning.
