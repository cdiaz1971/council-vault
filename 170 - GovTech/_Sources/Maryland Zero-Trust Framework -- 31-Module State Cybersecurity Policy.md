# Maryland Zero-Trust Framework -- 31-Module State Cybersecurity Policy

## Core Idea

Maryland's Department of Information Technology released a 31-module zero-trust cybersecurity framework that 22 Cabinet agencies must adopt within 18 months. The framework replaces a 200-plus page document from 2019 and shifts the state from "trust but verify" to "never trust, always verify" -- requiring continuous identity validation for access to high-value state systems.

## Key Details

The framework explicitly prevents lateral movement: once a bad actor gains entry to one system, zero-trust architecture makes it exponentially harder to move to another. Local governments and the state's legislative and judicial branches are not required to follow the new policies but are encouraged to adopt them.

CISO James Saunders, who joined in 2025, also added a statewide vulnerability disclosure program, expanded the Maryland Information Sharing and Analysis Center, and stood up several new working groups -- all within the past year. His framing: "This is a half-marathon, not a 5K."

Maryland's IT department previously consolidated disparate Active Directory domains across agencies and hired 15 information security officers to assist state agencies -- both prerequisites for a coherent zero-trust rollout.

## Why This Matters for César

As Frederick's M365/Azure Administrator, zero-trust is the direction Microsoft's entire stack is moving -- Entra ID, Conditional Access, and Intune are all zero-trust infrastructure. Maryland's 31-module framework is a concrete peer-government model Frederick could reference when building out its own cybersecurity governance. The "local governments encouraged but not required" note means Frederick has flexibility but also has cover to move in this direction proactively.
