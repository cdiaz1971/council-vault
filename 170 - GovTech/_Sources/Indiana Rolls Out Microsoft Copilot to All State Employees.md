# Indiana Rolls Out Microsoft Copilot to All State Employees

## Summary

Indiana has made Microsoft Copilot available to all state employees on an opt-in basis, with training as the primary driver of adoption. CIO Warren Lenard described the program at the 2026 NASCIO Midyear Conference, emphasizing that training is not just for frontline staff -- it extends to cabinet secretaries. The framing is that leadership adoption is necessary for the program to have real organizational impact.

## Key Points

The architecture of the rollout: voluntary availability rather than mandated use, but with training as the key enabler. The implication is that employees who complete training are more likely to adopt and find value, and that untrained employees who have nominal access won't actually change their workflows.

Extending training to cabinet-level leadership is notable. In most government AI deployments, senior officials are briefed but not trained. Indiana's model treats leadership as learners, not just sponsors. This likely drives buy-in at the decision-making level and creates a shared language for discussing what the tool can and cannot do.

No specific cost or productivity metrics are cited in this dispatch, but the program appears to be a leading example of broad-based government Copilot deployment.

## Relevance

This is directly relevant to Frederick's M365/Copilot deployment work. The Indiana model -- voluntary, training-first, leadership included -- is a plausible framework for how Frederick could approach Copilot rollout. The cabinet-secretary training point is worth noting for how to get elected officials and department heads engaged rather than just nominally supportive.
