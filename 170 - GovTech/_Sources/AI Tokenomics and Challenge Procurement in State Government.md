# AI Tokenomics and Challenge Procurement in State Government

## Summary
Government IT leaders are struggling to budget for usage-based AI pricing, while Connecticut showed a challenge-based procurement can finish in 10 weeks instead of 18 months. Both bear on how a city buys and pays for AI.

## Key Points
Tokenomics is GovTech's shorthand for the budgeting problem created by usage-based AI pricing. Unlike seat licenses, token and consumption charges scale with use and are hard to forecast in an annual budget cycle. The newsletter says best practices are emerging, but the email only teases the full article.

Connecticut CIO Mark Raymond used a challenge-based procurement to meet a federal Medicaid deadline, completing in 10 weeks a technology buy that would normally take 18 months. Vendors are asked to demonstrate a solution against a defined problem rather than answer a long specification.

Other items in the issue: Broward County's four-person web team used AI to work through 10,000 web pages for a site rebuild. Massachusetts CIO Jason Snyder says AI is changing how attackers choose targets and is driving more attacks on local governments. The NASCIO 2026 State CIO Survey covers AI maturity, and state CIOs are being pulled into the AI data center debate. A GovTech piece also asks how many states now pay more for AI from Anthropic than from OpenAI.

## Relevance
Usage-based AI pricing is a fiscal governance question for Frederick, not just an IT one. Any AI procurement policy should require cost caps, usage reporting, and a forecast method before approval. Challenge-based procurement is a tool worth knowing, but its speed also cuts against the deliberation and public input that the surveillance procurement fights have shown matter. The data center item connects to the ZTA fight.
