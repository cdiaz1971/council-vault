# CISA Funding Secured -- State and Local Cybersecurity Grant Program in Limbo

The May 2026 Homeland Security appropriations bill secured funding for CISA, preserving the agency's operational capacity. The more consequential uncertainty for local governments is the State and Local Cybersecurity Grant Program, which has not been renewed. State CIOs are actively pressing Congress to reinstate it.

The grant program has been a primary mechanism for smaller municipalities to fund cybersecurity infrastructure, threat assessment, and incident response capacity that they could not otherwise afford. Its lapse creates a gap precisely when AI-enabled cyber threats are accelerating.

NASCIO's Midyear Conference in Philadelphia surfaced the divide clearly: some state CIOs described improving cyber posture, while others flagged deteriorating conditions -- a "tale of two states" pattern shaped largely by whether states had invested in whole-of-state cybersecurity architecture before federal grant support became uncertain.

## Why this matters for César

Frederick's cybersecurity posture depends partly on state and federal funding flows that César has limited direct control over, but the lapse of the grant program is worth tracking. If the program isn't renewed, the burden shifts to cities to fund cyber resilience from general fund or CIP budgets. This connects to the broader question of how Frederick plans for technology infrastructure risk in an era of federal uncertainty.
