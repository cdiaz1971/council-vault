# GovTech -- Ransomware Threats and Montana Community Planning Platform

## Summary

GovTech Today's April 15 issue covers two threads relevant to Frederick: the FBI's 2025 Internet Crime Report identifying ransomware as a persistent top threat to critical infrastructure including government systems, and Montana's deployment of an AI-backed Community Planning Platform that cut manual processes and unlocked over $1 million in federal funding.

## Key Arguments

FBI ransomware findings: The Internet Crime Complaint Center identified more than 2,100 ransomware incidents in 2025 directed at US critical infrastructure, including healthcare and energy. Government systems remain a primary target category. The volume has not declined despite law enforcement attention.

The GovTech editorial also notes the Mythos angle: with automated exploit chaining now demonstrated at the frontier model level, CISOs need to think about adversarial AI as part of the threat model, not just human-operated ransomware groups.

Montana's Community Planning Platform: A geographically large, rural state with limited staff capacity used AI-backed technology from Snowflake to cut manual planning processes and better analyze geographic data. A state official described the platform as vital for serving rural residents who otherwise get left behind in planning processes. The result: reduced staff workload and more than $1 million in unlocked federal funding.

Also from the same issue: a GovTech editor's pick on Cleveland staffing up its IT and urban analytics leadership -- the city made its interim chief innovation and technology officer permanent and named a new director for the Office of Urban Analytics and Innovation.

## Relevance

The ransomware finding is directly actionable for the Frederick M365/Azure admin role. With 2,100+ incidents against critical infrastructure in 2025, the threat model for city systems -- especially systems running on older or hybrid infrastructure -- warrants regular review. The Montana platform story is useful for the GovTech track: AI-backed planning platforms that reduce staff workload while improving data quality are exactly the kind of tool Frederick should be tracking as the city's planning department grapples with capacity and the LMC review. Cleveland's urban analytics investment is a model for what a dedicated data/innovation function could look like at Frederick's scale.
