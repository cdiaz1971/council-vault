# AI Cybersecurity Arms Race -- Dual-Use Threat in Government Systems

## Summary

AI is both accelerating the volume and sophistication of attacks on government infrastructure and providing defenders with faster detection and response capabilities. For municipal IT administrators, this creates a moving target: adopting AI tools defensively while adversaries use the same technology offensively.

## Key Points

The GovTech May 2026 dispatch highlighted a fundamental tension in AI's role in public sector cybersecurity. On the offensive side, AI is enabling attackers to generate more phishing variants, automate reconnaissance, and increase attack frequency at a pace human defenders cannot match manually. On the defensive side, AI-powered tools are giving government security teams the ability to detect anomalies, correlate signals, and respond faster than traditional rule-based systems.

The Cook County, Illinois case was flagged as a counterpoint: the county actively pushed back on AI-powered jail surveillance, illustrating that not every AI security deployment is straightforward or politically neutral. The framing of "security" can justify surveillance expansions that carry civil liberties costs.

A separate item noted that AI upskilling is now considered a baseline requirement for IT and cybersecurity professionals who want to stay relevant, with the consensus being that the threat is not AI replacing these roles but rather AI-equipped adversaries outpacing practitioners who haven't integrated AI tools.

Maryland's new Data Privacy and Protection Act of 2026 was also flagged in this issue -- it limits how agencies collect and retain resident data and expands privacy requirements in procurement, with particular focus on third-party contractors.

## Relevance

As Frederick's M365/Azure administrator, the dual-use nature of AI in cybersecurity is directly relevant to infrastructure decisions. Vendor conversations and RFPs are increasingly AI-heavy. Understanding the difference between AI tools that genuinely improve posture versus those that introduce new surveillance or liability risks is a practical skill. The Cook County jail surveillance rejection is a useful political data point for any council-level conversation about AI procurement. The Maryland DPPA requirements add a procurement layer that affects vendor selection directly.
