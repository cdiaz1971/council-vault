# Colorado Eliminates Chief Data Officer Position

## Summary

Colorado is eliminating its Chief Data Officer position as part of a state restructuring of data governance functions, with CDO Amy Bhikha departing after serving since 2021.

## Key Points

Colorado CIO David Edinger announced the state is restructuring how it handles data work, resulting in the elimination of the standalone CDO role. Amy Bhikha, who served as CDO since 2021, is moving on. The specific rationale for the restructuring was not detailed in the GovTech report -- it's unclear whether the functions are being absorbed into another office, distributed across agencies, or reduced. This follows a broader pattern in some jurisdictions of questioning whether dedicated data leadership roles are sustainable or effective as standalone positions.

## Relevance

Interesting signal for GovTech and municipal technology strategy thinking. The CDO model has been touted as a best practice for data-driven government, but its sustainability depends on leadership buy-in and budget. Useful context for thinking about how Frederick and similarly sized governments approach data governance without the resources of a state agency. Worth watching whether other states follow a similar pattern. See [[Municipal Technology Strategy]].
