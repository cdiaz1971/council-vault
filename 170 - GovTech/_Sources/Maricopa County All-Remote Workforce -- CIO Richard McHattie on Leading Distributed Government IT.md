# Maricopa County All-Remote Workforce -- CIO Richard McHattie on Leading Distributed Government IT

## Summary

Maricopa County, Arizona (the fourth most populous county in the US) runs its IT department of several hundred employees entirely remote -- and was among the first government entities to deploy ChatGPT. CIO Richard McHattie offers a model for how large government IT organizations can operate in distributed environments and adopt AI tools at scale.

## Key Points

Maricopa County's all-remote IT workforce demonstrates that distributed government operations are viable at significant scale. The county's early ChatGPT deployment suggests a pragmatic, adoption-forward posture toward AI tools rather than a wait-and-see approach common in government.

The GovTech newsletter also covered Pennsylvania CIO Bry Pardoe's work modernizing IT by reorienting projects around service delivery -- the core goal being to ease resident access to government services rather than optimizing for internal IT metrics. This service-delivery framing is a useful design principle for Frederick's GovTech strategy.

Also in this issue: CISA's CI Fortify roadmap for state and local cyber resilience (see separate note), and a Code for America/Anthropic partnership to help government caseworkers use AI to navigate public benefits policy.

## Relevance

As the M365/Azure administrator for a private company and as Council President tracking Frederick's GovTech strategy, both the distributed workforce model and the service-delivery IT framing are relevant. The Code for America/Anthropic caseworker AI pilot is particularly interesting -- AI helping government workers navigate complex policy to serve residents faster is a near-term application worth watching for Frederick context.
