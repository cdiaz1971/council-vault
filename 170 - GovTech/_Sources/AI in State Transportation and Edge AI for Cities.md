# GovTech -- AI in State Transportation Data and Edge AI for City Services

## Summary

GovTech newsletter roundup covering AI reshaping state DOT data governance, edge AI for city pothole detection, Connecticut pausing AI use in criminal reports, and a new book on global state-sponsored hacking.

## Key Points

**AI and State Transportation Departments**

State departments of transportation are using AI to build new data governance processes and make better use of data they already collect. The piece highlights Caltrans as a case study in modernizing operations by unifying maintenance, permitting, and asset management systems.

**Edge AI for City Services**

A new form of AI (edge AI, running locally on devices rather than in the cloud) is enabling cities to spot infrastructure problems like potholes faster and more accurately than before. GovTech notes the trade-off: government must maintain privacy standards as these systems expand.

**Connecticut Pauses AI for Criminal Reports**

Connecticut state leaders have temporarily halted AI-generated criminal reports, citing concern about accuracy and accountability in the criminal justice context. This is a notable example of a state actually slowing down AI deployment pending review.

**Cybersecurity**

Allie Mellen's new book "Code War: How Nations Hack, Spy, and Shape the Digital Battlefield" provides an overview of state-sponsored cyber activity and what's ahead. GovTech's cybersecurity columnist recommends it.

**NLC AI Forum**

The National League of Cities launched an AI and Emerging Technology Forum to help cities, towns, and villages understand AI tools available to them.

**Mythos AI Cybersecurity Risk (Editor's Pick)**

A GovTech-highlighted report on Anthropic's Mythos AI notes it will further compress the time between vulnerability discovery and attack, pushing cyber teams to rethink defenses and operational risk.

## Relevance

The edge AI for city services piece is directly relevant to Frederick's GovTech posture and any future smart city infrastructure conversations. The Connecticut criminal report pause is a useful reference point for AI governance discussions. The NLC AI forum is worth checking -- as Council President, it's the right level of engagement for understanding what peer cities are doing with AI.
