## Digital ADA Deadline Extended -- Liability Remains

The federal deadline for local governments to meet digital accessibility standards under the ADA has been pushed back by one year. However, GovTech's coverage makes the key point clearly: the extension does not eliminate liability. Governments can still be sued for inaccessible digital services today, regardless of the compliance deadline.

The technical standard in question is WCAG (Web Content Accessibility Guidelines), which governs how websites and digital tools must work for people with disabilities -- screen reader compatibility, keyboard navigation, color contrast, and similar requirements. Many local government websites remain non-compliant.

The practical implication for Frederick: the extension buys time to remediate, but not to ignore. Any new digital service or tool procured during this window should be evaluated for accessibility compliance as a procurement requirement, not an afterthought.

## Why This Matters for Cesar

Digital accessibility compliance is a governance equity issue, not just a legal compliance box. Residents with disabilities depend on accessible city websites and digital services for basic interactions with local government. As Frederick expands its digital service footprint, accessibility must be a design requirement from the start.
