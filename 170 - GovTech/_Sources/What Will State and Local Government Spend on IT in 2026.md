# What Will State and Local Government Spend on IT in 2026?

## Summary
### Key Points
- **State/local IT spend outlook (2026):** Market projected **>$160B**, but many government IT leaders expect a **tough budget environment**; **AI is being positioned as a lever for efficiency** rather than net-new spend.
- **Cloud security procurement is shifting to shared assessments:** **North Carolina joined GovRAMP**, signaling continued momentum toward **standardized, reusable cloud security authorizations** to reduce duplicative vendor reviews.
- **Permitting is moving to end-to-end digital service delivery:** San Francisco launched **PermitSF** with **online payments**, reflecting a broader move toward **online permitting workflows** (and associated back-office integration).
- **AI skills and adoption support is becoming formalized:** A **federal AI Literacy Framework** was released to help scale **AI education** (relevant for workforce readiness and governance).
- **Infrastructure + governance frictions remain:**  
  - Communities (e.g., Ohio) are **grappling with data center approvals** (zoning, power, water—impacts cloud/AI capacity planning).  
  - A Connecticut town **turned off license plate readers** (illustrates ongoing **public trust/privacy constraints** on sensor + AI programs).
- **Operational resilience continues to matter:** A Connecticut city hall outage highlights persistent **availability/continuity risks** (reinforces need for DR, backups, incident response).

### Tools worth attention
- **GovRAMP** (shared cloud security authorization framework; evolved from StateRAMP): useful for accelerating public-sector vendor approvals and aligning controls/evidence across agencies.
- **OpenGov** (platform used for PermitSF): worth watching as an example of **packaged digital government workflows** (permitting + payments) that require cloud integration, identity, logging, and data governance.
- **Federal AI Literacy Framework** (framework, not software): practical reference for building **training plans, baseline competencies, and adoption governance** for AI in regulated environments.
