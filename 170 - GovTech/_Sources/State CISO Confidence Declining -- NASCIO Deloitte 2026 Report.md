# State CISO Confidence Declining -- NASCIO Deloitte 2026 Report

## Summary

A biennial NASCIO and Deloitte survey of state CISOs found declining confidence across the board, driven by slower funding, loss of federal support, and the added complexity that AI introduces to an already strained security posture.

## Key Points

The 2026 biennial NASCIO/Deloitte state CISO survey found confidence declining across all 50 states. Three drivers were identified: slower funding environments for state IT security budgets, loss of federal support (likely CISA-related given the current federal posture), and AI's dual impact as both a tool and a threat surface.

The Pennsylvania CISO specifically described wanting to be more "outcome-driven" -- a signal that the field is shifting from compliance-focused security posture toward demonstrable risk reduction.

West Virginia passed a new law reaffirming its cybersecurity office, requiring annual agency reviews, and expanding departmental oversight -- a legislative response to the confidence gap.

## Relevance

As city IT administrator, the state CISO landscape is a useful reference point for what municipal IT is likely to face as federal support contracts. The funding decline at the state level is typically followed by pressure on municipalities. If federal CISA grants and technical assistance dry up, Frederick's IT security posture becomes more dependent on what the city funds itself. Worth flagging in any budget conversation about IT modernization.
