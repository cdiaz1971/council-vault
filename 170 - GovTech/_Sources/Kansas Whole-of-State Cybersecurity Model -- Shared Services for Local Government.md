# Kansas Whole-of-State Cybersecurity Model -- Shared Services for Local Government

Kansas enacted two laws giving the state the ability to offer shared IT and cybersecurity services to local governments, school districts, hospitals, and other entities, while also requiring new cyber assessments for state agencies. The model is explicitly whole-of-state: instead of each jurisdiction building independent security capacity, the state acts as a shared services provider for entities that lack the scale to maintain their own.

Separately, Arizona is taking what GovTech calls a "first-in-nation" approach to data readiness, with Chief Data and Analytics Officer Josh Wagner building a framework to assess data quality and maturity across state agencies.

Also noted in this issue: an AI use case from the NASCIO conference where AI operated entirely on the vendor's side without touching government data -- a potential model for privacy-preserving AI deployment in municipal contexts. North Dakota is integrating FAA radar data to support drone operations for emergency response, infrastructure inspection, and agriculture. Anoka County, Minnesota has deployed AI to handle non-emergency calls.

## Why this matters for César

The Kansas whole-of-state model is directly relevant to the municipal technology strategy track. Frederick lacks the IT capacity of larger cities; a state shared-services model could provide a template for county-level or regional cybersecurity cooperation. The AI-without-touching-government-data framing is worth flagging for the data center and AI governance discussions.
