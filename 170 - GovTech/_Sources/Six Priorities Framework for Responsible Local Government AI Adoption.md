# Six Priorities Framework for Responsible Local Government AI Adoption

## Summary
A Queensland University of Technology research team drew on policy analysis, community surveys, and practitioner interviews to conclude that local government AI adoption is outpacing the governance built to manage it. They propose six priorities for closing that gap.

## Key Points
The six priorities are clear accountability (defining where AI can be used, who answers for its use, and when human oversight is required), organizational capability (elected officials, executives, and staff actually understanding AI's capabilities and limits, not just deploying it), data and technical safeguards (governance and privacy protections built in rather than bolted on, to limit bias and other harms), transparency and human review (residents knowing when AI is in use, how it shapes decisions, and how to request human review), community participation (residents getting a real say in how AI is used in the services that affect them, not just notice after the fact), and workforce preparation (investing in staff skills so AI augments rather than replaces the local government workforce).

The framing throughout is that residents hold local governments accountable for outcomes regardless of whether an algorithm made the call, so the governance structure has to be built for that reality rather than treated as an afterthought to the technology rollout.

## Relevance
This is a clean, citable framework to sit alongside the existing GovTech cluster, particularly [[Municipal Technology Strategy]] and [[AI Governance and Public Sector Procurement]]. It gives a six-point checklist that could structure a formal Frederick AI governance policy if that ever moves from watch item to active drafting, and it is a useful citation for the procurement discipline argument already developed in [[AI Translation in Government Requires Procurement Discipline, Not Blanket Adoption]].
