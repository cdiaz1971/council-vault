## Summary

Arizona's Chief Data and Analytics Officer Josh Wagner has built what GovTech calls a "first-in-nation" structured approach to data readiness: a framework for assessing the quality and maturity of data across state agencies. Rather than treating data as an IT asset, the framework treats it as a governance input -- assessing whether agency data is reliable enough to support policy decisions, performance management, and cross-agency collaboration.

The approach is notable for being systematic rather than project-by-project. Arizona is evaluating data maturity across agencies against defined criteria, creating a baseline that enables comparison and accountability over time.

The same GovTech issue flagged two adjacent items worth noting: (1) Virginia's new chief transformation officer comes from White House and Connecticut government backgrounds, signaling the value of federal-to-local career paths; (2) a new AI-backed real-time translation tool for 911 call centers (911 Translate) is offering mostly free access to emergency services -- directly relevant to immigrant communities and language-access equity.

## Why this matters for César

The Arizona model is the kind of municipal technology infrastructure Frederick doesn't have and probably needs. Before AI can meaningfully improve city services, the underlying data has to be trustworthy. A data readiness assessment would surface where Frederick's operational data is reliable versus where decisions are being made on guesswork.

The 911 Translate item is separately significant: real-time AI translation at emergency call centers is a concrete, low-cost equity intervention that directly serves Frederick's immigrant population. This is the kind of GovTech application that could anchor a council ask or pilot proposal.
