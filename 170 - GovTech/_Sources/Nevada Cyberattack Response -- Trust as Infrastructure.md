# Nevada Cyberattack Response -- Trust as Infrastructure

## Summary

At the 2026 NASCIO Midyear Conference in Philadelphia, Nevada CIO Tim Galluzi discussed how the state navigated its 2025 cyber incident. His core finding: the relationships he had built with counterparts across state government before the incident hit were the decisive factor in coordinating a rapid, effective response. Technical systems were necessary but not sufficient. Trust was the actual infrastructure.

## Key Points

The implication for IT leadership is that incident response planning cannot be purely technical. The relationships that enable fast decision-making -- who calls whom, who believes whom, who can authorize what without going through a three-day approval chain -- have to be built during calm periods. They cannot be improvised under pressure.

This maps onto the general principle that organizational resilience is a function of social capital as much as technical redundancy. A well-documented runbook is useless if the people who need to execute it don't trust each other or don't know each other's roles.

A separate story from the same GovTech dispatch: a local IT leader at the CoMotion Miami conference described shutting off workers' internet access entirely until they completed cybersecurity training. Procurement rules and worker resistance to training are the two biggest cybersecurity obstacles for city and county governments according to the tech leaders present.

## Relevance

Directly applicable to Frederick's M365/Azure administration context. As the city's M365/Azure administrator, building the trust relationships with department heads, IT staff, and city leadership before an incident matters as much as the technical configuration. The Nevada case is a concrete argument for investing in those relationships now.
