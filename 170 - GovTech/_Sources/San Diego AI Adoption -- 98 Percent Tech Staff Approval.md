# San Diego AI Adoption -- 98 Percent Tech Staff Approval

## Summary

San Diego CIO Jonathan Behnke reported that despite acknowledged drawbacks -- including some loss of entry-level knowledge-building -- 98% of the city's technology staff say AI saves them time and improves accuracy. Behnke's framing: AI proficiency will be part of every technology employee's job going forward, not an optional add-on.

## Key Points

The 98% figure is notable as a peer-city benchmark. San Diego is a large city with a mature GovTech posture, and this level of staff buy-in suggests that when AI adoption is managed with training and institutional support rather than dropped on staff cold, acceptance is high.

Behnke's language is worth flagging: "it will be part of every technology employee's job moving forward" is a mandate framing, not a pilot framing. That signals San Diego has moved past the experimentation phase into operational expectation-setting.

The acknowledged drawback -- reduced knowledge accumulation at entry levels -- is a real governance risk that is often glossed over in AI adoption enthusiasm. It deserves its own tracking note eventually.

Also in this GovTech issue: Code for America is partnering with Anthropic on a pilot to help caseworkers administer public benefits using AI-powered tools. This is directly relevant to the question of AI in civic services and how it can reduce friction in service delivery without replacing caseworker judgment.

## Relevance

San Diego's experience is usable as a benchmark when making the case for structured AI adoption in Frederick's municipal operations. The data supports a "train and trust" approach rather than either avoidance or ungoverned rollout.

The Code for America + Anthropic partnership is worth monitoring separately -- it represents exactly the kind of civic AI deployment model that bridges GovTech strategy and immigrant/social services work.
