# ADA Web Accessibility Deadline -- One-Year Extension for State and Local Governments

## Summary

The U.S. Department of Justice prefiled an Interim Final Rule one week before the original deadline, giving state and local governments one additional year to bring their websites into ADA digital accessibility compliance.

## Key Points

The DOJ's Interim Final Rule extends the compliance window for state and local governments by one year. The original deadline was imminent -- the extension was announced just days before it would have taken effect. The rule applies specifically to digital accessibility under the ADA, covering government websites and web-based services that must meet accessibility standards (generally WCAG 2.1 Level AA). This is particularly relevant for smaller jurisdictions that cited resource and capacity constraints in meeting the original timeline.

## Relevance

Frederick City has web-facing services and public-facing digital tools that fall under this requirement. As both Council President and the city's M365/IT administrator, this deadline affects César directly in two ways: policy oversight of city compliance posture, and the technical work of ensuring city digital properties meet standards. The one-year extension provides breathing room but does not eliminate the obligation. This is worth surfacing to the city's IT leadership and legal counsel if not already on their radar.
