# Physical AI -- What It Means for Government IT

## Summary

"Physical AI" refers to artificial intelligence embedded in robotics, sensors, and physical systems that operate in the real world rather than purely in software. GovTech's coverage draws on Davos insights and state readiness assessments to frame what this technology wave means for government IT leaders.

## Key Points

Physical AI extends the AI conversation from software and language models into hardware: autonomous vehicles, drones, robotic infrastructure inspection, smart sensors in public spaces. The key questions for government are readiness (do agencies have the procurement frameworks and workforce skills to adopt these systems?), liability (who is responsible when a physical AI system fails?), and governance (what oversight structures apply to AI that acts in the physical world?).

GovTech's columnist Dan Lohrmann frames physical AI as a near-term concern for IT leaders to understand now, before procurement decisions arrive on their desks. State-level readiness varies widely.

## Relevance

Less immediately applicable than the federal AI framework, but useful background for your AI governance thinking. As Council President, physical AI is likely to surface in conversations about smart city infrastructure, public safety technology, and data center permitting. The Flock ALPR work is a current example of physical AI governance questions you are already navigating.
