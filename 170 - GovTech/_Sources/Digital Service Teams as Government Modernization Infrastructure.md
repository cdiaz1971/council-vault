# Digital Service Teams as Government Modernization Infrastructure

## Summary

A new Beeck Center report finds that dedicated digital service teams are becoming essential to governments refreshing services, managing tighter budgets, and keeping residents at the center of digital transformation. The argument is that these teams are not a luxury but load-bearing infrastructure for modernization.

## Key Points

The Beeck Center framing is significant: digital service teams are described as "critical infrastructure," not a nice-to-have. The report focuses on how these teams enable governments to modernize resident-facing services without the overhead of large-scale procurement cycles or enterprise software contracts.

A companion piece profiled Melanie McDonough, Chief Innovation and AI Officer for Lebanon, New Hampshire -- a small city using AI to move faster than larger jurisdictions. The insight: smaller governments have fewer legacy systems and can experiment more quickly. Size that is often treated as a constraint becomes an advantage when agility is the goal.

The Exchange Northwest was also highlighted -- a regional collaboration hub in the Seattle area where civic tech leaders co-design solutions across jurisdictions. The model is relevant: shared infrastructure, shared learning, and collective bargaining power with vendors.

A separate GovTech item noted that Govineer Solutions (recently formed from three merged local gov software companies) received new private equity backing and now serves more than 2,300 local governments across 43 states. This level of consolidation in the local gov software market warrants attention from a procurement and vendor-dependency standpoint.

## Relevance

Frederick has no dedicated digital service team. Most technology work is distributed across departments or handled by external vendors. The Beeck Center argument that this is a structural gap -- not just a staffing gap -- is directly relevant to how César thinks about GovTech strategy for the city. The Lebanon NH example provides a peer-city framing: small is agile if you use it right. The Govineer consolidation is worth watching as a procurement risk.
