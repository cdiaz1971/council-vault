# Nash Ethics Hearing Due Process Gaps

## Summary
Cathy Anderson's account, based on the ethics file Nash shared with Council, lists fair-process failures in the Commission's handling of the complaint against Nash, and calls out Hempel Irani, Shackelford, and Adkins for demanding removal on the strength of that process.

## Confidentiality
The underlying ethics file was shared with Council by Nash. This note records Anderson's characterization of process only, not the substance of the complaint. Treat as internal context; verify against the file before citing anything publicly.

## Key Points
Anderson reports the Commission took six months to reach a $1,000 fine, at a cost above $26,000. Her list of process failures as they affected Nash: rules that dropped the accused's right to present evidence and cross-examine; one lawyer acting as investigator, adviser, and hearing gatekeeper; Nash never shown the complaint, the initial determination, or the preliminary investigation details, with charges read aloud only at the hearing; no fast route to records; her chosen representatives, her husband and Cesar, sent out of the room under an unwritten "attorney only" rule; questioning on evidence she had not seen, followed by a finding that she was not credible; no recording allowed; two binders of evidence delivered after she testified, with two weeks to respond; and no appeal.

On the Commission side she lists rules with no steps for referral, investigation, or reporting after a 2024 revision; rules described as Council-adopted but bearing only the chair's signature; no showing that the complaint was sworn as the ordinance requires; no recusal rule for commissioners; unsworn testimony; commissioners ruling on their own conflicts; no screening of weak allegations before investigation; no record of the initial determination or the vote to investigate; and no minutes, agendas, or votes for any meeting in the process.

She directs pointed questions to three members. To Hempel Irani: why call for a colleague to step down based on a process she knows is flawed and has power to fix. To Shackelford: why call Nash "proven to be unethical" from findings produced without basic process, and a reminder that the Artspiration matter is the Commission's to investigate, not Nash's. To Adkins: why draft a removal charter change before reforming the ethics process, and who would decide severity, appeals, and whether it too runs through the Mayor's office. She says she is not "Team Nash" and that ethics reform is her priority. A full report is promised.

## Relevance
Cesar was personally present and excluded from the hearing, which makes him a witness to at least one of these claims. The split this exposes (Hempel Irani and Shackelford against Nash, Adkins moving on removal) is the same crack in the governing coalition noted in the Council context file. Anderson's framing offers a way through that does not require taking a side on Nash: process reform first, before any removal mechanism.
