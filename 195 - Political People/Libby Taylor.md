## Political Profile

Taylor is a first-term At-Large council member and the author of the LMC modernization proposal before Government Operations Committee. She won her seat by defeating three Democratic incumbents and a former mayor in the September 2025 primary -- a notable electoral achievement for a first-time candidate with no prior elective experience. Her primary victory over established names like Kelly Russell, Donna Kuzemchak, and Jennifer Dougherty signals genuine grassroots organizing capacity and a constituency hungry for a generational change in how the council approaches development and land use.

Her ideological lane is center-progressive Democrat. Her professional background as a Medicaid policy analyst shapes her approach in a specific way: she is policy-implementation focused, not just advocacy-oriented. She frames every issue through a "what does this actually produce for residents" lens. Her public statements consistently push back on growth for its own sake and demand that development policy be evaluated against quality-of-life outcomes. She is not hostile to development -- she explicitly acknowledged during the campaign that developers are playing by the rules the city sets, making the LMC itself the reform target rather than individual developers.

Her LMC proposal is the most significant legislative initiative she has taken since taking office, and it reflects both her campaign platform and her governance instincts. She named LMC modernization as the underappreciated tool in the housing affordability conversation at the time of her inauguration, before any formal proposal was drafted. Following through with an actual proposal within months of taking office signals she came in with a defined agenda and the organizational capacity to move it.

Her power base is rooted in neighborhood-level organizing, the NAC network, and young-family constituencies. She does not appear to have ties to the real estate development industry, labor unions, or traditional Democratic machine infrastructure. She ran on ActBlue, raised small-dollar, and her largest institutional donor in the final report was St. John Properties -- a flag worth monitoring (see Vulnerabilities).

Her relationship to your priorities is strong on housing and LMC. Her position on the City Values Act and immigration enforcement has not been confirmed in research but given her broader platform framing around inclusive communities, a yes vote on the CVA is the expected baseline. Confirm the April 2 vote.

## Vulnerabilities and Controversies

### St. John Properties Donation -- MONITOR
St. John Properties is a major mid-Atlantic commercial real estate developer with active and ongoing interests in Frederick. A $500 contribution in the final pre-election reporting period is notable for a small-dollar campaign. It does not establish a conflict, but any vote Taylor takes on matters directly affecting St. John's Frederick projects warrants scrutiny. The LMC modernization proposal is the most likely intersection point.

### No Documented Controversies -- LOW concern
No ethics complaints, negative press, or controversial prior decisions identified. Her prior public role (NAC committee chair) generated no documented controversy. She is four months into her first term with a clean record.

### Primary Win Over Incumbents -- Context, Not Vulnerability
Her defeat of three incumbents and a former mayor in the Democratic primary could generate lingering resentment from establishment figures (particularly Dougherty, Russell, and Kuzemchak networks) that surfaces in future cycles. Not an immediate concern but worth tracking if any of those figures re-enter Frederick politics.
