## Political Profile

Adkins is the sole Republican on the Frederick City Council and arrived via appointment rather than election, having lost the November 2025 general election to Scott Lasher by a nearly 40-point margin. He described his acceptance of the seat as "bittersweet." His political identity is genuinely unusual: a lifelong Republican who openly contemplated running as a Democrat or independent, and who self-describes as a moderate. He has no apparent ties to national Republican infrastructure or ideological networks.

His policy worldview is shaped almost entirely by his 35-year career as a city planner. He approaches governance through a technical, process-oriented lens -- fiscal responsibility, core city services, neighborhood identity, and infrastructure management. He raised federal and state funding cuts as a top concern during the campaign, consistent with your council's own HHS-related budget risk flags. His housing position is supply-side but not progressive: he supports production and better developer contribution structures (transportation impact fees), but there is no evidence he supports renter protections, affordability mandates beyond current MPDU levels, or aggressive inclusionary requirements.

His geographic equity focus is real and worth taking seriously. District 4 covers the northeastern part of the city -- neighborhoods that have historically felt sidelined relative to downtown investment. His campaign explicitly named neighborhood identity and equity of infrastructure investment outside the downtown core as priorities. This is a lever he has already used in budget work sessions and it will recur.

On immigration, there is no documented public position. He did not campaign on the issue. His party affiliation is the only signal, and he has actively distanced himself from conventional Republican positioning. His vote on the City Values Act is not yet confirmed in research -- this should be verified.

He brings deep institutional knowledge of the city's planning and development apparatus. Staff know him, and he knows how the government works at a level that exceeds any other council member's pre-office experience. This is a resource and a risk: he may be a useful technical ally on LMC and housing production questions, but he is also capable of using process expertise to slow or complicate items he opposes.

## Vulnerabilities and Controversies

### Republican on All-Democratic Council -- MEDIUM political significance
Adkins is the only Republican on the council. He did not win his seat -- he lost the election and was appointed under a charter succession rule after the winner resigned. His electoral mandate is thin: 1,375 votes, 30.3% of the district. Any progressive legislation he opposes can be framed against his lack of an electoral mandate and his party affiliation. Conversely, his "moderate" positioning and genuine cross-party appeal (Donna Kuzemchak donated to his campaign) makes him harder to isolate than a conventional Republican would be.

### Ausherman Developer Donation -- MONITOR
Marvin Ausherman is among Frederick's most prominent real estate and development interests. A $500 contribution to Adkins' campaign is a notable signal. Any vote Adkins takes on development, zoning, LMC, or housing production items involving Ausherman-affiliated properties or projects should be flagged for conflict of interest scrutiny.

### No Documented Controversies -- LOW concern currently
No ethics complaints, negative press coverage, or controversial prior decisions identified in research. His 35-year city planning record is publicly praised, including by Mayor O'Connor. The record is clean as far as available sources show.
