## Political Profile

Ramos occupies the progressive-left lane of Baltimore Democratic politics. She is ideologically consistent, media-savvy, and legislatively active. Her public persona is direct and community-rooted. On X she has described herself as "unapologetically pro-choice and pro-same sex marriage" and noted she "drinks a little and curses a lot." This is authentic personal brand, not calculated moderation -- it reflects genuine ideological confidence and could read as arrogance or alienating in less blue contexts.

**Key ideological commitments:** Affordable housing, anti-vacancy, tenant protection, anti-predatory tax sale, immigrant rights, government accountability, police accountability, water equity, anti-surveillance (led termination of city's spy plane contract unanimously).

**Power base:** Progressive nonprofit sector, housing advocacy ecosystem, Working Families Party infrastructure, CASA in Action (immigrant rights), JUFJ Campaign Fund (Jewish social justice), labor (IAFF Local 964 firefighters).

**Mentors and political parents:** Mary Pat Clarke (predecessor; mentored Ramos for decades), Del. Maggie McIntosh (House Appropriations chair; mutual campaign relationship going back to 1995).

## Legislative Record

Ranked sixth-most-active bill sponsor on the Baltimore City Council over her first term (2020--2024).

- **122 ordinances sponsored:** 54 enacted, 15 withdrawn/vetoed, 53 in progress
- **123 resolutions sponsored:** 62 adopted or enacted

Key enacted legislation:
- **Inclusionary housing reform** -- overhauled the expiring inclusionary housing law to require affordable units in new mixed-income developments
- **Monthly property tax installment plan** -- allows older adults and property heirs without mortgages to pay in monthly installments
- **Inspector General Advisory Board reform** -- Charter Amendment removing elected officials from the OIG oversight board; replaced with randomly selected fraud/accounting professionals and council nominees
- **Short-term rental assistance** -- created program for residents in violence prevention programs or seeking work
- **In rem foreclosure expansion** -- working to accelerate city acquisition of vacant properties where liens exceed property value; block-by-block approach
- **TPS resolution** -- urged Biden administration to re-designate El Salvador, Nicaragua, Honduras, Nepal, Cameroon for Temporary Protected Status and support TPS for Guatemala
- **Residential parking permit areas** -- multiple neighborhood-level bills
- **Historic landmark designation** -- 3818 Roland Avenue
- **School renaming** -- renamed school after constituent Gertrude S. Williams

## Vulnerabilities and Controversies

### 1. The Johns Hopkins Shakedown (2024) -- PRIMARY VULNERABILITY

In April 2024, Ramos sent Johns Hopkins University a letter explicitly pairing threatened zoning legislation with a demand list exceeding $100 million in commitments. The list included: $20M/year for five years to the Affordable Housing Trust Fund, $10M for the Waverly neighborhood, $10M for a city land bank, a dog park near Stony Run, and various other demands unrelated to the university's proposed Data Science and AI Institute.

Hopkins' VP for government partnerships called the letter "unexpected" and "shocking" in writing, noting the university had consulted an attorney. Ramos revoked the letter on May 7 after receiving no response, then stated she would introduce the threatened legislation anyway.

Multiple opinion pieces and commentaries framed the episode as extortion. Common Cause Maryland offered a more neutral read -- calling it not ideal but common deal-making that usually happens behind the scenes.

Ramos defended the intent while acknowledging she might approach such situations "differently" in the future. She continued publicly opposing the AI center as recently as December 2025.

**Opposition research value: High.** Paper trail exists: letter on official letterhead, university response emails, attorney consultations on both sides, and media coverage from Baltimore Banner, Baltimore Sun, and FOX45. The written phrase "commitments are needed on each item to avoid introduction of the ordinance" is direct and quotable.

### 2. Student Housing Height Controversy

Ramos supported allowing a developer to build to 80 feet in Charles Village, exceeding the community's standard 68-foot limit. Neighbors adjacent to the project criticized the decision as prioritizing developer preferences over the neighborhood's Victorian rowhouse character. A community association leader defended the process, but the optics of a housing-advocate councilmember overriding neighborhood height limits for a university-adjacent student housing project are mixed.

### 3. Council Downsizing Opposition

Ramos actively opposed the November 2024 ballot measure to reduce the City Council from 15 to 9 members. She framed it as protecting marginalized communities. The measure was funded by David Smith, co-owner of the Baltimore Sun. While her stated rationale is consistent with her values, the position also protects her own seat -- a straightforward self-interest read exists.

### 4. Residency History

Ran for the 12th District council seat in 2011. Now represents the 14th District. Current residence is only a few blocks from the 12th District boundary. Not an active vulnerability but useful context if residency questions ever resurface.
