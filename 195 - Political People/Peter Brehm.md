## Political Profile

Brehm sits in the progressive-moderate lane of Frederick Democratic politics. His public persona is built around community service depth rather than ideological distinctiveness -- years of nonprofit board work and neighborhood advisory council involvement are the foundation of his political credibility. He is widely described as collaborative and detail-oriented, and endorsers emphasize his follow-through rather than his vision.

His three stated priorities -- expanding affordable housing, managing development, and operating the city sustainably -- align with the administration's consensus agenda. He does not appear to have staked out positions to the left of that consensus, which makes him a reliable vote on most items before the Council but not a driver of ambitious policy.

On immigration, Brehm is broadly supportive. During the 2025 campaign he affirmed his support for Frederick Police Department's non-inquiry policy on immigration status, endorsed the City Values Act philosophy, and described demonizing undocumented immigrants as "completely inappropriate." However, he also indicated openness to revisiting the noncitizen voting charter amendment (Resolution 24-24) in a public forum, citing constituent concern -- a notable hedge given the political climate. This is a point of divergence from César's position that warrants monitoring.

On elections, Brehm has taken the lead within the Government Operations Committee on examining alternative voting methods, including ranked-choice voting and approval voting. As of February 2026 he was actively convening sessions on potential changes to city election structure -- an independent initiative within his committee work.

His LGBTQ+ identity and organizational ties (Frederick Center founding treasurer, PFLAG Frederick, current member of Mayor's Affordable Housing Task Force) give him a distinct constituency base in the downtown district.

## Vulnerabilities and Controversies

### Noncitizen Voting Hedge -- LOW value as controversy, but politically relevant
During the August 2025 primary campaign, Brehm publicly indicated willingness to "revisit" Resolution 24-24 (the noncitizen voting charter amendment) in a public forum, citing constituent concern. He stopped well short of opposing the policy but created ambiguity. Given that the 64th Administration voted unanimously in favor of the City Values Act, this earlier hedge stands as a mild inconsistency. It is not a disqualifying controversy but worth noting as a signal of his attentiveness to district-level political pressure on immigration-adjacent issues.

No ethics complaints, investigations, or documented constituent service failures found.
