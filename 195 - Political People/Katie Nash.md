## Political Profile

Nash's ideological evolution is the most politically notable feature of her background. She ran for alderman as a Republican in 2013, losing that race. By 2021, she ran as a Democrat and won with more votes than any other aldermanic candidate, landing her the President Pro Tem role. She self-identifies publicly as a progressive, though critics from the left have questioned that framing. Her X bio reads: "Progressive homeschooling Mama, Jesus follower, professional advocate, business maven."

Her policy positioning is center-left on most issues, with a notable tension between pro-growth rhetoric and development skepticism in practice. She proposed targeted development moratoria in areas undergoing rezoning -- framed as responsible growth -- which developers and some growth advocates opposed. She has been vocal on police resourcing, supporting officers having needed tools, which puts her slightly right of the progressive wing on public safety. She championed pedestrian safety, housing, access to technology, and climate resilience during her time as Council President.

She appears to have had a productive working relationship with Mayor O'Connor in most respects, though she and Kuzemchak broke with the Mayor on the downtown hotel project state funding request, which went through the Mayor's office without the board's approval. She reportedly floated a mayoral run in November 2024 at a private event, before backing off and running for an at-large council seat instead -- and ultimately winning District 1. Colleagues MacShane and Kuzemchak both described her publicly as a good colleague.

Her faith identity (Episcopalian, Jesus-follower self-description) is present but not prominently ideological in her public record.

## Legislative Record

Nash carried forward the institutional transition from the Board of Aldermen structure to the new City Council structure, including the charter amendments approved in September 2024 that renamed the body, created district seats, instituted term limits (3 terms for council, 2 for mayor), and formalized the council as an independent legislative branch.

Key legislative and policy actions:
- Proposed targeted development moratoria for areas designated for rezoning (October 2023) to ensure development aligns with infrastructure capacity
- Co-seconded or supported Ordinance G-23-21 amending the City Ethics Ordinance (November 2023)
- Advocated for SB 0385 at Maryland General Assembly -- supporting favorable vote related to FCPS student enrollment funding
- Opposed the Mayor's unilateral outreach to the state requesting $15 million for the downtown hotel project, without full board endorsement
- Championed pedestrian safety infrastructure projects across city neighborhoods
- Advocated for performance-based budgeting and neighborhood-level decision-making
- Donated over $1,400 to Howard County Executive candidate Liz Hagen's campaign (2022)

## Vulnerabilities and Controversies

### Ethics Violation -- Lobbying While in Office -- MEDIUM value

In September 2022, the City of Frederick Ethics Commission found that Nash violated the city's ethics ordinance by using her position as Alderwoman to advance the interests of a lobbying client -- the International Association of Firefighters Local 3666 -- while being paid as their registered lobbyist. Specifically, she emailed Neighborhood Advisory Council (NAC) coordinators encouraging them to raise concerns with the county about the relocation of an Advanced Life Support unit from Montevue Lane to the new Northgate Fire Station. The commission found that NAC coordinators would give weight to her communications as an Alderman, and that she exploited that credibility to benefit a paying client.

The commission did not fine her. Nash read an emotional statement acknowledging the finding and expressed that she believed she had acted within prior ethics guidance. County Executive Jan Gardner (D) filed the complaint in a personal capacity. The commission had actually issued Nash an advisory opinion the day after her election (November 3, 2021) warning that serving simultaneously as an elected official and registered lobbyist "has the potential to raise several concerns." She proceeded anyway.

**Opposition research value:** Documented. Written commission opinion exists (public record). The narrative is: Nash received explicit advance warning, ignored it, and later called the finding a political attack. Her own statement called it "devastating" -- so it clearly landed. The fact that her lobbying client's firefighters union subsequently endorsed a Republican candidate for County Executive adds a cross-partisan conflict angle. Community comments at the time called for resignation.

**Important caveat for ally tracking:** This appears to have had limited lasting political damage. She won re-election in November 2025. But the ethics finding is on record and remains relevant context.

### Mayoral Trial Balloon -- LOW-MEDIUM value

In November 2024, Nash reportedly told attendees at a private event that she was running for mayor. Two separate attendees corroborated this account to the Frederick News-Post. Within weeks, she reversed course and announced she would seek an at-large council seat instead, citing wanting to continue good work "where I am." The reversal was framed as pragmatic given O'Connor's incumbency and strength. The episode signals some level of mayoral ambition that may re-emerge in 2029 when O'Connor terms out.

### Party Affiliation Switch -- LOW value

Nash ran as a Republican in 2013 and lost. By 2021 she was a Democrat, winning with the highest vote total. Community letter-writers noted this switch at the time of the 2022 ethics controversy, arguing she does not genuinely fit a progressive identity. For ally purposes: this is background context, not an active vulnerability. Worth knowing.

### Downtown Hotel Dispute with Mayor's Office -- LOW value

Nash and Kuzemchak broke publicly with Mayor O'Connor over his unilateral outreach to the state for $15 million in hotel funding without board approval. Nash said she was not against the project but objected to how the request was made. This reveals a pattern of Nash asserting the legislative branch's independence from the executive -- relevant to how she may interact with you as Council President.
