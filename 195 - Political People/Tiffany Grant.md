## Political Profile

Grant is a progressive-leaning Democrat running her first electoral campaign. Her ideological lane centers on community accountability, equitable access to services, housing affordability, and opposition to growth-without-consent -- particularly the December 2023 County Council vote to approve the 2,615-acre Critical Digital Infrastructure Overlay Zone (CDI-OZ) near Adamstown, which she has called a failure of community process.

Her power base is a coalition of anti-data-center residents, progressive organizations, LGBTQ+ community members, young voters, and immigrant-adjacent advocacy circles. She was an early signer of the Frederick County Data Center Referendum Committee petition, which gathered 22,169 signatures, and she has been closely identified with that movement throughout her campaign.

Her communication style is accessible and personal-narrative-forward: she leans heavily on her own story of housing instability, military family background, and lived experience as an ALICE household member. She frames all five of her priority areas through the lens of whether systems actually work for working people, not just on paper.

She ran as a challenger against two Democratic incumbents (Brad Young and Renee Knapp) and won the June 23 primary leading the field with 8,215 votes (unofficial Election Day results, with mail-in ballots still pending). She advances to the November 3 general election against Republicans Pamela Ciliberti and Jack Felton, both of whom ran uncontested in the Republican primary.

## Vulnerabilities and Controversies

### No Prior Legislative Record -- LOW value for opposition, asset for Grant
Grant has no voting record to attack. Critics (notably the MacRo Commercial Real Estate commentary, June 2, 2026) argued she and Newhouse focused on spending priorities without articulating a clear revenue strategy for Frederick County's future. This is a line of attack available to Republicans in the general. She should expect to be pressed on fiscal specifics -- particularly how she funds housing, transit, and educator pay without new revenue sources.

### Employer Non-Disclosure -- LOW value
She declined to name her current employer in her April 2026 News-Post interview, citing a desire to keep the campaign separate. Not a disqualifying issue, but could be probed if the employer later becomes relevant (e.g., regulated industry with county business).

### ALICE Household Status -- framing risk, not a vulnerability
She voluntarily identifies as a member of an ALICE household. Republicans could try to frame this as instability; Democrats and progressives are unlikely to see it negatively. Not a real vulnerability -- her biography is her strongest asset.
