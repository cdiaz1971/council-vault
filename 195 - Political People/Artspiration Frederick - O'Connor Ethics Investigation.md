# Artspiration Frederick - O'Connor Ethics Investigation

## Summary

Document review of Artspiration Frederick funding and support requests reveals a sustained pattern of city staff engagement initiated by the Mayor's personal referral to benefit his wife's nonprofit, raising probable prestige-of-office ethics violations under City Code Chapter 21.

## Organization Overview

Artspiration Frederick is a 501(c)(3) nonprofit arts organization co-founded by **Tammy O'Connor** (wife of Mayor Michael O'Connor) and **Natasha Valencia**, operating at 915 Toll House Ave., Suite 101, Frederick, MD 21701. It opened in late 2025 and is now seeking a $2 million Maryland state legislative bond grant for Phases III and IV.

Key contacts:
- Tammy O'Connor: toc21702@gmail.com, 301-667-4282
- Natasha Valencia: 240-409-7421
- External grant writer: Karin (contact@karinabma.com)
- State bond sponsors: Senator Lewis Young and Delegate Simpson

## The Mayor's Direct Role

The relationship between the Mayor's office and Artspiration predates the organization's opening. In **December 2024**, Tammy emailed city building official Dustin Nusbaum stating the Mayor personally referred her. That referral opened sustained access to city staff across multiple departments.

In **March 2026**, Tammy emailed the Mayor's official city address (moconnor@cityoffrederickmd.gov) requesting a same-day letter of support, copying Kara Hemphill and Gayon Sampson. This routing of a support request through the Mayor's own city email is the most direct documented instance of the relationship between his office and the organization.

## City Staff Engagement Pattern

Documented contacts across at least five city departments:

**Dustin Nusbaum, Building Official** (Dnusbaum@cityoffrederickmd.gov, 301-600-3801): Conducted walkthroughs at 915 Toll House Ave. after the Mayor's referral. Also consulted on a second potential site at 900 East Patrick St. and provided electrical blueprint guidance for the ceramics studio buildout in Suite 211.

**Laila Abdul-Hadi Jadallah, Manager of Arts and Culture, Mayor's Office** (ljadallah@cityoffrederickmd.gov): The most extensively documented relationship. The two were already exchanging personal emails in **February 2025** while Artspiration was still searching for a space. Laila proactively added Artspiration to the city's Cultural Asset Map after the October 2025 ribbon cutting, provided cultural sensitivity guidance on exhibition naming, consulted Tammy on official city gifts for incoming council members (including César), and met with Tammy socially at Artspiration -- in one email offering to bring carryout from The Orchard on her way. A December 2025 email from Laila congratulates Tammy on "stellar election results."

**Mary Ford-Naill, Economic Development** (MNAILL@cityoffrederickmd.gov, 301-600-6362): Navigated Artspiration through the city's GROW grant program (maximum $10,000) in December 2025, confirmed eligible expenses, and arranged a meeting with Artspiration's external grant writer.

**Shana Knight, Community Partnerships, Office of Public Affairs** (sknight@cityoffrederickmd.gov): Deployed to facilitate an AARP Maryland partnership with Artspiration, coordinating meeting scheduling across multiple months.

**Stephanie Chaiken, Weinberg Center** (schaiken@cityoffrederickmd.gov): Invited to and accepted Artspiration events. Forwarded the invitation to a personal Gmail account (sceusa@gmail.com). Declined the Mosaic Pillar sponsorship request ($8,000) but offered auction vouchers.

## Ethics Analysis

**City of Frederick Ethics Ordinance, Chapter 21, Section 21-4(f) -- Prestige of Office:**

"An official or employee may not intentionally use the prestige of office or public position for the private gain of that official or employee or the private gain of another."

The ordinance specifically prohibits "using, or attempting to use, the official's or employee's official position improperly to unreasonably request, grant, or obtain any unwarranted privileges, advantages, benefits or exemptions for the official or employee or another person."

The Ethics Commission applied this standard against Nash in 2022 for conduct that was more indirect than what is documented here. The Mayor's direct referral by name to city staff is the clearest applicable fact.

**Section 21-4(b) -- Restrictions on Participation:** Officials may not participate in any matter in which a qualified relative has an interest. If the Mayor played any role in directing, approving, or facilitating city staff engagement with Artspiration, this is a direct participation violation.

**Section 21-4(c) -- Financial Interest:** The state bond application lists the Mayor's home address as the grantee address, warranting inquiry into whether he has a financial interest in the organization's success.

## Political Context

This investigation comes in the context of active political conflict between Nash and O'Connor. Nash filed a complaint against O'Connor in March 2026 alleging he weaponized the Ethics Commission against her. The Ethics Commission found Nash in violation on May 11, 2026 (three counts, $1,000 fine, cease-and-desist on data center and energy matters). Any action on the Artspiration matter will be read through the lens of that ongoing conflict.

The Artspiration matter is analytically distinct from the Nash dispute. It should be treated as its own question about the use of public office for private benefit, not as a counter-move in the Nash conflict.
