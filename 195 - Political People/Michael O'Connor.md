**Conflict of interest flag -- ACTIVE:**

No formal ethics complaint or finding on this matter has been identified, but the structural conflict is documented and ongoing.

## Political Profile

O'Connor is a moderate-progressive Democrat who has governed Frederick from a centrist-liberal lane, emphasizing community investment, immigrant inclusion, and quality-of-life infrastructure while consistently claiming fiscal restraint ("no tax increases since 2017"). His public persona is methodical and measured -- a media professional who knows how to frame narratives and rarely makes unforced errors on camera. He has cultivated a competent-manager image that insulates him from critique in normal cycles.

His progressive commitments have become increasingly assertive in his second and third terms: noncitizen voting rights (2024), a proposed taxpayer-funded immigrant legal defense fund (November 2024), direct confrontation with newly elected conservative school board members (November 2024), and budget vetoes to protect progressive spending against council-passed cuts (June 2025). He is a Democrat operating in an increasingly contested medium-density suburb and appears to be leaning into the national progressive-vs.-MAGA frame to animate his coalition, rather than tacking toward the center.

O'Connor has strong institutional stature beyond Frederick: he served as MML President (2024-2025), sits on the Metropolitan Washington Council of Governments Board, and was elected to the National League of Cities Human Development Federal Advocacy Committee. He has a personal relationship with Governor Wes Moore, who endorsed him for his third term. This institutional network makes him harder to isolate and positions him as a state-level player, not just a local mayor.

His relationship with the new (64th) City Council is untested and is already showing friction. He vetoed the FY26 budget passed by the prior council. The new council -- of which Cesar Diaz is President -- enters the term with shared housing priorities but is already navigating a live political conflict: O'Connor has been named in a counter-ethics complaint by Council Member Katie Nash (District 1), who alleges he weaponized the city Ethics Commission against her as a political opponent.

## Vulnerabilities and Controversies

### 1. Nash Counter-Ethics Complaint Against O'Connor -- HIGH value (LIVE as of March 2026)
This is the most significant active controversy. Council Member Katie Nash (District 1), herself the subject of a city Ethics Commission complaint over data center lobbying conflicts, has filed a counter-complaint against Mayor O'Connor, alleging he weaponized the Ethics Commission against her as a political opponent. Reported by the Frederick News-Post within the 48 hours prior to March 20, 2026.

The specific allegations against O'Connor: (a) he approved via email the hiring of outside attorney Leslie Powell to investigate Nash before it was clear the city attorney had authorized that hire -- the FNP obtained and published this email; (b) the city has not investigated prior ethics complaints filed by residents against O'Connor himself; and (c) Nash's documented political opposition to O'Connor is being used as context in the complaint filed against her. Nash describes herself in the counter-complaint as being in political "opposition" to O'Connor and reportedly considered challenging him for mayor in 2025 before choosing not to.

O'Connor said he was unaware Nash had filed a complaint against him and was content to let the process play out independently. His email approving Powell's retention remains on the record. Nash has a hearing set for March 24, 2026.

*Opposition research value:* HIGH. Live, named, documented allegation with a published paper trail that the mayor used a quasi-judicial body against a political opponent on the council. This is an active story in local press. The prior complaints against O'Connor referenced in Nash's filing are an urgent follow-up target.

### 2. Previous Resident Ethics Complaints Against O'Connor -- UNKNOWN VALUE
Nash's counter-complaint explicitly references prior ethics complaints filed by city residents against O'Connor that were allegedly not investigated. No detail on the nature or number of those complaints has been confirmed in available sources.

*Opposition research value:* Unknown but potentially high. Need to obtain city Ethics Commission records or follow FNP coverage of this specific thread. This is the highest-priority new intelligence gap to close.

### 3. Immigrant Legal Defense Fund -- HIGH value (political)
In November 2024, immediately after Trump's election, O'Connor held a press conference pledging to use city taxpayer funds to create a "Legal Advocacy Fund" for immigrants facing deportation. He also announced Frederick Police would not question immigration status. The move drew national right-wing attention (Fox News, FAIR, Libs of TikTok). Frederick County Sheriff Chuck Jenkins publicly condemned it. O'Connor included the fund in his proposed FY26 budget; the prior council cut it 3-2; he vetoed the budget and restored it.

*Opposition research value:* High for center-right audiences and for progressive critics who see it as performative. The routing mechanism (through "community partners") lacks specifics. Worth pressuring on: how much, to whom, with what accountability metrics.

### 4. Noncitizen Voting -- HIGH value (contextual)
In September 2024, the prior Board of Aldermen passed a charter amendment extending local voting rights to noncitizens -- estimated 6,400 people in a city of ~86,000. O'Connor supported and defended the move publicly. FAIR and conservative media characterized it as manufactured political dependency. For Cesar specifically: the new council inherited a policy it did not create, which creates room to distinguish positions.

### 5. FY26 Budget Veto and Council Friction -- MEDIUM value
O'Connor vetoed a 3-2 council-passed budget because it cut $3M from a $238M budget. He framed it as protecting services; critics framed it as protecting signature political spending. Council members warned his budget relied on surplus and unfunded positions. He ultimately signed the tax cut into law after opposing it. The pattern -- vetoing council-passed budgets to protect mayoral priorities -- is likely to recur with the new council.

### 6. MPDU Developer Fee-In-Lieu -- MEDIUM value (housing vulnerability)
Developers can pay fees instead of building moderately priced dwelling units (MPDUs). O'Connor acknowledged most developers choose to pay the fee rather than build. His 2025 primary opponent Ron Beattie said the system is "working backwards against the people." The fee pool has reportedly grown to approximately $6M with limited production to show. This is a structural accountability gap directly relevant to Cesar's Housing Production Target Resolution.

### 7. Tammy O'Connor / Arts Sector Conflict of Interest -- LOW-MEDIUM value (watch)
Tammy O'Connor serves as Development Director at the Frederick Arts Council, a city-funded nonprofit, and co-founded Artspiration Frederick in 2025. A 2012 city meeting record documents Alderman O'Connor formally abstaining from a FAC vote "due to a conflict of interest" -- establishing prior acknowledged conflict. No formal complaint or finding on the mayor's current relationship with city arts funding has been identified. If any future city grants go to FAC or Artspiration, this immediately escalates.

### 8. School Board Remarks -- LOW-MEDIUM value
In November 2024, O'Connor publicly criticized newly elected conservative school board members Jaime Kiersten Brennan and Colt Morningstar Black. Both fired back. Critics characterized it as a municipal mayor weaponizing the mayor's office to weigh in on a county-level elected body -- which has some resonance in light of the Nash counter-complaint alleging similar institutional overreach.

### 9. Cricket Stadium -- LOW value
A proposed 25,000-seat cricket stadium near I-70 and the Monocacy River was criticized by all three 2025 mayoral candidates as poorly sited, with inadequate infrastructure. O'Connor defended the concept but acknowledged site concerns. Useful as a symbol of large aspirational projects without sufficient community buy-in.
