# Spanish Speaking Community of Maryland

## Summary

The Spanish Speaking Community of Maryland, Inc. (SSCM) is a 501(c)(3) nonprofit founded in 1971 that serves low-income and limited English proficiency residents across Maryland, with offices in Frederick and Silver Spring. Its mission is to empower immigrants and low-income families by promoting self-sufficiency, social change, education, and legal rights. SSCM is nationally accredited by the Department of Justice to provide immigration legal services and has over 50 years of experience serving the Maryland immigrant community.

## Programs and Services

**Immigration Support Services** -- DOJ-accredited legal immigration assistance. Case managers assist with applications for citizenship, permanent residence, employment authorization, and document translation. No referral or fee required; donations accepted.

**Community Development** -- Outreach and capacity-building initiatives aimed at systemic barrier removal for immigrant and LEP families.

**Housing / Tenant-Landlord Intervention** -- Language access support for navigating leases, resolving dangerous living conditions (including mold cases), and understanding tenant rights.

**Healthcare Navigation** -- Connects low-income clients with local healthcare organizations to address medical needs and promote self-sufficiency.

**Education and Outreach** -- Community presentations, workshops, and educational programs on legal rights, domestic violence, and community resources.

**Domestic Violence Services** -- In partnership with Frederick County Public Schools, SSCM provides confidential services to victims of domestic abuse, including case management and community education. In the most recent reporting period, services were provided to over 224 victims and over 823 individuals were reached through community presentations on domestic violence.

## Organizational Finances

SSCM operates on a roughly $650k annual budget, funded primarily through contributions and program service fees. The organization has maintained positive net assets throughout the reporting period, though recent years show tighter margins.

| Fiscal Year | Revenue | Expenses | Net Income | Net Assets |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| FY2024 (Sept) | $654,561 | $725,801 | -$71,240 | $532,027 |
| FY2023 (Sept) | $651,932 | $595,387 | +$56,545 | $603,267 |
| FY2022 (Sept) | $578,142 | $569,673 | +$8,469 | $546,722 |
| FY2021 (Sept) | $677,137 | $515,335 | +$161,802 | $538,253 |
| FY2020 (Sept) | $508,200 | $591,001 | -$82,801 | $376,613 |

**Revenue mix (FY2024):** Contributions 58.6% ($383,512) -- Program service fees 31.3% ($204,916) -- Net fundraising 3.7% ($24,423) -- Other 5.1% ($33,596) -- Investment income 1.2% ($8,114)

**Expense mix (FY2024):** Executive compensation 23.6% ($171,454) -- Other salaries and wages 36.5% ($264,686) -- remaining expenses account for the balance. Personnel costs represent approximately 60% of total expenses, consistent across all reported years.

**Key observations:** The FY2021 surplus of $161k likely reflects pandemic-era relief funding. Net assets peaked in FY2023 at $603k and declined to $532k following the FY2024 deficit. The FY2024 deficit of $71k against a $532k asset base is worth monitoring but not immediately alarming. The organization has run deficits in two of the five most recent fiscal years (FY2020, FY2024). Maria Herrera's salary has remained consistently near $99,800 across all reported years.

**Charity Navigator rating:** 3 out of 4 stars (81%), based on Accountability and Finance metrics. EIN: 52-0889386.

## Why This Matters for César

SSCM is a direct peer and complementary organization to [[Centro Hispano de Frederick]], covering overlapping service populations with a longer institutional history and a presence in both Frederick and Montgomery/Prince George's counties. Their Frederick office on South Jefferson Street places them squarely within the City's immigrant-serving nonprofit ecosystem. SSCM's DOJ-accredited immigration legal services, domestic violence work, and tenant-landlord intervention programs are all highly relevant to César's policy priorities around the [[Municipal ID]], the [[City Values Act]], and language access. Any council work on immigrant services, housing tenant rights, or community investment should consider SSCM as a key stakeholder alongside Centro Hispano.
