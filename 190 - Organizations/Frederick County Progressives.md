# Frederick County Progressives

## Summary

Frederick County Progressives (FCP) is an all-volunteer, non-party affiliated grassroots advocacy organization and the Frederick County chapter of Progressive Maryland, a statewide 501(c)(3) and 501(c)(4) advocacy organization. FCP's stated mission is to empower communities to act for social and economic justice by developing leaders, cultivating allies, and building grassroots power to advance economic, racial, and environmental equity for all Frederick County residents. The organization operates through candidate endorsements, policy research, community forums, and electoral organizing. It is led by Dr. William Reid, who serves as Chair.

## Organizational Structure

Frederick County Progressives operates as a chapter of Progressive Maryland, a statewide organization founded in 2004 that describes itself as building power for working families through grassroots organizing, public education, and legislative advocacy. Progressive Maryland operates across nine county chapters, with FCP serving as its Frederick County presence.

FCP is explicitly non-party affiliated, meaning it does not formally align with the Democratic Party even though its endorsees are predominantly Democrats. The group states it is "the only party independent progressive group specifically for Frederick residents," which it argues allows it to focus on Frederick-specific issues and work across party lines where relevant.

The organization is entirely volunteer-run with no paid staff. It has no 501(c)(3) or 501(c)(4) filing of its own; its formal organizational and financial infrastructure runs through Progressive Maryland.

## Leadership

**Dr. William Reid** — Chair of Frederick County Progressives. Reid has led the organization through its most active electoral and policy periods, including the 2024 city council primary cycle and the 2025-2026 push on rent stabilization. He has been publicly quoted extensively in the Frederick News-Post and served as the primary spokesperson for FCP's rent stabilization forums in January-March 2026. His background includes academic research, reflected in the "Dr." title. He conducted or commissioned polling on Frederick City residents' views on rent stabilization as part of FCP's housing advocacy work.

No other named officers are publicly documented as of early 2026.

## Values and Issue Areas

FCP's stated values include anti-racism, economic justice, grassroots democracy, disability rights, community-uplifting education, intersectionality, and social justice. The organization's primary issue focus areas in recent years have been:

**Housing and Rent Stabilization** — FCP has made rent stabilization its flagship policy campaign as of 2025-2026. The organization commissioned or conducted polling on Frederick City resident support for rent stabilization, organized a major public forum in January 2026 at Asbury United Methodist Church in Frederick featuring Montgomery County Council Member Will Jawando and Rockville City Council Member Izola Shaw, and planned additional follow-up forums in February and March 2026. FCP positions rent stabilization as the central housing affordability demand for Frederick City, distinct from but complementary to housing production policy.

**Electoral Endorsements** — FCP conducts candidate endorsements at the city council, county council, school board, and state legislative levels. Endorsements are made through a questionnaire process evaluated by a subcommittee of members. In the 2025 Frederick City Council primary, FCP endorsed seven candidates, four of whom won. In the 2024 school board primary, FCP endorsed Mahesh Aitha, Janie Monier, and Chad King Wilson Sr. for three open seats. FCP has endorsed César Díaz in his council races, and Díaz's candidacy was featured prominently in FCP's 2025 election communication materials.

**Civic Engagement and Voter Outreach** — FCP works with Progressive Maryland on digital campaign infrastructure, including targeted text messaging and email outreach. In the weeks before the 2025 city council primary, FCP and PMD jointly reached nearly 40,000 Frederick City residents.

**Education and School Board Organizing** — FCP has endorsed candidates for the Frederick County Board of Education and engaged with school board issues as a consistent part of its endorsement work.

**Immigration and Immigrant Rights** — FCP's broader alignment with Progressive Maryland's statewide agenda includes immigrant justice and anti-287(g) organizing, which intersects with César's policy work and FCP's endorsement history.

## Electoral Record

FCP has endorsed in Frederick City Council, Frederick County Council, Maryland General Assembly, and Frederick County Board of Education races. Selected recent record:

**2025 Frederick City Council Primary** — FCP endorsed seven candidates. Four won in the primary. César Díaz was among the endorsed candidates and won election to represent District 2, subsequently being elected Council President by his colleagues in December 2025.

**2024 Frederick County Board of Education Primary** — FCP endorsed Mahesh Aitha, Janie Monier, and Chad King Wilson Sr. for three open seats. Monier and Wilson had also won union endorsements from FCPS employee unions.

**2018 and prior cycles** — FCP was conducting endorsements as early as the 2018 cycle, when it endorsed Ben Jealous for Governor alongside Progressive Maryland. The website shows an active endorsement history going back multiple years.

## Key Activities and Recent Campaigns

**Rent Stabilization Forum (January 2026)** — FCP organized a public forum at Asbury United Methodist Church, Frederick, with approximately 30 people in person and 40 attending online. Featured Montgomery County Council Member Will Jawando and Rockville City Council Member Izola Shaw. Multiple Frederick City and County council members attended, including Council President César Díaz, Council Members Peter Brehm and Derek Shackelford, County Council President Brad Young, and County Council Members Kavonté Duckett and Renee Knapp. Follow-up forums were planned for February and March 2026.

**2025 Primary Electoral Campaign** — FCP partnered with Progressive Maryland to run a digital outreach campaign reaching approximately 40,000 Frederick City residents ahead of the city council primary. The campaign focused on housing affordability and rent stabilization as central issues.

**Candidate Questionnaire Process** — FCP sends detailed policy questionnaires to candidates before endorsing, covering housing, environment, economic equity, immigration, child care, and other issues. Questionnaire responses from 2025 candidates are publicly archived on the FCP website.

## Relationship to Progressive Maryland

FCP operates under the Progressive Maryland chapter structure, which provides organizational infrastructure, digital campaign tools, staff support, and access to the statewide network. Progressive Maryland's Executive Director Larry Stafford Jr. is publicly affiliated with FCP's campaign communications. PMD runs nine chapters across Maryland as of early 2026. The chapter structure gives FCP access to resources a standalone all-volunteer organization would not otherwise have, while maintaining a local focus and non-party identity.

Progressive Maryland operates as two entities: a 501(c)(4) advocacy arm and a 501(c)(3) educational arm (Progressive Maryland Education Fund). FCP's activity flows through both entities depending on whether it involves direct advocacy or educational programming.

## Why This Matters for César

Frederick County Progressives is the primary grassroots progressive electoral organization in Frederick City and County and directly relevant to César's council work and political context. FCP endorsed César in 2025, actively campaigned for his election, and organized the January 2026 rent stabilization forum that César attended as Council President. The organization's housing and immigrant justice agenda aligns closely with César's policy priorities.

As Council President, César will encounter FCP consistently in two capacities: as a constituent advocacy organization pressing for rent stabilization legislation and housing policy commitments at the city level, and as an endorsing body in future electoral cycles. The relationship is collegial but not institutionally captured — FCP will apply the same questionnaire and endorsement process to César's future campaigns as to any other candidate.

FCP's rent stabilization forum series in early 2026 is directly relevant to César's council work, as the City Council is likely to face formal advocacy pressure on rent stabilization ordinances in the 2026 term. The organization's polling and public outreach infrastructure gives it the ability to generate constituent contact campaigns targeting council members.

The organization's non-party affiliation is worth noting. It creates a coalition partner that is not formally tied to the Frederick County Democratic Party structure, making it valuable for cross-partisan civic work while also positioning it to the left of the Democratic Party mainstream on housing and economic justice issues.
