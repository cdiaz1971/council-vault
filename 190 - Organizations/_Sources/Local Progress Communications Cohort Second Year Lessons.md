# Local Progress Communications Cohort -- Second Year Lessons

## Summary

Local Progress ran its second communications cohort for 18 local elected officials across 16 states, producing three key lessons: investing in local leaders works, communications is no longer optional, and under-resourced officials must focus rather than attempt everything.

## Key Points

The Local Progress Communications Cohort is a nine-session virtual program covering communications theory, skills, and best practices. The second cohort ran January to April 2026 with 18 members from 16 states, from school board members to small-town mayors. An in-person DC trip included visits to WAMU, Groundwork Collaborative, and four Capitol Hill communications staffers.

**Three lessons from the second year:**

1. **Investing in local leaders works.** Without dedicated staff or communications support, most local officials abandon the communications work needed to keep communities informed. Skills training not only builds capacity but boosts confidence -- which is often the binding constraint.

2. **Communications is no longer optional.** Shrinking newsrooms and short-form video dominance mean officials can no longer treat communications as something to get to eventually. In 2026, keeping communities informed requires intentional, skilled effort. There are fewer intermediaries than ever.

3. **Focus beats completeness.** Forcing an all-platforms approach on officials without staff leads to decision paralysis. The cohort emphasized picking the platform that connects best with your community and doing it well. Immediate action on a single channel outperforms scattered presence on many.

## Relevance

The third lesson is directly applicable to César's communications work. The comms skill file and active Bluesky presence reflect a similar instinct: pick the channel, do it with discipline, and don't spread thin. The cohort's curriculum -- navigating press relationships, short-form video, reaching non-English-speaking audiences -- maps onto Frederick's multilingual constituency and the active communications work around the City Values Act and Municipal ID.
