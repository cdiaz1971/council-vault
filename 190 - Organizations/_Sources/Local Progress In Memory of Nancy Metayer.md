# Local Progress -- In Memory of Nancy Metayer

## Summary

Local Progress tribute to Nancy Metayer, Coral Springs Vice Mayor, the first Haitian American and Black woman elected to that role, who created the city's first Office of Sustainability and was a founding member of the Local Progress Women's Caucus.

## Key Points

Nancy Metayer served as Coral Springs Vice Mayor in Florida. She was the first Haitian American and first Black woman elected as city commissioner in Coral Springs, and later the first to serve as Vice Mayor. She joined Local Progress in 2017 and was active in the Florida chapter, the Black Caucus, and was a founding member of the Women's Caucus.

Her policy work focused on climate justice and environmental stewardship. As Broward County Soil and Water Supervisor, she spearheaded policies for renewable energy adoption, electric vehicle integration, waste reduction, and green infrastructure investment -- responding to acute water crises left by successive hurricane seasons. As Vice Mayor she created Coral Springs' first Office of Sustainability, institutionalizing climate action across municipal operations.

The tribute, written by Local Progress Managing Director of Membership and Organizing Francesca Menes, emphasizes Metayer's model of organizing with care, integrity, and passion -- and her belief that government can be a force for good. She passed away in April 2026.

## Relevance

Metayer's model of elected official as organizer -- building relationships across movements, creating institutional infrastructure (the Office of Sustainability) rather than one-off initiatives, and grounding policy in explicit values -- is the same model César is building in Frederick. The Local Progress network she helped shape through the Women's Caucus and Black Caucus is the same network César participates in. Her work is worth naming as a reference point.
