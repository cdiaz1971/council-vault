# Civic Design Association of Frederick (CDA)

## Executive Summary

This document proposes the creation of the **Civic Design Association of Frederick (CDA)**, a new community development corporation intended to provide the City Council with independent, professional, interdisciplinary policy and legislative support. The CDA would also partner with public, nonprofit, and private entities to design, fund, and deliver civic capital projects.

The City Council is asked to adopt a resolution establishing the CDA and to commit to recurring annual funding covering at least 50 percent of operating costs, estimated at $500,000 per year. The remaining funds would be raised through grants, partnerships, and other fundraising activities.

Governance would be provided by a five-member oversight board consisting of City Council leadership, the Mayor or designee, and two community members. A future phase envisions converting the CDA into a Civic Design Authority with bonding capacity.

**Why this matters:** Frederick is not inventing something untested. Cities and states already invest in independent policy and design capacity to improve decision-making and project delivery.

**What makes CDA different:** The CDA combines two proven models—independent legislative policy support and nonprofit community design—into one structure tailored to Frederick’s needs.

**What this is not:** This does _not_ create a new executive department or grant bonding powers. It is a scoped, accountable, nonprofit support entity reporting to elected officials.

## Top Five Benefits

### 1. Independent Legislative Capacity for the City Council
The CDA would provide the City Council with its own source of professional policy analysis and legislative drafting, reducing reliance on executive-branch departments and strengthening legislative independence.

### 2. Integrated, Interdisciplinary Analysis
By combining planning, fiscal, human services, and community engagement expertise in a single entity, the CDA enables more holistic evaluation of complex policy proposals.

### 3. Improved Community Trust and Coordination
The proposed community engagement framework, including District Advisory Councils and transparent reporting, creates clearer feedback loops between residents and policymakers and may improve public confidence in City decisions.

### 4. Capacity to Advance Civic and Capital Projects
Unlike advisory-only bodies, the CDA would actively help fund, design, and deliver civic projects, helping bridge the gap between policy adoption and on-the-ground implementation.

### 5. Long-Term Strategic Flexibility
The option to later convert the CDA into a Civic Design Authority provides a potential pathway to expanded infrastructure financing tools without requiring immediate commitment.

## Downsides and Risks

### 1. Recurring Fiscal Commitment
The City would be committing to a $500,000 annual subsidy. This ongoing obligation could place pressure on the general fund, particularly during economic downturns.

### 2. Overlap with Executive Functions
The CDA’s role in drafting legislation and managing projects may blur the line between legislative and executive responsibilities, raising charter, legal, or administrative concerns.

### 3. Oversight and Accountability Challenges
A small oversight board with both legislative and executive representation may limit robust accountability and concentrate influence over staffing and work priorities.

### 4. Uncertain Fundraising Assumptions
Half of the operating budget relies on external fundraising. The proposal does not specify guaranteed sources or contingency plans if fundraising targets are not met.

### 5. High-Stakes Implications of Future Authority Model
Conversion to a bonding authority would significantly affect City finances and governance. Early design decisions could unintentionally pre-commit the City to this path without sufficient public deliberation.

## Bottom Line

The CDA proposal offers meaningful benefits by strengthening the City Council’s policy capacity and creating a coordinated vehicle for civic investment. At the same time, it introduces notable fiscal, governance, and legal risks that would require careful conditions, scope limitations, and accountability measures prior to adoption.
