# Centro Hispano de Frederick

## Summary

Centro Hispano de Frederick is a nonprofit organization founded in 2006 that serves the Hispanic and limited English proficiency (LEP) immigrant population of Frederick County. Its mission is to equip the Hispanic community to become thriving, contributing members of Frederick County by breaking down language and cultural barriers. The organization operates out of 5 Willowdale Drive, Suite 18, Frederick, MD 21702. EIN: 30-0430736. Tax-exempt since January 2008.

## Programs and Regular Events

**English Instruction** -- Beginner English classes focused on daily living and work. Monday through Wednesday, 6:00-7:30 PM.

**Citizenship Classes** -- Guides clients through the full citizenship process, including N-400 application assistance and interview preparation. Tuesdays and Thursdays (first three weeks of each month), 6:00-7:30 PM.

**Entre Mujeres (Between Women)** -- Education, training, and advocacy for women facing social, economic, language, and cultural barriers. First and third Friday of every month, 6:00-7:30 PM. Includes a sewing program taught in eight-week sessions year-round.

**Computer Classes** -- Digital literacy training for daily tasks and community success. Every Wednesday, 6:30-8:00 PM.

**Años Dorados / Golden Years** -- Enrichment and learning activities for Latino senior citizens (ages 62-96). Every Saturday, 9:00 AM - 12:00 PM.

**Éxito STEAM Program** -- Summer science, reading, and life skills program for children 1st grade through high school, Monday-Friday in July and August. A school-year component supports at-risk Hispanic youth in partnership with Frederick County Public Schools.

**Family Support Program** -- Case management and navigation services for families.

**Support Services** -- Portfolio of over 20 basic needs services addressing root causes of barriers for LEP residents.

**Hispanic Heritage Festival** -- Annual community celebration during Hispanic Heritage Month (September 15 - October 15).

**Key observations:**

The organization has grown dramatically -- from under $70k in annual revenue in 2019 to over $860k in 2024. The 2023 dip is notable: revenue fell to $168k while expenses held at $337k, burning through reserves and nearly halving net assets. The 2024 rebound is sharp and likely reflects new grant funding secured under the incoming executive director. Revenue is almost entirely contribution-based; the organization reports zero program service revenue, meaning it does not charge clients for services.

**2024 expense breakdown:**
- Total salaries and wages: $308,342 (43% of expenses)
  - Executive Director (Salichs): $75,748
  - Former ED (Maria Shuck, transition): $15,625
  - Other staff: $292,717
- Net fundraising: -$12,831 (fundraising events cost slightly more than they raised)
- Total liabilities: $74,795

The organization has no earned revenue from program services, making it fully grant and donation dependent. The 2023 deficit year warrants a question about what drove the revenue gap and whether it has been structurally resolved.

## Why This Matters for César

Centro Hispano is a key partner organization for César's council work on immigrant support, the [[Municipal ID]] legislation, and the [[City Values Act]]. The organization serves nearly 2,000 clients annually and represents a significant constituency in Frederick City. Their leadership includes legal expertise in immigration law (Lucelia Rodriguez Justiniano) and community health (Manny Casiano), both relevant to policy conversations. OFC Maggie Lee's community liaison role reflects the kind of trust-building between law enforcement and immigrant communities that intersects with public safety policy discussions at the council level. Any ordinances or resolutions touching immigrant services, language access, or community investment should involve or reference Centro Hispano's work.

The financial profile -- fully grant-dependent, with a volatile revenue history -- is relevant context for any city funding conversations or partnership discussions. If the city were to consider direct support or contract services, the 2023 deficit year would be worth understanding in detail.
