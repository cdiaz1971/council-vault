# Civic Design Association of Frederick (CDA)
## Counterpoints and Risk Mitigation Strategies

This document outlines counterarguments to commonly identified downsides of the proposed [[Civic Design Association of Frederick]], along with practical mechanisms the City Council can use to manage fiscal, legal, and governance risks.

---

## 1. Recurring Fiscal Commitment

**Stated Risk:** The City would commit approximately $500,000 annually on a recurring basis.

**Counterpoint:**
The proposed funding level is modest relative to the breadth of services provided and may reduce existing expenditures on consultants, external legal drafting, and fragmented studies. Centralized, in-house capacity can prevent costly policy missteps and delays.

**Risk Management Tools:**
- Include a sunset clause requiring reauthorization after two or three years.
- Tie continued funding to defined deliverables and performance metrics.
- Cap City contributions regardless of total CDA budget growth.
- Require an annual report comparing CDA costs to consultant expenditures avoided.

---

## 2. Overlap With Executive Functions

**Stated Risk:** CDA activities could blur legislative and executive responsibilities.

**Counterpoint:**
The CDA would not execute policy or manage City departments. Its role is limited to analysis, legislative drafting, and project facilitation at the request of the City Council, functions commonly supported by legislative service offices in other jurisdictions.

**Risk Management Tools:**
- Define the CDA as non-executing and advisory in the enabling resolution.
- Restrict work to formal Council requests or referrals.
- Prohibit unilateral project initiation by CDA staff.
- Require a charter compliance review prior to final adoption.

---

## 3. Oversight and Accountability

**Stated Risk:** A small oversight board may concentrate authority and limit transparency.

**Counterpoint:**
A compact board supports efficiency and alignment with elected leadership, while inclusion of community members provides external accountability. Transparency requirements can mitigate concentration concerns.

**Risk Management Tools:**
- Require public meetings, published agendas, and annual work plans.
- Mandate annual reporting to the full City Council.
- Preserve Council authority to confirm or remove the Executive Director.
- Require periodic performance audits or reviews.

---

## 4. Fundraising Uncertainty

**Stated Risk:** Half of the operating budget depends on external fundraising.

**Counterpoint:**
The CDA’s interdisciplinary structure and project-oriented mission align well with state, federal, and foundation funding criteria that the City currently lacks capacity to pursue consistently.

**Risk Management Tools:**
- Phase staffing so fundraising-dependent positions activate only after funds are secured.
- Require a formal fundraising plan and pipeline.
- Prevent automatic City backfilling of fundraising shortfalls.
- Allow programmatic scaling if external funding underperforms.

---

## 5. Future Conversion to a Civic Design Authority

**Stated Risk:** Granting bonding or assessment powers would raise major fiscal and governance concerns.

**Counterpoint:**
The proposal treats conversion as optional and future-oriented. Establishing the CDA as an association allows performance evaluation before considering any expansion of authority.

**Risk Management Tools:**
- Explicitly prohibit authority conversion in the initial resolution.
- Require a separate ordinance, public hearing, and fiscal impact study for any future change.
- Consider supermajority Council or voter approval before granting bonding authority.
- Prohibit issuance of debt, assessments, or liens under the initial structure.

---

## Council Framing Note

With these safeguards in place, the City Council can pursue the benefits of independent legislative capacity while retaining fiscal control, preserving charter integrity, and maintaining the ability to modify or discontinue the CDA if it fails to meet expectations.
