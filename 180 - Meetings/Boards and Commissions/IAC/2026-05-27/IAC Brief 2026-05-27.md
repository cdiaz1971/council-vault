# IAC Meeting Briefing -- May 27, 2026

## Summary

Pre-meeting briefing for the Frederick County Immigrant Affairs Commission meeting on May 27, 2026. Key watch items are the unresolved ICE office lease issue, Immigrant Heritage Month planning, and the County Executive's proposed Immigrant Liaison budget position.

## Meeting Details

**Date:** May 27, 2026 | 5:00 PM
**Location:** Winchester Hall, 12 E Church Street, Frederick, MD 21701
**Commission membership:** Claudia Hernandez (Chair), Dr. Ben Chepkoit, Danielle Haskin, Dr. Katherine Gull, Cody Rosenbarker, Seema Sharma, Zohreh Rezazadeh, Sofi Ibrahim, Andrea Diaz-Gomez

## Bottom Line

This is a working meeting with no votes expected. The substantive items are Immigrant Heritage Month (June), two upcoming community events, and a resource guide revision. The most politically significant carry-forward from April is the ICE office lease issue, which generated significant public pressure and a letter from the Commission to County Executive Fitzwater that has not been answered. That thread is live and may surface again in public comment.

## Agenda Items

**I. Minutes Approval.** Routine.

**II. Roll Call.** Routine.

**III. Immigrant Heritage Month.** The Commission voted in March to feature individual immigrant stories rather than a panel format. As of April, they were recruiting volunteers to conduct interviews and needed a sign-up sheet. Expect an update on where that stands -- whether speakers are confirmed, logistics are set, and whether event dates (June 15 and June 22, 6-8 PM) are holding.

**IV. Asbury Block Party / Monocacy Middle School International Night.** The Commission voted in March to host a table at the Asbury Block Party (July 18, 11 AM-4 PM). Monocacy Middle School International Night was May 21 -- that event has already passed, so this item likely involves a debrief. Expect an update on sign-up status for the Block Party and a report-out on how the school event went.

**V. Resource Guide Revision.** This has been an ongoing item. In April, a public commenter raised pointed concerns that the guide does not adequately serve undocumented residents. The Commission was directed to revisit and edit. Expect a working discussion on what changes are being proposed and who is driving them. The question of whether city and county guides explicitly address undocumented residents is politically meaningful given the current enforcement climate.

**VI. June Meeting Date.** The Commission voted in March to meet June 17. This item likely confirms that date or adjusts it pending room availability.

**VII. OEI Update.** Office of Equity and Inclusion update from Tiffani Kenney (Chief, Equity and Inclusion). In April there was no update. Watch for any mention of the immigrant liaison position proposed by County Executive Fitzwater -- this is a budget item before the County and the Commission has been asked to advocate for it.

**VIII. Public Comment.** The ICE office lease has generated sustained community pressure across multiple meetings. The April letter to Fitzwater has not received a response. That silence is likely to fuel public comment again tonight. As Council President, you may be asked directly about the City's position or next steps. The CVA is the relevant City-side instrument; implementation and FPD compliance remain an active intelligence gap.

## Background Intelligence from April Minutes

Commission members Claudia and Seema attended and spoke at a City Council hearing on April 21 to advocate for the County Executive's proposed full-time Immigrant Liaison position, framing it as essential to accelerating the Commission's work.

Vice President Kavonte Duckett attended in person and provided an update on the City Values Act, indicating revisions were underway. That statement is now dated -- the CVA passed 7-0 on April 2. If you attend tonight, the Commission will likely want a status update on implementation rather than the legislative vote.

A community member proposed creating a quick-reference booklet for families explaining what to do in the event of an unexpected encounter with immigration enforcement. Tiffani Kenney indicated a similar resource may exist at the county level and committed to sharing it. Whether that was ever shared is an open question and may come up in the resource guide discussion.

## Watch Items

The ICE lease question and the Immigrant Liaison budget position are the two items with the most active political valence. The resource guide discussion is lower-profile but touches the same underlying question of how explicitly city and county materials address undocumented residents. If you plan to speak, those three threads are where your engagement will have the most traction.
