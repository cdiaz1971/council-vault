# Council Meeting Briefing — 2026-03-19 Regular Public Meeting

## Summary

Regular public meeting on March 19, 2026 at 6:00 PM, City Hall. The headline item is the introduction of G-26-1, the City Values Ordinance. The council also votes on pension plan amendments, a state municipal revenue task force resolution, and an actuarial services contract. Traffic calming is an informational presentation only.

## Action Items

**Pension Plan Amendments — Resolutions 26-3 and 26-4**
Amends the City's 25-year and 30-year pension plans to restore eligibility for part-time employees working 1,000 or more hours per fiscal year. Eligibility was removed in a 2011 amendment; this reverses it. Council reached consensus at an October 2025 workshop. Fiscal impact: $52,000/year beginning April 1, 2026. Also includes housekeeping changes: removal of outdated "rule of 90" reference, gender-neutral pronoun updates, IRS-compliant payroll language, and updated job titles. Staff recommends approval. Routine vote, low risk. Equity note: part-time workers skew toward lower-wage and non-traditional-schedule employees -- restoring defined benefit access is a meaningful step.

**Actuarial Services Contract — Bolton Partners Inc. (RFP 26-I)**
Approval of agreement with Bolton Partners for pension plan actuarial services. Scored highest among seven proposals at 93.5/100. Supports the required pension plan valuation cycle. Not a small business reserve solicitation (pre-award determination). Standard approval item.

**Resolution 26-6 — Support for HB 1142 (Municipal Revenue Task Force)**
Introduced by Council President Díaz on behalf of the administration. Formally supports HB 1142, a state bill to create a task force studying modernization of county and municipal revenue sources. Key framing: Maryland is one of only six states that neither shares sales tax revenue with local governments nor allows them to collect it, driving over-reliance on property taxes. Urges House Ways and Means and Senate Budget and Taxation to issue a favorable report. Aligns City with Maryland Municipal League. Political positioning resolution -- no binding fiscal commitment. Watch for member reluctance framing this as a prelude to new taxes.

**Introduction of Ordinance G-26-1 — City Values Act**
Introduction only -- no vote on the merits tonight. Adds Chapter 33, Article II to the City Code. Key provisions:
- Definitions using ACLU-aligned terminology ("immigration enforcement operation," "law enforcement officer")
- Facility access restrictions: judicial warrant or exigent circumstances required to enter non-public areas
- Prohibition on use of City property for immigration enforcement operations (surveillance, staging, processing, detention, vehicle storage)
- Status inquiry limit: City employees make inquiries only if legally required
- Legal Department guidance mandate
- Departmental protocol and designee requirements
- Two-tier reporting: quarterly reports to Council within 45 days of quarter close; annual report by March 31
- Confidentiality protections and no-criminal-penalty clause
- Facility designation and signage requirements (§33-12)

Equity Impact Statement from Lakisha Wade is supportive, flagging training, communication, and legal boundary clarification as implementation priorities. No fiscal impact listed. Next step: Legal to incorporate any changes from the March 12 work session before the ordinance proceeds through standard legislative process.

**Note:** Benford's surveillance technology and FPD tracking/reporting provisions will not be taken up at this meeting.
