# Council Hearing Brief Gateway Property 2026-08-06

## Bottom Line

August 6 is the decisive night for the Gateway Property. Two votes are on the table: adoption of Ordinance G-26-7 rezoning 24.33 acres from General Commercial to Institutional for a cricket special events center, and adoption of a resolution amending the 2009 annexation resolution to update the transportation contribution and parkland obligation. The procedural loop is closed -- the applicant accepted the Council's conditions in writing on July 28 -- so the vote can proceed. Both items were introduced under your name on behalf of the administration.

The conditions in the enrolled draft ordinance are substantively the same as what the Council established on July 16 with one notable change: the professional cricket match cap has been revised downward from 20 per year in the applicant's July 7 proposed conditions to 15 per year in the first calendar year in the final draft, with subsequent years also capped at 15 unless the COO approves an increase in consultation with the stakeholders group. That is a tighter constraint than the applicant originally proposed and reflects a Council-driven adjustment.

The annexation resolution is a clean two-part amendment to Resolution 09-23: a tripled transportation contribution ($1.5 million total, structured as $500k now and $1 million within two years) and a reformulated parkland obligation converting the original 16-acre floodplain dedication into a 14-acre public recreation area along the Monocacy River with a boat launch, 10-foot path, and city discretion over whether to take the land in fee simple or by easement.

## VIII.D.2 -- Zoning Map Amendment Ordinance G-26-7 (Vote)

**What the Council is voting on.** Adoption of an ordinance applying the Institutional (IST) floating zone to 24.33 acres of the Gateway Property, currently zoned General Commercial, within the Airport Overlay and partially within the Highway Noise Impact Overlay. The purpose is to permit a Special Events Center -- specifically a cricket facility -- as a permitted use under IST.

**Procedural posture.** Introduction was July 16, 2026. At the conclusion of that hearing the Council voted to impose conditions of rezoning under LMC Section 307(b). Planning notified the applicant (attorney Bruce Dean, McCurdy Dean and Graditor) on July 27. The applicant accepted all conditions in writing on July 28 -- the acceptance letter is in the packet. That closes the conditional rezoning loop. The Council may now approve the rezoning.

**Required findings.** The ordinance contains explicit findings of fact the Council must make to adopt the IST floating zone, covering the six criteria in LMC Section 306(e)(7) and the four criteria in Section 409. The findings in the draft cover: nonresidential use will not generate additional population; public facilities access confirmed with APFO evaluation deferred to site plan; significant transportation impacts managed through Events Management Plan; compatibility with adjacent institutional, industrial, and agricultural uses; support for 2020 Comprehensive Plan policies; and IST zone criteria on land availability, site selection, roadway access, compatibility with neighbors, and environmental compliance. The ordinance takes effect August 16, 2026 if adopted and signed.

**The 17 conditions of rezoning.** All conditions from the July 16 Council decision are incorporated into the draft ordinance. Key provisions:

Events Management Plan (Condition 1) is required for all events regardless of size, scalable below 1,500 attendees but fully triggered at that threshold. Components include: traffic and parking management including a prohibition on residential street parking enforced by Frederick County Sheriff; security, emergency services, and neighborhood protections; facilities and utilities; sound equipment disclosures; and a sketch plan for offsite parking and shuttle logistics. The EMP must be approved by the COO before unconditional final site plan approval, updated annually by April 1, and always remain in effect.

Condition 2 builds in the Special Events Commission handoff: if the City subsequently establishes an Events Management Commission by resolution, ordinance, or executive order, that commission takes over the COO's role in administering and enforcing the EMP. This is the explicit link between the Gateway rezoning and the Special Events Commission ordinance introduced tonight. If G-26-9 advances and a commission is constituted, it steps into the COO's shoes here automatically.

Condition 3 prohibits parking in the SHA Park and Ride lot unless SHA expressly approves.

Condition 4 caps professional cricket matches at 15 in the first calendar year. Subsequent years are also capped at 15 without COO approval in consultation with the stakeholders group. This is tighter than the applicant's July 7 proposal of 20 per year with no ramp-up distinction.

Condition 5 caps large events (1,500-plus attendees, excluding professional cricket) at 10 in the first year, with any increase requiring COO approval in consultation with the stakeholders group.

Condition 8 is the MDE permit backstop: the owner must obtain a Nontidal Wetlands and Waterways Permit authorizing floodplain grading before the facility can open. If MDE does not issue the permit, the facility must be relocated outside the floodplain or the project does not proceed. This is the single largest contingent risk in the deal.

Condition 9 bars aerial fireworks, drones, lasers, and upward-pointing lights without Airport Manager approval.

Condition 11 requires on-site sound monitoring equipment to assist FPD in enforcing the noise ordinance. This is an addition beyond the baseline noise compliance requirement -- a specific operational tool the applicant is committing to provide.

Condition 16 allows the cricket field to be configured for at least two additional sporting uses within its boundaries.

Condition 17 sets an 11 p.m. cutoff for cricket matches and special events.

Condition 10,000-person threshold (Condition 16 of the draft): any event expected to draw 10,000 or more must comply with the Special Events code for Large Private Events. This is the gateway to the commission's jurisdiction for the largest events at the facility.

**What to watch in the room.** The applicant has accepted all conditions. Public comment has been ongoing throughout the process and a batch was filed through August 6. The Planning Commission voted 3-0 for a positive recommendation. Staff support adoption. The political dynamic going in is that this has broad support and no publicly declared opposition among council members. If anyone moves to amend the ordinance from the floor, that would be unusual procedurally at second read with conditions already accepted -- flag to Gray if that arises.

## VIII.D.3 -- Annexation Resolution Amending Resolution 09-23 (Vote)

**What the Council is voting on.** A resolution amending two specific conditions in the 2009 annexation resolution: Paragraph 7(a) on the transportation contribution, and Paragraph 7(b) on parkland.

**Background.** The original Resolution 09-23 required a $500,000 transportation contribution due by November 19, 2019. That payment was never collected. The applicant (AcersInfra, LLC, contract purchaser from 3M Farm LLC) agreed to a substantially increased contribution structured to coincide with the rezoning approval.

**Transportation contribution -- new Condition 7(a).** Total obligation is $1.5 million, structured as follows: $500,000 due within 30 days of Council approval of the rezoning with the expiration of the appeals period and no appeals filed, or by August 30, 2026, whichever is earlier. The remaining $1 million is due within two years of the first payment. Critically, APFO-required road improvements or contributions are explicitly additive and cannot be credited against the $1.5 million regional transportation contribution.

The August 30, 2026 outside date means the first payment is essentially due immediately if the rezoning is approved tonight and no appeals are filed within 30 days. Watch whether the appeals period is 30 days or longer under Maryland law -- if an appeal is filed and not resolved within 30 days, the August 30 date becomes the hard backstop.

**Parkland -- new Condition 7(b).** The original obligation was to dedicate approximately 16 acres of floodplain to the City. The applicant intends to fill portions of the floodplain for the development, and has negotiated a replacement: a 14-acre public recreation area along the Monocacy River improved with a small boat launch for canoes and small boats, a 10-foot wide path from East Patrick Street south to the I-70 right of way, and additional amenities the owner chooses to add. City has sole discretion on whether to take the land in fee simple or accept a perpetual public use easement. Parkland improvements require approval by both Parks and Recreation Commission and Planning Commission via site plan.

The MDE permit condition in the rezoning ordinance (Condition 8) has a parkland knock-on: if MDE does not approve floodplain grading, the Council's resolution includes a backstop that parkland must still be dedicated on the property even if the Monocacy River location is not viable. That protection is in the Planning Commission's recommendation, though confirm it is carried in the final resolution text -- the draft resolution does not explicitly restate it.

**Institutional support.** Parks and Recreation Commission voted 7-0 with one abstention in support. Planning Commission voted 3-0 for positive recommendation with conditions, including the APFO additionality clause and the MDE floodplain backstop. COO Zack Kershner signed off via email July 27. CAO Barkdoll and City Attorney Gray both initialed the cover sheet.

## Watch List

The MDE Nontidal Wetlands and Waterways Permit is the single largest open risk in the whole deal. The rezoning passes tonight and the annexation resolution passes tonight, but the facility cannot open until MDE issues that permit. If MDE denies it, the project either relocates outside the floodplain -- a significant redesign -- or does not proceed. The $500,000 first transportation payment is still due regardless, since the payment trigger is Council approval plus expiration of appeals, not MDE permit issuance. Confirm whether the applicant understands they are on the hook for the transportation contribution even if MDE ultimately kills the project as designed.

The Condition 2 link between Gateway and the Special Events Commission is worth naming publicly tonight if the opportunity arises. If G-26-9 advances and a commission is constituted, that body automatically steps into the COO's role for EMP administration at the Gateway facility. The two items are connected in a way the public may not recognize.

The applicant's proposed conditions (July 7) had 20 professional cricket matches per year. The final ordinance has 15, with COO approval required to go higher in subsequent years. If the applicant's business model assumed 20 matches, there is a potential pressure point down the road to seek COO approval for increases. The stakeholders group mechanism -- including a Council member designated by the Council President -- gives the Council ongoing visibility into that process. That seat is yours to assign.

The 11 p.m. event cutoff is enforceable under the conditions but will require active monitoring. The on-site sound monitoring equipment commitment (Condition 11) is the tool for FPD to enforce the noise ordinance. Confirm with FPD before the facility opens that they have a protocol for using that equipment and acting on readings that exceed the ordinance threshold.
