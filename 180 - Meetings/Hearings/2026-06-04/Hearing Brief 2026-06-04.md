# Council Meeting Agenda Brief -- 2026-06-04

## Summary

Regular Public Meeting, June 4, 2026, 6:00 PM, City Hall. Substantive meeting. The two legislative introductions and one approval are the items that matter most. G-26-3 (taxicab/vehicles for hire overhaul) is introduction only. G-26-4 (Mixed Use phasing) is up for final approval and carries direct implications for how housing production moves in the Renn Quarter and future MU projects citywide. The pension resolutions are significant and equity-positive. The remainder of the agenda is capital expenditure and consent business. No items are directly hostile to César's priorities. One item warrants proactive framing (G-26-4). Two warrant monitoring at public comment (FBI firing range optics, Winpenny annexation).

---

## Action Items

### IX.C.1 -- Resolutions 26-11 and 26-12: Pension Plan Amendments
**Vote to approve.**

These resolutions restore pension eligibility to part-time city employees who work 1,000 or more hours per fiscal year, effective July 1, 2026. Part-time employees were stripped of eligibility in March 2011. Council members were explicitly excluded from the expanded eligibility following October 2025 consensus.

The package also includes housekeeping cleanup: removal of a leftover "rule of 90" reference, elimination of male pronoun language throughout, updated job titles, and updated IRS guidance language on payroll service purchase payments.

Fiscal impact: $52,000 per year in additional city funding.

Equity read: Strong. Part-time workers are disproportionately lower-wage, women, and non-white. The 2011 exclusion cut benefits from a workforce segment that is heavily ALICE. This is a restoration of a benefit that should not have been taken in the first place. Worth noting publicly.

Risk flag: None. HR Director Karen Paulson submitted. Legal reviewed. Budget cleared. 7-0 vote is the expectation.

### IX.D.2 -- Introduction of Ordinance G-26-3: Vehicles for Hire (Taxicab Code Overhaul)
**Introduction only. No vote on substance tonight.**

Key changes: marijuana removed from the prohibited drug testing list for driver licensing (alcohol and other illegal drugs remain); permit caps eliminated entirely; permit fees reduced; permit validity extended from one year to two years; mandatory denial period for certain criminal convictions adjusted.

Policy read: The framing is sympathetic to permit holders and drivers as workers dealing with rising costs. Reducing barriers to licensure expands who can legally drive for hire. The marijuana provision will likely draw public comment. The legislative rationale is solid -- Maryland has legalized adult use marijuana, and the coherent position is to treat it the same as alcohol (prohibited while operating) rather than as a disqualifying factor for licensing. Be ready to explain that distinction if asked.

Risk flag: Introduction only, so the vote is procedural.

### IX.D.3 -- Approval of Ordinance G-26-4: LMC Amendment, Mixed Use Phasing
**Final approval vote. Most consequential legislative vote tonight.**

Amends LMC Section 417 (Mixed Use Districts) and Section 310 (Master Plans) to replace the rigid standard construction phasing schedule for MU-1 and MU-2 projects with a developer-proposed phasing schedule subject to Reviewing Authority approval.

Current rule: No more than 75% of residential units may receive building permits until at least 25% of the non-residential area is built, and vice versa.

What this changes: Developers propose their own phasing schedule as part of the Master Plan process. The Reviewing Authority -- Council for MU-2 rezonings, Planning Commission for MU-1 base zone -- must approve the schedule. Modifications go back to the same Reviewing Authority. Changes affecting a certificate of adequacy under APFO go through the full Master Plan revision process.

#### Planning Commission History

The Planning Commission held hearings on June 6, August 11, and September 8, 2025. On September 8, 2025, the Commission voted 4-0 to recommend denial. The Commission found the proposal inconsistent with the Land Management Code and the 2020 Comprehensive Plan, and cited the absence of empirical economic data demonstrating that current regulations are problematic. Council is overriding a unanimous Planning Commission denial recommendation if it votes yes tonight.

The ordinance was subsequently revised in response to February 26 work session feedback: the administrative Department modification pathway was removed and replaced with Reviewing Authority approval. That revision addressed the public oversight concern the Commission raised. It did not address the substantive delivery concern.

See also: [[City Council Work Session - 022526]]

#### Developer Background: Matan

The driving applicant is the Renn Quarter developer, Matan Companies (East Church Street and Monocacy Boulevard). The stated reason is that market conditions have slowed nonresidential construction.

Matan is a family-owned, Frederick-based firm with over $3.5 billion in transactions and 7 million square feet in its current portfolio. Their active development pipeline is primarily industrial and warehouse properties. They are not a distressed developer.

The "market conditions" argument is weakened by two facts verified as of June 3, 2026.

First, Matan's current website continues to market Renn Quarter as "a new, vibrant neighborhood connected to the heart of downtown Frederick" with "convenient retail shops and amenities" steps away, and actively solicits tenants for "future retail space available at the heart of 1,000+ new residences." They are using the retail promise to attract both residents and commercial tenants while simultaneously arguing the retail cannot be delivered on the original schedule.
Source: https://mataninc.com/properties/renn-quarter

Second, in March 2026 the Planning Commission granted final site plan approval for the last residential phase of Renn Quarter -- 101 townhouses at the corner of Monocacy Boulevard and Monroe Avenue, completing the 1,050-unit residential build-out of the 221.63-acre master plan.
Source: Frederick News-Post / Yahoo News, March 2026 -- https://www.yahoo.com/news/planners-sign-off-final-residential-045900925.html

The timing is the core problem. Matan is requesting phasing flexibility at the precise moment residential build-out is complete. Once the remaining units are permitted, the city's leverage is gone. The proposed ordinance contains no specific mechanism to prevent permanent deferral of the 105,000 square feet of nonresidential space that was the other half of the original community promise.

#### Strategic Read

This is a developer flexibility ordinance. It weakens the linkage designed to ensure mixed-use projects deliver the non-residential component proportionate to housing built. The residential-first pattern is the profitable pattern; this amendment gives it a formal legal pathway. The Reviewing Authority approval requirement is a real check, but it places the burden on the city to reject a developer-proposed schedule rather than requiring the developer to hit a fixed standard.

Planning staff supports it. The Reviewing Authority revision is a meaningful guard. But the core question from the February 26 work session remains unanswered: what specific mechanism prevents Matan from permitting the last residential units and then letting the retail site plans lapse permanently?

Consider putting on the record: what monitoring or enforcement mechanism ensures the nonresidential component actually gets built, given that residential build-out is now complete?

### IX.A.3 -- FEMA Levee Certification: Bayland Consultants and Designers ($395,000)
Engineering contract, CIP #350024. Funds analyses to certify three Carroll Creek levee segments per FEMA and Army Corps requirements (44 CFR § 65.10). Goal is FEMA accreditation of the levee system and an updated Letter of Map Revision. Twenty-two properties in NAC 12 along East Church Street and Highland Avenue are directly affected. Without accreditation, affected properties face mandatory flood insurance reclassification -- a direct cost hit to homeowners least able to absorb it. Bayland scored highest (91 of 100) in competitive RFP. Staff recommends approval.

Note: The equity impact statement in the packet says "N/A" -- inaccurate. Flood risk and insurance burdens fall disproportionately on lower-income homeowners. Not a reason to oppose, but the equity statement was underdeveloped.

### IX.A.6 -- FPD Live Fire Range Turning Target System: $192,800 (FBI-Reimbursed)
Two-part item: special ordinance to amend FY26 budget for FBI reimbursement funds, and a purchase order for $192,800. The FBI has an MOU with FPD for range use and has committed up to $450,000 for reimbursement. The current pneumatic system dates to approximately 2005 and is no longer functional. FPD bears no net cost.

Risk flag: No fiscal exposure. The political optics of upgrading a police firing range may generate public comment, particularly given open questions about FPD compliance with the City Values Act (Ordinance G-26-1, passed 7-0 April 2, 2026). If asked, the honest answer is this is federal money for existing infrastructure with no net city cost, and the alternative is a non-functional training range.

Watch item: R4 annexation at the city's fringe. Low-density residential adds land supply at the density level least favorable to affordability goals. Not a battle for tonight, but worth tracking as it moves through Planning Commission.
