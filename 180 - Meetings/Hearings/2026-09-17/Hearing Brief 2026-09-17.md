# Hearing Brief 2026-09-17

**Regular Public Meeting -- September 17, 2026 at 6:00 PM, City Hall**

## Bottom Line

Two things need real attention tonight. First, Second Read of Ordinance G-26-11, the Maryland Housing Certainty Act implementation ordinance you introduced September 3 -- this is the last chance to amend it before it's locked, and it sits directly on top of the LMC review timing risk already flagged in the vault. Second, three linked items for the Worman's Mill Court Apartments (Standard Wormans Venture LP): a PILOT agreement, an MPDU agreement, and a $240,000 parkland fee-in-lieu resolution. Together they lock in a 306-unit development where all units are income-restricted at or below 60% AMI for 40 years, which is a genuinely strong equity outcome worth naming publicly. Everything else -- the airport crack seal, the Monocacy Blvd design contract, the HMF Paving IFB, weatherization contracts, Carroll Park Manor pest control, and the CAPER public comment period -- is routine and staff-recommended with no flagged opposition. There is no separately listed consent agenda on this packet; all substantive items are filed under Public Hearing (see note below).

## Action Items

**A.1 -- Monocacy Blvd Improvements design contract, $77,500 (Lingg Property Consulting).** Additional design funds for CIP #320037 (Storage Way to South Street), covering stormwater management and a railway pedestrian gate crossing requested by MDSHA/MTA that weren't in the original scope. Brings total design cost to $2,536,777. Staff recommends approval; routine.

**A.2 -- Airport Runway 12/30 crack seal, $94,555 purchase order plus a special ordinance to amend the FY2027 budget.** First maintenance on this runway since 2019, required to maintain FAA/MAA rating. State MAA grant expected to cover 75% (up to $100,000); City share $23,639 from Airport fund balance. County match for FY27 is uncertain. Routine, staff-recommended.

**A.3 -- HMF Paving Contractors IFB 27-01, miscellaneous concrete work.** Indefinite-delivery, indefinite-quantity contract for sidewalk, curb, gutter, and ADA ramp repairs, funded from the $2M FY27 Trip/Fall Sidewalk Repairs line plus several smaller CIP lines including Equity Emphasis Areas funding. Routine.

**A.4 -- Weatherization services contract, Epic Home Solutions / General Maintenance II / Zerodraft Maryland.** Grant-funded (FY2027) continuation of HHS's weatherization program for income-eligible households -- repairs and energy-efficiency work that lowers utility bills. Directly serves the ALICE population this office tracks. Routine, staff-recommended, positive equity impact stated.

**B.1 -- Carroll Park Manor pest control reimbursement, up to $65,280.** Follows the recently adopted Rodent Control and Abatement ordinance (G-26-6). Covers one year of pest control for all 204 properties in the HOA at $320/unit, funded from the Mayor's Contingency Fund. NAC 5. Responds to a real rodent-infestation complaint pattern; staff-recommended.

**B.2 -- CDBG PY2025 CAPER, public comment period.** No Council vote required -- this is the Citizen Participation Plan's required public hearing before HUD submission (due September 30). Worth a moment to note publicly that CDBG funding targets low- and moderate-income areas, but nothing to act on.

**B.3 / B.4 / C.1 -- Worman's Mill Court Apartments (Standard Wormans Venture LP), three linked items.** The development is 306 multifamily units plus a ~5,000 sq ft nonresidential component on 11.54 acres east of US 15 (rezoned MU-2 in October 2024), across three buildings. B.3 is a PILOT agreement (fiscal terms not detailed in the exec summary you have; flag if you want the PILOT term sheet pulled before the vote). B.4 is the MPDU agreement: although the MPDU Ordinance (Chapter 19) only requires 12.5%/39 units, the developer is restricting **all 306 units** to households at or below 60% AMI for at least 40 years (with 15 units at 50% AMI), tied to a public-funding source that takes precedence over the MPDU Ordinance where the two conflict. C.1 sets the parkland fee-in-lieu at $240,000 for the 2.93 acres the project can't dedicate on-site (Section 608, LMC), based on a fair-market appraisal; the developer is providing 0.58 acres of private amenities (tot lot, dog park, community gardens) toward the requirement. Council's vote on the fee is also what triggers unconditional final site plan approval. Planning Commission conditionally approved the site plan September 8, 2025. All three carry a staff recommendation to approve and a stated positive equity impact. This is a strong deal on paper -- fully income-restricted units are unusual and worth highlighting -- but it's also a large enough commitment (306 units, four decades of restriction, $240,000 fee, a PILOT) that it deserves more than a nod-through if you want the political credit for it.

**D.1 -- Second Read, Ordinance G-26-11 (Maryland Housing Certainty Act implementation).** You introduced this September 3 through City Attorney Rachel Gray. It's an interim, uncodified ordinance aligning City review and approval processes with HB548/SB325 ahead of the Act's October 1, 2026 effective date: defines "complete application" and requires a 30-day completeness determination, vests development rights under the law in effect at complete-application submission (5 years, phased projects treated as separate projects), and restricts impact-fee collection to no earlier than 30 days before final inspection. Staff frames this as a bridge measure while broader LMC conflict-cleanup drafting continues. Fiscal impact is a timing shift only -- impact fee revenue arrives later in the process, not less of it. This is the last point to amend the text before it locks in; see Watch List below for why that matters more than the staff summary suggests.

## Watch List

**Ordinance G-26-11 and the LMC review are on a collision course, and tonight's Second Read is the last easy chance to address it.** The vault's synthesis note on this ([[Maryland Housing Certainty Act ↔ Frederick LMC Reform Synthesis]]) lays it out: once this ordinance is in force, developers who submit complete applications lock in current zoning and fee structures for five years, which means any standards produced by the LMC review won't apply to applications already on file when they were submitted. Libby Taylor's proposed building moratorium during the LMC review was designed to prevent exactly that kind of lock-in, but the moratorium and this ordinance haven't been analyzed together, and the open questions flagged in that note back in April are still open: how many complete applications are already in the pipeline, whether the moratorium (if it advances) would need to halt applications rather than just approvals, and whether the City Attorney has reviewed the interaction. Passing G-26-11 as-is tonight doesn't foreclose fixing this later, but it does start more vesting clocks running while it's unresolved. Worth at least raising the interaction on the record, even if the ordinance itself passes as drafted.

**Worman's Mill Court Apartments is a genuinely good, fully-income-restricted deal, and the politics reward saying so.** All 306 units restricted at or below 60% AMI for 40 years is more than the MPDU Ordinance requires by a wide margin (12.5% vs. 100%). If housing production and affordability is a message you want to reinforce, this is a clean example to point to, distinct from a routine procedural nod-through.

**Nash dissented 5-1 on the Celebrate Frederick MOU at the September 3 meeting, immediately preceding this one.** No reason is recorded in the official minutes. Not itself part of tonight's agenda, but worth knowing about walking in given it's your closest ally on the dais.
