# Council Hearing Brief 2026-08-20

## Bottom Line

Low-conflict meeting. Four grant-acceptance ordinances on consent, two procurement items, and three ordinances up for second read. The only item needing real preparation is Mixed Use Vertical (G-26-10), which you are sponsoring. It resolves the open question from the August 6 brief with a specific numeric threshold rather than a discretionary design standard. Fee Schedule and Automated Enforcement are clean state-law conformance items. One consent agenda item, a $500,000 grant to the Downtown Frederick Hotel project, is worth a look given the dollar size and optics even though it is procedurally routine.

---

## VIII.A.1 -- Summit Anchor Fall Protection Contract (Vote)

$1,448,601.57 contract plus $1,545,571 purchase order for OSHA-mandated fall protection upgrades at 13 water and sewer facilities. Fully funded in the FY26 CIP (130003WS). MWBE participation is 5.2% of subcontractor spend. No NAC impact, internal safety infrastructure. Staff recommends approval. Low risk.

## VIII.A.2 -- A. Morton Thomas Change Order, Worman's Mill Pedestrian Bridge (Vote)

$325,120.30 not-to-exceed for bridge design, part of East Street Rails with Trails Phase 3 (CIP 380004). The Worman's Mill Court Apartments developer is contributing $1,188,000 total toward design and construction, leaving roughly $862,880 for the future construction phase after this design change order. Developer-funded infrastructure. Low risk.

## VIII.D.1 -- Fee Schedule G-26-8 (Second Read)

Housekeeping. Reconciles the taxicab fee schedule with G-26-3 (Vehicles for Hire, passed July 2). Fees drop to the $25 to $50 range. Finance-reviewed, no opposition on record. Clean yes.

## VIII.D.2 -- Automated Enforcement G-26-4 (Second Read)

Introduced by Hempel Irani on June 18. Replaces the flat $40 school-zone speed camera fine with Maryland's HB 182 tiered structure ($40 to $425 by speed over the limit), which took effect statewide October 1, 2025. Frederick's current flat fee is technically out of compliance with state law, so this is conformance, not discretionary policy.

Process flag: the executive summary's equity impact statement discloses that Copilot AI was used to draft it. Not a vote-blocking issue, but worth a quiet word with Rachel Gray about whether AI-drafted equity statements are becoming standard departmental practice, since those statements are supposed to reflect genuine departmental analysis.

## VIII.D.3 -- Mixed Use Vertical LMC Amendment G-26-10 (Second Read)

You are the sponsor. Amends LMC Sections 604, 838, and 1002 to allow residential use on the first floor of mixed-use buildings in MU-1 and MU-2 districts. Driven by the Brickworks project in NAC 11. Planning Commission recommended approval 4-0 on December 8, 2025, citing consistency with the 2020 Comprehensive Plan.

This closes the question the August 6 brief flagged, whether the design standards were specific enough to protect ground-floor retail citywide. The draft ordinance answers it with a bright line rather than a discretionary standard: residential is only allowed on the first floor where total first-floor area is 35,000 square feet or greater.

That is large-footprint-only, so it will not touch small storefronts, which limits citywide retail-activation risk. But it is also a blunt instrument. A large single-use building could qualify regardless of local retail context, and a smaller building where ground-floor retail genuinely is not viable gets no relief. Worth deciding before the vote whether 35,000 square feet is the right line, and worth asking Collard on the record whether that number reflects a policy judgment or was sized to Brickworks' actual first-floor area. Effective date if passed is August 30, 2026.

## Consent Agenda

Four special ordinances, all grant-acceptance budget amendments. No procedural anomalies, but sizes vary.

S-26-15, $4,500 CLG education and training grant from the Maryland Historical Trust. De minimis.

S-26-16, $27,613 DOJ Byrne JAG grant for a radar unit and shields for FPD's Special Response Team. Routine equipment grant.

S-26-17, $150,000 MD DHCD Community Legacy grant for building improvements at the National Museum of Civil War Medicine. Ties directly to the lease and MOU council approved unanimously in April.

S-26-18, $500,000 MD DHCD Strategic Demolition Fund grant for predevelopment work at the Downtown Frederick Hotel and Conference Center, 20 South Carroll Street. This is the largest dollar item on consent, and it is public grant money supporting a $104 million private hotel and conference project that has already drawn $76 million in private capital and $17.52 million in other intergovernmental grants. Procedurally this is just accepting an already-awarded state grant into the CIP, so fiscal and legal risk to the City is low. But given the scale and the optics of stacking public subsidy on a private luxury hospitality development while housing affordability is the Council's stated priority this cycle, it is worth deciding whether to pull it for the record rather than let it pass silently.

## Public Comment Watch

No speakers are listed on the published agenda. If anyone turns out, the most likely draws are the Downtown Frederick Hotel grant given the dollar figure, and Mixed Use Vertical given it is a citywide zoning text change triggered by a single project. Automated Enforcement sometimes draws camera-enforcement objections even when, as here, the ordinance only adjusts penalty tiers rather than expanding the program.

## Watch List

Mixed Use Vertical needs a decision on the 35,000 square foot threshold before the vote, and Collard should be asked on the record whether that number was policy-driven or fit to Brickworks. The Downtown Frederick Hotel grant needs a decision on whether it passes quietly on consent or gets pulled given the subsidy optics against your housing agenda. The Copilot-drafted equity statement on G-26-4 is a process question for Gray, not a vote issue. Nothing else on this agenda carries meaningful political risk.
