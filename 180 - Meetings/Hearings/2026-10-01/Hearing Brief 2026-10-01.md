# Hearing Brief 2026-10-01

**Regular Public Meeting -- October 1, 2026 at 6:00 PM, City Hall**

## Bottom Line

This is your meeting. Resolution 26-15, your affordable housing production resolution, is scheduled for an up-or-down vote, and the packet version fixes some but not all of the problems the vault flagged in [[Housing Production Accountability Ordinance]]. That note, written this week, planned for First Read in November or December; the Clerk has it on the agenda for action now, so the repairs have to happen before Thursday or be accepted as-is. The second substantive item is the Joyfield PILOT, pulled on September 17 and back with an enhanced City credit. Everything else is either routine or introduction only, though the two annexations (about 356 acres combined) and the forest conservation fee deserve a first look because they come back for real votes soon. October 1 is also the effective date of the Maryland Housing Certainty Act, which G-26-11 implemented on September 17.

## Action Items

**VIII.C.1 -- Resolution 26-15, Declaring the City's Commitment to Advancing Affordable Housing Production.** Sponsored by you. Adopts the Mayor's Affordable Housing Task Force report (Opening Doors in Frederick, July 2026) and the TPMA Housing Data Report (February 2026) as the City's evidence base, declares housing essential infrastructure and a human right, adopts the task force's 4,000 additional units over ten years as a production goal, lists nine priority recommendations (annual housing priorities process, an Affordable Housing Navigator and implementation team, tiered AMI-based APFO exemptions, MPDU threshold 25 to 10 with deeper affordability and in-lieu fee revisions, 8 to 10 month expedited review, financing tools, City land disposition, missing-middle and ADU zoning, a production dashboard), commits resources to a Frederick County AMI standard, adds an anti-displacement section, requires an HHS annual Housing Production Report starting FY2028, and calls on the County to set comparable production goals. It updates the draft workshopped September 10.

Checked against the four repairs in [[Housing Production Accountability Ordinance]]:

- **Repair C (the 8,133 figure) is fixed.** The packet correctly describes 8,133 as cost-burdened households earning under $75,000, not a unit shortage.
- **Repair A is only half done.** The "minimum floor" language is gone, and Section 3 now acknowledges 800 to 1,377 units a year of need. But the DHCD 2030 target for the City (5,194 units, 2026 to 2030) is not mentioned anywhere, so the adopted goal of roughly 400 a year still sits at about half the State's number with nothing on the record explaining why.
- **There is a new internal contradiction.** The last finding says the 13,774-unit need compares with "the estimated 4,000 units achievable under current conditions." The preceding finding says 4,000 is what the task force projects from implementing its full package. Those are opposite claims about the same number. Adkins will see this.
- **Repair B is not done.** The 15 percent at or below 80 percent AMI target, with a third of those at or below 60 percent, from the May draft does not appear. Section 3 notes that more than a third of demand is at or below 60 percent AMI but sets no affordability target.
- **Repair D is partly done.** Section 5(D) now pairs the MPDU threshold cut with deeper affordability and in-lieu fee revisions. Section 7 still does not name the Real Rent Ordinance or the water affordability work. Reporting now sits with HHS from FY2028, which is fine if the reporting ordinance follows.

Also worth knowing before the vote. The executive summary's Fiscal Impact, Equity Impact, and Staff Recommendation fields are blank, yet Section 5(B) (Navigator and implementation team) and Section 6 (FCAMI development) commit resources, so expect a fiscal question from Brehm or the Administration. The resolution's "Approved for Legal Sufficiency" line is unsigned in the packet and Notion still shows legal review not cleared. Section 9's language that the County "has an obligation" not to rely on municipalities for growth lands the same night the County Executive is a co-signer on the Joyfield PILOT; it is defensible but will be read in Winchester Hall. Finally, the vault still disagrees with itself about whether the May Housing Production Target Resolution was ever adopted; the public framing ("builds on what we passed" versus "first adoption") depends on that answer from Phyllis Hane.

On the resolution-versus-ordinance question, Section 10 says plainly this creates no enforceable obligations. It is worth passing only as step one of the sequence already mapped: this resolution, then the Chapter 19 reporting ordinance. Saying that from the dais on Thursday puts the second step on the record.

**VIII.B.1 -- Joyfield at Wormans Court PILOT (Standard Wormans Venture LP, joint with Frederick County).** Pulled from the September 17 agenda; the MPDU agreement and parkland fee-in-lieu for the same project passed 5-0 that night. The project is 306 units in three buildings, all at or below 60 percent AMI for at least 40 years, with 15 units (5 percent) at 50 percent AMI. The draft gives a City base credit of $468.59 per unit plus an Enhanced City credit of $325.41 per unit, $794 total, which is $242,964 a year across 306 units, and a County credit of $520 per unit ($159,120 a year). The City collects no less than $100 per unit ($30,600 a year floor). Credits are fixed dollars, not a percentage, so as assessments and rates rise the City's net collection rises too. Over a 40-year term the City credit totals roughly $9.7 million in nominal dollars. The enhanced credit is justified under Resolution 24-05 on two grounds: the 15 deeper-affordability units and the assemblage of low-value abandoned edge lots. HHS signals it wants zero-tax PILOTs reserved for projects with more deeply affordable units relative to size, which is why this one gets "enhanced" rather than full. Protections are solid: 90-day transfer notice, City and County consent on unaffiliated transfers, audited annual financials, a continuing-need certification from year 15, recapture on units taken out of compliance, and a City/County right of first refusal (subordinate to transfers to Standard affiliates). Legal (IA 9/21) and Budget (RR 9/25) have signed off. Staff recommends approval.

The equity case is strong: 306 permanently income-restricted units is the kind of production Resolution 26-15 is about, and voting on both the same night is a clean message. The honest limit is depth. 5 percent at 50 percent AMI and nothing at 30 percent AMI means the households at the bottom of the ALICE distribution are not served here. Small drafting issues you might ask Legal to clean up before signature: the executive summary title says "Woman's Court," Section 16 refers to Exhibits A and B while the attachments are labeled B and C, and the fixed credit amounts have no escalator in either direction.

## Consent Agenda

One item, release of a $21,000 cash surety to FCSCTF, Inc. for 109 West 13th Street (35th Student House). Routine, nothing to pull.

## Appointments

Nine appointments, most routine. Four worth a glance:

**Fair Housing Commission, Melissa Cooper.** Well qualified (military housing policy, fair housing training). The real news is in the backup: the Commission has 4 vacancies out of 5 full members. A one-member Fair Housing Commission cannot hear complaints. For a council whose priorities are renters and immigrants, this is worth raising from the dais and with HHS: how many fair housing complaints are pending, and what is the recruitment plan? It also connects to the anti-displacement language in Resolution 26-15.

**Planning Commission, Kathryn Stewart.** Real estate, land use, and municipal law attorney at Bowles Rice, advising the City of Martinsburg. Staff and the Mayor recommend. Her application says she and her husband are new to Frederick and describes "professional roots I'm planting in West Virginia." Two fair questions: confirm City residency meets the Planning Commission requirement, and ask how she will handle recusals if Bowles Rice represents applicants before the Commission. She will be voting on the LMC review and both annexations below.

**Ethics Commission, Phil Bowers (reappointment).** Routine on the merits. Politically, the Ethics Commission has the Nash complaint history and Nash's complaint against the Mayor in front of it, and the Nash story made the paper September 17. Expect nothing, but be ready if anyone links the reappointment to those matters. Backup shows term ending 10/02/2029; agenda says 10/1/2029.

**NAC 3 Coordinator, John Cetrone.** The agenda calls it an appointment; the Mayor's note calls it a reappointment, and the backup form's checkboxes are blank. Cetrone is the Tuscarora Creek HOA president; NAC 3 is also where both annexations below would land. Worth a quick check with the Clerk that the agenda wording is right.

Lee Hand (ZBA), Rob Fox (Parks), and the three Weinberg board appointments are routine. The ZBA also has a second full-member vacancy, which matters because G-26-12 routes the bigger home businesses to ZBA.

## Introductions and Announcements (no discussion or public comment Thursday)

**G-26-12, Home Occupations.** Repeals and replaces LMC 829 with three tiers. No-Impact (including telework) needs no zoning permit. Low-Impact allows personal and professional services by appointment, unlimited in RO and downtown zones with parking, but capped at five appointments a week and one at a time everywhere else. Conditional Use (ZBA approval) allows two outside employees and up to 20 visits a week. Planning Commission voted 5-0. The staff equity statement, citing APA, notes restrictive rules fall hardest on women, minority, disabled, older, and single-parent business owners. This is a good ordinance on your equity terms. One gap: the only public comment in the packet is from Sara Elijah, a 73-year-old acupuncturist in the Market Square (MU-1) neighborhood living on Social Security, who sees about ten patients a week, one at a time. Under the five-per-week cap she would still need a ZBA conditional use. If the five-visit cap is the right number for R districts, it may be too low for mixed-use districts like MU-1 that were marketed as live-work. That is an amendment question for second reading on October 15.

**G-26-13 and G-26-14, Forest Conservation and fee-in-lieu.** G-26-13 updates LMC 721 for State SB 234 and SB 526, adds specimen tree mitigation at twice the mature canopy of healthy trees removed, drops the street-tree bonding change after the August 13 work session, and adds public notice for administratively approved plans. G-26-14 moves the fee-in-lieu of afforestation from the Code into the fee schedule and raises it from $0.30 per square foot, which staff say does not cover the cost of planting. **The G-26-14 fee ordinance itself and the new rate are not in the Drive packet** (the unopened OneDrive zip may contain it). Get the number before October 15. The housing question is the interaction: a higher fee-in-lieu is a cost on every project, including deed-restricted affordable ones, and Resolution 26-15 Section 5(C) and (F) points toward fee exemptions and deferrals for affordable housing. An affordability exemption or deferral for deed-restricted projects is worth raising with Planning before the vote.

**Milestone annexation (PC26-176ANX), 156.446 acres west of US 15 north of Sundays Lane, R8.** Petition states the intent is a PND with 970 units plus MPDUs, which would be one of the larger production gains available to the City. Three things to watch as it moves: City taxes deferred up to 15 years or until connection to services (the Yellow Springs petition asks for 7); the County sewer map shows the site as "No Planned Service," so sewer is a County decision; and the petition says the City will not require additional off-site improvements. It abuts Bloomfields, an age-restricted community, so expect organized comment when it comes back. The petition dates the Milestone deed March 3, 2005 in one place and 2025 in another; the 2025 Liber number suggests 2005 is a typo.

**Yellow Springs annexation (PC26-153ANX), 200.011 acres at Winpenny Drive and Yellow Springs Road, R4, plus an Institutional floating zone on the 18.28-acre Yellow Springs Elementary parcel.** R4 low-density single-family on 200 acres is the opposite of what Resolution 26-15 argues for on the same agenda. Taxes deferred up to 7 years, and indefinitely on the Kline Trust parcel until it sells or connects. Also contains the no-off-site-improvements clause. A natural place to test whether the Council means what it adopts in 26-15.

Both annexations go to technical review, then Planning Commission workshop and hearing, then back to Council. Neither has a fiscal or equity analysis yet.

## Public Comment

No scheduled speakers. The housing resolution is the most likely draw; housing advocates and task force members may come out for it. The Fair Housing Commission vacancy could prompt comment if it becomes known. The Nash controversy drew comment and council remarks on September 17 and could resurface during Council Business.

## Watch List

**Your resolution's numbers are the only real risk on the agenda.** The 4,000 "achievable under current conditions" line contradicts the finding right above it, and the DHCD target is absent. Adkins objected to the methodology in committee; a public correction on your own resolution is avoidable. Options before Thursday are a floor amendment or accepting the packet version and fixing it through the reporting ordinance. The redraft language is chat work.

**Coalition math.** On September 17 Taylor and Adkins were absent and the Council ran 5-0 on everything. Hempel Irani called on Nash to step down as Vice President and Government Operations chair, and Shackelford criticized leadership without naming names. Both were rated likely favorable on the resolution. Do not assume the split between Nash and Hempel Irani stays out of the housing vote, and consider who moves and seconds.

**September 17 minutes have small errors worth fixing on approval.** "Council member Hempe Irani" (VIII.A.2.a), "Mixed Use deployment project" (should be development, VIII.C.1), "Maryland Certainty Act" (Maryland Housing Certainty Act, VIII.D.1), "raided concerns" (raised), and "stated that the annual In the Streets to be a great success." Otherwise they confirm the vault record, including G-26-11 passing 5-0.

**Fair Housing Commission quorum.** One of five seats filled after Thursday. Quiet but material for renters.
