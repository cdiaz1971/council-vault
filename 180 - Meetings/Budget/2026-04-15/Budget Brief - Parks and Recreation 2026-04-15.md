# FY2027 Budget -- Parks and Recreation Department (Hearing 2026-04-15)

## Summary

Parks and Recreation budget hearing held April 15, 2026. The department operates at a real scale of $9-12 million annually across all divisions (Parks, Recreation Centers, Golf, Urban Forestry). The FY2027 budget reflects significant salary growth, a 25% electricity cost increase, and a new dedicated youth programming coordinator position. The Reduced Registration Program -- the department's primary low-income access mechanism -- is growing fast but delivering thin per-household benefit. Westside Regional Park Community Center is at 60% design and remains on track.

## Budget Numbers (Full Department -- 5-Year Historical Basis)

The memo summary figures ($1,439,882 expenditures / $1,225,500 revenues) represent only a subset of the department, likely the Recreation administrative cost center. Full department actuals:

- **FY2025 actual total expenditures:** $12,167,762
- **FY2024 actual:** $10,307,438
- **FY2023 actual:** $9,224,900
- **FY2026 CYTD:** $9,493,635 (with months remaining)

Parks Division alone (div. 4111) shows $3,071,458+ proposed in the detail PDF. Do not use the memo summary totals for comparison purposes.

## Key Cost Pressures

- **Salaries (Parks Division):** $2,238,804 proposed vs. $2,093,236 adopted -- $145,568 increase (7%). Significantly above inflation. Reflects either headcount growth or compounding step increases from FY2026 reorganization.
- **Electricity:** $202,500 proposed vs. $150,000 adopted -- $52,500 increase (25%). No narrative justification in line items. Ask whether this reflects rate increases, expanded facilities, or deferred cost recognition.
- **Workers compensation:** $123,027 proposed (up from $117,166). Highest workers comp of any department reviewed at this hearing. Physical workforce, large headcount. 5% annual increase on a high base is a structural cost pressure.
- **Overtime:** Budgeted at $85,000. Historical actuals: $113,390 (FY2024), $79,454 (FY2025), $50,305 (FY2026 CYTD with months remaining). Volatile -- Parks runs over in high-use years.
- **Capital:** $235,000 proposed vs. $60,000 adopted FY2026. Items: parks vehicles and equipment (mowers, utility vehicles), HVAC at Talley Recreation Center (two units at end of service life), flooring at Talley, pool furniture at Diggs Pool. All operational maintenance.

## New Resource Request: Recreation Coordinator -- Youth Programming

New position to manage dedicated youth programming oversight. Justification is strong:

- Program registration trending toward 30% increase from FY2025
- Reduced Registration Program (low-income access) enrollments up 102% in FY2026 to date
- Afterschool programs now at four FCPS elementary schools: Butterfly Ridge, Parkway, Monocacy, Waverley
- Early childhood programming launched this FY, already expanding

The 102% increase in Reduced Registration Program participation is the key equity data point -- the program specifically serves households qualifying for free/reduced meals, and its fastest growth year aligns exactly with this new position request.

## Reduced Registration Program -- The Equity Gap

This is the department's primary low-income access mechanism. Current numbers:

- **Qualifying households:** 399
- **Total savings delivered FY2026 to date:** $8,767
- **Implied average benefit per household:** ~$22

$22 per household is a thin benefit. It suggests either the per-program subsidy amount is low, participation rates among qualifying households are modest, or both. The 102% enrollment increase suggests awareness is growing but the benefit ceiling may be limiting actual usage.

**Ask Director Smith:** What is the average benefit per qualifying household? What barriers prevent higher uptake? Does the FY2027 budget include any increase in the program's funding capacity or per-household subsidy ceiling?

## Westside Regional Park Community Center

At 60% design as of this hearing. Design completion expected in FY2027 with construction procurement to follow. WSRP serves a population with demographic overlap with District 2. This project is a multi-year equity infrastructure investment. Ask for: construction procurement timeline and anticipated total project cost.

## Urban Forestry -- Notable Grant Win

$494,000 secured from the Chesapeake Bay Trust Urban Trees Grant for 1,190 trees in underserved areas identified by the 2021 DNR Urban Tree Canopy Assessment. Planting began mid-March, scheduled for completion by summer. Externally funded equity win -- worth public acknowledgment.

## Geographic Equity in Park Renovations

The completed and near-completed project list is notably non-downtown-centered: Hill Street, Lucas Village, College Estates, Overlook, Linden Hills. This contrasts favorably with the broader city capital project list pattern flagged in the April 8 budget work session. The Hill Street Skate Park design/build (contract awarded March 2026, completion late winter 2027) involves extensive community outreach and is a direct investment in a working-class neighborhood.

## Park Metrics (NRPA Comparisons)

Department is performing above national averages on all tracked metrics:
- Acres of parkland per 1,000 residents: 11.5 (national avg 10.2)
- Residents per park: 1,139 (national avg 2,411)
- Programs offered annually: 336 (national avg 250)
- Every resident within 0.5-1 mile of a park or recreation area

## Questions for Tonight's Hearing

1. The electricity line increased 25% with no narrative explanation -- what is driving that increase?
2. The Reduced Registration Program is growing fast but the average benefit per qualifying household appears to be around $22. Is there a funding ceiling on the program, and does FY2027 include any increase in per-household benefit capacity?
3. When does construction procurement begin for the Westside Regional Park Community Center, and what is the anticipated total project cost?
4. Salary costs in the Parks Division increased 7% -- is this headcount growth, step increases, or both?
5. What is the plan for language access and outreach in the Hill Street Skate Park community engagement process, given the demographic profile of that neighborhood?
