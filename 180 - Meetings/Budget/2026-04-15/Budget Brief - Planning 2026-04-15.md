# FY2027 Budget -- Planning Department (Hearing 2026-04-15)

## Summary

Planning Department budget hearing held April 15, 2026. Department requests $3,071,458 in total expenditures (up 11% from FY2026 adopted of $2,766,944), with two explicitly labeled one-time items totaling $287,000. Top priorities are OpenGov implementation, East Frederick Form Based Code adoption, and the Let's Move Frederick active mobility plan. No new housing production investment in the Mayor's proposal.

## Budget Numbers

- **Proposed expenditures:** $3,071,458
- **FY2026 adopted:** $2,766,944
- **Increase:** $304,514 (11%)
- **Revenues:** $299,000 (covers ~10% of expenditures)
- **Net general fund draw:** ~$2.77 million

Note: The OpenGov budget summary page shows different totals ($1,936,517 proposed / $1,718,529 adopted). The detail PDF figures above are the authoritative accounting scope and include full benefit allocations.

## Key One-Time Items

Both are explicitly tagged ONE-TIME in the detail PDF:

- **Mill Pond House stabilization:** $187,000 (Maintenance Contracts line). Engineering, structural support, masonry, regrading of a c.1746 historic structure in Worman's Mill. State bond bill for $180,000 introduced by Sen. Lewis Young et al. -- city funding serves as supplement or backstop.
- **Document scanning:** $100,000 (Professional Services line). Converts paper files to digital, frees cabinet space for future staff growth.

Combined one-time total: $287,000 (~9% of proposed expenditures). Removing these, the recurring budget increase is modest and structurally clean.

## Recurring Cost Pressures

- **Salaries:** $1,727,795 proposed vs. $1,658,728 adopted (+4%). Reflects FY2026 reorganization positions now at full-year cost.
- **25-Year Retirement Plan:** $126,785 vs. $83,669 adopted -- a 34% jump. Structural pension cost pressure, citywide pattern.
- **Professional Services (recurring):** $178,300 recurring, including $60,000 archaeology consultant (Sites of Enslavement project), $60,000 general consultants, $38,800 commission stipends.
- **Forest Conservation Fees:** up 50% ($10,000 to $20,000) -- reflects development activity volume confidence.
- **Advertising:** up 50% ($10,000 to $15,000), all recurring, for commission public notice requirements.

## Department Priorities and Strategic Flags

### OpenGov Implementation
Highest operational priority. Ten application types must be online by October 31, 2026 (APFO, Annexation, Master Plans, Zoning Map Amendments, Preliminary Plats, Final Site Plans, Final Plats, Forest Conservation Plans, Fence Modifications, Demolition Review). Concrete, testable commitment -- useful follow-up benchmark.

### East Frederick Form Based Code
Stated top policy priority but still in "policy discussions" phase as of FY2027. Eventual outcomes include comprehensive rezoning, new LMC section, potential City Code amendments, and new CIP projects. Timeline to ordinance introduction is unspecified. **No dedicated consultant line in the professional services budget for this work** -- department appears to be doing it in-house. Ask: is staff capacity sufficient to hit their timeline?

### Let's Move Frederick (Active Mobility Plan)
Updated format with measurable goals and framework for bicycle/pedestrian infrastructure investment. Expected to inform CIP and grant applications. Adoption in FY2027.

### MARC Station TOD Designation
Application filed through MDOT. TOD designation can unlock state funding and influence zoning near transit. Intersection with housing production target resolution worth understanding -- ask for status and implications.

### Comprehensive Plan Monitoring Report
First annual report completed FY2026. Will incorporate public engagement component going forward. Annual cadence will inform Work Plan and budget priorities including CIP.

## Equity and Accessibility Flags

The equity section of the memo is thin -- limited to plain language documents and broader audiences for historic preservation. Notable absences:

- No anti-displacement analysis in planning documents
- No language access standards for public engagement (beyond implicit accessibility framing)
- No displacement risk modeling for East Street / Golden Mile corridors, which overlap with higher-density immigrant and working-class neighborhoods
- Sites of Enslavement project (Phase 2) is active and meaningful but not a housing or displacement equity tool

The East Street Form Based Code and Golden Mile Small Area Plan are the two planning initiatives most likely to affect District 2-adjacent communities. Neither memo nor budget contains equity safeguards for those processes.

## Questions for Tonight's Hearing

1. What is the realistic timeline to Form Based Code ordinance introduction? Is staff capacity sufficient given no dedicated consultant funding?
2. Given the East Street and Golden Mile corridors overlap with immigrant and working-class neighborhoods, will community engagement include Spanish-language outreach and displacement risk modeling?
3. What is the status of the MARC Station TOD designation and how does it interact with the city's housing production targets?
4. The OpenGov October 31 deadline is a concrete commitment -- what is the contingency if implementation falls behind?
