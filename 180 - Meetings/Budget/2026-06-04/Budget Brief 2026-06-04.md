# Budget Review Meeting Brief -- 2026-06-04

## Meeting

Participants: César Díaz, VP Katie Nash, Budget Manager Lacey Gordon, CFO Katie Barkdoll

---

## Bottom Line

This meeting is a structured debrief on the FY2027 process and a work plan alignment session for the twelve initiatives on Lacey's agenda. You have clear positions on several of them and meaningful gaps on others. The most politically loaded item is rental licensing and its City Housing Fund implications. Priority-driven budgeting is the most consequential structural item for future cycles.

**Rental Licensing / City Housing Fund**

This is the most substantive item. Brehm is driving a comprehensive rewrite of Article V (Chapter 12.5). The fee cut from $240 to approximately $150 per biennial license reduces City Housing Fund revenue. The 8-year new construction exemption is a significant carve-out. The LIHTC interaction with the government-funding exemption needs clarification. The workshop was completed May 26. The fee rate decision has direct downstream implications for the Real Rent Ordinance threshold. You have not yet staked out a public position on the fee level. Ask Barkdoll for a revenue impact projection on the fee cut before the rewrite advances.

**Tax Reform**

Two distinct sub-items.

Differential / Tiered Property Tax Rate: the concept is a graduated rate structure where higher-assessed properties pay a higher rate and lower-assessed properties pay a lower rate. This shifts tax burden away from lower-value properties, which disproportionately include owner-occupied homes in working-class neighborhoods and smaller rental properties. The equity case is strong given District 2 demographics and the 43% city ALICE rate. Key threshold question: Maryland law may require state enabling legislation for a tiered rate structure, and preemption risk has not been confirmed. Do not commit to a legislative vehicle until that answer is in hand. Ask Barkdoll whether Finance has engaged with the City Attorney on preemption risk.

Business Personal Property Tax Reinstatement: the city previously had this tax and it was eliminated. Reinstating it generates new revenue from business equipment and inventory rather than real property. Expect pushback from the chamber and commercial landlord interests. The equity framing is that it shifts some burden from residents to commercial actors. Same caution applies: ask whether Finance has a revenue estimate and what the legal pathway looks like.

Your position for this meeting: supportive of exploring both; want to understand legal feasibility before committing to a timeline; request a written feasibility memo from Finance with a revenue projection and a preemption analysis before either item goes to committee.

**Legislative Services Contract**

Two options: hire a lobbyist directly for the Council, or expand the city's existing lobbying contract and have the Council use it.

The political distinction matters. A Council-specific contract gives the Council independent advocacy capacity separate from the Mayor's priorities. The shared contract is cheaper but means the Council's legislative agenda runs through the same firm that represents the administration. Given the strained relationship with O'Connor and the Council's active housing and tenant protection agenda, a shared contract creates a structural conflict risk.

Key questions: what is the current city contract, who is the vendor, and what is it costing? What would a Council-only contract cost? Has the City Attorney weighed in on whether a separate Council contract is permissible under the charter? Gap: no contract details, vendor name, or cost figures are in the vault or Notion. Ask Barkdoll to bring the current contract to the next meeting if she does not have it today.

**Crossing Guard Contract**

Notion status: Pre-Legislative, Q2, Gov Ops Committee vehicle. Purpose is to review the allocation process and prioritize community needs. Gap: no contract terms, no current allocation methodology, no equity analysis on file. Ask Lacey who currently manages the contract and what the equity concern driving the review is.
