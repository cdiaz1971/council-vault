# Budget FY2027 -- Work Session 1 April 8 Notes

## Summary

First FY2027 budget work session, April 8, 2026. Departments presenting: Mayor's Office, Finance, and Legal. No major policy announcements. Three substantive moments: a geographic equity challenge to the Mayor's project list, a Finance proposal to end water shutoffs for non-payment, and an Adkins ask for dedicated infrastructure maintenance funding.

## Key Points

**Mayor's Office**
The Mayor's Office presentation was light on substance. They highlighted projects that have received state funding, but the list was limited to downtown Frederick. Nash and César called this out directly. The pushback upset[[ Gayon Sampson]] and Alan Etzler. No new positions were proposed and nothing of policy significance came from this section.

**Finance**
Finance floated a program to prevent water shutoffs for non-payment. [[Derek Shackelford]] opposed it strongly, framing his objection around non-payment abuse. César expressed support and indicated the idea warrants further exploration. This is the most substantive policy proposal from the session and a potential FY2027 amendment target.

Adkins raised the need to set aside dedicated funding for major infrastructure maintenance, specifically concrete. This is consistent with his prior focus on capital maintenance backlogs and worth tracking as a potential budget amendment or capital request.

**Legal**
Rachel Gray presented for the Legal department. Nothing actionable emerged. Libby Taylor raised a question about the increase in public information act (PIA) requests. The general response from the room was that the volume increase reflects constituent difficulty rather than a systemic issue -- a framing worth watching given the CVA implementation context.

## Relevance

The water shutoff proposal is directly aligned with César's housing stability and tenant protection priorities and could connect to the rental junk fees ordinance framework. If Finance is willing to pursue this administratively, it could move without council legislation -- but council support would accelerate it.

The downtown-only project list is a geographic equity problem. César and Nash's challenge on the record is useful baseline for the May 14 revision authority deadline. District 2 constituents in Hillcrest, Shookstown, and West Patrick are not represented in a downtown-centered capital and grants narrative.

The PIA framing deserves attention: characterizing increased public records requests as constituent "difficulty" runs counter to transparency norms and may merit a direct response from the Council President.
