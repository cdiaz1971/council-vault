## General Fund Overview

Total proposed General Fund: **$160,590,483** vs. $147,832,296 adopted FY2026 (+$12.8M / +8.6%).

Major revenue drivers: real estate tax growth +$3.4M (assessment growth), personal income tax +$3.1M, investment interest +$2.4M (now budgeted to match actuals rather than the previous $1M underbudget).

**Critical structural concern:** The budget proposes drawing **$36,987,995 from fund balance** to balance. This is one-time money covering recurring and capital needs -- a signal that recurring expenses are outpacing recurring revenues. The four draw pools are: Rainy Day Fund ($17.3M), CIP ($12.9M), capital equipment ($5.5M), Rental Licensing Program ($1.3M). The CFO must be asked for the post-draw fund balance and the multi-year sustainability projection.

## Cross-Cutting Themes

**The $37M fund balance draw** is the budget's structural load-bearing problem. Ask the CFO on the record. Do not let this be answered in writing after the fact.

**The equity inversion:** The Equity and Inclusion office is cut 29% (-$102K) in the same year the Office of Opportunities and Transformation grows 54% (+$284K) and the Office of Vendor Engagement and Compliance is created ($529K). Combined new spending on "equity-adjacent" functions: ~$1.34M. Cut to the named equity office: $102K. The Mayor needs to articulate a coherent equity theory of change that explains this pattern.

**New office accountability gap:** Three units in the Mayor's cluster (OOT, OVEC, and the expanded Public Affairs) are growing fast with no performance metrics visible in the budget. The council's leverage is conditioning full funding on submission of outcome reports.

**Regressive cost opportunity:** The city budgets $125K in credit card processing fees that are passed to residents as surcharges. Eliminating this surcharge -- already in the budget -- would be a concrete equity win at no net new cost.
