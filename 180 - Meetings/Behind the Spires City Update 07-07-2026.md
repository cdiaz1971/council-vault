# Behind the Spires: City Update for 07.07.2026

## Summary

Weekly city podcast summary covering the June 11 data center work session, June 25 Gateway Property/cricket stadium rezoning discussion, recent council approvals, and upcoming events including the skate park community meeting and Declaration of Independence reading.

## What You Missed

### Data Centers Work Session (June 11)

- A full work session was dedicated to data centers. No votes were taken; the purpose was information gathering before any zoning changes move forward.

**Loudoun County Supervisor Mike Turner:**
- Loudoun has the world's largest cluster of data centers and draws large tax revenue with little traffic or service cost.
- Power demand has now outgrown the grid there.
- Urged Frederick to settle noise, power, water emissions, setbacks, and appearance standards before discussing revenue.
- Recommended setbacks of at least 500 feet from homes.

**Maryland Office of People's Counsel:**
- Focused on electric bills. Data centers (mostly in Virginia and Ohio) are already driving up regional power costs that Maryland households help pay.
- New state rules aim to make data centers cover their own share of power costs upfront.

**City Director of Economic Development:**
- Data centers pay more in taxes and wages than warehouses, but create very few jobs and use far more power and water.
- Recommended setting strong standards before deciding where data centers can go.
- Cautioned that large projects are unlikely in Frederick given limited land and water supply.

**Council and Public Comment:**
- Council agreed standards must come before site approvals and stressed caution over urgency.
- Public comment was extensive, with most residents opposed, citing rising electric bills, strain on the Monocacy River, air quality, and the low number of jobs created.
- Several speakers called for a moratorium or further study before the city acts.

### Gateway Property Rezoning Work Session (June 25)

- Council spent most of the session on a request to rezone 24 acres east of the city, south of I-70, for a special events center built around a cricket stadium.
- Applicant is the real estate arm of the Washington Freedom Professional Cricket Team.
- Proposal includes:
  - 10,000-person capacity cap
  - Up to 20 matches per year
  - Busing most fans in from the fairgrounds rather than providing on-site parking
- Discussion centered on traffic along two-lane Route 144.
- Council also discussed updating the 2009 resolution that originally brought the property into the city:
  - Road improvement payment raised from $500,000 to $1.5 million, with the first $500,000 due by August 30.
  - A roughly 14-acre public recreation area along the Monocacy River, built at the owner's expense, with a boat launch and a path from East Patrick Street to I-70.

**Public Comment:**
- Most speakers supported the cricket project for its economic and tourism potential and the chance to grow a local cricket community.
- Some raised concerns about Route 144 traffic, noise, and lighting for nearby county homes.
- No decision was made on either item; both return for further discussion.

### Recent Council Actions

**June 18 Council Meeting:**
- Approved a repair contract for the Carroll Creek parking garage roof.
- Forgave a 1993 loan to the Interfaith Housing Alliance for the Weinberg House Senior Apartments.
- Gave second read to new taxicab rules, cutting driver permit fees from $2,500 to as little as $100.

**July 2 Council Meeting:**
- Taxicab overhaul received final approval.
- Updated school zone list approved.
- New multi-year agreement with the Downtown Frederick Partnership approved.

**Planning Commission:**
- Approved an updated Brickworks retail plan off East Street.
- Approved a new hangar building at Frederick Municipal Airport.

**Historic Preservation Commission:**
- Met twice in June; approved a new addition at St. John Cemetery and reviewed several smaller residential cases downtown.
