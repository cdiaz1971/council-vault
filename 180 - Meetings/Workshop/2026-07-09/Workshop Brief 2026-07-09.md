# Council Work Session Brief -- 2026-07-09

## Summary

Full legislative workshop with five items. Three are direct César introductions: E-Bike ordinance, Real Rent, and Rules of Procedure. Two come from other sponsors: LMC vertical mixed-use ZTA (Brickworks applicant) and Rodent Control (Nash). No votes. All items are at the council feedback stage before formal introduction. Real Rent and the Rules amendments carry the highest political stakes. Two structural conflicts require resolution before any ordinance is introduced: both Real Rent and Rodent Control are drafted to add Chapter 12.5, Article VI, which is a direct collision.

---

## Bottom Line

This session is about gauging member appetite and surfacing structural objections before introduction. César controls three of the five items. The Article VI numbering conflict between Real Rent and Rodent Control must be raised with Rachel Gray before either ordinance goes to the Clerk. The LMC ZTA has a two-draft introducer problem that needs to be resolved. AMAC's four outstanding concerns on the E-Bike ordinance are the remaining gate before that item is ready for City Attorney review.

---

## Item A: E-Bike (EPMD) Ordinance

Third workshop. César introducing on behalf of the administration. The draft repeals Chapter 22, Article VI and replaces it with Chapter 33, Article II. Shared-use paths default to permitted unless posted otherwise. Downtown sidewalks prohibited. Commercial dockless rentals banned citywide at $400 per incident. Public Works Committee voted 2-1 for the default-permitted posture on shared-use paths.

AMAC's May 5 letter identifies four unresolved issues:

**E-moto definition gap.** The draft's e-moto definition does not tie cleanly to any Maryland statutory category. AMAC recommends pegging it to "motorized minibike" under Transportation Article 11-134.4. This is a real enforcement clarity problem that needs a fix before introduction.

**Road speed language (33-6(g)).** As written, the provision effectively bans EPMDs from 25 mph local roads. AMAC wants it realigned to mirror state bicycle law. This needs a fix before introduction.

**Passenger restriction (33-6(e)).** The prohibition as written bans cargo bikes with child seats. AMAC recommends allowing passengers when the device is designed or properly modified for them. A straightforward fix.

**Parking fine disparity.** Private EPMD parking violation is $100. Dockless commercial vehicle violation is $30. Motor vehicle ticket is $50. AMAC recommends capping private EPMD at $30. A private device facing a higher fine than a commercial operator is hard to defend.

Action: Drive toward resolution on all four issues. The e-moto definition and road speed language are the substantive legal problems. Get members on record before City Attorney review.

## Item B: LMC Text Amendment -- Vertical Mixed-Use First-Floor Residential (PC25-249ZTA)

Applicant is GG Frederick Holdings, the Brickworks developer at the Monocacy/South Street site. The ZTA amends LMC Sections 604, 838, and 1002 to allow residential on the first floor of mixed-use buildings in MU-1 and MU-2 zones, subject to a 35,000 sq ft minimum first-floor area. Planning Commission voted 4-0 to recommend approval December 8, 2025. Staff supports.

The 35,000 sq ft threshold limits applicability to larger buildings where full retail activation of the first floor is commercially unviable. This is a pro-housing production amendment that removes a barrier discouraging vertically integrated mixed-use development. No obvious opposition signals in the packet.

**Introducer conflict.** The packet contains two draft ordinances: one introduced by César, one by Nash. This must be resolved before the item is scheduled for formal introduction. One draft needs to be withdrawn.

Action: Confirm who carries this before the session. Do not let two competing ordinance drafts reach the Clerk.

## Item C: Rules of Procedure -- Proposed Revisions

César's item. Proposed amendments to Resolution No. 26-1. Key substantive additions:

**Rule 5.8 (Committee Reports).** Each committee chair must give an oral summary at work sessions and a written summary to all members and the Clerk within five business days of any committee meeting with substantive information. Written summary filed as a public record. This is a meaningful transparency and accountability mechanism.

**Section 6 (Special Committees).** New authority for the Council President to form special committees including non-council members, who must be city residents. Duration set by the President.

**Section 11 (Interactions with Staff / Council Communication of Intent).** Formalizes the CCI as the mechanism for the Council to express collective policy direction to staff. Any member can initiate. Requires majority vote to take effect. President signs and assigns official designation. Legislative Manager maintains a tracking log.

**Political flag.** Section 11 will draw scrutiny. It touches the legislative/executive boundary. Rule 11.1 explicitly defers to any agreement between the Mayor and Council leadership, which is the right framing to neutralize a separation-of-powers objection, but expect the Mayor's office to push back. Be prepared to explain the CCI as a transparency tool, not an executive override.

Action: Present the key changes, invite member feedback, signal timeline for a full council vote.

## Item D: Real Rent Ordinance (Rental Fees and Disclosures)

César's item. Adds Chapter 12.5, Article VI. Effective January 1, 2027.

Core structure: All mandatory costs of tenancy must be rolled into Total Rent. Landlords cannot advertise below total rent. Prohibited fees include admin/management fees, move-in/out fees, technology/portal fees, payment processing fees, pest control and maintenance fees charged separately, utility admin fees, lease renewal fees, and any charge that recovers ordinary operating costs. Pet deposits must be disclosed as refundable and returned within 45 days with itemized deductions. Application fees capped at actual screening costs, refunded if no screening performed. Utility charges permitted only if based on actual metered usage or a disclosed allocation formula.

Enforcement: $500 first violation, $1,000 second, $2,500 third or subsequent per calendar year. Violations also grounds for rental license denial or revocation.

**Article VI conflict.** Both this ordinance and the Rodent Control ordinance (Item E) add Chapter 12.5, Article VI. This is a direct collision that must be resolved with Rachel Gray before either ordinance goes to the Clerk.

**Key issues to watch in discussion:**

Anti-circumvention: The "mandatory fee" definition uses a 25% threshold presumption and a denial-of-lease test. This is stronger than peer models. Members may raise enforceability questions.

Private right of action: The draft does not include one. Enforcement is purely through municipal infractions and license action. This has been a contested point in prior research. Have a position ready if members raise it.

The January 1, 2027 effective date gives landlords roughly six months to comply. Reasonable and defensible.

Action: Gauge member appetite, surface structural objections early, defend the all-in pricing model as the core principle. Raise the Article VI conflict with Rachel Gray.

## Item E: Rodent Control Ordinance

Nash's item. Adds Chapter 12.5, Article VI (see Article VI conflict under Item D). Establishes owner and occupant rodentproofing duties, inspection and compliance mechanisms, a RAT Plan for widespread infestations, an annual public report, and civil penalties up to $5,000 for repeat violations. César's role is supportive non-co-sponsor.

**Issues to surface:**

Tenant liability under Sec. 12.5-92 assigns rodent control responsibility to occupants in areas they occupy. In substandard housing with structural rodentproofing failures, this can put the burden on the wrong party. Preferred fix: an affirmative defense provision tying occupant duty to landlord compliance with rodentproofing obligations.

COMAR 15.05 preemption: The ordinance's certified pest control applicator requirements reference COMAR 15.05. Confirm with Rachel Gray that the City's enforcement framework does not conflict with state licensing authority.

Vacant building inspection mandate (Sec. 12.5-93(g)): Annual inspections by a certified applicator, certified to the Department within seven days. New meaningful obligation on vacant property owners.

Action: Supportive posture. Surface the tenant liability concern and the Article VI numbering conflict. Do not carry the item.

## Structural Flags

**Article VI collision.** Both Real Rent (Item D) and Rodent Control (Item E) are drafted to add Chapter 12.5, Article VI. One must be redesignated before either is introduced. Raise with Rachel Gray before this session ends.

**LMC ZTA introducer conflict.** Two competing ordinance drafts in the packet, one under César's name and one under Nash's. Resolve before scheduling for introduction.

---

## Public Comment Watch

Real Rent will draw landlord opposition. Expect pushback on the mandatory fee presumption and enforceability. The Brickworks ZTA may draw neighborhood voices. No Washington Freedom cricket project presence expected on this agenda (their Gateway property special events matter was a separate item at the June 25 session).
