# Workshop Brief -- September 10, 2026

**Work Session -- September 10, 2026, time and location not confirmed in the materials on file (recent work sessions have started at 6:00 P.M.)**

## Bottom Line

Four items, all discussion only, no votes tonight, since work sessions don't take votes. Three of the four are housing items, and together they're a useful test of whether the council is driving the housing agenda or reacting to the Mayor's task force: the Joyfield PILOT is a concrete 306-unit affordable project headed to a vote September 17, the Affordable Housing Action Resolution is your own vehicle for claiming the Task Force recommendations as council priorities before the Mayor frames them as his, and the Housing Certainty Act ordinance is a compliance-driven interim measure the Administration needs before the state law takes effect October 1. The fourth item, a Husky Park correction, is a small but pointed admission that the record on CIP 320044 still isn't clean.

## Action Items

**C -- Resolution, "Declaring the City of Frederick's Commitment to Action on Affordable Housing."** This is your resolution, at the workshop-feedback stage, not yet introduced. It formally endorses the Mayor's Affordable Housing Task Force final report and the TPMA housing data report, sets a production floor of 4,000 units over ten years against a documented 13,774-unit need by 2035, lists nine high-priority Task Force recommendations (Affordable Housing Commission, a Housing Navigator position, APFO reform, MPDU modernization, expedited review, financing tools, land disposition reform, zoning reforms, an annual production dashboard), and directs HHS to publish an annual Housing Production Report starting FY2028. [[MAHTF Final Report Policy Brief -- 2026-07-16]] is the direct prep document for this item: it argues the Task Force report is a mayoral instrument and that you need a public position distinguishing the council's agenda from the Mayor's before he claims the frame. Two things worth raising tonight, drawn from that brief and from [[Housing Affordability Policy Brief -- 2026-09-08]]: the resolution's Section 4 lists MPDU threshold reduction (25 to 10 units) without pairing it to the fee increase and depth requirements that make the change net-positive rather than net-negative for affordability, and the resolution doesn't name tenant protection as a parallel track even though the underlying Task Force report is entirely supply-side. Both are fixable in the redraft this item is headed toward.

**D -- Ordinance implementing the Maryland Housing Certainty Act.** Already had its First Read September 3; tonight's discussion precedes Second Read. City Attorney Rachel Gray is the actual sponsor, introduced through you on the Administration's behalf. It's a narrow, interim measure: defines "complete application," locks in a five-year vesting period once a housing project is fully approved, and delays impact fee collection to within 30 days of final inspection, to bring the City into compliance with state law (HB 548/SB 325) before it takes effect October 1. The substantive risk isn't in this ordinance, it's in what it interacts with: [[Maryland Housing Certainty Act ↔ Frederick LMC Reform Synthesis]] flags that every complete application filed from now on vests under whatever rules exist at filing, which means the LMC review (and Libby Taylor's building moratorium proposal, if it moves) is racing a clock that just started running. Worth asking staff directly tonight how many complete applications are already in the pipeline.

**B -- PILOT agreement for Joyfield at Worman's Mill.** A joint City-County Payment in Lieu of Taxes agreement for a 306-unit, 100% affordable (50-60% AMI) development from Standard Communities at 60/62/64/66 Worman's Mill Court, annual tax credit to the project of $242,964, staff recommends adoption, and a vote is already scheduled for September 17. The site sits in Council Member Adkins' District 4 (which includes Worman's Mill). Adkins' profile describes him as supply-side but not progressive on housing, supportive of production but with no documented record on affordability mandates, so a straightforward PILOT for a fully affordable project is more likely to land as a production win in his own district than as a fight. One packaging gap: B.1's executive summary lists a City-prepared PowerPoint on the PILOT among its attachments, but only Standard Communities' own decks (B.3, B.4) are in the folder. Worth confirming staff brings that deck, or that it existed at all, before the September 17 vote.

## Presentations and Reports

**A -- Correction on Husky Park forest easement (CIP 320044).** Informational only, no fiscal impact, no vote. COO Zack Kershner is correcting the record from the August 27 work session: staff had believed the Renn Final Forest Conservation Plan extinguished the 1995 forest conservation easement on Husky Park, and it did not. A future City action to formally release and abandon that easement will be needed. This lands directly on the documentation dispute [[Workshop Brief - Husky Park Rebuttal 2026-08-27]] covers in detail: that memo already identified the missing FCP 94-8 boundary map and the unresolved easement status as the single strongest point in resident Kevin Hluch's case against CIP 320044, and recommended asking staff on the record whether they intend to commission a new survey and file a 2:1 replacement plan. Tonight's correction confirms the underlying problem was real. The Notion Legislative Roadmap tracks this as "Husky Park -- CIP 320044 Bond Street Extension (Documentation Dispute)," Status: Study Underway, Priority: High, still blocked on the City Attorney's PRK-zoning opinion, the FCP 94-8 documentation, and a Phase I environmental assessment, none of which this correction resolves.

## Watch List

The Housing Certainty Act ordinance (D) and the LMC review/moratorium track are on a collision course that hasn't been resolved on the record. Every month of delay on the LMC review is a month of complete applications vesting under current, unreformed rules.

The Affordable Housing Action Resolution (C) needs to read as council-owned, not as a ratification of the Mayor's task force. The task force report was written for Mayor O'Connor, and his transmittal letter already frames implementation as his administration's work with the council in a supporting role.

Brehm's rental licensing fee reduction proposal (cutting the biennial fee from $240 to roughly $150) is in direct tension with the Housing Fund capitalization case this resolution is built on, since the Task Force itself names rental licensing revenue as a funding source. That conflict is unresolved and could surface if anyone connects the two tonight.

The Joyfield PILOT (B) sits in Adkins' district; watch for him raising developer-contribution or process questions given his planning background, though nothing in his record suggests opposition to production itself.
