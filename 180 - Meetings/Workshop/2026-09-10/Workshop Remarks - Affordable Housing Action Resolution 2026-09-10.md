## Formal Remarks

Thank you. I want to walk through the resolution in front of you tonight declaring the City's commitment to affordable housing action.

This isn't a new commitment. It builds directly on the housing production target this Council already adopted in May. What it does is take the Mayor's Affordable Housing Task Force report, which came out in July, and turn the pieces of it that fall within our authority into something the Council owns and drives, rather than something we simply react to.

The numbers behind this aren't new either. We need roughly 13,774 units by 2035 to meet documented demand. The Task Force projects that its full recommendation package, if implemented, gets us to about 4,000 units over ten years. I want to be direct about that gap. Four thousand is not thirteen thousand seven hundred seventy four. This resolution names that gap publicly rather than treating the Task Force report as if it solves the problem on its own.

What the resolution actually asks of the Administration is nine specific things drawn from the Task Force's own highest priority findings. An Affordable Housing Commission. A Navigator position to help stalled projects pencil. Reform to the Adequate Public Facilities Ordinance. Changes to the MPDU threshold and rate. Faster permitting review. New financing tools like TIF and PILOT programs. A better process for city owned land. Zoning reforms for missing middle housing and faith owned land. And an annual public report on production, starting in fiscal year 2028.

Two things I want your feedback on specifically tonight. First, the MPDU section as drafted lowers the unit threshold but doesn't pair that with the fee increase and depth requirements that make the change actually strengthen affordability rather than weaken it. I plan to fix that before this comes back for introduction. Second, this resolution needs a clearer connection to the tenant protection work already in front of this Council, the Real Rent Ordinance and the rental licensing rewrite, so production and protection read as two tracks moving together, not one substituting for the other.

I'm not asking for a vote tonight. I'm asking for your reaction to this list, and specifically whether there's anything here you think shouldn't be on it, or anything missing that should be. I'll take what I hear tonight, work with staff on a revised draft, and bring it back for introduction at a future meeting.

## One-Page Handout for Colleagues

**Affordable Housing Action Resolution -- Work Session Discussion, September 10, 2026**

**Sponsor.** Council President Díaz. Not yet introduced, discussion only tonight.

**What it does.** Formally adopts the Mayor's Affordable Housing Task Force findings as the City's evidence base for housing policy, sets a production floor of 4,000 additional units over ten years against a documented need of 13,774 units by 2035, and directs the Administration to treat nine Task Force recommendations as an active work plan.

**The nine priority items.**
- Affordable Housing Commission
- Affordable Housing Navigator position
- APFO reform with tiered AMI weighting
- MPDU threshold reduction, 25 units down to 10
- Expedited review pathway targeting 8 to 10 months
- New financing tools, TIF, CDFI partnerships, PILOT programs, fee waivers
- City land disposition reform and RFP process
- Zoning reforms for missing middle housing and faith owned land
- Annual public Housing Production Report, starting FY2028

**Two items the sponsor is already flagging for revision before introduction.**
- The MPDU threshold change needs to move together with the in lieu fee increase and deeper affordability requirements, not alone
- The resolution needs a direct link to the Real Rent Ordinance and the rental licensing rewrite already before this Council

**What's being asked tonight.** Not a vote. Feedback on the list of nine priorities, and on the two flagged revisions, before this comes back for introduction at a future public meeting.
