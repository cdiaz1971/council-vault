# Workshop Brief -- September 24, 2026

**City Council Work Session -- September 24, 2026** (time and location not stated on the published agenda; recent work sessions have started at 6:00 PM per the September 10, 2026 official minutes)

## Bottom Line

Three substantive items, none of them votes. SPARK is a low-stakes budget-signaling exercise worth five minutes of real thought, since your allocation becomes part of the record staff use to frame the FY2028 budget. The real property tax discussion is the one to walk in prepared for: staff has modeled a Land Value Tax that raises taxes on 99%+ of Hillcrest and Amber Meadows properties -- both in your own district -- while cutting apartment/multifamily taxes by up to 46%, and nobody on staff has flagged that as an equity problem yet. Brehm's major-site-plan text amendment is a genuine governance question -- more Council leverage over land use, at the cost of up to three additional months of discretionary review on exactly the missing-middle housing types (5+ unit townhouse and multifamily) you're trying to accelerate -- and it's moving on its own track outside the LMC Review Commission that's supposed to be looking at approval-process reform holistically.

## Action Items

**B -- Discussion of Real Property Taxes.** CAO Katie Barkdoll is presenting three alternative structures to the current uniform $0.7055/$100 rate: split residential/nonresidential rates, a tiered rate by assessed value (which staff's own deck flags as running into constant-yield and Homestead Tax Credit administration problems), and a Land Value Tax, either pure or a 2:1 hybrid split-rate. All three are framed as revenue-neutral. The LVT modeling is the one that matters most for you specifically: preliminary parcel-level data shows Hillcrest and Amber Meadows -- both District 2 -- would see 99.5-99.7% of properties pay more (median increase $263 under the hybrid, $822 under pure LVT), while Worman's Mill and Dearbought would see taxes fall for roughly 70-98% of properties. Citywide, apartments and multifamily properties see the largest reduction of any category (-14.1% hybrid, -45.9% pure LVT), while residential overall picks up 1.9-3.8% more of the total levy. Staff's equity impact statement is a one-line "additional analysis would be necessary" -- there's no framing yet of who actually benefits from the apartment-tax relief (owners, not necessarily tenants, absent a pass-through requirement) or why a lower-income, majority-minority district would be asked to pay more under a tax reform pitched as encouraging investment. Worth raising directly: whether any LVT proposal comes back with a tenant pass-through mechanism and a per-neighborhood equity analysis before it goes further, and whether the same revenue-neutral framing could be run holding District 2 harmless. No staff recommendation and no next steps are stated in the packet, so this reads as genuinely exploratory -- an opening to shape the analysis before it hardens into a specific proposal.

**C -- Text Amendment, Final Approval of Major Site Plans.** Brehm's draft amends LMC Section 309 so Council can vote, within 60 days of a major site plan application's submission, to assume final reviewing authority over that application -- currently the Planning Commission's job -- with a 30-day clock to approve, approve with revisions, or deny once Council takes it up (silence defaults to the Commission's recommendation). It applies to any 5+ unit townhouse/quadraplex/multifamily development, all new non-residential construction, and major expansions or redevelopments. Brehm's rationale is sound as far as it goes: annexation and zoning votes happen before traffic, parking, and design details are known, so Council is often asked to approve a project's shape before it knows what that shape actually is. The tension worth naming out loud: this is exactly the housing production question the LMC Review Commission is supposed to own. [[LMC Review Commission -- Draft Resolution Analysis -- 2026-04-12]] tasks that commission with reviewing zoning and approval processes for "predictability, effectiveness, and equity" as part of a 24-month, council-appointed review -- Brehm's amendment moves the same question on a separate, faster track before that process even starts. It also cuts directly against housing production: a discretionary Council review layer, on top of the existing process, on precisely the 5+ unit missing-middle projects that are the housing type Frederick most needs more of. That doesn't mean it's wrong -- more Council accountability over site plans is a legitimate ask -- but it's worth deciding tonight whether you want it decided piecemeal here or folded into the Review Commission's mandate once that's seated. This is Brehm's item for initial feedback only; he takes tonight's comments into the draft before submitting it to the Planning Commission. Not yet in Notion as a legislative item -- added tonight at Workshop status, Owner Brehm, so it's tracked going forward.

## Presentations and Reports

**A -- SPARK Tool Overview.** Ericka Rohrbaugh and Lacey Gordon are introducing SPARK ("Strategic Priority Alignment & Resource Kickoff"): each Council member gets 100 points to allocate across the six CommUNITY 2030 strategic goals (Sustainable Urban Planning, Social Well-Being, Safe & Vibrant Community, Competitive Employment, Enhanced Mobility, Civic Engagement) and their objectives, signaling relative priority ahead of the FY2028 budget. Staff is explicit that this isn't a budget vote or a department ranking -- it's input into the budget process, due back to Rohrbaugh by October 9, with results returning to Council October 22. Worth noting the family resemblance to the Priority-Driven Budgeting framework VP Nash introduced at Government Operations in April: SPARK is the administration's own version of asking "what outcomes matter most" before money is allocated, which is precisely the PDB inversion (results first, then funding) you've been building an equity case around. [[Priority-Driven Budgeting -- Strategic Position]] has you as an active co-champion of Nash's PDB push without getting ahead of her -- SPARK gives you a low-cost, administration-sanctioned opportunity to put points behind housing and equity-adjacent objectives and cite it later as evidence the Council was already signaling those priorities, without this being read as a PDB move at all. Worth deciding your own point allocation with that connection in mind rather than as a standalone exercise.

## Watch List

The Land Value Tax modeling under Item B is the item most likely to matter politically later, and it's currently framed by staff in purely fiscal terms with a one-line equity disclaimer. If this proceeds without a District 2-specific accounting or a tenant-pass-through requirement for the multifamily relief, it will be very easy for this to become "the Council President raised residential rates in his own district's lower-income neighborhoods to cut apartment landlords' taxes" -- worth getting ahead of.

Brehm's site plan amendment (Item C) and the pending LMC Review Commission are on a collision course similar to the Housing Certainty Act/LMC review timing tension flagged in the September 10 brief: two vehicles moving on overlapping process questions without a stated plan for reconciling them. Worth asking tonight whether this amendment should wait for the Review Commission's evaluation framework (Section 7B of the draft resolution) rather than move independently.
