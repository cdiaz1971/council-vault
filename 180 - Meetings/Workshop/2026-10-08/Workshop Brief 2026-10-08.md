# Workshop Brief -- October 8, 2026

**City Council Work Session -- Thursday, October 8, 2026 at 6:00 P.M., City Hall**

## Bottom Line

No votes tonight, but this is the Council's real window on the bond issue. Once the Bond Ordinance is introduced October 15, the project list is effectively set, and adoption follows November 5. The $37.6M package is mostly routine infrastructure, but it includes $3M for CIP 320044, the Bond Street Extension through Husky Park, which still has an unreleased 1995 forest easement, a missing Forest Conservation Plan, no City Attorney PRK-zoning opinion, and no Phase I environmental assessment. The packet also asks Council to authorize debt without showing outstanding debt, remaining capacity under the Charter limit, or the "established affordability measures" the presentation cites. The data center moratorium is the item most likely to draw a crowd.

## Item B: FY2027 Bond Issue (deep dive)

### What is being proposed

CAO Katie Barkdoll presents a $37.6M general obligation issue (Public Improvement Bonds of 2026), financed with a $36.36M par amount plus about $1.86M of premium. Davenport & Company is financial advisor, DLA Piper is bond counsel. Each fund repays its own share.

| Fund | Projects funded | Par | Annual debt service | Total interest, 20 yrs |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| General Fund | $10.20M (27%) | $9.86M | ~$750K | $5.39M |
| Water and Sewer | $14.06M (37%) | $13.59M | ~$1.04M | $7.43M |
| Stormwater | $13.38M (36%) | $12.92M | ~$985K | $7.06M |
| **Total** | **$37.63M** | **$36.36M** | **~$2.77M** | **$19.88M** |

Source: 2.4 Project List and 2.5 Davenport preliminary numbers (rates as of August 27, 2026, subject to change).

**Project list**

- General Fund: Christopher's Crossing / Millie's Delight $2.0M, Rocky Springs Bridge Replacement $2.5M, **Bond Street Extension (320044) $3.0M**, Motter Ave and 13th Street $1.5M, Downtown Streetscape $1.2M.
- Water and Sewer: Ballenger Creek Interconnect $1.5M, 40 West / LR Dingle Pump Station $3.05M, Linganore Water Plant Filters Rehab $2.2M, Disinfection / By-Products Enhancements $2.95M, Water Meter Upgrades $3.36M, Gas House Pike WWTP EQ Upgrade $1.0M.
- Stormwater: Downtown Area Stormwater Improvements $9.675M, Motter Ave Stormwater $1.6M, Carroll Creek Flood Control $2.1M.

### Deal terms worth knowing

- **Cost.** True interest cost 4.06%, all-in TIC 4.18% (includes issuance costs). Total debt service $56.2M, so the City pays about $0.53 in interest for every dollar of projects. Issuance costs are $581,800 ($400K cost of issuance plus $181,800 underwriter's discount), about 1.6% of par.
- **Structure.** 20-year level debt service, final maturity December 1, 2046. Maturities through 2041 carry 5% coupons priced at a premium, which is where the extra $1.86M comes from. Bonds after 2036 are callable at par on December 1, 2036, so there is a refinancing option in ten years if rates fall.
- **Ratings.** The packet lists AAA (Fitch), Aa1 (Moody's), AA+ (S&P). The vault's January 2026 note [[Frederick Bond Information]] lists Aa2 / AA / AA+. Either the City was upgraded since, or that note was wrong. Worth asking on the record, because an upgrade is good news and a talking point.
- **The half-point slide checks out.** On $37M over 20 years, 4.5% versus 4.0% is about $2.4M in interest, as the deck says.

### Issues to raise

**1. Bond Street Extension ($3M, CIP 320044).** This is the sharpest point. Staff's framing ("bond timing is a financing decision, not a project scheduling decision") is accurate as a description of cash flow, but adopting the ordinance is still Council voting to borrow $3M for a project whose prerequisites are unresolved. On September 10, COO Kershner confirmed the 1995 forest easement was never extinguished and will need a formal release ([[Workshop Brief 2026-09-10]]). The City Attorney's PRK opinion, FCP 94-8, and a Phase I ESA are all still outstanding per the Notion Roadmap ([[Workshop Brief 2026-08-27]]; [[Workshop Brief - Husky Park Rebuttal 2026-08-27]]). Tax-exempt bond rules generally expect proceeds to be spent within about three years; if the project stalls, the money sits idle or has to be reallocated. Questions: Does the ordinance let the administration move proceeds between projects without Council action? Could Bond Street be held out of this issue and funded in a later one once the documentation is complete? What happens to the $3M if the easement release or PRK question goes the wrong way? Also worth asking, since the 2017 MOU had the developer paying for this road: why is it now public debt?

**2. Debt capacity is missing from the packet.** Slide 3 says debt "is evaluated within established affordability measures," but the packet does not show them. Ask for total outstanding GO debt, outstanding debt as a share of the Charter limit, and General Fund debt service as a share of expenditures. The vault also has an unresolved conflict on the Charter limit itself: [[Revenue, Taxes, and Debt]] says 3% of assessed value (Art. VII, § 13), while [[Frederick Bond Information]] says 8%. Get the right figure from Finance.

**3. Reserves and the ratings story.** The executive summary credits strong reserves for the ratings. The FY2027 budget drew $37.0M from fund balance to balance, including $17.3M from the Rainy Day Fund ([[FY2027 Budget -- Cycle Index]]; [[City Budget and Fiscal Condition]]). Rating agencies will see that in November. Ask how the administration plans to present it and what the post-draw unrestricted balance is. The new General Fund debt service (~$750K a year) is small against a $160.6M General Fund, about 0.5%, so this issue alone is not a budget threat. The question is the cumulative trend.

**4. Water and Sewer debt is paid by ratepayers.** $1.04M a year lands in the water and sewer rate base. The NewGen rate study (December 2024) already recommends increases through FY2030, and VP Nash wants a deep dive on operating costs ([[Government Operations Committee Brief 2026-10-05]]). Ask whether this issue's debt service is already in the rate model or adds to it. Utility rates are regressive, and that is the bridge to the low-income credit in the [[Water and Sewer Affordability Ordinance]]. Two projects carry real public-health or affordability value. Disinfection by-product work is drinking-water compliance. The $3.36M Water Meter Upgrades could enable automatic leak alerts, which would reduce the leak bills now handled through one-time forgiveness. Ask whether the new meters are AMI (smart) meters, what usage data they collect, and how long it is kept.

**5. Stormwater concentration downtown.** Downtown stormwater ($9.675M), Carroll Creek flood control ($2.1M), and Downtown Streetscape ($1.2M) total about $13M, roughly 35% of the whole issue. Stormwater debt is repaid from stormwater revenue citywide. That may be the right engineering call given the levee and flood exposure ([[Frederick Water, Sewer, and Stormwater]]), but ask for the rationale and whether other neighborhoods have deferred drainage needs, especially with Mason's Law inventory work due July 1, 2027.

**6. The equity statement is boilerplate.** It says the financing mechanism has no disproportionate impact and defers to the project level. That is true of the mechanism but sidesteps who pays (water and sewer customers, stormwater payers) and where the money goes. A short ask: a one-page map of project locations by NAC.

**7. Gas House Pike WWTP ($1M).** Routine on its face. Given that Trammell Crow and Amazon hold land on Gas House Pike ([[Data Center ZTA PC25-626ZTA -- Opposition Master Strategy Note]]), it is fair to ask whether the equalization upgrade adds capacity for any prospective large user or only addresses existing flows and the phosphorus compliance problem.

### Suggested posture

Support the issue overall. It is long-lived infrastructure, mostly utility work with compliance value, at a good rate. Press on items 1 and 2 before introduction, because October 15 is when the list hardens. A reasonable ask is written answers on the Bond Street questions and the debt capacity figures before October 15.

## Item A: Frederick County Transit Presentation

You invited Transit (Strategic Plan SG5, Enhanced Mobility). The deck is an overview: 51 vehicles, about 100 staff, 13 urban and 2 rural fixed routes, Transit-plus paratransit, 34 shelters and 60+ benches systemwide, fare-free service with "sustainable funding," a network redesign with higher frequency, the new 15 Connector (downtown to Ballenger Creek), and record ridership in FY25 and FY26. The forward-looking item is the 2030 Census: the Frederick urbanized area will likely be designated large urban, which changes federal transit funding formulas significantly. The Transit Development Plan update is due in FY27.

Good asks, from [[Frederick Streets, Traffic Safety, and Transit]]: route-level headways, evening and weekend span, on-time performance and missed trips, and how the City can join the TDP update. The shelter slide lists barriers (ridership, property ownership, owner consent, budget); ask which City stops are blocked only by City right-of-way or permitting, since those are fixable by the City. **The bus stop inventory (Transit Bus Stops_City of Frederick_2026)** lists 245 County Transit stops inside the City, with amenities and site notes for each. Only 20 have a shelter and another 17 have a bench without one. 53 have a concrete pad only, and 152 (62%) list no amenities at all, just the sign. The barriers Transit cites are mostly physical: 65 stops have a narrow right of way, 32 sit against private or residential property, and 12 are in the historic district.

The part that points at the City is the plan review record. Transit has asked for pads, benches, or shelters through the City's development review process on at least 10 projects (Westridge Square, Worman's Mill Court Apartments, Lucas Village, the Walmart at Frederick Trading Center, Hillmeade Square, 57 Thomas Johnson Drive, BMW of Frederick, and others). Two of those requests were denied by the City:

- **Willowdale Drive at Thames Drive** (PC23-672FSI, July 2024), pad and bench denied.
- **West 7th Street at North Market Street**, pad and bench denied on the grounds that a nearby "stage will serve as bench when not in use." That stop is temporarily closed until this month.

The sheet also names two stops where the City's own right of way is the only thing needed: **West Patrick Street at Bentz Street** ("excellent candidate for a bench, City ROW") and **North Market Street at 6th Street** ("excellent candidate for a bench, adjacent to apartments and social services"). Several stops are already being upgraded through City projects (Willowdale intersection, East Street, 16th Street bike lanes, Prospect Center).

Questions this sets up for tonight:

1. Why were the two plan review requests denied, and should stop amenities be a standard condition when Transit asks for them during site plan review? That is a Council-level policy question, possibly an LMC fix.
2. Can the City install the benches at West Patrick and Bentz and at North Market and 6th now, since no private owner is involved?
3. **Bond tie-in.** Three stops sit on Motter Avenue near 13th and 14th Streets, where the bond issue funds $1.5M for Motter Ave and 13th Street plus $1.6M for Motter Ave stormwater. Transit notes it wants a shelter at 14th Street and Motter on FCPS property. Ask whether the Motter Avenue design includes stop improvements. The same question applies to the $1.2M Downtown Streetscape, which covers historic district stops where right of way is tight.
4. The Golden Mile cluster (McCain, Hillcrest, Himes, Seneca) is the densest run of bare stops in the sheet, mostly narrow right of way. Hillcrest at Seneca is flagged as an "excellent candidate for a bus shelter" but blocked by an unresponsive HOA. That corridor serves the residents who depend on transit most.

## Item C: Proposed Data Center Moratorium (Shackelford)

Draft ordinance G-26-__ would, for two years, bar the City from accepting, processing, or approving any LMC amendment to allow data centers (CDI facilities) in any zone, including applications already filed. That reaches PC25-626ZTA (Trammell Crow, M1/M2 by-right), now before the Planning Commission. It restates that no permits can issue because the use is not listed, and requests a broad impact study (noise, water, air, grid, GHG, fiscal comparison, siting standards). It expires on new zoning, the two-year term, or repeal. Legal sufficiency is still blank for City Attorney Rachel Gray.

This aligns with your position ([[Data Center Regulation and Local Authority]]; [[Data Center Resource and Community Impacts]]), and it fits the regional trend: Montgomery (18 months), Prince George's (two years), Howard, and the County Executive's pause through July 1, 2027. Points to raise: (1) applying it to a pending application is the legally exposed part, so get Gray's view on that clause specifically; (2) the study has no funding, owner, or deadline, and "Mayor's office or consultants" leaves it to an adversarial administration, so consider a Council-directed scope with an interim report date; (3) a two-year term with no study deadline could expire before the study is done. VP Nash remains under the Ethics Commission order on data center matters, so expect her to step out of this item. Expect organized public comment.

## Watch List

- **Bond Street in the bond list.** If Council adopts the ordinance with it included, the administration will cite the vote as Council endorsement of the road. Decide before October 15 whether to ask for it to be pulled or conditioned.
- **Ratings and reserves.** The $37M fund balance draw is the vulnerability heading into the November rating meetings.
- **Rate increases.** Water and Sewer debt service plus the NewGen schedule means rate increases are coming. Pairing them with the affordability ordinance is the opening.
- **Bus stop denials.** The City turned down two of Transit's amenity requests in plan review. Expect Transit or riders to raise it, and it is an easy fix to champion.
- **Moratorium retroactivity.** The "before or after the effective date" clause is where a Trammell Crow challenge would land.
