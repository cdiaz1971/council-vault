# Work Session Brief 2026-07-23

Two substantive items tonight. The COG presentation is low-stakes as a formal matter but contains economic intelligence directly relevant to your housing and affordability portfolio. The environmental assessment text amendment is Brehm's bill at an early feedback stage. It is sound in concept and aligns with your equity and tenant-protection priorities, but the draft has real gaps that are worth flagging tonight. No votes. No binding decisions.

## Item V.A: COG Presentation

**Presenter:** Clark Mercer, COG Executive Director

**What this is:** A general orientation briefing on COG's regional programs and Frederick's participation. COG is a nonprofit association of 24 local governments serving the DMV region, including 300+ elected officials. Mayor O'Connor sits on the COG Board. Relevant city staff participate in several COG policy committees (transportation, environment, housing, emergency preparedness, food and agriculture).

**Frederick's current COG investment:** $81,699 total in FY26 (member dues $74,288 + Regional FARM Fund $7,411). COG claims Frederick has received over $420,000 in grants and technical assistance since FY22, including transportation land use and roadway safety planning grants. On that math, the return-on-dues ratio is favorable and worth noting if membership questions arise.

**Intelligence you should walk away with:**

The regional economic data in the slide notes is significant. The Washington MSA lost 100,500 jobs from May 2025 to May 2026, of which 54,500 were federal jobs. Federal employment in the region hit a 30-year low. The City of Frederick has a median federal worker earnings figure of $114,000, and federal workers make up 11 percent of the city's total workforce. Regional inflation reached 4.1 percent in May 2026, more than double the prior year's rate. These numbers have direct implications for housing demand, affordability stress, and ALICE household formation in Frederick. If any colleagues raise housing demand projections in future sessions, this data grounds a counter-narrative.

**Stuart Campbell's role:** Your HHS Director sits on COG's Human Services Policy Committee and Housing Directors Advisory Committee. The presentation notes that COG provides direct technical assistance on housing affordability and homelessness. Worth confirming with Stuart whether Frederick is fully using available COG technical assistance on your housing production work.

**COG cooperative purchasing note:** COG's cooperative purchasing program covers road salt (Frederick uses it, saving ~$5,000/year), ALPRs, and subscriber radio systems among others. The ALPR item is worth flagging given your surveillance monitoring posture on Flock.

**Watch item:** COG is encouraging Frederick to join the Regional Environmental Fund. The contribution would be small given Frederick's size. Jenny Willoughby (sustainability manager) is already embedded in COG environmental work. No ask is being made tonight, but this may surface as a future budget line.

**Action you may want to take:** Ask Clark Mercer whether COG has any technical assistance programs relevant to environmental site assessment, food system resilience in food-desert areas, or AI governance. These align with your active threads.

## Item V.B: Environmental Assessment Text Amendment

**Presented by:** Council Member Peter Brehm
**Also present:** Rachel Gray, City Attorney; Zack Kershner, COO

**What this is:** Brehm is proposing a new LMC section (Sec. 700) requiring Phase I Environmental Site Assessments (ESAs) as a condition of development applications, and Phase II ESAs if contamination is identified. This is a pre-decisional feedback session. Brehm intends to take feedback, potentially revise, and then submit for formal Planning Commission review. No vote tonight.

**The policy concept is sound.** Frederick has 281 years of industrial and commercial history. Environmental testing is common in commercial lending due diligence but not routinely required for city development approvals. The public currently has no guaranteed access to remediation documentation. Requiring Phase I ESAs at the plat or site plan stage and tying Phase II to certificate-of-occupancy issuance is a reasonable gap-fill.

**Comparing the two draft versions:**

The revised version (titled "Proposed Text Amendment Language: Environment Assessment to Protect Public Health") strengthens the original in three ways. First, it adds a public-health framing tie in the purpose clause. Second, it fills the blank grandfather date in the exemptions (January 1, 2027 for unconditionally approved master plans). Third, it clarifies the subdivision exemption with examples (lot line adjustments, lot consolidations). Fourth, it rewrites the compliance provision to make certificate of occupancy denial mandatory on its face rather than discretionary ("A City certificate of occupancy will not be issued" vs. "The City may refuse to issue").

The Phase II trigger changed in a way that requires scrutiny. The original version uses "recognized environmental conditions" as the Phase II trigger, which is the standard ASTM E1527 terminology. The revised version replaces that with "previous uses that may have resulted in accumulation of hazardous substances exceeding state standards environmental conditions." That language is garbled and internally inconsistent. It appears to be an incomplete edit where new language was inserted without removing the old. This needs to be cleaned up before the text goes to Planning Commission.

**Gaps and questions worth raising tonight:**

The text is silent on public disclosure. Phase I and Phase II ESA reports would be submitted to the City but there is no provision requiring those reports to be made publicly available. For a proposal whose stated purpose includes ensuring the public receives relevant information, that gap is material. Ask whether Brehm intends to add a public records access provision or a disclosure requirement.

There is no provision addressing properties where remediation is ongoing at the time of the certificate of occupancy application. The continuous obligation clause (f) covers MDE correspondence and closure documents, but it does not explicitly address what happens if a property owner applies for a certificate of occupancy while a Phase II is in process and remediation is not complete.

The exemptions remove city projects from scope. That may be appropriate operationally, but it creates a political optic: private developers face the requirement, city-initiated projects do not. Worth knowing Brehm's rationale.

**Fiscal impact:** The executive summary leaves the fiscal impact section blank. Phase I ESAs typically run $1,500 to $5,000 and are a standard developer cost. Phase II ESAs can run $10,000 to $50,000 or more depending on scope. Those costs land on developers and property owners, not on the city, but you should understand whether Rachel Gray or Zack Kershner see any city cost exposure in administering the continuous obligation and MDE correspondence tracking.

**Your equity framing:** Contaminated sites are not distributed equally. In Frederick, industrial history and older commercial corridors are concentrated in and near majority-minority and lower-income neighborhoods, including parts of District 2. An environmental assessment requirement before development is a protection for existing residents as much as future ones. You can support the concept and push for disclosure provisions as a complement.

## Watch List

**COG ALPR participation:** The cooperative purchasing slide notes that ALPR systems are available through COG's cooperative purchasing program. This is how jurisdictions can acquire Flock and similar systems cheaply. No active Frederick procurement appears imminent, but given your surveillance posture, it is worth knowing that COG is a procurement conduit for this technology class.

**Federal job losses and housing:** The 11 percent of Frederick workers in federal employment and the median federal worker earnings of $114,000 mean that federal workforce reductions may be softening upper-end housing demand while simultaneously increasing ALICE household pressure in the middle. If you are building the record for the Housing Production Target Resolution, this regional data is useful.

**Brehm dynamic:** The environmental assessment proposal is genuinely good policy and aligns with your priorities. Supporting it publicly tonight costs you nothing and builds goodwill with Brehm on a non-contested item. The feedback you provide should be substantive rather than political.
