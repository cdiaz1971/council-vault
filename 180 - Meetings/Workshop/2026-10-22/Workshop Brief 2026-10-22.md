# Workshop Brief 2026-10-22

**City Council Work Session, Thursday, October 22, 2026, City Hall**

## Bottom Line

Your own item carries the night. Item D is the text amendment you are co-sponsoring with Brehm, and you are presenting the 10/19 revision, not the packet version. The packet (D.2, dated 10/9) has the Council approve or deny every application by majority vote at a public hearing before it can go to the Planning Commission. The revision replaces that with a non-voting review at a meeting you schedule. Colleagues and the public will have read the packet, so get the revision to the Clerk and members before Thursday if you can, and open the item by saying the binding vote has been dropped and why. The RCV presentation is a precursor to a Charter fight, not a vote, and it quietly pitches proportional RCV, which reaches the two at-large seats.

## Action Items

**II. Approval of Minutes, October 8.** The only vote. Check one detail before moving it. The official minutes say the Council approved 5-0 "the minutes of the September 10, 2026, City Council Work Session," but the October 8 agenda listed the September 24 minutes. Either the minutes misstate which minutes were approved or the wrong set was approved. Ask the Clerk to correct it before approval. The minutes otherwise match what happened: Nash and Taylor absent, transit and bond presentations, Shackelford's data center moratorium discussion with public comment. See [[Workshop Minutes 2026-10-08]].

## Presentations and Reports

**IV.D, Text amendment on the filing process (Díaz and Brehm).** Feedback only. The executive summary says you will take comments, revise, and submit to the Planning Commission. It also leaves Fiscal Impact, Neighborhood Advisory Council Impact, and Equity Impact Statement blank; expect someone to ask about all three, and fill them before submission.

What the packet version does: limits filing to an elected City official or the Division (residents and property owners struck), and has the Council "approve or deny the text amendment by a majority vote" at a public hearing before Planning Commission review. That applies to Division filings too. What your later Drive draft does instead: Council members discuss the concept at a public meeting the Council President schedules and take no vote, and Division filings only require notice.

Dropping the vote is the stronger position: it answers the charge that four members could kill a code change before the Commission weighs in, and avoids the notice requirements a "public hearing" carries. What it opens instead is open-ended scheduling control with the Council President, which Adkins can fold into his leadership-removal argument. Offer a fixed scheduling deadline before anyone asks. The revision still has gaps: the justification still says "Requiring Council approval" though there is no vote, Division filings get notice only while officials' filings get a review meeting, there is no transition rule for the pending Trammell Crow data center ZTA (PC25-626ZTA), there is no public process or disclosure when a developer asks a member to sponsor, and no fee recovery for sponsored amendments. The justification also refers to "the Planning Department and City Council members" while the text says "elected City official," which includes the Mayor.

Likely reactions in the room: the development community is the strongest opposition, with "politicizing the code" and "red tape" arguments; Taylor shares the diagnosis (her modernization plan pauses private text amendments) and may want it folded into the LMC rewrite; Adkins may press on Council President power; removing "City resident" is the line most likely to draw a hostile public comment or headline. Lead with the rebuttals: map amendments and project approvals are untouched, Frederick County and Montgomery County already route text amendments through their councils, and every applicant still has a door through a Council sponsor. Full analysis in [[Text Amendment Filing Authority Policy Brief 2026-10-19]].

**IV.A, Ranked Choice Voting Maryland.** Advocacy presentation, no proposal attached. The backup is two fact sheets from the RCV Resource Center. A.1 reports that counties using RCV score at or above the national average on election administration measures and improve after adoption. It is the advocacy organization's own summary of a paper, so ask for the underlying study before citing it. A.2 is about proportional RCV for multi-winner contests, used in Cambridge, Minneapolis, Albany, and Palm Desert, and pitched partly as a Voting Rights Act remedy for vote dilution.

That second sheet is the one to notice. Frederick elects five district seats and two at-large seats, and the vault already flags the at-large seats as the most likely target of a vote-dilution claim given the City's growing Latino and Black communities ([[Voting Rights and Elections]]). Proportional RCV for the at-large seats could serve both the reform case and a litigation-risk case, which is a stronger argument for a Charter change than turnout alone. Election method is set by the Charter, changed by Council resolution, and not subject to veto, though referendum is possible.

Questions to ask: who would administer and tabulate a City RCV election, given the 2029 County coordination request, and whether the County's equipment can count ranked ballots; what a Maryland municipality would need from the State, if anything (outside your notes, verify: Takoma Park has used instant runoff voting in its municipal elections); cost and voter education; and whether they propose single-winner RCV for Mayor and districts, proportional for at-large, or both. Your tracker posture is to stay close to Nash, Taylor, and Brehm and be ready to co-sponsor when a formal Charter amendment appears ([[Ranked Choice Voting - Charter Reform]]). Brehm has led the alternative voting methods work in Government Operations.

**IV.C, SPARK Tool Results.** No backup in the folder. These are the results of the September exercise where each member allocated 100 points across the six CommUNITY 2030 strategic goals, due October 9, as input to the FY2028 budget. Look at where housing and equity goals landed relative to your own allocation and whether the administration intends to weight budget requests by these scores. This is part of the Priority-Driven Budgeting work you co-champion with Nash, and she controls the pace. Ask that results be released by goal and by member, or at least by goal, so the public can see the Council's priorities.

**IV.B, City Health and Safety Manual Update.** No backup in the folder. Likely an administrative update. Two questions: what changed and why now, and whether any change affects working conditions in a way that would become a bargaining subject once the collective bargaining legislation moves. Otherwise routine.

You are presenting the 10/19 revision while colleagues hold the packet version, so say clearly that the binding vote has been dropped and why, or you will spend the discussion on a version you no longer support. Keep the Item D discussion off the merits of the data center ZTA unless you intend the transition rule to reach it; Shackelford's moratorium (October 8) is already carrying that fight. On RCV, avoid any signal that commits the Council to a 2029 change before the County administration question is answered. On the minutes, a small fix now prevents an incorrect public record.
