# Work Session Brief -- August 13, 2026

**City Council Work Session -- August 13, 2026, City Hall**

## Bottom Line

No votes tonight. This is a workshop session on three zoning text amendments (ADUs, Home Occupations, Forest Conservation) plus a presentation of the Mayor's Affordable Housing Task Force final report. The item needing closest attention is A: the draft ADU ordinance eliminates owner occupancy, which is exactly what the LMC Zoning Amendments strategy brief calls for, but the Planning Commission voted 4-0 to recommend keeping owner occupancy and adding a parking exemption the draft doesn't include. A public decision is needed on whether to side with staff's draft or the Commission's recommendation before this comes back for a vote. Item D, the MAHTF presentation, is not a vote but is the more consequential political moment of the night. A developed position on it already exists in the vault; tonight is where the Mayor's office starts framing it in public, and Council should be ready to stake out its own framing in the room.

## Action Items

None require a vote tonight. All three ordinances are at the discussion stage, with second reading or introduction to follow.

**A. ADU Ordinance (PC25-614ZTA).** Initiated by VP Nash. Eliminates the owner occupancy requirement, reduces parking (transit proximity exemption, smaller space dimensions), and allows detached ADUs up to 1,200 SF (up from 800) with Planning Commission modification approval. Staff and Planning Commission are not aligned. The Commission voted 4-0 in favor with three conditions: no additional parking required in DB, DR, and DBO districts even where existing parking is removed to build the ADU; retain owner occupancy; and if occupancy is eliminated anyway, consider a deed restriction to prevent ADUs from becoming a vehicle for absentee investment. The draft ordinance in the packet does neither of the first two. It strikes owner occupancy entirely and does not carry the district based parking exemption. This is consistent with the LMC Zoning Amendments strategy brief, which identifies owner occupancy elimination as the single most consequential lever for ADU production, but it means walking into a room where the Commission chair has gone on record against it. A decision is needed before the meeting on whether to defend the draft as written or signal openness to the deed restriction compromise raised in public comment (tying larger ADUs to ALICE income tenants with a term contract).

**B. Home Occupations Ordinance (PC25-615ZTA).** Modernizes home based business rules, adds a new Low Impact tier allowing scheduled customer visits, and removes permit requirements for genuinely no impact businesses (teleworkers). Clean posture: Planning Commission voted 5-0 with no modifications, staff and Commission are aligned, next step is introduction. The only real substance for the room is the public comment, flagged below.

**C. Forest Conservation Ordinance (PC24-224ZTA).** Updates Section 721 to conform to state SB234 and SB526, and extends short term maintenance bonding from two years to five. Planning Commission voted 4-0 in favor but with two open items staff has not yet resolved: what "12 inches of organic topsoil" means and whether it should apply to all planting sites or just disturbed urban soils, and how to measure tree mitigation (staff is leaning toward diameter based rather than circumference based measurement, possibly canopy area based). Staff is explicit in the executive summary that these are not settled. A separate fee schedule ordinance raising the fee in lieu rate from $0.30/sq ft is coming later and is tied to this one.

## Consent Agenda

None. This agenda has no consent agenda section. It is a work session structured as Call to Order, Minutes Approval, Old Business (committee updates), Workshop (A through D), and Elected Officials' Comments.

## Presentations and Reports

**D. Mayor's Affordable Housing Task Force Final Report.** Presented by HHS Director Stuart Campbell and Housing Policy Advisor Jodie Ostoich. This is the public unveiling of the report to Council; it was released via press conference on July 15. Six core recommendations: reform regulations, prioritize action, fund the City Housing Fund on a recurring basis, create an Affordable Housing Commission, invest in staffing, and launch a public education campaign. The existing MAHTF brief flags that this is fundamentally a mayoral instrument that names Council as an implementing actor rather than a co-author, and that several of its highest urgency items (APFO AMI tiered reform, MPDU threshold and fee changes, targeted LMC amendments) are things Council is already driving on its own timeline. Tonight is a presentation, not a discussion item requiring Council response, but it is the moment the Mayor's framing enters the public record. The existing brief recommends getting ahead of that framing with a public statement naming which recommendations Council will move on and on what timeline, before the administration owns the narrative.

## Public Comment

No speakers are scheduled on this agenda; the standard three minute comment period follows each work session item per the agenda's noted policy. Two items carry pre-submitted written comment worth having in mind if verbal comment follows the same pattern.

Item A drew three letters: a supportive account from an ADU owner describing years of code enforcement pressure over an under the radar unit; a critical letter from Citizens for Responsible Growth arguing the 1,200 SF cap and occupancy removal favor absentee investors over the stated affordability purpose of Section 802; and a proposal from a resident to condition larger ADUs and non owner occupied units on long term ALICE income tenancy contracts.

Item B drew one letter worth being ready for if raised verbally: a home based acupuncturist describes a decade of inconsistent zoning enforcement, a lost lease, and forced retirement, and states plainly that no city staff ever told her a special exception process existed. This is a strong, sympathetic, on the record example of exactly the population Section 829 modernization is meant to serve, and it may come up in public comment tonight.

## Watch List

The Item A conflict between the draft ordinance and the Planning Commission's recommendation is the sharpest issue tonight. The Commission chair's letter is on the record recommending the opposite of what the draft does on the owner occupancy point. If the draft is to move forward as written, an explanation should be ready for why Council is not adopting the Commission's condition, since that will be visible and can be characterized as Council overriding its own Planning Commission on a community character issue.

Item C carries an affordability tension that cuts against the night's broader housing message. A Frederick Builders Association letter argues the extended five year bonding period, larger required tree stock, and lack of a grandfathering clause for in progress projects will raise development costs, and the fee in lieu increase discussed for a future ordinance compounds that. This is happening the same night as the MAHTF presentation on reducing barriers to housing production. A throughline answer is worth having ready if anyone connects the two.

Item D is not on tonight's agenda for Council action, but the MAHTF brief flags Brehm as a likely skeptic given his rental licensing fee reduction proposal cuts against the report's finding that the Housing Fund needs the rental licensing revenue stream, and Adkins as reachable given his planning background, particularly on APFO reform. Getting Adkins on record early, before the Mayor's office does outreach, is in the brief's recommended next steps.
