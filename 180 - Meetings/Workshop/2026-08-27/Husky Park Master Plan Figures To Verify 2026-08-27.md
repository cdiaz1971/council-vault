# Husky Park -- Master Plan Figures To Verify

## RESOLVED (2026-08-25)

Every figure checks out, verbatim, against Cesar's own copy of the plan.

**The 928/978/50-acre citation is exact**, not approximate. Page 167 of the plan has a table headed "Acres of parkland per 1,000 residents," sourced explicitly to "2020 GIS Acres":

- Inventory, Frederick Parks (2020 GIS Acres): **928**
- Current ratio, 2020: 12.3 acres per 1,000 population (population 75,281)
- Projected population, 2025: 79,344
- Total acres needed to maintain current ratio with growth: **978**
- Acres to add: **50**
- Footnote on the table: "does not include 70 acres of undeveloped park land currently in the system inventory"

This is a different table, with a different sourcing label, from the "approximately 950 acres" line that appears repeatedly in the plan's narrative/executive-summary prose (pages 4, 138, and elsewhere, reused as a pull-quote). The 950-acre line is a casual rounding used in prose; the 928-acre figure is the specific, GIS-sourced number the plan itself uses for the acres-needed calculation. Hluch cited the correct, more precise table. His distinction from the Comprehensive Plan's separate 114-acre, 25-year figure was also correct, that is a different document with a different methodology (NRPA standard, longer horizon), not a competing figure from the same plan.

**The Husky Park quote is verbatim.** From the plan's site-by-site inventory table (appears at line ~15544 and again at ~17346 of the extracted text, i.e. the same table reproduced in two sections of the report): "Area is well served and limited residential near this property lowers the priority for this property." Exact wording. Preceded by: "Property appears closed to public access and is utilized as DPW storage yard. Open turf and diamond field fall South of boundary, belonging to Frederick Fair." Priority rating: Low.

**Three mentions, confirmed.** The inventory-table row (reproduced twice in the document) plus a third listing as a line item under "the following properties that are currently undeveloped but are maintained by the department (listed in order of size)." All three are data-table rows, exactly as Hluch characterized it.

**New fact, not in Hluch's document: Husky Park is the largest of the City's 12 undeveloped properties.** The full undeveloped-properties list, in order: Husky Park (23 acres), Parkland 2 (17), Parkland 10 (8), Parkland 12 (6), Hillcrest Orchard Park (4), Airport Parkland (4), Parkland 8 (3), Parkland 6 (2), Parkland 11 (1), Parkland 5 (1), Parkland 7 (0.2), Parkland 3 (0.1). Total: 69.3 acres, matching the plan's own "69 acres" and the footnote's "70 acres" (rounding). Husky Park alone is roughly a third of all the undeveloped park land the City has left in its inventory.

## What This Changes

The rebuttal memo's equity section previously said not to concede the 50-acre shortfall. That was wrong, written before this document was accessible. Correct it: the citywide shortfall is real and verified, cite it as such. The proximity argument (distances to Baker Park) is still valid and still worth using, it rebuts a different claim, the geographic "west side versus east side" framing, not the citywide acreage math. Keep both, but stop treating the acreage figure as disputable.

The "largest of the 12 undeveloped properties" fact is new leverage for Hluch's side, not Cesar's. It means the road proposal is not just converting an unused DPW yard, it is consuming close to a third of the City's entire remaining undeveloped park land bank in a single project.
