# Work Session Brief -- August 27, 2026

**City Council Work Session -- August 27, 2026, 6:00 P.M.**

## Bottom Line

No votes tonight. Two substantive items. Item A/B is the sharper one: resident Kevin Hluch has built an unusually thorough documentary case that CIP 320044 (the $3M Bond Street Extension road through Husky Park, PRK-zoned parkland) may not have cleared prerequisites the administration would need to defend before bidding it out at year end -- a 1995 forest conservation covenant, an unlocatable Forest Conservation Plan, a 2015 shared-use-path condition (Ordinance G-15-22) that was never built, a 2021 EPA grant application describing a different intended use for the site, and no environmental assessment of a filled quarry with a history of industrial use. He is explicitly not asking Council to kill the project -- only to require written documentation before bid authorization. Staff's companion presentation (Item B) is purely informational, no recommendation, no fiscal impact stated. Item C, the LMC gas-station amendment, reads as routine (Planning Commission voted 5-0, staff supports) but has expanded considerably past what was in the vault from March: it now touches the Use Matrix (Sec. 404), not just the pump-count clarification in Sec. 816, and rewrites Sec. 824's pump-setback standard in a way that tightens some districts and loosens others near residential zoning. Worth a substantive look before this reaches First Read, not a rubber stamp.

## Action Items

No votes tonight -- this is a workshop discussion. Both items are headed toward Introduction at a future Regular Meeting per staff's stated next step on Item C; Item A/B has no stated legislative vehicle yet (it is administration-driven CIP spending, not an ordinance).

**A/B. Husky Park -- CIP 320044 Bond Street Extension.** Resident presenter Kevin Hluch (113 W. All Saints St.) is not opposing the $3M road extension outright but is asking Council to require the administration to document, in writing and on the record, that ten legal prerequisites are satisfied before bid authorization (expected end of 2026). The core threads: (1) the site is PRK-zoned (Park District), where roads are not a clearly permitted use, and he wants a formal City Attorney opinion on that question; (2) a 1995 recorded Forest Conservation Covenant (Liber 2147, Folio 1046) covers roughly 3.96 acres of the disturbance area, and the Forest Conservation Plan needed to map its boundary (FCP 94-8) cannot currently be located -- confirmed in writing by both the City Attorney's office and the DPW records custodian on August 13, 2026; (3) the Forest Stand Delineation on file expired in February 2026; (4) a 2015 ordinance (G-15-22, Condition 2) required the original developer to build a shared-use path through the same area, which was never completed, and Hluch argues that obligation should be enforced instead of building a new road; (5) a 2017 MOU shows the road was originally to be funded entirely by the developer, and he is asking why it is now $3M in public debt; (6) a 2021 EPA Brownfields grant application for this site described its future use as housing plus a shared-use path -- not a road -- and EPA confirmed no assessment work was ever completed under that $300K grant; (7) the site includes a filled quarry with a documented history of industrial use, and no Phase I/II Environmental Site Assessment has been done. His asks: a written City Attorney opinion on zoning, production of the specific missing documents before bid authorization, a Phase I ESA, and deferral of bid authorization until all of this is resolved -- with the already-required G-15-22 path built in the meantime. Staff's Item B presentation (submitted by COO Zack Kershner, with Tracy Coleman, Bob Smith, Richard Griffin, and Gabrielle Collard) covers the site's acquisition and use history and the Bond Street plans, but takes no position and proposes no next step -- it reads as background-setting ahead of the resident's challenge, not a rebuttal to it. The property (530 Highland Street, NAC 12) is currently used for DPW purposes.

**C. LMC Amendment -- Convenience Stores with Gas Sales (PC25-248ZTA, Wormald).** Filed by The Wormald Companies, LLC after their Monocacy Center site plan (west side of Monocacy Boulevard) was constrained by the existing pump-count rules. Planning Commission voted 5-0 on April 13, 2026 to recommend approval; staff (Planning Director Gabrielle Collard) supports it, with one drafting fix (Table 824-1 should read "Development" not "District" for PND). What it does: reclassifies Convenience Stores with Gas Sales in MU-1/MU-2 districts from a ZBA conditional use to a Mixed-Use Conditional Use reviewed through the master plan process; replaces "ZBA" with "Reviewing Authority" throughout Sec. 816 (a terminology fix); and replaces the current 200-foot proximity-based pump restriction in Sec. 824 (which staff's own report says makes the use "effectively... not viable" near residential zoning) with a flat 100-foot setback from residential lot lines or the RC district boundary, plus a new per-district pump-density table (GC 0.4, MU 0.2, PND 0.2 pumps per 1,000 sf, up from PND's current 0.003; RC/RO/PB/DR remain at zero). Staff's report also flags a specific redevelopment case this would help -- 1180/1200 W. Patrick Street, a former Carroll Motor Fuels/Sunoco site consolidating 20 pumps into 12 -- and a title-lot-line quirk (private rights-of-way like Warren Way at Braemar Condominiums) that staff says justifies measuring the new setback from the nearest residential building wall rather than the platted lot line in some cases. Scope note: the vault's March 2026 note on this item described it as a single narrow change to Sec. 816 (making pump-count discretion explicit). What's now in the packet is materially broader -- a Use Matrix reclassification plus a full rewrite of Sec. 824 -- worth flagging since it changed shape between filing and Council review.

## Watch List

**Husky Park is the item that needs the most preparation.** The documentation gaps Hluch has surfaced -- an unlocatable Forest Conservation Plan, an expired Forest Stand Delineation, an unenforced 2015 path obligation, a funding structure that shifted from developer-paid to $3M in public debt, and no environmental assessment of a site with a filled quarry -- are all independently verifiable, and two of them (the missing FCP, the funding shift) are already confirmed in writing by the City Attorney's office and DPW. If the administration walks in without answers, this becomes a transparency and due-diligence story, not a parks story. Given the equity and accountability lens this project applies broadly, a resident doing unpaid legal and records research to hold a $3M capital project accountable before it's bid out is worth taking seriously on the merits, independent of how the room reacts.

**The LMC gas-station amendment is being framed as routine and mostly is** -- unanimous Planning Commission vote, staff support, a real applicant problem (pump math that made an otherwise-approvable site plan infeasible). But the net effect for MU and PND districts is a loosened siting standard near residential zoning: the old rule effectively blocked gas pumps within 200 feet of a residential lot; the new rule allows them at 100 feet, with a nonzero pump allowance in PND specifically increased. That's a quality-of-life question (noise, traffic, air quality) for whoever lives in MU/PND-adjacent housing, which in Frederick often means renters and mixed-income corridors. Nothing in the packet suggests this got scrutiny through that lens -- it's being sold entirely as a consistency and redevelopment-feasibility fix. Worth asking staff directly whether any residential-proximity impact analysis was done, separate from the fire-code (NFPA 30A) and peer-jurisdiction (Brunswick, Middletown) setback comparisons in the staff report.
