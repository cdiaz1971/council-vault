# Husky Park / CIP 320044. Prep Memo on Hluch's Ten Prerequisites

## Bottom Line

Hluch built a real document. Three of his points are genuinely strong and need action or acknowledgment, not rebuttal, two from his ten numbered prerequisites, plus the citywide parkland shortfall from his equity section, which is now fully verified against the source. Most of the rest are open procedural questions that staff can close quickly, or framing choices that fall apart under a second read. His overall structure, ten items with equal-weight headers, makes weak and strong points look like the same size problem. That is the thing to undercut first.

Also worth knowing going in. This is a staff presentation item, not a vote. B's own Executive Summary form lists no fiscal impact, no equity impact statement, next steps N/A. Nothing on tonight's agenda commits money or approves the road. The actual decision point is bid authorization, expected by year end. That gap is useful. You do not need every answer tonight, you need to show the record is moving, not stalled.

## The Two Points That Are Actually Strong

**Item 2, the 1995 Forest Conservation covenant and the missing FCP 94-8 boundary map.** This is his best material. A recorded, perpetual easement the City itself acknowledged in the 2017 MOU, over ground the City's own 2021 engineering drawing admits the road disturbs by 3.96 acres. Both the City Attorney and DPW's records custodian confirmed in writing, on the same day, that the underlying 1994 boundary plan cannot be located. You cannot argue this one away. The move is to get ahead of it. Ask staff directly, on the record at the workshop, whether the City intends to commission a current survey and file a 2:1 replacement plan rather than keep searching for a document that two separate City officials have already said does not exist. That converts a gap into a plan of action before Hluch gets to frame it as a cover-up.

**Item 8, no Phase I or Phase II environmental assessment.** The site was assembled from two industrial parcels, includes a filled quarry, and sits in a corridor where a Phase I/II next door already confirmed arsenic, benzo(a)pyrene, lead, and mercury above residential thresholds. EPA recommended a Phase I before any transfer, in writing. A Phase I ESA costs a few thousand dollars and is standard due diligence before construction on a former industrial site regardless of this fight. Do not fight this one. Ask for it. It costs you nothing politically and protects the City if contamination turns up during construction instead of before it.

Everything below this line is where the document is weaker than its formatting suggests.

## Item by Item

**Item 1, PRK zoning, road not a listed permitted use.** Weak. Most zoning codes, including most LMC frameworks, treat public rights-of-way and infrastructure as outside the principal-use table entirely, they are not a "use" the table regulates the way it regulates a building. Hluch is asking for a written determination, not asserting a violation, his own status line says "contested, written determination requested." That is a fast, cheap ask for the City Attorney's office, a one-paragraph opinion likely resolves it. Ask Rachel Gray to produce it before the next meeting so this stops being a live question.

**Item 3, expired Forest Stand Delineation.** Weak as a blocker. FSDs expire and get renewed constantly during multi-year projects, this is routine, not evidence of anything. Confirm with Planning whether a renewal is already scheduled. If not, ask staff to schedule one. This should not be treated as equivalent in weight to Items 2 or 8.

**Item 4, the 2023 No Net Loss law.** Hluch's own document calls this "applicability undetermined." He is flagging a question, not a violation. Answer it and it disappears from the list.

**Item 5, DNR's intervention right under LMC §721(2)(B).** This is not a separate obstacle, it is a procedural step inside the same process as Item 2. DNR gets a chance to weigh in once the City actually files the easement modification. It resolves itself the moment you act on Item 2.

**Item 6, Ordinance G-15-22 Condition 2, the shared use path.** This is his rhetorical centerpiece and it is also his weakest legal argument once you read his own footnotes. He documents, in his own words, that the 2017 MOU's attempt to substitute a street-adjacent path for Condition 2 rests on an MOU that was never executed by the City by its own terms (§9.4, blank signature block), scoped by its own text only to the Highland Street segment, and triggered by a developer election that was never made. In other words, Hluch has already built the case that Condition 2 remains the developer's live, unmet obligation, independent of whatever the City does with Husky Park. That means the honest framing is not "the road or the path," it is "both." The City can proceed with CIP 320044 on City land and separately direct staff to enforce Condition 2 against the Renn Quarter developer, who still owes it. His own final ask, defer the road and force the path, is a false choice his own research undercuts. Use his footnotes against his framing.

**Item 7, floodplain law and MDE permit.** Real requirement, wrong sequencing. MDE permitting and FEMA CLOMR review are conditions of construction, not conditions of bid authorization. Cities routinely authorize a bid while permits are still in process, permits gate notice to proceed, not the bid itself. Confirm this sequencing with Rachel Gray so you can state it plainly. This point does not support deferring the bid.

**Item 9, Historic Preservation Review, MHT Act and Section 106.** Hluch hedges this himself, Section 106 only applies "if federal funds or approvals are involved." Ask staff a direct factual question tonight, is CIP 320044 funded with any federal dollars, or is this local capital funds and G.O. debt only. If it is purely local, this item is off the table entirely, no hedge needed.

**Item 10, Comprehensive Plan consistency under Md. Land Use §3-205.** Worth a real answer, not worth a real fear. §3-205 is triggered by adoption of new arterial or collector streets under the City's official Master Streets Plan process. Whether a Bond Street extension of this scale counts as a "new street" under that specific statute, versus a capital improvement to existing City property, is a legal classification question, not a settled violation. Ask Gray for a written read. If she confirms it does not trigger §3-205, this item closes in one paragraph.

## The Four Requests to Council

Three of the four are cheap and you should not resist them, resisting them costs you more than granting them.

- A written City Attorney opinion on the PRK question. Low cost, likely resolves fast, asked and answered above.
- Document production. Most of what he is asking for either already exists in scattered form or is a reasonable ask before $3 million commits. Granting this makes the City look transparent rather than defensive.
- A Phase I ESA. Already addressed above, do this regardless.
- Deferring bid authorization. This is the one real ask, and you do not have to grant or deny it tonight. Bid authorization is not on tonight's agenda. You can say plainly that the Council will expect these items closed out before that vote comes up, without committing to a deferral you have not decided on.

## The Equity Argument

Split this into two separate claims. One is weak. One is confirmed and should not be contested.

**The geographic framing is weak.** "West Frederick has Baker Park, east Frederick is asked to give up its only park" is a proximity claim, and the actual distances undercut it. Straight line distance from your own house in District 2, the actual west end of the city, to Baker Park is 2.9 miles. From Hluch's address at 113 W. All Saints Street to Baker Park is 0.8 miles. From that same address to Husky Park is 1.1 miles. He lives closer to both parks than you do, and closer to Baker Park than to the park he says the City is taking from the east side. Geocoded confirmation, US Census Bureau address locator (straight-line, not drive time): your house to Baker Park, 1.47 miles; Hluch's address to Baker Park, 0.27 miles; Hluch's address to Husky Park, 0.85 miles; your house to Husky Park, 2.34 miles. He lives roughly five times closer to Baker Park than you do. Use this directly against the "west side versus east side" framing specifically, not against the acreage point below, they are different claims.

**The citywide acreage shortfall is real. Confirmed verbatim against the actual plan, not a secondary source.** Page 167 of the 2023 Parks and Recreation Master Plan has a table, sourced explicitly to "2020 GIS Acres," showing current inventory at 928 acres, a current ratio of 12.3 acres per 1,000 residents, a 2025 projected-population figure of 79,344, acres needed to hold that ratio at 978, and acres to add at 50. Every number in Hluch's citation is exact. Do not dispute this figure at the workshop. If staff or another member questions it, the answer is that it is the plan's own table, not his interpretation.

**New fact that cuts the other way: Husky Park is the largest of the City's 12 undeveloped properties.** The plan's own list, in order: Husky Park (23 acres), then eleven smaller undeveloped parcels totaling roughly 46 more acres, for 69 acres of undeveloped park land citywide. Husky Park alone is close to a third of everything the City has left in that category. The plan's own site-visit note on Husky Park, verbatim, reads: "Area is well served and limited residential near this property lowers the priority for this property." Low priority for park development, not zero relevance, and not "no park there to destroy." But do not overcorrect into "destroying a park" either, there is no built park on site, and the actual land consumed by the road is a partial, specific slice of a 22.58-acre parcel, not the whole thing. See the footprint discussion below for what is and is not established about how much.

## What I Verified Independently, City GIS and CIP Records

None of this came from Hluch's document. Pulled directly from the City's own ArcGIS server and its FY2026-2031 Capital Improvements Program book.

**Parcel and zoning.** 530 Highland Street is 22.58 acres (GIS calculated acreage, matching Hluch's 23.036-acre plat figure closely), zoned PRK as its actual base zoning, confirmed by City GIS parcel data, not an overlay. This corroborates his claim and his rebuttal of the "floating zone" description some City officials used in 2019.

**The missing easement, confirmed a second way.** The City's own Forest Conservation Easements GIS layer, 259 records covering the whole city, has zero entry for this parcel, for "Renn," for "Husky," or for file number "94-8." The earliest records in that layer date to 2007. The 1995 easement is missing not just from the PIA response Hluch received, it is missing from the City's own spatial database entirely. Stronger confirmation than anything in his document.

**Floodplain, corrected.** Only about 1.04 acres (4.6 percent) of the Husky Park parcel itself sits in the FEMA AE flood zone, per the City's own floodplain layer. The "completely within the 100 year floodplain" language in Hluch's document refers to the separate, adjacent 78-acre Renn Quarter parkland tract, not to Husky Park. He is careful about this distinction in his own text, but it is easy to read past it. Flag it if raised.

**CIP 320044's own scope, and the footprint question that is not settled.** The City's CIP book describes the project as 1,600 linear feet of new road plus a full reconstruction of Highland Street with a 10-foot shared use path. Hluch's 3.96-acre figure, sourced to a 2021 engineering drawing not included in the packet, is described in his document as the road's "disturbance footprint" overlapping the forest easement, not necessarily the permanent paved footprint. Those are different things. Construction disturbance, grading limits, erosion control, staging areas, is routinely wider than what ends up permanently paved, and disturbed ground outside the final pavement typically gets regraded and revegetated. A 24 to 40-foot paved cross section (travel lanes plus a 10-foot path) over the portion of 1,600 linear feet that actually crosses new ground on the Husky Park parcel, as opposed to reconstructing the already-existing Highland Street right-of-way, would be a meaningfully smaller permanent footprint than 3.96 acres. Neither figure is confirmed. What is fair to say: 3.96 acres is the disturbance figure as cited, it is not confirmed as the permanent loss, and the honest position is that both "minimal land lost" and "a park is being destroyed" overstate what is actually established. The real number sits somewhere between a fully engineered permanent footprint and the disturbance figure, and only the 2021 engineering drawing itself would resolve it.

**A separate, budgeted complication: CIP 120009, "Relocation of Husky Park," $1.25 million, not yet funded.** Its actual scope is relocating the DPW storage yard operation off this site, because neighboring developers asked the City to stop the outdoor material storage as incompatible with new development around it. Husky Park has no built park amenities today, it is currently a working DPW storage yard. But per the Master Plan itself (see [[Husky Park Master Plan Figures To Verify 2026-08-27]]), it is officially counted in the City's park inventory as one of 12 undeveloped properties, and it is the largest of the 12. "No built amenities" is accurate. "No park there to destroy" overstates it in one direction, and "minimal land lost" overstates it in the other, neither is established. What is established: a real, PRK-zoned, 22.58-acre parcel, of which some unconfirmed portion, bounded on the high end by a 3.96-acre disturbance figure that may include temporary construction impact, gets consumed by the road. State it that plainly rather than picking whichever framing is more convenient.

## The Weakest Material, Flag but Do Not Spend Time On

The "2015 logic in reverse" section, built on an Alderman O'Connor quote from an unverified video transcription. Hluch says so himself, "not yet checked against certified minutes... not as a verified quotation for the public record." Do not let this get traction as fact, it is explicitly labeled as unverified by its own author.

## Recommended Posture for the Workshop

Concede Items 2 and 8 openly and ask staff for a timeline on each, a survey and 2:1 replacement filing, and a Phase I ESA. That buys you credibility on everything else. Then work through Items 1, 3, 4, 5, 7, 9, and 10 as a batch of factual questions staff can answer in writing before the next meeting, not as unresolved legal exposure. Reframe Item 6 using his own footnotes, the path obligation survives independent of the road. On the equity argument, split it the way this memo does: push back hard on the geographic west-side/east-side framing using the distance data, but concede the citywide 928/978/50-acre shortfall outright, it is verified against the plan's own table. On the land itself, do not reach for either "no park to destroy" or "minimal land lost," neither is established. The accurate statement is that a specific, currently unconfirmed portion of a 22.58-acre PRK-zoned parcel, the largest undeveloped park property the City has, gets consumed, bounded on the high end by a 3.96-acre disturbance figure that has not been shown to be the permanent footprint. If pressed for a firmer number, the honest answer is that only the underlying engineering drawing settles it, and that is a reasonable thing to ask staff for directly. Note plainly that bid authorization is not tonight's business and the Council will expect these items closed before that vote.
