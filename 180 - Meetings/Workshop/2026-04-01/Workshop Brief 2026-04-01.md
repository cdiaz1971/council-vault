# FY 2027 Budget Work Session Briefing
**April 1, 2026 | Council President César Díaz**

---

## Bottom Line

Tonight is the orientation session for FY 2027 budget review. No votes are taken. O'Connor's proposed budget is built on a slowing but still healthy tax base, prioritizes Public Works and Police, and contains no housing affordability programming. The Council has until May 14 to negotiate before revision authority fully transfers. The adoption deadline is June 20 -- miss it and the Mayor's budget becomes law automatically.

---

## Process and Authority

The council approves the budget, tax rate, and fee schedule by simple majority. Tax rate must go to Frederick County by end of May. Budget adoption deadline is June 20.

**KEY DATE: May 14.** The Mayor retains revision authority through May 14. After that date, the council holds sole revision power, exercised as part of the motion to adopt. All council amendments must keep the budget balanced. If the council fails to adopt before June 20, the Mayor's proposed budget takes effect automatically.

## Mayor's Proposed Budget: Key Numbers

**Total operating revenue: ~$209 million**

Taxes are the dominant source at $116.8M (56% of total). Charges for services follow at $59M (28%). Intergovernmental revenue is $13.2M. Licenses, permits, fines, and miscellaneous make up the remainder.

**Expenditures by department (operating funds):**

| Department | Amount |
|-----------|--------|
| Public Works - Operations | $60,185,458 |
| Frederick Police Department | $54,586,534 |
| Debt Service | $22,969,178 |
| General Government | $21,176,544 |
| Fund Balance (Reserves) | $17,261,261 |
| Parks and Recreation | $14,982,320 |
| Housing and Human Services | $12,646,219 |
| Engineering, Permits and Inspections | $12,303,898 |
| Economic Development | $12,044,184 |
| Planning | $3,071,458 |
| Contingency | $910,000 |

**Compensation:** 2.44% COLA citywide. 3% merit for non-sworn staff. Insurance costs up 11.17% -- the largest cost driver and the Mayor's primary justification for resisting new spending.

**Real property tax trajectory:** Net assessable base grew from ~$7.3B in FY 2017 to a projected ~$13.8B in FY 2027. Growth peaked at ~11% in FY 2024, now decelerating to ~8% for FY 2027. Real property tax revenue rises from ~$52M (FY 2017) to ~$97M budgeted (FY 2027). FY 2026 actuals appear slightly below the $93M budget target. The deceleration is real and will be the Mayor's core fiscal argument throughout negotiations.

## Mayor's Stated Priorities

O'Connor frames the budget around four themes:

**Fiscal responsibility and tax rate stability.** No tax rate increase. Focus on Public Works, Public Safety, Parks.

**Modernization and transparency.** Digital permitting, infrastructure tracking, real-time financial transparency tools.

**Managing growth responsibly.** Growing city demands without overexpanding government. Addresses traffic, development, and service pressures.

**Investing in community.** Childcare and family programming, economic development, arts and culture, sustainability, and "neighborhood equity" (undefined in materials).

There is no housing production language, no affordability target, and no tenant protection programming anywhere in the Mayor's proposed priorities.

## Watch List

**May 14 is the hard deadline for council leverage.** Any amendments must be finalized before that date or introduced as part of the adoption motion on May 21. The departmental review window is the negotiation period.

**April 9 -- Housing and Human Services -- is the highest-priority session.** Press on what the City Housing Fund contains in FY 2027, what $12.6M in H&HS actually delivers against a documented 8,133-unit shortage, and whether Nash's $250k affordable housing RFP line can be incorporated.

**The affordable housing RFP line is seed money, not investment.** Nash's $250k funds program design, not direct affordability outcomes. Clarify whether it feeds the housing production target resolution or stands independently before April 9.

**Police at $54.6M is 30% of General Fund expenditures.** April 23 matters both for the budget size and because any FPD reporting requirement under the City Values Act will need a budget hook. Watch for FPD claiming implementation costs.

**The 11.17% insurance cost increase will compress every department.** Expect it as the blanket justification for resisting council add-ons. Prepare offsets or an explicit prioritization argument.

**The housing study reference is a timing risk.** Nash's slide 6 ties housing policy development to the housing study release. If this is being used to defer action, the production target resolution may need to be framed as independent of that timeline.

**The $250k legal analysis consultant line is unspecified.** Find out whether it can cover preemption review for the rental junk fees ordinance and future tenant protection work.
