# Council Workshop Briefing -- Gateway Property and Penalties -- 2026-06-25

## Bottom Line

This session has two distinct tracks with very different stakes. The Gateway items (B and C) are the main event. Items B and C are linked: the rezoning and the annexation amendment rise or fall together. The applicant is pushing toward a July 16 regular meeting vote. The penalty discussion (Item A) is a policy conversation with real equity exposure that the City Attorney is flagging herself.

---

## Item A: Penalties for Violation of City Laws

**What this is.** State law changed October 1, 2025. The maximum misdemeanor fine and municipal infraction fine both increased from $1,000 to $5,000. City Code Section 1-7 has not been updated. Rachel Gray is asking council for direction: raise some or all penalty amounts? If yes, staff drafts amending ordinances.

**The ask.** No vote tonight. Policy direction only. Gray wants to know whether to prepare ordinances.

**Equity flag.** Gray's own executive summary acknowledges the risk directly: higher fines disproportionately affect lower-income residents less able to absorb them. She suggests monitoring citation data. That acknowledgment opens the door for a meaningful council conversation about which offenses, if any, warrant increases and whether a hardship waiver or payment plan framework should accompany any increases.

**Watch items.** The misdemeanor list (A.2) includes Sec. 15-27.2 (panhandling) and Sec. 6-21 (camping on public property). These are poverty-related conduct offenses. Any increase in those categories will directly affect the unhoused population and should be called out. The municipal infraction list (A.3) includes Sec. 49-20 (failure to comply with rental registration) at $500 per violation. If rental registration enforcement is a goal, this has direct connection to the Real Rent Ordinance strategy.

**Question worth raising.** Before directing staff to draft increases across the board, ask which specific categories are chronically non-compliant under the current penalty structure. Increases only make sense where enforcement is occurring and the deterrent is demonstrably failing.

## Items B and C: Gateway Property (Cricket Stadium ZMA and Annexation Amendment)

These are a package. A vote on either item without the other does not make sense. The public meeting has not been scheduled. The annexation item is tentatively scheduled for a July 16 regular meeting. No date appears for the ZMA public meeting.

### What the ZMA Does (Item B)

Rezones 24.33 acres on the east side of the city from General Commercial (GC) to Institutional Floating Zone (IST) to establish a Special Events Center for cricket matches and community events. Applicant is AcersInfra / Washington Freedom (Major League Cricket). Planning Commission voted 3-0 in favor (July 2025).

### What the Annexation Amendment Does (Item C)

Amends two conditions of the 2009 Gateway annexation resolution.

Condition 7(a): Increases the regional transportation contribution from $500,000 (never paid, due in 2019) to $1.5 million. Structured as $500,000 within 30 days of rezoning approval or by August 30, 2026, plus $1 million within two years of the first payment. APFO improvements are separate and not creditable against this contribution.

Condition 7(b): Replaces the prior 16-acre floodplain dedication requirement with approximately 14 acres of public recreational area along the Monocacy River, including a 10-foot-wide trail from East Patrick Street to the I-70 right-of-way and a small boat launch, approved via site plan through Parks and Recreation Commission and Planning Commission. City retains sole discretion over whether the land is dedicated in fee-simple or retained by the applicant with easements.

Parks and Recreation Commission voted 7-0 with one abstention in favor (June 2025). Planning Commission voted 3-0 in favor (July 2025).

### Airport Commission

Does not oppose the project. Conditions absorbed into proposed rezoning conditions 8 and 9:

1. This evaluation covers the complex only. Any future residential, multi-use, commercial, or other additions require a separate compatibility evaluation.
2. No aerial displays of fireworks, drones, lasers, or bright upward-pointing lights without prior airport approval, which will be limited in nature.
3. Disclosure to all users of the complex about the Airport Overlay district and 24/7 aircraft operations, noise, and emissions.

The carve-out in condition 1 is significant. The remaining 27-plus acres are still zoned GC. Any future development phase will require a new Airport Commission review.

### What Has Changed Since March 26

Events Management Plan (EMP) now required for all events regardless of attendance (previously only above 1,500). EMP approval authority moved from Planning Director to Chief Operations Officer. HOA representative from adjacent subdivision added to stakeholder group. Annual update requirement added (by April 1). Residential parking prohibition language tightened. The FEMA Conditional Letter of Map Revision (CLOMR) is now in the record as B.16.

### The Fiscal Case

RCLCO analysis (draft, February 2026, commissioned by Washington Freedom) projects $895,000 annually to the City of Frederick once stabilized: $755,000 in admissions and amusement tax and $140,000 in property tax. Projects 98,000 visitors annually across 20 events (10 MLC matches plus 10 local events). Total construction spending estimated at $45 million across two phases. These are client-provided assumptions, not independent analysis. The document is explicitly labeled draft.

### Affirmative Case for Voting Yes

The rezoning and annexation amendment are stronger together than either is alone. Voting yes locks in $1.5 million in regional transportation contributions that were already owed and never collected. The original $500,000 was due in 2019. Seven years of non-payment is being resolved with a tripled commitment and a hard payment schedule tied directly to rezoning approval.

The 14-acre Monocacy River recreational area is a genuine community asset. A 10-foot trail, boat launch, and public recreational access along the river are improvements the city would otherwise have to fund itself or forgo. The applicant builds and pays for it.

The property has sat undeveloped since 2009. Any productive use generates tax revenue the city is not currently receiving.

The cricket audience demographic has genuine resonance for District 2. The sport draws heavily from South Asian, Caribbean, and African diaspora communities. Frederick's foreign-born population is concentrated in exactly those origin communities. A venue that draws and celebrates those audiences is not a neutral amenity.

The conditions of rezoning are meaningfully stronger than what the Planning Commission originally recommended. The applicant has been responsive to each work session concern.

The unresolved issues, particularly MDE permitting and the LMC parking question, are real but they are post-approval conditions or site plan issues. Condition 6 stops public use until the MDE permit is in hand. The parking compliance question gets resolved at final site plan. A yes vote does not waive those requirements.

### Traffic Concerns

The core concern from public comment is that Route 144 runs at 55 mph with no crosswalks or sidewalks on that stretch and cannot safely handle pedestrian movement from off-site parking to the venue.

The proposal to eliminate the Park and Ride lot to improve traffic flow does not hold up. The Park and Ride is a state asset controlled by SHA. The city cannot unilaterally eliminate it. Removing it reduces parking supply the venue is counting on and pushes vehicles onto local streets. The proposed conditions already prohibit event parking in the SHA lot unless SHA expressly approves it.

The EMP is required to include a plan preventing pedestrians from crossing MD 144. The COO cannot approve the EMP without that element resolved. The venue cannot open until the EMP is approved. That is a real procedural check.

Question to put to staff tonight: Has SHA completed an independent traffic impact study, or has the city only received the applicant's consultant letters?

### MDE and Floodplain

This is the most legitimate unresolved concern. Significant portions of the proposed cricket field sit within 100-year and 500-year FEMA special flood hazard areas. Building the venue requires substantial fill. The MDE Nontidal Wetlands and Waterways Permit has not been obtained. The FEMA CLOMR is conditional, not final.

Condition 6 of the rezoning prohibits public use until the MDE permit is in hand. So the council is not approving construction in the floodplain tonight. It is approving the land use change, with public use gated behind state permitting.

The harder question is what happens if MDE denies the permit. The conditions do not answer that. The applicant would be in a difficult position and would likely have to return to the council with a materially different site plan.

The downstream flooding concern from the public comment deserves an answer on the record. The commenter argued that filling the floodplain could push high-velocity water onto properties outside the city boundary, including Pinecliff Park, the Frederick Quarry, and Amtrak rail lines along the Monocacy. Whether the city hydrologist's review included downstream impacts outside city limits is not clear from the record.

Question to put to staff: Did the city hydrologist's review include downstream flooding impacts on properties outside city limits?

### LMC 607 Parking

The public comment raises a specific LMC compliance argument: LMC Section 607(d)(2)(A) requires off-site parking to be within 1,320 feet of the site. The applicant's traffic management plan relies on regional off-site parking in Baltimore, Bethesda, Tysons Corner, and Leesburg. This appears to be a direct conflict. The parking compliance question gets resolved at final site plan, but the staff executive summary does not directly address the commenter's argument.

Question to put to Collard: How does the applicant intend to comply with LMC 607 at final site plan?

### Questions for the Record Before July 16

1. Has SHA completed an independent traffic impact study?
2. What is the status of the MDE Nontidal Wetlands permit application and what is the anticipated timeline?
3. Did the city hydrologist's review include downstream flooding impacts outside city limits?
4. How does the applicant intend to satisfy LMC 607 parking requirements at final site plan?
5. What is the consequence to the rezoning if MDE denies the permit?
