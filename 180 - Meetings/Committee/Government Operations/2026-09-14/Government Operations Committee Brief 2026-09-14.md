# Government Operations Committee Brief 2026-09-14

**Meeting:** Government Operations Committee | September 14, 2026 | 4:00 PM | Nash chairs
**Format:** Workshop and old business only. No votes.

---

**Bottom line.** Four workshop items, nothing requiring a vote tonight. The headline item is new and outside the routine Charter-update cadence: a $15M bond-funded revolving loan fund for affordable housing, pitched directly to Nash and the Committee by Rockville's Housing Director. The other three are Charter amendments continuing the comprehensive Charter update begun in fall 2024, one of which (eminent domain/public property) picks up a thread already flagged as "live" back in August, and one of which (administration) restarts an unresolved three-way disagreement among Brehm, Nash, and Adkins. None of tonight's four items currently appear in the Notion Legislative Roadmap, this is their first formal appearance there.

---

**A. Alternative Funding for Affordable Housing -- Frederick Housing Accelerator Fund (Ryan Trout).** Ryan Trout, Director of Housing for the City of Rockville and a Frederick resident, has submitted a memo and companion summary proposing a $15 million General Obligation bond, explored for FY2028, to capitalize a new independent nonprofit revolving loan fund (the Frederick Housing Accelerator Fund, or HAF). The mechanism: bond proceeds fund subordinate construction loans covering 15-30% of a project's capital stack at roughly 5-6% interest, replacing private mezzanine debt that runs 12-20% annually, in exchange for a binding affordability set-aside. Trout's illustrative example (a $60M, 100-unit project) shows the cheaper capital saving about $1.68M per year, enough to convert 40 of 100 units to permanent affordability (20 at ≤30% AMI, 20 at ≤50% AMI) with room to spare, and argues the same mechanism can be the difference between a stalled deal happening at all. Estimated annual debt service is $1.0-1.2M, under 0.7% of the FY2027 general fund, offset partly by roughly $900K/year in loan interest flowing back. Recommendation is phased: (1) authorize a feasibility study and enabling ordinance in FY2027, (2) issue the bond and open the fund by late 2027, (3) evaluate performance and plan a larger second tranche in 5-7 years.

This is directly on-model with two things already in the vault: Montgomery County's Housing Opportunities Commission fund ([[Montgomery County Housing Production Fund as State Model]]), which is the explicit template Trout cites, and the pending Maryland statewide $250M revolving fund proposal now circulating in Annapolis ([[Maryland Statewide Housing Revolving Fund Proposal]]). A City-level fund would position Frederick to draw down state matching capital if that statewide program advances, rather than starting from scratch later. Worth noting for the file: a page titled "Housing Accelerator Fund -- $250k CIP" already exists as an empty placeholder in the Notion roadmap, that figure is a different, much smaller idea and should not be conflated with tonight's $15M bond ask when this gets logged.

On the standing principle that uncompensated affordability mandates suppress production, this proposal is on the right side of it, the affordability set-aside is explicitly exchanged for below-market capital, not imposed without an offset, which is the structural distinction that research base cares about.

Politically, this is a bond authorization, not a resolution, so it eventually needs Finance sign-off (Roark) and full Council approval, a heavier lift than most items that land in this committee. Trout has no formal City role, so expect the committee's first move to be about process (referring it to staff or Finance for a feasibility read) rather than substance. Given the O'Connor administration's general fiscal caution and friction with council-driven housing measures, watch how CAO Barkdoll's office frames any early feedback.

---

**B. Charter Consideration, Public Property / Eminent Domain (Nash).** Would amend Charter Article XIII: Section 2 (Disposal of Property) extends the public notice period from 20 to 30 days and adds a new 30-day right-of-first-refusal letting nonprofits serving City residents purchase disposed City property at a "market-competitive price"; Section 5 (Eminent Domain) adds language explicitly authorizing eminent domain to address blighted or under-utilized property conditions. This is a continuation, not a new idea, it was flagged as "still live" with affordable-housing preservation potential in the August 24 brief, and Brehm's own notes on the Charter Changes list cite Code Sec. 27-4 as the City's existing blight-related eminent domain authority. No vote tonight; per the standard process this returns to Committee for a member vote on whether to advance it, then goes to City Legal and the Board of Election Supervisors.

The nonprofit right-of-first-refusal piece is worth flagging as a genuine, if narrow, affordable-housing and community-land-trust tool, it is the kind of mechanism that could feed directly into the CLT work already in the vault ([[Frederick Community Land Trust -- Policy Brief]]) if a nonprofit housing partner wanted to use it to acquire disposed City parcels.

---

**C. Charter Consideration, Public Sidewalks (Nash, Brehm).** Would amend Charter Article X by adding a new subsection letting the City Council, by majority vote, assume financial responsibility for sidewalk maintenance and repair. This is enabling language, not a mandate, it opens the door without committing the City to the cost. The backup includes the current Chapter 22 ordinance text (Sec. 22-24 through 22-28), which today places the repair duty and cost squarely on abutting property owners, enforced as a $100 municipal infraction for noncompliance. That is a real equity question worth naming directly: repair costs currently fall on individual property owners, a meaningful burden in lower-income, higher-renter-turnover areas like District 2, and this amendment is the mechanism that would let the City take that cost on instead. Keep this legally and financially distinct from the "Sidewalk Cost Review" already tracked in the Notion Roadmap under Public Works Committee (a $1M-per-mile cost benchmarking study), that is a cost study, this is a Charter authority question, they will eventually need to connect but are not the same item.

---

**D. Charter Consideration, City Administration (Brehm).** Would amend Charter Article VI(A) to define "essential City administration structures," aimed at consistency across administrations and building council input into key appointments. The draft language itself (listed in the packet as prepared by Brehm) was not included in tonight's backup, only the current Article VI(A) text. This restarts a thread the August 24 brief flagged as unresolved: Brehm, Nash, and Adkins each hold separate, not-yet-reconciled positions on Article VI(A) reporting structure and council confirmation authority, and Adkins' narrower proposal (limiting council confirmation to positions reporting directly to the Mayor) was the version on the table in August, not Brehm's. The CAO model debate tabled August 10, strong mayor versus weak mayor versus city manager, is the direct backdrop for this conversation. Worth confirming with Brehm's office before the meeting whether his draft language reconciles with or conflicts with Adkins' August position, since without the actual text in the packet that is not yet knowable from the paper alone.

The Administration Generally Charter thread remains genuinely unreconciled across three members, tonight's item is Brehm's alone, not a consensus draft, so don't treat it as further along than it is.
