# GovOps Agenda Brief -- 2026-06-22

## Summary

Two items on the June 22 Gov Ops agenda. The water interest waiver ordinance is a substantive equity item worth engaging on if Nash opens the floor. The charter amendments list is reference-only with no action expected and nothing endorsed as of June 15.

---

## Bottom Line

The water ordinance is good policy for District 2 constituents and carries no political risk. Show up ready to ask one pointed question about CAO rulemaking timeline. The charter document is background -- no votes, no decisions. Monitor the Taylor items (undocumented council members, renter displacement) as potential openings for later engagement.

## Item II-A: Proposed Ordinance -- Water Account Interest Waiver (Workshop)

**What it does.** Adds subsection (f) to Code Sec. 25-2, giving the Chief Administrative Officer discretion to waive or reduce interest on past-due residential water bills. Relief is not automatic. The customer must apply in writing, demonstrate active participation in a financial assistance program or reasonable steps to obtain one, and enter into a payment plan with the City.

**Equity read.** District 2 carries a poverty rate 1.5x the county median. Past-due utility debt with compounding interest is a displacement mechanism -- it converts a water bill into a first lien on the property under the county tax-collection mechanism. The payment plan requirement is reasonable. The assistance-program requirement uses the phrase "reasonable steps to secure assistance," which is appropriately flexible and should stay that way. Any move to narrow eligibility to active program enrollment only would undermine the relief for the people who need it most.

**Watch item.** The waiver is discretionary with the CAO. No standards are codified for how that discretion is exercised beyond the three eligibility conditions. The ordinance requires the CAO to adopt implementing regulations, but does not set a timeline or require council review of those regulations.

Suggested question: What is the timeline for the CAO to publish implementing regulations, and will the council have visibility before they take effect?

**Political posture.** Supportive. No risk. Public comment may come from constituents who have faced shutoffs -- that is the natural voice for this item.

## Item III-A: Proposed Charter Amendments List (For Reference)

**Status.** Reference only. No topic has an endorsed change as of June 15, 2026. The document reflects positions collected through mid-June from Nash, Taylor, Brehm, and Adkins.

**Alignment map.**

Nash and Brehm are aligned on most items: exploring redistricting reform, creating a special election process for council vacancies, requiring a delivery date for the Mayoral financial conditions report, clarifying City responsibility for sidewalk maintenance, and creating a nonprofit right-of-first-refusal on property disposal. That Nash-Brehm alignment is the working majority on charter work.

Taylor raises two items of direct relevance. First, she flags the residency qualification requirement as a potential barrier for renters who get displaced from their district -- a housing equity issue worth tracking. Second, she raises whether an undocumented council member could complete required IRS employment paperwork. That question has no current answer in the document and is live given Frederick's demographics and the CVA passage.

Adkins wants a removal mechanism for council members convicted of crimes or severe misconduct, and a staff-input requirement on legislative proposals. The staff-input provision is worth watching -- depending on how it is drafted, it could slow council's legislative pace or be used as an administrative delay mechanism.

**High-interest items.**

The Nash proposal on nonprofit right-of-first-refusal for public property disposal (Article XIII, Section 2) is a housing production tool. A 30-day window for nonprofits to bid before property goes to market is the mechanism that peer cities use to channel underutilized public land toward affordable development. Worth understanding how it would be structured.

The eminent domain language strengthening (Article XIII, Section 5) is relevant to housing production. Code Sec. 27-4 already allows blighted property acquisition under state law; the open question is whether stronger charter language changes anything in practice or is redundant.

**César's recorded position.** None. The document does not reflect a position from César on any charter topic. That is appropriate for observer posture. The charter process remains open if any item warrants engagement.

**Suggested question.** Is there a timeline for the committee to move from reference to action on any of these topics, and will there be a full council workshop before anything goes to a vote?
