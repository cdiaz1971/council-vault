# Gov Ops Committee Brief -- June 8, 2026

**Meeting:** Government Operations Committee | 4:00 PM | Nash chairs
**Format:** Workshop only. No votes. Observer role.

---

**Redistricting workshop.** The ACLU of Maryland and Frederick County NAACP are presenting on voting rights and redistricting. Their main argument: the Supreme Court's decision in *Louisiana v. Callais* gutted federal VRA Section 2 vote dilution protections, making race-conscious redistricting legally risky as a remedy. Maryland responded with SB 255 as part of the Maryland Voting Rights Act, allowing state-level challenges to vote dilution in county and municipal elections.

The ACLU's ask is that the Council consider ranked-choice voting as a race-neutral tool to protect minority voting power without drawing new race-based maps. SB 848/HB 1448 (just signed) creates administrative infrastructure for RCV in municipal elections, though Takoma Park remains the only Maryland jurisdiction currently using it. Limited voting and cumulative voting are also mentioned as court-approved alternatives.

The ACLU presentation slides name César's election as Council President as evidence that Frederick's structural reforms are working. Be aware of this if public comment or colleagues ask about it.

No commitment should come from this session. Nash has been the lead on charter work and her position on RCV should be known before any response.

---

**Charter amendments -- reference only.** A working list updated May 19, 2026. Nash, Taylor, and Brehm have significant overlap on redistricting provisions, a special election process for vacant seats, and sidewalk maintenance responsibility. The nonprofit right-of-first-refusal on city property disposals (Nash and Brehm) is worth tracking for affordable housing preservation potential.

Taylor raises two items worth knowing. First, she questions whether the district residency requirement starting in 2029 could disadvantage renters displaced from their district. Second, she flags an unresolved legal question about whether an undocumented elected official can complete IRS employment forms as a city employee. Neither is being acted on at this session, but both could generate press attention.

Taylor also proposes a non-interference provision modeled on the county charter, barring Council from directing individual executive branch employees while preserving the right to request written information for legislative purposes. Standard governance language, unlikely to be controversial, but worth reading given the relationship with the O'Connor administration.
