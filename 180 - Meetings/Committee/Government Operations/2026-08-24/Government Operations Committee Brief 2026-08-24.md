# Government Operations Committee Brief 2026-08-24

**Meeting:** Government Operations Committee | August 24, 2026 | 4:00 PM | City Hall Boardroom | Nash chairs
**Format:** Workshop and old business only. No votes.

---

**Bottom line.** Four workshop items, nothing requiring a vote. Two are governance power questions with real long term stakes, who gets council confirmation over appointed positions, and whether council gets guaranteed direct access to city staff. One is Nash's proposal to route the $4.3M School Mitigation Fee balance to FCPS with a restricted use list that touches ESL and early childhood, close to César's priorities but missing an equity impact statement. The fourth is the six month charter scheduling calendar. Best use of this session is getting positions on record early on Adkins' appointed positions language and Nash's staff access language, since both will come back for real drafting.

---

**A. School Mitigation Fee Account (Nash).** Proposes amending Sec. 4-22(d) to require biannual transfers, January 1 and July 1, of the school mitigation fee account balance to FCPS. Eligible uses include Career and Technology Center expansion, ESL program facilities, early childhood education programs, and vocational and technical investments at Frederick High and Thomas Johnson High. Account balance is roughly $4.337M as of August 19. Nash is seeking initial feedback before refining language for a future Council vote, not presenting a final draft. The equity impact statement field is blank despite the ESL and early childhood provisions being directly relevant to working class and immigrant families in City schools. Worth asking that it be completed before this returns for a vote.

---

**B. Charter Consideration Schedule (Nash).** Informational six month forecast for GovOps charter work through late February 2027. Confirms this session covers Appointed Positions and Council Staff Access. Confirms the non-citizen candidates and non-interference provisions land in October, both authored by Taylor.

---

**C. Charter Change, Appointed Positions (Adkins).** Would amend Article VI(A) Sec. 3 so only positions reporting directly to the Mayor require council advice and consent, naming budget and administration, economic development, finance, housing and human services, legal, planning, police, and public works explicitly. Everything else would move to standard hiring with no council confirmation role. HHS Director Stuart Campbell's role stays confirmable under Adkins' language as written.

---

**D. Charter Change, Council Operations Staff (Nash).** Would add language to Article VI(A) Sec. 2 guaranteeing the council direct access to city staff and their full cooperation, notwithstanding the Mayor's supervisory authority over departments. First real move toward loosening the strong mayor administrative model in favor of council capacity. Likely to read as sensitive to the O'Connor administration even at workshop stage. Frame any support around oversight function, not administration criticism.

---

**Old Business.** IV.A is reference only, the full unfiltered charter changes list grouped by member. Nothing to act on, but two entries are worth tracking. Taylor's unresolved question of whether an undocumented council member could complete required IRS employment forms as a city employee is still sitting there, first flagged in June. The Nash and Brehm proposal for a 30 day nonprofit right-of-first-refusal on disposed city property is still live, with affordable housing preservation potential worth keeping on the radar. IV.B, For Action, has nothing listed.

---

**Public comment.** Permitted after each workshop item, three minutes per speaker, plus Granicus e-comment. No scheduled speakers in the packet. Low profile committee session, but the appointed positions item touches HHS leadership structure closely enough that advocacy groups could take interest if it advances further.

---

**Watch list.** Adkins' appointed positions language and the broader Administration Generally charter thread (Brehm, Nash, and Adkins all have separate unresolved positions on it) are not yet reconciled and will take more than one session.

The CAO discussion from August 10 was tabled, not resolved, members split between strong mayor, weak mayor, and city manager models. Not on this agenda but is the backdrop for the appointed positions conversation.

Taylor's undocumented council member and IRS forms question is squarely in César's lane and comes up formally in October under Non-Citizen Candidates. Worth having a position ready before then rather than reacting live.

The school mitigation fee item has real equity relevance and no equity impact statement. That is a clean, low friction ask.
