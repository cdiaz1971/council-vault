# Public Works Committee Brief 2026-09-08

**Public Works Committee -- September 8, 2026 at 4:00 PM, City Hall Boardroom**

**Bottom Line**

Three workshop items, no votes today. The street pavement overview is routine and informational. The other two are the more consequential ones: the E-Bike ordinance is back for a second round of committee feedback with the shared-use path default, the equity and legal problem flagged in May, still unresolved in the September 1 draft. The noise ordinance is a substantive new draft with real enforcement and business-community implications and has no prior vault research behind it, so today is effectively the first time this specific text gets scrutinized on the record.

**Action Items**

None. All three items are workshop discussion items with no votes scheduled. Both ordinance drafts remain at the committee-feedback stage ahead of formal introduction to full Council.

**Consent Agenda**

Not applicable, this is a workshop-only agenda.

**Presentations and Reports**

*Item 1, Street Pavement Overview.* Presented by Tracy Coleman (Director of Public Works), Mike Winpigler (Superintendent of Street Maintenance), and Shraddha Praharaj (Project Engineer II, Traffic). Purely informational, staff recommendation is "presentation for Council information," no action requested. Covers the pavement condition survey (O'Connell Lawrence, 2021 to 2022), standard maintenance protocols, contractor versus in-house staffing tradeoffs, and the five-year paving plan. The city now maintains 360+ centerline miles and 1,300+ lane miles, up 41 centerline miles (roughly 164 lane miles) since 2020. FY2027 fiscal picture: $3.3 million in Street Maintenance operational expenditures against $4.7 million in Highway User Revenue Fund revenue, plus $3.5 million budgeted under CIP 320029. Staff's own equity impact statement says the plan "prioritizes safety and accessibility by directing resources to neighborhoods with the greatest need" and "minimizes financial burdens on homeowners through City funded repairs." Worth pressing staff on which neighborhoods land in Year 1 of the plan, District 2 corridors specifically, since that framing invites the question directly.

*Item 2, E-Bike (EPMD) Ordinance Draft.* Sponsored by Council Member Brehm on behalf of the Administration, revised September 1 following the July 9 Work Session. This is the second committee round; goal is to prepare the draft for full Council consideration. [[EPMD Ordinance Brief -- 2026-05-26]] and [[EPMD Ordinance -- Equity Argument, Class 1 and 2 Path Access]] cover this draft's predecessor in depth and recommended three fixes. Checking the September 1 text against those asks:

The shared-use path default is still unresolved. Section 33.6(b) still prohibits EPMD use on sidewalks and shared-use paths by default, with access opening only where the Public Works Director has affirmatively designated and posted a path as suitable (33.6(c)). This is the same opt-in structure that inverts Maryland Transportation Article 21-1205.2's permissive default, and the same provision District 2's e-bike-dependent commuters raised through Raymond Brown's constituent letter and the AMAC letter. Since this is the second workshop round and the stated goal is preparing for full Council introduction, this is close to the last chance to get the fix into the draft before it moves.

The passenger provision is only partly addressed. The new language permits passengers when "the device was designed to accommodate" them, an improvement, but Section 33.10 still bans aftermarket modifications that increase a device's manufacturer-designed passenger capacity. A retrofit child seat on a cargo bike, the scenario the May brief flagged as a working-parent transportation issue, would still be caught by that provision.

The road-speed language AMAC flagged appears resolved. The operating-regulations section (33.8) no longer contains language that would functionally ban EPMDs from local streets with speed limits above the device's top speed.

*Item 3, Noise Ordinance Draft Continued.* Sponsored by Council Member Brehm on behalf of the Administration, presented with Michael Fry (Assistant City Attorney) and John Corbett (Acting Deputy Chief of Police). Revised following the July 14 Public Works Committee meeting, which has no corresponding brief or notes in the vault, a documentation gap worth noting. This is a substantial new Chapter 34 repealing the City's existing scattered noise provisions (Sections 3-7, 6-18, 15-21.1 through 15-21.8) and replacing them with zoning-based decibel limits: residential 62 dBA daytime and 52 dBA nighttime, commercial 67/62, industrial 75/75, with a tighter 45/35 dBA standard for noise carrying between townhouse and multifamily units. Construction is capped at 90 dBA daytime, and recreational vehicles need a 300-foot buffer from neighboring residences.

Two provisions carry real political and administrative weight. First, Article II specifically targets entertainment noise from eating and drinking establishments (plainly audible at 100 feet), which puts this ordinance in direct contact with downtown and Golden Mile business interests, worth knowing before the item resurfaces at full Council. Second, the special-event and public-project variance process (34.18(g), 34.20, 34.21) routes noise variance requests back through City Council for a public hearing and vote, meaning this ordinance creates a recurring Council workload rather than a one-time decision. The executive summary submitted with this item leaves the equity impact and fiscal impact fields blank, the same gap flagged on the Housing Solutions Fund Ordinance in the July 28 committee brief. Given that the EPMD ordinance sitting on the same agenda got real equity scrutiny, it is a fair question to ask staff why this one didn't.

**Public Comment**

Public comment is permitted after each workshop item, three minutes per person. No scheduled speakers are noted in the packet, but the E-bike item has an active constituent record (Raymond Brown) and AMAC engagement, and the noise ordinance's entertainment-establishment provisions could draw downtown business voices now that specific decibel limits are on the table.

**Watch List**

The E-bike shared-path default is the single most consequential unresolved item on today's agenda. It has now gone unaddressed through one full revision cycle since the May committee round. If it isn't raised again today, it is likely to carry through to formal introduction unchanged.

The noise ordinance's special-event variance process creates future Council votes on individual noise variances, this is worth flagging now rather than after the ordinance passes and the first variance request arrives.

Both ordinances are described in their own executive summaries as preparing for "future consideration by the full City Council," meaning Brehm may be signaling intent to move one or both out of committee soon. Worth reading the room today for that signal, since it changes the window for further amendments.
