# Public Works Committee Minutes 2026-10-13

## Summary

Chair Peter Brehm called the meeting to order at 4:01 pm, with Council Members Shackelford and Hempel Irani present. The September 22, 2026 minutes were approved as written. Three workshop items were discussed, and the meeting adjourned at 5:02 pm.

## Outcomes

**Rental Licensing Ordinance Update.** The committee reviewed proposed updates covering local property representatives, rental unit classifications, smoke alarms, pest prevention, inspection procedures, and clear identification of City inspectors. Further revisions addressed annual licensing fees, use of special funds for administrative costs and up to six months of tenant displacement assistance, and possible financial help for landlords with fewer than 10 rental units for repairs. Members discussed the substantial existing fund balance, appropriate uses of the funds, and possibly lowering licensing fees to reflect administrative costs. Members agreed fund allocation and rental assistance programs need more discussion. The proposed ordinance was approved to advance to a City Council workshop. No vote count is recorded.

**Housing Solutions Fund Ordinance.** The committee reviewed the draft (DLP is the Development Loan Program) and its proposed financing from MPDU program proceeds and other revenue designated by Council. Discussion covered the legal considerations of allocating rental licensing fees to the fund and spending parameters across affordable housing development, homebuyer assistance, home repair, rental and eviction assistance, and administrative costs. Staff will review current spending, administrative costs, and staffing with Housing and Human Services and consult the City Attorney on eligible funding sources. Public commenters stressed adequate staffing, prioritizing households at 30% AMI, subsidies for lower-income residents, funding for small-scale housing projects, quarterly public reporting, and predictable annual City funding.

**Noise Ordinance.** The committee reviewed revisions including new decibel standards, a definition of vibration, possible motor vehicle noise rules, revised penalties, and mitigation procedures for businesses, special events, and public projects. Members discussed enforcement, fine levels, permitted hours, and balancing quiet with downtown vibrancy, and recommended more stakeholder consultation before advancing.
