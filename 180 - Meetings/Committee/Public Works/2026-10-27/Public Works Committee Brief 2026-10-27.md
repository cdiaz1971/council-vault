# Public Works Committee Brief 2026-10-27

**Public Works Committee -- October 27, 2026 at 4:00 PM, City Hall Boardroom**

Chair Peter Brehm, members Derek Shackelford and Sarah Hempel Irani. You are not a committee member, your window is Elected Officials' Comments and any informal word with Brehm and Shackelford.

## Bottom Line

Two workshop items, no votes, both Brehm's. The one that matters is the MPDU rewrite: Brehm and Shackelford are now drafting their own Chapter 19 update, and the first language drops the threshold from 25 to 10 units and moves to a Frederick County AMI, but says nothing about the $2.00 per square foot fee-in-lieu. A lower threshold paired with an unchanged $2 fee mostly produces more small fee checks, not more affordable units. This is the moment to get the fee recalculation and a tiered threshold into their draft before their framework hardens around yours.

## Action Items

None. Both items are workshop discussion only. The only formal business is review of the October 13 minutes (see Presentations and Reports).

## Consent Agenda

Not applicable, workshop agenda.

## Presentations and Reports

**Minutes, October 13, 2026.** Routine. The official minutes record that the Rental Licensing Ordinance update was approved to advance to a full Council workshop, the Housing Solutions Fund ordinance was discussed with staff checking eligible revenue sources (including rental licensing fees) with the City Attorney, and the noise ordinance was held for more stakeholder consultation. Public commenters on the fund asked for priority at 30% AMI and quarterly reporting. Filed in the vault as [[Public Works Committee Minutes 2026-10-13]].

**Item III.1, MPDU Ordinance Update: Initial Discussion.** Shackelford and Brehm are working with staff on Chapter 19, and this is Brehm's first set of policy points. Four pieces:

*Purpose and intent section.* Strong language: documented affordability crisis, mixed-income communities, deconcentration of poverty, and housing for households at 30 to 80% AMI. Good for you to endorse on the record. But the operative tiers today are 60, 70, and 80% AMI for rentals, and nothing in the draft creates a 30% tier. A purpose clause that names 30% AMI without a mechanism invites the question of how the program will actually reach those households, which is exactly what commenters raised on October 13.

*Threshold to 10 units, flat.* Projects of 10 or more units must set aside 12.5% or pay the fee, and later additions of 10 or more units are also covered. This is the Task Force's version, and it is the one your September 10 workshop brief warned about: a threshold cut without the fee increase is close to net-neutral for affordability. At 12.5%, a 10-unit project owes 1.25 units, so nearly every project in the 10 to 24 range will pay the fee. At $2.00 per square foot that is about $20,000 for a 10-unit building of 1,000 square foot units. Your strategy brief's tiered structure (full requirement at 15+, fee only at 7 to 14, exempt below 7) does the same work with less compliance burden on small builders and captures more missing-middle production. Two drafting questions worth asking: how fractional units are rounded, and whether there is an anti-segmentation rule to stop a developer from splitting a project into 9-unit phases or adjacent parcels.

*Frederick County AMI ("FCAMI") in place of HUD's Washington-area figure,* unless another funding source requires HUD. This is a real win and lines up with [[Frederick City AMI - Analysis and Options]]: the regional figure lets a "60% AMI" two-bedroom rent above the City's median rent. Three cautions. First, a County figure is not the City figure your analysis used ($112,741 City median family income), and the County number is likely higher, so the gain is smaller than the City-based estimate. Ask staff which data source and household-size method they will use, who publishes it, and how often. Second, lower income limits mean lower controlled rents, which raises the per-unit cost to developers, so expect FCBIA and FCAR to argue it reduces feasibility. That argument is stronger if the fee is still $2, because the fee becomes even more attractive than building. Third, the Code already says "Frederick County area median income," so ask whether HHS has actually been using HUD's Washington figure in practice.

*Exemption for 100% affordable projects and a separate incentive article.* Sensible, since Joyfield at Worman's Mill showed a fully affordable LIHTC project still needed an MPDU agreement. The gap: the draft doesn't define "100% affordable." The parallel park-fee draft uses 80% AMI with a covenant for the life of the affordability term. If a LIHTC project's compliance period is shorter than the MPDU's 40-year control period, the exemption could trade 40-year affordability for something shorter. Ask that the definition require an income ceiling and a minimum term at least as long as the MPDU period. The new incentive article is also a natural home for ideas already on your list: fee deferral to certificate of occupancy for deed-restricted units, and the APFO school-test adjustment.

*What's missing.* No fee-in-lieu rate, no use of the § 19-6.1(b)(3) recalculation formula (its three-year mark falls around November 2026, which is the legally anchored route to a higher fee), no deeper-affordability option, no CLT compliance option, and no answer on whether MPDU standards vest under the Housing Certainty Act. Fiscal impact, equity impact, and staff recommendation fields are blank; the staff recommendation points to a September 22 Public Works presentation that isn't in this packet or the vault.

**Item III.2, Park Facilities Impact Fee Ordinance.** Brehm's rewrite of City Code § 11-2. The text extraction lost the strike-through formatting, so read the amounts carefully in the PDF, but the substance appears to be:

*The fee moves to the annual Fiscal Fee Schedule,* set by Parks and Recreation and approved by Council, replacing the fixed $568 or $868 per unit (with or without a community pool) and $199 or $304 per ADU. This lets the fee keep pace with costs. The new language says the fee "should generally be calculated" from assessed costs of new development. Impact fees have to bear a reasonable relationship to the impact, so "should generally" is weak; ask that the ordinance require a documented cost study behind each year's number.

*New exemption for 100% affordable projects* at or below 80% AMI with a covenant, verified by HHS. Good, and it should use the same AMI definition the MPDU rewrite settles on, so the two programs don't use different measures.

*Spending tied to location.* Fees go first to parks within a quarter mile of at least 75% of the new units (not across an arterial), then to the next nearest park. This is legally sound, since it keeps the money tied to the development that paid it, but it has a distributional effect worth naming: new development is concentrated in annexation and greenfield areas, so the money will follow newer subdivisions, while finding (5) bars using fees to fix existing park deficiencies. Older neighborhoods, including much of District 2, will see little of it. That isn't a reason to oppose the rule, it's a reason to ask what General Fund or CIP plan covers the existing deficiencies the City says it "assumes responsibility" for.

*New annual report and a public spending process:* draft plan aligned with the Parks Master Plan, notice to NACs and main street organizations, a three-week comment period, at least one public meeting, Parks and Recreation Commission vote, then Council approval. Real transparency gain. It is also a long process, so ask how long a typical project would take from start to Council approval.

*Drafting issues to flag.* Collection is set "prior to the issuance of a certificate of occupancy," but G-26-11 and the Housing Certainty Act limit collection to final inspection and no more than 30 days before it; the text should track that rule. The annual report section cross-references "subsection (l)" where it means the location standards in (k). The old definitions list appears to remain alongside the new one, the use-of-proceeds clause reads "for only for," and the title block still says "August ___, 2026." Fiscal and equity impact fields are blank again, the same gap flagged on the Housing Solutions Fund and noise ordinance.

## Public Comment

Three minutes per speaker after each item, plus Granicus e-comments. No speakers are listed. On MPDU, expect FCBIA and FCAR, who testified in July against fee increases and for incentives, and possibly Beyond Shelter and Habitat, who were closer to your position. The 30% AMI and staffing points from October 13 commenters may return. The park fee is unlikely to draw much comment unless builders object to annual increases.

## Watch List

*Two MPDU tracks.* Your [[Inclusionary Zoning Reform]] item (lead Díaz, Pre-Legislative) and [[MPDU Modernization and Housing Fund Capitalization]] planned to coordinate with Brehm's committee rather than compete. Brehm and Shackelford now own a drafting process with staff. The cleanest path is to feed your fee recalculation, tiered threshold, and CLT option into their draft as amendments now, rather than carrying a second bill later. Shackelford co-leading is useful, since he's a swing vote and this gives him ownership.

*Fee and fund capitalization are linked.* The Housing Solutions Fund draft is financed mainly by MPDU proceeds. A threshold cut with a $2 fee leaves that fund undercapitalized, which is the core problem the July brief flagged (Beyond Shelter's $2.5 million five-year projection). Raise the two together.

*Vesting clock.* Every complete application filed while the fee sits at $2.00 may vest for five years under the Housing Certainty Act. Ask staff and Rachel Gray whether MPDU and park fee standards vest, because the answer sets how much the delay costs.

*Rental licensing revenue.* Staff is asking the City Attorney whether licensing fees can go to the Housing Solutions Fund while the rental licensing rewrite cuts the fee. That conflict is still unresolved.

*Records gap.* There is no vault brief or notes for the September 22 Public Works meeting, which both the MPDU executive summary and the October 13 minutes reference.
