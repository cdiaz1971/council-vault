# Public Works Committee Brief 2026-08-25

**Public Works Committee -- August 25, 2026 at 4:00 PM, City Hall Boardroom**

## Bottom Line

One item on tonight's agenda: a presentation and discussion of the citywide Sidewalk & ADA Ramp Condition Survey (CIP 340102/340104), delivered by Public Works Director Tracy Coleman and the consultant team from O'Connell & Lawrence. No vote is scheduled, and staff's own recommendation is "presentation for Council information." The substance matters anyway: this is the first complete, data-driven inventory of Frederick's sidewalk and ADA ramp network, and it sets up a multi-year repair program with planning-level cost estimates running from roughly $35.7 million to $79.7 million depending on scope. Nothing is funded beyond what's already budgeted.

## Action Items

None. This is a workshop-format discussion item; the Committee Report line on the executive summary is blank (no motion, second, or vote), consistent with an information-only presentation.

## Consent Agenda

None. Committee workshops don't carry a consent agenda.

## Presentations and Reports

**Sidewalk & ADA Ramp Condition Assessment (O'Connell & Lawrence, Inc., via RFP 25-Q).** The City surveyed 343 miles of sidewalk and inspected 6,196 ADA curb ramps, scoring 4,649 sidewalk segments on a 0-100 Sidewalk Condition Index (SCI). Citywide average SCI is 78 (Satisfactory). By mileage: 74% (255 miles) rate Good or Satisfactory, 12% (42 miles) Fair, and 14% (46 miles, 740 segments) Poor or Very Poor. Poor and Very Poor segments cluster in and around historic downtown, attributed to heavy pedestrian traffic and older brick sidewalks. The most common defect is grade differentials (raised joints/offsets) -- 223,643 found citywide, 96% low or medium severity and not required for repair under the current program.

On ADA ramps, 62% failed compliance review (3,841 of the ramps inspected). The dominant cause is a missing tactile warning pad -- 3,282 of 3,841 failures, or 85% -- which staff note is also the cheapest defect to fix. Failures concentrate most heavily in southwest neighborhoods and historic downtown.

Equity framing is built into the methodology: sidewalk condition is scored evenly regardless of neighborhood (79 SCI inside Equity Emphasis Areas versus 77 outside, essentially flat), and the recommended prioritization formula weights condition at 50 of 100 points with the remaining 50 split across proximity to schools (15 pts), Equity Emphasis Areas (15 pts), downtown/historic district (15 pts), and transit (5 pts). District-level SCI varies more than the EEA comparison does -- District 3 is lowest at 71, District 5 highest at 84.

Planning-level cost estimates (FY26 pricing, not bid prices): $5.0M-$5.8M for priority repairs on the 740 Poor/Very Poor segments; $11.2M-$53.4M for a citywide grade-differential program depending on precision-cutting versus full replacement; $19.5M-$20.5M for ADA ramp compliance, with the low end assuming detectable-warning paver retrofits on 123 historic all-brick ramps rather than full reconstruction. Current committed funding is far short of any of these: $2.0M in the Sidewalk Repair Program operating budget (offset by $500K in estimated revenue) plus $1.5M in CIP 340104 for FY27 construction.

Staff's recommended next steps are to adopt the ranked priority list as the basis for a multi-year program, fund a first phase pairing citywide precision cutting with replacement of the highest-priority segments and ramp retrofits, load the segment and ramp data into GIS for work-order tracking, and re-survey on a five-year cycle.

**Watch this as a lead-in to a future budget ask.** Tonight is informational, but "fund a first phase" is an explicit staff recommendation, and the gap between current funding ($3.5M) and even the low end of the combined estimates ($35.7M) is large enough that this will likely resurface as a CIP or budget item. Worth flagging into the Legislative Roadmap once a funding proposal actually appears on an agenda.

## Watch List

The 62% ADA ramp failure rate and the $19.5M-$20.5M compliance price tag are the numbers most likely to get picked up if this reaches press or advocacy groups. The historic-downtown carve-out -- cast detectable-warning pavers to preserve brick streetscape instead of full ramp reconstruction -- is a deliberate compromise that could draw pushback from either direction: preservation interests over any change to the brick, or accessibility advocates over settling for a lighter-touch fix. District 3's low average SCI (71, versus 84 in District 5) is worth knowing for constituent conversations if it comes up. And this is the same committee that was mid-discussion on the noise ordinance and MPDU reform at its August 11 meeting; neither is on tonight's agenda, but both remain open committee work to track separately.
