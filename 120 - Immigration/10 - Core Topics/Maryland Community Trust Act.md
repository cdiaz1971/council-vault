# Maryland Community Trust Act

## Decision frame and current baseline

The Community Trust Act (CTA), **SB791 / Chapter 872**, became law without the Governor's signature on **May 31, 2026**, effective immediately as an emergency measure. It regulates Maryland police and correctional participation in immigration enforcement. It is not a prohibition on independent federal operations. [Official legislative record](https://mgaleg.maryland.gov/mgawebsite/Legislation/Details/SB0791?ys=2026RS).

For Frederick, the immediate question is whether City procedures accurately implement the applicable state rules alongside the [[City Values Act]]. Police work, local jail custody, state prison custody, information disclosure, and federal activity require separate analysis. City Council does not direct the County sheriff.

**Review date: September 29, 2026.** The enactment and Attorney General guidance are verified. No later dispositive order or injunction was located in the reviewed public material, but this is not a complete current docket certification. Obtain the latest orders before relying on litigation status or changing operational policy.

## Read the final chapter, not the deleted amendments

The enacted PDF retains crossed-out proposals. Plain-text extraction can make those proposals look like law. Visual review of pages 5, 7, and 8 confirms that the proposed agency-policy mandates, employee-penalty provisions, and express actual/punitive-damages provisions were **deleted**. The prior version of this page and the legal-summary paragraph in [[Frederick County Immigration Enforcement -- Jenkins, Community Harm, and the Limits of Reform]] carried those provisions forward incorrectly.

What remains is **Correctional Services §8-805(e)**: the Attorney General or an affected individual may bring a civil enforcement action under that section, and courts may issue temporary, preliminary, or permanent injunctive relief. Do not describe Chapter 872 as creating the deleted general damages remedy or mandatory employee-penalty policy. Other potential remedies require separate legal analysis. The original October 1 effective-date language was also deleted in favor of immediate effectiveness. [Enacted Chapter 872, pp. 5, 7–8](https://mgaleg.maryland.gov/2026RS/chapters_noln/Ch_872_sb0791E.pdf).

Written procedures, training, and accountability remain sensible implementation measures; their specific legal basis must be identified rather than attributed to deleted CTA language.

## Which rules apply to which institution?

### Police performing regular police functions

Criminal Procedure §5-104 applies to certified law enforcement agents and expressly excludes correctional-facility employees from its definition. Its existing restrictions address status inquiries during stops/searches/arrests, detention for civil immigration purposes, transfers unless required by federal law, and status-based coercion. Routine booking, information material to a criminal investigation, and voluntary disclosure to obtain a legal protection have distinct treatment. The CTA adds a restriction on supplying federal immigration authorities with information obtained through an officer's duties unless required by a valid court order. [Chapter 872, pp. 6–7](https://mgaleg.maryland.gov/2026RS/chapters_noln/Ch_872_sb0791E.pdf).

For FPD, do not substitute the local-jail conviction exception for this police framework. A person having a past conviction does not by itself turn a removal operation into a criminal investigation.

### Local correctional facilities

Under §8-805(b), distinguish three actions—**inquiry, custody notification, and transfer**—from **detention or prolonged detention**. The statutory conviction exception applies to the first three, not to the detention restriction. Absent the applicable exceptions, notification requires a valid court order or judicial warrant and transfer requires a valid judicial warrant. Routine booking, NCIC entries, and voluntary access to specified legal protections are addressed separately.

The defined term is **“convicted individual”**, covering a person convicted of:

- A felony under Maryland law;
- An offense requiring sex-offender registration;
- An offense for which the sentence is to be served at a State correctional facility; or
- An offense in another state with a sentence of at least five years **and at least five years actually completed**.

This replaces the earlier shorthand “any U.S. felony” and “12–18 months served.” A charge alone is not a conviction; not every conviction satisfies this definition. [Chapter 872, pp. 2–4](https://mgaleg.maryland.gov/2026RS/chapters_noln/Ch_872_sb0791E.pdf).

### State correctional facilities and judicial release decisions

State prisons are not subject to the same set of restrictions as local jails. The CTA requires state facilities to notify federal immigration authorities within 48 hours before releasing someone subject to the Act's defined active detainer, which includes an accompanying **Form I-205 removal/deportation warrant**. The statutory Baltimore pretrial-facility exclusions must also be considered. Both state and local facilities are prohibited from status-based coercion, intimidation, or threats.

Separately, new Criminal Procedure §5-201.1 allows a commissioner or judge to consider a qualifying immigration detainer when deciding pretrial release or bond/bail for a defendant **charged with a felony**. It does not command detention in every such case. [Chapter 872, pp. 2, 4–5, 8](https://mgaleg.maryland.gov/2026RS/chapters_noln/Ch_872_sb0791E.pdf).

## Attorney General interpretation: important, but not a court ruling

The **June 22 guidance** explains several operational points:

- Citizenship/status information is treated differently from addresses, locations, and other identifying information; the guidance treats sharing status information as voluntary under state law.
- Advance release notice for a local-jail detainee who does not satisfy the conviction definition falls within the notification/transfer restrictions.
- An administrative NCIC hit is not a criminal warrant; officers should verify the warrant rather than prolong a stop to investigate a civil immigration matter.
- State prisons remain subject to constitutional detention limits even where local-jail statutory restrictions do not apply.

The guidance also identifies an obsolete Baltimore facility reference and explains OAG's interpretation. These are official legal interpretations, not amendments to the statute or adjudicated holdings. [OAG immigration-enforcement guidance, Parts B–E](https://oag.maryland.gov/FederalActionsResponse/Documents/pdfs/Law%20Enforcement%20Immigration%20Guidance%2006.22.2026%20Update%20%28FINAL%29.pdf).

The **June 15 advice letter** interprets §5-104 to permit information sharing for actual criminal investigations, including qualifying joint-task-force work. It acknowledges ambiguity and calls for precautions against treating civil removal as criminal enforcement. Task-force membership is not a blanket exemption; the purpose of the communication still matters, with deputization questions requiring separate analysis. [OAG advice to DPSCS](https://oag.maryland.gov/FederalActionsResponse/Documents/pdfs/Community%20Trust%20Act%20Letter%206.15.26%20%28FINAL%29%28signed%29.pdf).

## Practical distinctions for reviewing a request

| Distinction | Council/staff review question |
|---|---|
| Judicial versus administrative process | Who issued it, what does it authorize, and which statutory provision applies? The Act excludes DHS/DOJ-issued documents from its judicial-warrant/court-order definitions. |
| Permitted versus required action | Does an exception allow cooperation, or does a law/order actually require it? Do not describe permission as an obligation. |
| Civil removal versus criminal investigation | What is the documented purpose? A criminal record does not transform removal into criminal prosecution. |
| Hold versus notification versus transfer | Which action was requested and taken? Permission for one does not automatically authorize the others. |
| Statutory exception versus constitutional authority | What independently authorizes any seizure or continued custody? |
| Local versus state facility | Which legal definition applies, not simply which government owns or staffs the building? |

A judicial-warrant exception does not by itself create a universal duty to perform every requested action. Counsel should evaluate scope, validity, custody authority, and other applicable law.

### What would a CTA ruling mean for the CVA?

Neither “the CVA automatically falls” nor “the CVA is unaffected regardless” is justified. Review:

1. The provisions challenged and the actual relief ordered;
2. Who is bound, geographic scope, effective date, and any stay;
3. Whether the reasoning reaches City inquiries, records access, property control, or another distinct provision;
4. Severability and independently applicable constitutional/state limits; and
5. A provision-by-provision City Attorney assessment.

The CTA has a severability clause. Losing a restriction does not automatically authorize every previously prohibited action, and the City's separate enactment does not immunize it from federal preemption. [[Local Authority and Federal Preemption]] provides broader context; current orders control over predictions in older research.

## Implementation choices and tradeoffs

| Choice | Responsible participants and benefit | Cost or limitation |
|---|---|---|
| Reconcile written procedures with final law and OAG advice | Legal and FPD can resolve inconsistent instructions before the next request | Legal/staff time; ambiguous or contested questions need escalation |
| Use supervisory review and scenario training | FPD can test civil/criminal, warrant, NCIC, task-force, and transfer distinctions | Training and response-time demands; avoid collecting unnecessary personal data |
| Establish a request/response audit record | Legal, FPD, and records custodians can assess compliance and correct errors | Retention, security, redaction, and audit workload |
| Request County implementation information | City leadership can clarify the separate jail and sheriff baseline | Cooperation required; no City command authority over the Sheriff |
| Provide accurate public explanations and referrals | Communications and qualified providers can explain actual protections and complaint routes | Interpretation/accessibility costs; do not promise prevention of all federal arrests |
| Prepare for a material court order | Legal can identify affected procedures and rapidly brief staff/Council | Monitoring and contingency work; change procedures based on the order, not speculation |

These are implementation options, not newly introduced legislative projects. No additional planning-stage ordinance is established here. Existing proposals remain in [[Immigration - In-Progress Plans]].

The revised legislative fiscal note anticipated no local expenditure effect and identified potential federal-revenue risk if governments were deemed noncompliant with federal law. That is a fiscal-analysis assumption, not a Frederick implementation quote or notice of an actual grant loss. Budget staff should identify real training, legal-review, records, and grant-exposure amounts. [Enrolled–revised fiscal note](https://mgaleg.maryland.gov/2026RS/fnotes/bil_0001/sb0791.pdf).

## Measures and Council checklist

Use aggregate, privacy-protective reporting to track:

- Requests by agency, action, purpose, and document type; responses and legal review;
- Any custody extension, release notification, or transfer and its recorded legal basis, separated by institution;
- Use of an exception and documentation supporting it;
- Complaints, substantiated findings, corrective action, and recurrence;
- Training completion and whether scenario reviews reveal inconsistent practice;
- Actual staff/legal costs and confirmed funding actions.

CVA reports can capture relevant City interactions but are not a County-jail dataset or a census of federal arrests. Measures of trust, crime reporting, or service access need careful interpretation; this review establishes no causal evaluation of the CTA's local effect.

Before accepting a staff report or directing a change:

- [ ] Identify the actor, facility, requested action, and legal provision.
- [ ] Use final enacted language; exclude deleted proposals.
- [ ] Distinguish police from corrections and civil removal from criminal investigation.
- [ ] Record why an exception applies and whether action is permitted or required.
- [ ] Confirm current orders in both challenges and their precise effect.
- [ ] Reconcile CTA, CVA, privacy law, and constitutional requirements with Legal.
- [ ] Specify implementation owner, resources, complaint route, and review date.
- [ ] Ensure public statements describe the protection without guaranteeing a federal outcome.
