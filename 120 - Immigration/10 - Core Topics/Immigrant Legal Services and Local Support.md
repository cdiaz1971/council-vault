# Immigrant Legal Services and Local Support

## Purpose

Use this page to evaluate a grant, service contract, coordination arrangement, or advisory structure supporting immigrant residents. The central questions are **which unmet need the City would fund, who can competently deliver the service, how it connects to existing assistance, and whether residents actually receive help**.

Keep four functions distinct: immigration legal advice and representation; benefits and service navigation; household stabilization; and community participation and economic inclusion. One organization may perform several, but an outreach contact or legal consultation is not a fully represented case.

For the evidence of harm and barriers to access, see [[Effects of Immigration Enforcement]]. For City enforcement rules, see [[City Values Act]]. Unintroduced local ideas belong in [[Immigration - In-Progress Plans]].

## Definitions that affect program design

| Service or designation | Meaning | Funding consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Legal information / know-your-rights education | General explanation of rights and procedures | Do not count attendance as individualized legal advice or representation |
| Screening or consultation | Qualified assessment of a person's legal situation and possible next steps | Specify whether the service ends with advice or includes an accepted referral |
| Direct representation | An authorized practitioner undertakes an agreed case scope | State whether detention, immigration court, appeals, and related state-court work are included |
| DOJ-recognized organization | Nonprofit authorized to practice through accredited representatives | Recognition of the organization does not establish every employee's qualifications |
| Accredited representative | Authorized nonattorney working through a recognized organization | Partial accreditation covers USCIS; full accreditation also covers EOIR immigration courts and the BIA |
| Navigation / accompaniment | Help reaching a service, completing practical steps, or attending an appointment | Does not itself authorize legal advice or establish an attorney-client relationship |
| Grant award | Approved funding for a defined purpose | Does not prove disbursement, added capacity, completed cases, or favorable outcomes |

The recognition/accreditation distinctions come from [DOJ EOIR's program FAQ](https://www.justice.gov/eoir/recognition-and-accreditation-program-frequently-asked-questions). Attorneys have a separate authorization route; organizations practicing solely through attorneys do not need DOJ recognition. Verify the assigned practitioner's authority for the contracted work.

### Existing City funding: start here before designing another fund

The City's **FY2027 Community Promotion Grants** list **$10,000 for Centro Hispano** and **$10,000 for SSCM**. The program provides an existing mechanism for nonprofit services benefiting City residents. Its published grant totals do not identify which legal services, if any, these awards purchase. [City grant program and grantee list](https://www.cityoffrederickmd.gov/1023/Community-Promotion-Grants).

The separate **SECURE Initiative** lists awards of **$30,000 to Centro Hispano** and **$46,500 to the Asian American Center of Frederick**. Treat these as evidence of a wider support-funding system, not as a dedicated immigration-defense allocation. [City SECURE Initiative](https://cityoffrederickmd.gov/1874/SECURE-Initiative).

A **$20,000 Legal Advocacy Fund** appears in the Mayor's **proposed FY2026** executive summary. That document is a concrete research lead, not proof of the fund's current balance, final appropriation, recipient, or expenditures. Obtain the adopted budget line, any award agreement, and Finance's spending/carryforward record before describing it as available funding or claiming Frederick is starting from zero. [Proposed FY2026 executive summary, community grants table](https://www.cityoffrederickmd.gov/DocumentCenter/View/24205/The-City-of-Frederick-FY-2026-Mayors-Proposed-Budget---Executive-Summary).

**First decision test:** what new service capacity would the requested appropriation add beyond existing City, County, philanthropic, and provider resources? Ask for the current agreements and service scopes. Do not simply add awards from different fiscal periods into a supposed annual legal-services budget.

## Need, access, and continuity

Civil removal proceedings do not carry a general statutory entitlement to government-paid counsel: 8 U.S.C. §1362 provides for representation at no expense to the government. This is the access problem local funding may address; it does not settle every question about particular federally supported programs or special protections. [8 U.S.C. §1362](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?edition=prelim&req=granuleid%3AUSC-prelim-title8-section1362).

**Federal children's-program disruption is documented, but local capacity must be measured separately.** Acacia's July 31, 2026 statement reports the end of its federal contract covering a network of nearly 100 providers serving more than 20,000 unaccompanied children. Acacia is an affected contractor and advocate; its statement is direct evidence of its account of the contract loss, not an independent measure of every child's current representation. [Acacia, July 31 statement](https://acaciajustice.org/trump-administration-ends-unaccompanied-children-program-contract-with-acacias-legal-service-providers/).

[[Maryland Legal Aid Groups Build Referral Network to Fill Federal Immigration Defense Gap]] preserves September 2 reporting on Amica and Chacón Center referral and pro bono work. Its caseload and foster-care screening figures are dated reporting, not a current promise of available slots for Frederick children. The earlier claim that fewer than 10% of unrepresented children succeed is not retained: the original page did not establish the outcome definition, cohort, or comparison needed to use it responsibly.

Provider demand and access can move in opposite directions. March 2026 Frederick reporting describes decreased service access alongside increased demand at Centro. Use it to investigate barriers and capacity, not to calculate unmet legal need. [[Immigration Enforcement -- Frederick County WYPR]]; see [[Effects of Immigration Enforcement]] for the evidence limits and public-charge changes.

## Authority map

| Actor | Relevant role | Limit / review needed |
|---|---|---|
| City Council and administration | Budget decisions, authorized grants or contracts, City service access and implementation | Establish municipal public purpose, lawful funding source, procurement route, eligibility, and accountability for the specific program |
| County government / IAC | County services, grants, advisory input and coordination | County work is not interchangeable with City authority; commission recommendations are not appropriations |
| FCPS, health and social-service agencies | Education, care, and benefits functions within their own authority | Use agreed referrals and necessary information sharing, not City direction of their professional decisions |
| Maryland | Municipal enabling law, professional regulation, state programs and funding conditions | State and program-specific constraints still apply to locally funded assistance |
| Federal agencies / immigration courts | Status and benefit adjudication, removal proceedings, and recognition/accreditation | City money cannot confer immigration status or guarantee case outcomes |
| Qualified providers | Legal advice, representation, confidentiality and professional judgment | Funding agreements should preserve independent advice and not transfer client case files to Council |

Legal starting points: [Maryland Local Government §5-202](https://mgaleg.maryland.gov/2026RS/Statute_Web/glg/5-202.pdf), [City Community Promotion Grants and policy link](https://www.cityoffrederickmd.gov/1023/Community-Promotion-Grants), [EOIR provider rules](https://www.justice.gov/eoir/recognition-and-accreditation-program-frequently-asked-questions), and [8 U.S.C. §1362](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?edition=prelim&req=granuleid%3AUSC-prelim-title8-section1362). See [[Local Authority and Federal Preemption]] for the broader framework.

The City's existing nonprofit grants demonstrate a funding mechanism. They do not establish that every proposed service, recipient selection, or eligibility rule is lawful. Status-neutral wording alone does not answer applicable benefit restrictions, grant conditions, or public-charge questions. Obtain a program-specific City Attorney review.

## Peer funding examples: what transfers and what does not

### Portland: an enacted appropriation, not just a preliminary vote

**Ordinance 192151 passed March 4, 2026**, reallocating **$150,000** from Portland's Legal Priorities Reserve Fund for refugee and immigration legal services through SOAR Immigration Legal Services. The official impact statement identifies screenings and direct representation and describes no additional ongoing obligation beyond FY2025–26. [Enacted ordinance and impact statement](https://www.portland.gov/council/documents/ordinance/passed/192151).

This replaces the earlier page's February 27 “advanced” status. Portland demonstrates a defined appropriation and nonprofit delivery model. Its planned reach is not a measured outcome or a transferable cost per completed Frederick case. The earlier debate about speed, consultation, and selecting one organization remains useful procurement context in [[Portland 150K Immigrant Legal Services Appropriation -- Model and Internal Tensions]].

### Harris County: unresolved litigation, not settled Maryland authority

On **June 26, 2026**, the Texas Supreme Court ordered Harris County to stop disbursing money through its Immigrant Legal Services Fund and Immigration Resource Hotline until further order. The court expressly made **no merits determination**. [Court order, No. 26-0171](https://www.txcourts.gov/media/1462944/260171_stay-order.pdf).

The original page's assertions that the program continued uninterrupted and the court had definitively upheld its public purpose are therefore unsuitable as current guidance. The June order is the latest controlling document located in this review; recheck the docket before relying on its current status. Texas law and that interim order do not determine Frederick's Maryland authority.

[[Local Government Authority to Fund Nonprofit Immigrant Legal Services (Harris County)]] and [[Immigrant Legal Services -- Peer City Funding Models and Frederick Options]] remain historical research notes. Their confident legal conclusions and proposed $50,000–$75,000 Frederick scale have not been adopted here as legal advice or a supported cost estimate.

## Policy options and tradeoffs

These are alternatives for consideration. They are not introduced measures or commitments to a provider.

| Option / best use | Implementer and expected benefit | Recurring costs and tradeoff | Outcome / evidence test |
|---|---|---|---|
| Expand screening and referral for residents unsure of their options | Qualified legal provider with trusted community intake; earlier identification of case needs | Practitioner time, interpreters, scheduling and follow-up; screening alone can expose needs with no representation available | Time to consultation; percentage of referrals accepted and completed |
| Fund direct representation for a defined case group | Authorized specialist provider; continuity through agreed proceedings | Lawyers or fully accredited representatives, supervision, experts, interpretation, travel, fees and appeals; fewer people served per dollar than brief advice | Counsel retained through milestones; deadlines met; case outcomes by type without promised success rates |
| Fund navigation and household support | Community organizations plus housing, health, school and benefits partners; reduce practical access barriers | Navigators, accompaniment, childcare/transport support, assistance funding and coordination | Completed access and household stability; do not count navigation as legal representation |
| Establish shared referral coordination | City/County and providers agree on intake, consent, urgency, and handoff | Coordinator time, maintained resource directory and provider capacity; avoid a centralized sensitive case database | Fewer failed handoffs, shorter delays, documented coverage gaps |
| Strengthen resident advice to City government | Coordinate with County IAC or assess a City advisory channel | Staff support, interpretation, accessible meetings, outreach and follow-through; a volunteer commission is not costless | Recommendations receiving a documented response and resulting service changes |
| Improve trusted information and economic access | Community partners and relevant City service/economic-development staff | Translation, legal review, content updates, business or workforce navigation | Successful referrals and sustained participation; assess access beyond Spanish-speaking residents |

The first two options have an implemented spending example in Portland, but that is not an effectiveness evaluation. The remaining options draw on local provider functions and retained research; they need a Frederick baseline and implementation assessment.

The multilingual newsroom, cultural-organizing, diaspora-network, and business-succession ideas remain useful ways to reach residents and support economic participation. They are **design leads**, not demonstrated Frederick programs or justification for importing national business projections. See [[Immigrant News Coalition and the Disinformation Defense Function]], [[Art as Immigration Organizing Infrastructure During the 2026 World Cup]], and [[Immigrant Entrepreneurs as a Tool for Main Street Ownership Transitions]].

## Funding and contract decision framework

Before approving a grant or contract, resolve:

1. **Service and population.** City residents or a regional arrangement? Advice, applications, detained representation, children's cases, or an entire proceeding? Specify income rules, case exclusions, accessibility, and who screens eligibility.
2. **Additional capacity.** Identify existing awards, staffing, vacancies, waitlists, and unmet requests. Price additional capacity by case type; do not derive a unit cost by dividing Portland's appropriation by its planned reach.
3. **Delivery structure.** Choose a direct provider agreement, multiple awards, or a coordinating intermediary. Compare speed and administrative burden with fair access for providers, language coverage, conflicts, and geographic reach.
4. **Full cost and continuity.** Budget salaries, supervision, interpretation, administration, insurance, and necessary case expenses. State what happens when the fiscal year ends while cases remain open.
5. **Professional independence and data.** Verify practitioner authority. Require aggregate performance reporting and appropriate financial documentation; avoid collecting immigration files or unnecessary status details. Have counsel resolve audit, retention, disclosure, and confidentiality requirements.
6. **Referral accountability.** Identify who accepts a referral, confirms a handoff with consent, handles urgent deadlines, and assists clients a provider cannot take.
7. **Outcome review.** Set reporting dates, definitions, and a continuation decision. An adverse legal result does not alone mean poor service; a “win rate” alone can reward avoiding difficult cases.

These are recommended contract specifications, not claims about current City agreements.

## Minimum dashboard

| Measure | What to report | Safeguard |
|---|---|---|
| Access | Unique City residents screened, consulted, and accepted for representation, separately | Deduplicate within provider systems; do not merge identifiable client lists at City Hall |
| Unmet demand | Waiting time and turnaways by capacity, eligibility, conflict, and service mismatch | Disclose how many requests remain unresolved |
| Continuity | Active cases, completed scopes, transfers, missed deadlines, and unfunded continuing commitments | Separate administrative closure from substantive completion |
| Outcomes | Appropriate case milestones, completed service referrals, and housing/care continuity where relevant | Explain case mix and missing follow-up; do not promise immigration relief |
| Equity | Language and accessibility needs met, City/County coverage, barriers reported voluntarily | Minimize sensitive information and suppress small groups |
| Cost | Spending and cost by service type, staffing, overhead, and remaining obligations | Do not compare a consultation with full representation as one identical “case” |

No outcome target is set here without an agreed local baseline.

## Council checklist

- [ ] Current City and County awards and any Legal Advocacy Fund balance have been reconciled.
- [ ] The request buys a defined service and additional capacity.
- [ ] Provider credentials and court-representation scope are verified.
- [ ] Program-specific authority, eligibility, procurement, and funding conditions are reviewed.
- [ ] Resident access includes language, disability, transport, and digital barriers.
- [ ] Budget covers continuing cases and provider operating costs.
- [ ] Referral responsibilities, confidentiality, retention, and aggregate reporting are clear.
- [ ] Success measures distinguish contacts, consultations, representation, and outcomes.
