# Local Authority and Federal Preemption

## Purpose

This page answers one question before any immigration-related ordinance, resolution, contract, or public statement: **what can the City of Frederick lawfully do, and where does legal exposure start?** It separates three things that are often blurred together:

- what the City may **decline** to do (strong ground),
- what the City may **require of its own employees and property** (strong ground if drafted neutrally), and
- what the City may try to **regulate about federal agents or federal operations** (weak ground, frequently enjoined).

It also tracks the live litigation that could change the answer. For what the City has already enacted, see [[City Values Act]]. For the state law, see [[Maryland Community Trust Act]].

## Definitions

| Term | Meaning | Why it matters for Council |
|---|---|---|
| Anti-commandeering | Tenth Amendment rule that the federal government cannot compel state or local officers to administer a federal program (*Printz v. United States*, 1997; extended to prohibitions on state policymaking in *Murphy v. NCAA*, 2018) | The main legal shield for non-cooperation policies |
| Supremacy Clause / preemption | Federal law displaces conflicting state or local law. Comes in three forms: express, conflict, and field | The main federal sword against sanctuary policies |
| Intergovernmental immunity | State and local governments may not directly regulate the federal government or discriminate against it | Why rules aimed at federal agents' conduct tend to fail |
| Judicial warrant | Signed by a judge, based on probable cause | The City and State standard for access and detention |
| Administrative warrant / detainer | Issued by DHS officers (Forms I-200, I-205, I-247A), not a judge | Compliance is voluntary; honoring one without probable cause can itself violate the Fourth Amendment |
| 8 U.S.C. § 1373 | Bars state and local governments from restricting their employees from sharing citizenship or immigration-status information with DHS | The one federal statute that directly limits local policy, though several courts have held it unconstitutional after *Murphy* |
| Home rule | Maryland municipalities legislate under Article XI-E of the State Constitution and may not conflict with State public general law | Why a City ordinance must also be consistent with the CTA |

## Current baseline

### Where the law is settled

- **Localities may decline to assist.** Courts have consistently treated ICE detainer compliance as voluntary, and anti-commandeering bars Congress from forcing local officers to enforce federal immigration law ([[End Sanctuary Cities Act and the Anti-Commandeering Defense]]; [CRS, "Sanctuary" Jurisdictions, R44795](https://www.everycrsreport.com/reports/R44795.html)). A recent federal-level illustration: after an October 8, 2026 ICE shooting in Manhattan, DHS Secretary Markwayne Mullin claimed New York's governor had agreed to help ICE target "over 100" individuals; her office called this inaccurate, and legal observers noted a detainer is a voluntary request a jail may decline absent a judicial warrant; a locality's own policy, not the detainer itself, determines whether any are honored. The episode shows how a detainer's voluntary legal status is frequently misstated by federal officials after a high-profile incident, which is relevant to anticipating how Frederick's own non-cooperation record could be mischaracterized after a local event. [[ICE Detainers Are Requests Not Mandates -- Fact-Checking DHS Secretary Mullin's New York Claims]]; Documented NY, October 15, 2026.
- **Local officers in Maryland cannot arrest on a civil immigration basis alone.** In a Frederick County case, the Fourth Circuit held that a local officer's suspicion or knowledge of a civil immigration violation does not supply probable cause to arrest ([*Santos v. Frederick County Board of Commissioners*, 4th Cir. Aug. 2013](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCOURTS-ca4-12-01980/pdf/USCOURTS-ca4-12-01980-0.pdf); [LatinoJustice case summary](https://www.latinojustice.org/en/cases/roxana-orellana-santos-v-frederick-county-board-commissioners-frederick-county-sheriff)). This is binding circuit precedent for Frederick.
- **State non-cooperation laws have survived appellate review.** The Ninth Circuit upheld California's SB 54, which limits state and local cooperation with ICE, as consistent with anti-commandeering (April 2019). It struck one provision of a companion law, AB 103, because it singled out and burdened federal detention operations ([JURIST, Apr. 18, 2019](https://www.jurist.org/news/2019/04/federal-appeals-court-upholds-californias-sanctuary-state-law/)). The Supreme Court declined review.
- **Inquiry limits are defensible; information-sharing bans are the exposed edge.** Localities may restrict when employees ask about status. A flat ban on employees sharing status information runs into § 1373, although district courts in Chicago, Philadelphia, New York, and San Francisco held § 1373 unconstitutional after *Murphy* ([CRS R44795](https://www.everycrsreport.com/reports/R44795.html)). *verify:* no appellate or Fourth Circuit ruling on § 1373's validity is documented in the vault.

### Where the law is being contested now

- **United States v. Maryland**, No. 1:26-cv-02719-MJM (D. Md., Baltimore). DOJ filed July 9-10, 2026 against the State and AG Brown. Four counts: express preemption under §§ 1373 and 1644, conflict preemption with the federal removal scheme, unlawful regulation of the federal government, and discrimination against the federal government. It targets the CTA's inquiry, detention, notification, transfer, and information-sharing limits and its exclusion of federal administrative warrants ([DOJ complaint](https://www.justice.gov/opa/media/1451931/dl); [[DOJ Files Suit Against Maryland Over Community Trust Act Claiming Supremacy Clause Preemption]]). Maryland moved to dismiss on July 31 on Tenth Amendment and anti-commandeering grounds, and DOJ opposed on August 17 ([Fox Baltimore](https://foxbaltimore.com/news/local/doj-urges-court-to-reject-marylands-effort-to-dismiss-community-trust-act-lawsuit)). AG Brown's position is that the Constitution does not let the federal government compel states to carry out immigration enforcement ([Maryland OAG, July 13, 2026](https://oag.maryland.gov/News/pages/Statement-from-Attorney-General-Brown%C2%A0on%C2%A0the-U.S.-Department-of-Justice's-Lawsuit%C2%A0Concerning%C2%A0Maryland's-Community-Trust-Act.aspx)). **Status as of this review: motion to dismiss pending, no injunction.**
- **Gahler v. Moore**, No. 8:26-cv-02057 (D. Md., Greenbelt). Seventeen sheriffs, including **Frederick County's**, represented by FAIR, filed May 26, 2026 ([complaint](https://marylandmatters.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/Complaint-Gahler-v.-Moore-No.-8-26-cv-02057-FILED-5-26-2026-1.pdf); [[Frederick County Sheriff Joins Lawsuit Challenging Community Trust Act]]). *verify:* no public ruling or injunction found as of this review.
- **DOJ v. Maryland, et al.** (D. Md.), filed October 1, 2026, the day Maryland's SB 1 police mask ban took effect. DOJ sued the State, Prince George's County, and Montgomery County to block laws barring officers, including federal agents, from wearing masks on duty, on an intergovernmental-immunity theory: a state cannot directly regulate how federal officers perform federal duties. Maryland's own Attorney General had warned before signing that applying the ban to federal officers carried significant Supremacy Clause risk, a concern DOJ's complaint leans on directly. [Maryland Matters, Oct. 1, 2026](https://marylandmatters.org/2026/10/01/trump-administration-challenges-marylands-ban-on-police-using-masks/); [[DOJ Sues to Block Maryland Police Mask Ban]]. *verify:* no ruling documented as of this review. This is the pattern drafting principle 2 above describes: a rule reaching federal agents' conduct is the exposed version, while the same requirement applied only to City or County officers is not.
- **The national campaign.** DOJ filed 27 suits against more than 20 jurisdictions after January 2025. Results depend heavily on the judge: Philadelphia's agent mask and unmarked-vehicle rule was enjoined while six companion ordinances took effect, and DOJ's suits against four New Jersey cities were dismissed ([[DOJ Sanctuary Lawsuit Campaign National Pattern and Maryland]]).
- **Funding conditions.** Executive Orders 14159 and 14218 direct withholding of federal funds from sanctuary jurisdictions. A preliminary injunction in *City and County of San Francisco v. Trump* (N.D. Cal., April and August 2025) blocks withholding unrelated funds, but it protects **only the plaintiff jurisdictions**, and no Maryland jurisdiction is a plaintiff. The case is on appeal to the Ninth Circuit ([Civil Rights Litigation Clearinghouse](https://clearinghouse.net/case/46136/)). On DOJ's August 2025 sanctuary jurisdiction list, Baltimore County was the only Maryland entry ([Bethesda Magazine](https://bethesdamagazine.com/2025/08/06/moco-removed-from-list-sanctuary-jurisdictions/)). *verify:* whether any later list names Frederick, since the CVA postdates that list.

### Data limitations

There is no public Frederick record yet of how often ICE requests access to City facilities or information. The CVA's quarterly reports (§33-11) are the only local data source. The first, for Q2 2026, reported no incidents ([[City Values Act]]).

## Authority map

| Action | Level that controls it | City's position |
|---|---|---|
| Declining to honor ICE detainers or administrative warrants | Local, protected by anti-commandeering | **Direct authority.** Also required statewide by the CTA |
| Requiring a judicial warrant for non-public City space | City, as property owner and employer | **Direct authority.** Enacted in CVA §33-4 |
| Barring City property from use as enforcement staging | City | **Direct authority.** Enacted in CVA §33-5 |
| Limiting employee status inquiries | City | **Direct authority.** Enacted in CVA §33-6 |
| Barring employees from sharing status information with DHS | Federal (§ 1373) | **Exposed.** CVA §33-9 preserves federally required sharing, which avoids a direct conflict |
| Limiting City data collection and retention | City | **Direct authority.** Not collecting data is stronger than refusing to share it |
| Directing the Frederick County Sheriff or jail | County (independently elected Sheriff) | **None.** State law (the CTA) governs the Sheriff |
| Regulating what federal agents wear, drive, or do | Federal | **Weak.** Intergovernmental immunity; Philadelphia's mask rule and California's mask ban were enjoined |
| Zoning or permitting private detention facilities | City land use, subject to federal challenge | **Partial.** Howard County's permit revocation was overridden in court; zoning restrictions on private operators are the stronger tool ([[ICE Detention Infrastructure]]) |
| Blocking detention on federally owned land | Federal | **None** |
| Creating a civil remedy against federal officers | State | **State only.** Maryland's No Kings Act, effective October 1, 2026 ([Maryland Matters, May 13, 2026](https://marylandmatters.org/2026/05/13/no-kings-act-masons-law-among-more-than-200-bills-signed-into-law/)) |
| Funding legal services or a municipal ID | City spending and service powers | **Direct authority**, if status-neutral in design ([[Immigrant Legal Services and Local Support]]; [[Municipal ID -- Legal Risks and Federal Preemption]]) |

## Drafting principles that follow from the case law

1. **Govern the City, not the federal government.** Rules about City employees, City property, and City data sit on anti-commandeering ground. Rules about federal agents' conduct invite intergovernmental immunity claims.
2. **Don't single out federal officers.** California's mask ban failed because it exempted state officers. AB 103 failed because it targeted federal detention alone. A neutral rule that applies to all law enforcement is more defensible.
3. **Condition access; don't ban categories.** The CVA's warrant-for-access structure is more defensible than Howard County's ban on private detention contractors ([[Warrant-Based ICE Restrictions Create Legal Exposure Not Prevention]]; [[Howard County Immigration Facility Case Shows Limits of Local Permitting Power Against ICE Infrastructure]]).
4. **Preserve federally required information sharing, and minimize what exists to share.** Pair a § 1373 compliance clause with limits on collection and retention.
5. **Keep eligibility status-neutral.** Programs framed around residency and service access avoid field preemption ([[Municipal ID -- Legal Risks and Federal Preemption]]).
6. **Include severability.** Philadelphia kept six of seven ordinances after one was enjoined.
7. **Describe the effect accurately.** Warrant-based rules create legal exposure and a remedy after the fact. They don't prevent encounters in public or adjacent spaces.

## What a CTA loss would mean for Frederick

This is the unresolved question. *confidence: low, sources disagree.* One view is that the CVA rests on the City's own authority over its employees and property and would stand regardless ([[DOJ Files Suit Against Maryland Over Community Trust Act Claiming Supremacy Clause Preemption]]). The other is that a ruling adopting DOJ's reasoning would reach any local law built on the same warrant structure ([[County-Level Trust Acts Proliferate as Community Trust Act Faces DOJ Preemption Challenge]]). Two points narrow the risk: the CVA has no provision on jail detention or transfers, which are the core of the DOJ complaint, and its §33-9 clause preserves federally required sharing. The closest overlaps are the CVA's inquiry limit (§33-6), which parallels a CTA provision DOJ challenges, and its limit on assisting access to City records (§33-4(b)), which §33-9 keeps clear of a direct § 1373 conflict. A written City Attorney opinion would settle this for Council. None is documented in the vault.

## Decision framework

Before voting on any immigration-related measure, Council should be able to answer:

- Does it govern City employees, property, or data, or does it try to govern federal agents?
- Does it treat federal officers differently from other law enforcement?
- Does it restrict sharing of status information, or only collection and inquiry?
- Is it consistent with the CTA, and does it still stand if the CTA is enjoined?
- Has the City Attorney reviewed it for preemption and intergovernmental immunity?
- Does it have a severability clause?
- What federal funding could be targeted, and is Frederick covered by any injunction? (Currently it is not.)
- Does public messaging promise prevention where the law only provides remedy?

## Metrics to watch

- Docket status in *United States v. Maryland*, *Gahler v. Moore*, and *DOJ v. Maryland* (mask ban): ruling on the motion to dismiss, any injunction.
- CVA quarterly reports: count of access requests, warrant presentations, and incidents.
- Any federal funding action naming Frederick, or its appearance on a DOJ list.
- First uses of the No Kings Act after October 1, 2026.

## Council checklist

- [ ] Measure regulates City conduct, not federal conduct
- [ ] Applies neutrally to all law enforcement
- [ ] § 1373 compliance clause included where information is involved
- [ ] Data minimization instead of a sharing ban
- [ ] Severability clause
- [ ] City Attorney preemption review on record
- [ ] Funding exposure identified
- [ ] Public description matches what the law actually does

## Evidence notes

Controlling sources are the enacted CVA, the CTA text, *Santos* (binding in the Fourth Circuit), and the court dockets. The Ninth Circuit's *United States v. California* is persuasive but not binding in Maryland. District court rulings on § 1373 are persuasive only. News coverage of the pending Maryland cases should be rechecked against the docket before any decision, since a ruling on the motion to dismiss could come at any time.
