# ICE Detention Infrastructure

## Decision frame

Detention infrastructure is a land-use, service-capacity, fiscal, and rights issue as well as an immigration issue. Frederick's available tools depend on **the parcel's jurisdiction, owner, operator, actual use, applicable law, and current court orders**. “ICE facility” is not a sufficient legal classification.

The first local task is to establish those facts and apply existing law. An opposition resolution, a permit action, an environmental challenge, and a federal lease dispute have different legal effects. No reviewed record establishes a City of Frederick detention-center proposal or an adopted new City detention-siting measure. Review date: **September 29, 2026**; the litigation developments below are dated, not certifications of present operating status.

## Maryland and Frederick baseline

| Site or issue | Documented baseline | What remains to verify |
|---|---|---|
| Washington County warehouse near Williamsport, often called Hagerstown | Maryland challenged federal purchase/conversion; the AG's April 15 announcement describes a preliminary injunction stopping conversion and operation while allowing limited security and building-integrity work. | Latest order, any stay or environmental review, present construction/operating status, and final proposed use |
| Elkridge, Howard County | August 10 federal opinion grants preliminary relief restoring permits for a privately owned building intended for direct federal operation, largely offices with temporary holding space. | Appeal/stay outcome and subsequent orders; actual opening or use |
| Frederick County ICE office lease | [[IAC Brief 2026-05-27]] records an April commission letter and its then-unanswered status. | Parcel, lease parties and term, current County response, actual use, and governing jurisdiction |
| City of Frederick | No parcel-specific detention proposal was established in this review. | Planning, property, permit, procurement, and utility records—not assumptions from regional news |

An office can include holding rooms, and a “processing” label does not settle its legal use. Conversely, an ICE office is not automatically a long-term detention center. Do not merge the County lease controversy, Washington County warehouse, and Howard County project into one Frederick project.

## What the legal examples actually establish

### Washington County: a federal environmental-law route

The Maryland AG's **April 15, 2026** account says the preliminary injunction prevents conversion and operation of the warehouse while the case proceeds, with limited security and repair work to preserve the building. This is narrower than a permanent rejection and more restrictive than a general permission for “interior work.” The lawsuit invokes federal administrative and environmental obligations. [AG statement](https://oag.maryland.gov/News/pages/Statement-from-Attorney-General-Brown-on-the-Court%E2%80%99s-Order-to-Halt-Construction-and-Operation-of-Unlawful-ICE-Detention-Fac.aspx).

For Frederick, the transferable lesson is to document environmental, infrastructure, and procedural facts early and coordinate with the state. Environmental review is a process with specific legal requirements; it does not give a municipality an automatic veto. Obtain the actual order and subsequent docket before relying on the injunction's current scope.

### Howard County: ownership is not the same as operation

In **Genesis GSA Strategic One, LLC v. Howard County**, No. 26-cv-915, the August 10 opinion describes 6522 Meadowridge Road as a privately owned property being renovated for federal operation. Most space is offices; roughly 1,100 square feet is temporary holding cells and a shower. The court's preliminary-injunction analysis turns on the Supremacy Clause and application of state restrictions to that federal project—not merely the developer's losses. [Court opinion, ECF 69](https://docs.justia.com/cases/federal/district-courts/maryland/mddce/1:2026cv00915/601125/69).

The available docket records an August 24 appeal and stay request and an August 27 appellate case number, **26-2172**. It does not establish their eventual disposition. [Docket listing](https://dockets.justia.com/docket/maryland/mddce/1%3A2026cv00915/601125).

**Decision implication:** private ownership does not by itself make every local restriction enforceable against a federal use. Preliminary relief is not a final resolution of every claim, nor does this ruling invalidate all neutral building or safety requirements everywhere.

### Maryland HB1017: enacted law with a contingent alternative

HB1017 became **Chapter 173 on April 28, 2026**, effective upon enactment. Section 1 prohibits state/local approval of construction or operation for use by a private entity as a detention facility and prohibits private operation/occupancy, subject to statutory definitions. It authorizes AG enforcement and civil penalties up to $10,000 per day. [Legislative status](https://mgaleg.maryland.gov/mgawebsite/Legislation/Details/HB1017?ys=2026RS).

The chapter also contains a **contingent Section 2** requiring express zoning authorization, use/occupancy approval, and public process. It is not safe to present both versions as simultaneously operative. Section 3 ties the alternative's activation to judicial unenforceability and notice to Legislative Services. This review did not verify a triggering notice. Counsel should establish the operative version and any controlling order before applying it. [Enacted chapter, especially §§1–3](https://mgaleg.maryland.gov/2026RS/Chapters_noln/CH_173_hb1017e.pdf).

## Classify the facility before choosing a tool

| Question | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Inside City limits, unincorporated County, or another county? | Determines whose zoning, permitting, and elected oversight are relevant |
| Federal owner, private landlord, or public landlord? | Affects immunity analysis, lease authority, and tax treatment |
| Federal operator or private detention operator? | Can be legally decisive even where title is private |
| Offices, temporary holding, processing, transport, or overnight confinement? | Determines the actual service and building demands; marketing labels are insufficient |
| Proposed, permitted, under construction, occupied, or abandoned? | Changes available remedies, reliance interests, costs, and urgency |
| Which court orders and statutory version apply? | A general policy summary cannot override an injunction or jurisdiction-specific ruling |

Bed capacity, contracted capacity, funded capacity, and actual population are different measures. The original page's national expansion figures combine dates and measures. Keep those in the snapshot as historical context; do not use them as a current census or estimate of local impact. Verify any proposed site's staffing, occupancy, and contract documents directly.

## Local options, benefits, and tradeoffs

| Option | Appropriate use | Cost or constraint |
|---|---|---|
| Parcel and facility inventory | Identify ownership, applications, permitted use, operator, and proposed changes | Planning/legal staff time; distinguish confirmed records from leads |
| Apply enacted law and neutral safety requirements | Review an actual application on a documented factual basis | Federal immunity, preemption, and court-order questions require counsel; avoid selective or improvised enforcement |
| Review local code for a demonstrated gap | Assess clarity of detention/holding definitions and procedures | State law may already control; drafting, hearings, and litigation costs; introduction is not established here |
| Environmental and infrastructure review | Quantify water, sewer, traffic, stormwater, emergency-response, and site impacts | Requires technical evidence and the correct legal process; does not promise denial |
| Review City property or contracting involvement | Apply actual ownership, lease, procurement, and CVA obligations | Federal property and existing contractual rights need separate analysis |
| State/federal coordination and records requests | Seek plans, contracts, oversight findings, and appropriate litigation support | External response times and litigation outcomes are outside Council's control |
| Public resolution and community engagement | Express policy, obtain resident evidence, and organize lawful participation | Political expression alone does not suspend construction or a federal contract |

Do not treat refusal of utility service, denial of occupancy, or a targeted zoning change as automatically lawful. Establish statutory authority, service obligations, nondiscrimination, technical grounds, and any federal conflict first. [[Local Authority and Federal Preemption]] supplies the broader framework.

The [[City Values Act]] governs City property and employee conduct within its terms. It is not a citywide ban on federal real-estate transactions. A possible detention-siting code review from the research collection is tracked in [[Immigration - In-Progress Plans]] as a research option, not an introduced measure.

## Fiscal and service analysis

Build a parcel-specific estimate rather than transferring another community's headline:

- **Tax base:** current assessed value and actual City/County revenues; ownership and exemption rules; acquisition date; effect on each taxing body. Federal acquisition and a private lease can have different consequences.
- **Services:** projected and actual water/sewer demand, fire/EMS calls, traffic, road work, and staff time; separate one-time capital costs from recurring costs.
- **Payments:** contractual payments, utility charges, grants, and reimbursement terms; distinguish promised, billed, collectible, and received amounts.
- **Displacement and opportunity cost:** prior use, alternative development, lost employment or revenue, and impacts on neighboring properties.
- **Risk:** litigation expense, staff diversion, contract exposure, and the uncertainty range around occupancy assumptions.

The Pennsylvania fiscal example in [[ICE Detention Infrastructure Fiscal Fallout When Federal Property Removes Local Tax Base]] concerns particular taxing districts, not a Frederick forecast. Do not assume the federal Payments in Lieu of Taxes program replaces revenue on every federal property. Check eligibility and the relevant agreement before including any offset.

## Conditions, access, and accountability

A siting decision is not a complete detention-conditions review. If a facility is proposed or operating, obtain the applicable contract and standards, inspection findings and corrective actions, medical and disability-access provisions, grievance procedures, attorney visitation and confidential communication arrangements, language access, and emergency plans.

The retained Rodney Taylor, Delaney Hall, and Adelanto notes raise disability, medical-care, and detained-person advocacy concerns. Attribute claims to the specific letter, report, or organization and date; neither an allegation nor an operator's compliance statement substitutes for inspection evidence. They do not establish conditions at a Maryland site.

For private-operator analysis, distinguish contract ceiling, guaranteed payments, actual expenditures, occupancy, and profit. [[Private Prison Corporations and the Deportation Industrial Complex]] is a policy/advocacy analysis, not a parcel-specific contract audit. Legal services and family access are covered in [[Immigrant Legal Services and Local Support]].

## Measures and pre-decision checklist

Track verified sites by jurisdiction and stage; completeness of application and operator records; unresolved safety findings; service demand versus estimate; net recurring fiscal effect; and compliance with disclosure and public-process requirements. Keep proposed capacity separate from actual population.

Before Council acts, obtain:

1. Parcel and boundary confirmation, ownership, operator, lease/contract, plans, and actual proposed use.
2. Counsel's written analysis of the operative state statute, current orders, federal immunity, and the specific local action.
3. Fiscal and infrastructure estimates with assumptions and responsible departments.
4. A record of public notice and input where required, and a response to documented concerns.
5. Clear authority, implementation responsibility, cost, and a review trigger for the proposed action.

Recheck upon any court order, ownership/operator change, revised application, or proposed Council action. Unknown operating status is not evidence that a project has opened, closed, or been abandoned.
