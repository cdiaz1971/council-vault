# City Values Act

## Purpose

This page is the reference for Frederick's own immigration enforcement law. It answers four questions Council gets asked most often:

- What does the ordinance actually require, section by section?
- Has it been implemented, and what does the reporting record show?
- What does it not cover?
- If Council wants to strengthen it, which options sit on solid legal ground?

For the legal limits on any amendment, read [[Local Authority and Federal Preemption]] alongside this page. For the state law that now overlaps it, see [[Maryland Community Trust Act]].

## Definitions (§33-3)

| Term | Enacted meaning | Decision consequence |
|---|---|---|
| City facility | A building the City operates that provides services to the public | Where the §33-4 warrant rule applies |
| City property | Real property the City owns, leases, or manages, including parking lots, garages, and vacant lots | Broader than facilities. §33-5 reaches City parking lots |
| Immigration enforcement operation | An operation whose main objective is civil immigration detention or removal, or prosecution for immigration-status offenses | The purpose test. Ordinary criminal policing is outside it |
| Law enforcement officer | Any federal or state employee authorized to arrest and carry a firearm | Covers ICE, CBP, and other federal agents |
| Judicial warrant | Signed by a judge or court | The only document that unlocks non-public access. An ICE administrative warrant does not |

## What the ordinance does

| Section | Rule | Scope limit |
|---|---|---|
| §33-4(a) Facility access | No employee consent to entry of non-public areas for an enforcement operation without a judicial warrant, court order, or exigent circumstances | Public areas are unaffected |
| §33-4(b) Records and systems | No employee assistance accessing records, equipment, or databases for an enforcement operation without a judicial warrant. Warrants go to the designated supervisor, and access is limited to the warrant's terms | Subject to §33-9 |
| §33-4(c) No interference | Employees may not impede an officer once access is denied | Protects employees from obstruction exposure |
| §33-5 City property | No use of City property for surveillance, staging, processing, detention, or vehicle storage without a judicial warrant or where required by law | Excludes immediate public-safety threats and areas open to the public |
| §33-6 Inquiries | No inquiry into status, citizenship, country of origin, birthplace, or nationality unless required by law, including for benefit eligibility | Applies to every purpose |
| §33-7 Guidance | Legal Department issues staff guidance. Each director designates supervisors, identifies non-public areas, and sets department protocols | Implementation duty, not yet documented |
| §33-8 Regulations | Mayor may issue implementing regulations | |
| §33-9 Federal law | Nothing prevents sending or receiving status information consistent with federal or constitutional law | The § 1373 safety valve |
| §33-10 Penalties | No citation, fine, or criminal penalty. Supervisor-directed discipline only | No private right of action |
| §33-11 Reporting | Director incident reports within 30 days of quarter close; consolidated, redacted, publicly posted Council report within 45 days; annual report by March 31 | The only local data source |
| §33-12 Signage | Written inventory of public and non-public areas filed with the City Attorney; prescribed signs at entrances and transitions | Excludes federally owned property leased to the City |
| §33-13 Cost recovery | Mayor may document and bill DHS, ICE, and CBP for City costs of federal enforcement activity | Authorizes billing. Collection depends on federal willingness to pay |

**FPD is covered.** Every operative rule applies to "a City employee," including police officers. The limit is purpose: FPD's ordinary criminal work is outside the ordinance because it is not immigration enforcement, not because FPD is exempt ([[City Values Act -- Enacted Ordinance G-26-1]]).

## Applied record

### Taskers Chance, September 3-5, 2026

A roughly 40-hour ICE operation in the Taskers Chance neighborhood ended with no arrests and a confrontation with community observers on the night of September 5. Full account: [[ICE Enforcement in Maryland and Frederick]].

- **FPD's role matched the ordinance.** The City Manager said FPD received no advance notice, made no arrests, and "was not there to assist ICE in carrying out its federal enforcement operation" ([News From the States, Sept. 8, 2026](https://www.newsfromthestates.com/article/officials-demand-answers-after-violent-ice-confrontation-frederick-protesters)).
- **The joint Mayor and Council President statement** quoted §33-6 and confirmed that City employees and officers would not ask about status unless legally required ([[ICE Raid Press Coverage Brief -- 2026-09-09]]).
- **What the operation exposed is outside the ordinance.** The CVA governs what City staff do *for* federal agents. It says nothing about what an officer may do when a federal agent uses apparently unlawful force in front of them. That is the subject of the September 21 joint letter asking the Attorney General for an investigation and written guidance on the State duty-to-intervene statute ([[AG Investigation and Guidance Request Letter]]).
- **Federal accountability requests.** Maryland's congressional delegation asked DHS and ICE for body camera footage and use-of-force policies, with a response requested by September 30, 2026 ([CBS Baltimore, Sept. 17, 2026](https://www.cbsnews.com/baltimore/news/maryland-congressional-delegation-demands-answers-ice-clash-in-frederick/)).

### Earlier context

The ICE action near Hillcrest Elementary in early March 2026, about 200 yards from the school during drop-off, entered the record at the March 5 meeting and was the direct context for introduction ([[Hearing Brief 2026-03-19]]).

## What the ordinance does not reach

| Gap | Why | Where it's handled |
|---|---|---|
| Encounters in public space, sidewalks, private homes | The CVA conditions City consent and City property. It can't bar federal agents from public or private places | Know-your-rights material; [[Warrant-Based ICE Restrictions Create Legal Exposure Not Prevention]] |
| Officer response to federal use of force | Outside the ordinance's subject | Pending AG guidance ([[AG Investigation and Guidance Request Letter]]) |
| The County Sheriff and detention center | The Sheriff is an independently elected County officer | State law ([[Maryland Community Trust Act]]) |
| Commercial and federal data | The CVA covers City-held records only | [[ICE Surveillance and Data]] |
| Surveillance technology procured by departments | Not addressed | [[Police Surveillance Technology]] |
| Private detention facility siting | No land-use provision | [[ICE Detention Infrastructure]] |

**Messaging accuracy.** The ordinance creates legal exposure and a remedy after the fact. It doesn't make an encounter impossible. Some secondary coverage overstates it. One aggregator described a ban on sharing information with federal authorities and a language-access mandate, and neither is in the enacted text ([Frederick Today](https://nationaltoday.com/us/md/frederick/news/2026/04/03/frederick-passes-city-values-act-limiting-immigration-enforcement/)).

## Legal footing

The ordinance regulates City employees, City property, and City records, which is the strongest ground under the anti-commandeering doctrine. Specifics are in [[Local Authority and Federal Preemption]]. Two provisions carry the most exposure:

- **§33-6 (inquiries)** parallels the CTA provision DOJ is challenging in *United States v. Maryland*. Inquiry limits have survived review elsewhere, notably California's SB 54.
- **§33-4(b) (records access)** limits assistance with City records. §33-9 preserves federally required sharing, which keeps it clear of a direct conflict with 8 U.S.C. § 1373.

What a CTA loss would mean for the CVA is unresolved (*confidence: low*). A written City Attorney opinion would settle it for Council. None is documented.

## Options if Council revisits the ordinance

These are options drawn from the drafting record and peer jurisdictions, not introduced proposals. Planning-stage items go in [[Immigration - In-Progress Plans]].

| Option | Source | Legal footing | Tradeoff |
|---|---|---|---|
| Codify the civil-detainer rule for FPD | February 16 draft ([[City Values Act -- Legislative Development Note 2026-02-16]]) | Strong. Already State law under the CTA and *Santos* | Duplicates State law. Useful as a backstop if the CTA is enjoined |
| Data minimization: limit what City systems collect and retain | February 16 draft "second layer" | Strong | Requires a department-by-department records review |
| Surveillance technology procurement rules and FPD tracking | Council Member Benford's proposals, not taken up March 19 ([[Hearing Brief 2026-03-19]]) | Strong if limited to City procurement | Staff capacity. The April 1 brief notes FPD reporting needs a budget hook ([[Workshop Brief 2026-04-01]]) |
| Zoning limits on private detention facilities | San Antonio model ([[ICE Detention Infrastructure]]) | Partial. Howard County lost a permit revocation | No federal land reach |
| Translated plain-language public summary | February 16 draft | Strong | Low cost |
| Charter-level protection | February 16 draft "second layer" | Strong | Requires a charter amendment process |
| Guidance on officer response to federal force | [[AG Investigation and Guidance Request Letter]] | Depends on the AG's answer | Belongs in FPD policy, possibly not in the ordinance |

## Decision framework

Before amending the ordinance or relying on it publicly, Council should be able to answer:

- Has the Legal Department issued the §33-7(a) guidance, and do departments have §33-7(b) protocols?
- Are §33-12 inventories filed and signs posted?
- Was the Q2 report posted as §33-11(b) requires, and will Q3 capture Taskers Chance?
- Does the change govern City conduct only, and does it treat federal agents the same as other officers?
- Would it still stand if the CTA were enjoined?
- Is there a cost, and where does the budget hook sit?

## Metrics

- Quarterly: number of incidents by provision (§33-4, §33-5, §33-6), warrants presented, access granted.
- Whether each quarterly report was filed and posted on time.
- Status of the §33-7 guidance and §33-12 signage (done or not done).
- Any cost-recovery invoices under §33-13 and the federal response.
- Complaints or discipline under §33-10.

Ordinance G-26-1, adds Chapter 33, Article II (Immigration Enforcement Guidance) to the City Code. Introduced March 19, 2026 by Council President Díaz. Passed 7-0 April 2, 2026. Signed by Mayor O'Connor April 6, 2026, certified for legal sufficiency by City Attorney Rachel Gray. Sets a judicial-warrant standard for ICE access to non-public City facility areas and City property, limits City employee inquiries into immigration status, and (§33-13) authorizes the Mayor to seek cost recovery from DHS/ICE/CBP for enforcement-related City costs. Cost recovery is enacted law under G-26-1 itself; the March 20 draft that first proposed it separately is archived as a superseded source, not tracked as separate legislation. Applies to all City employees, including FPD, for immigration-enforcement-related activity; does not carve out FPD. Q2 2026, the first required quarterly report under §33-11(b), reported no ICE activity (per Díaz; not yet corroborated by a filed copy of the report).
