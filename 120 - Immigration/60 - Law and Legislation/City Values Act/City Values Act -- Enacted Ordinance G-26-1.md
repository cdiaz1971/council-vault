# City Values Act -- Enacted Ordinance G-26-1

**Title:** An Ordinance to Address Immigration Enforcement Activities in the City of Frederick
**Introduced by:** Council President César Díaz
**Date: Introduction:** March 19, 2026
**Date: Approved:** April 2, 2026
**Signed by Mayor Michael C. O'Connor:** April 6, 2026
**Approved for legal sufficiency by:** City Attorney Rachel S. Gray

Adds Chapter 33, Article II, "Immigration Enforcement Guidance," to The Code of the City of Frederick, 1966 (as amended).

## Stated purpose (preamble)

For the purpose of upholding the City's values of equity, inclusiveness, dignity, and justice for all; clarifying how City departments should respond to ICE actions; providing model policy language and guiding principles for consistent departmental practices; maintaining community trust by separating City services from federal immigration enforcement; and limiting the use of City property for federal immigration enforcement activities to strengthen public trust, ensure equitable access to public facilities, and preserve City resources for local governmental purposes.

## §33-3. Definitions

- **City facility** -- a building operated by the City that provides services to the public.
- **City property** -- any real property owned, leased, or otherwise managed by the City, including parking lots, garages, and vacant lots.
- **Immigration enforcement operation** -- an operation whose main objective is to identify or apprehend a person to subject them to civil immigration detention, removal or deportation proceedings, or to criminally prosecute them for immigration-status offenses under specified federal statutes.
- **Law enforcement officer** -- any federal or state government employee authorized to arrest for criminal or civil violations of federal or state law and authorized to carry a firearm in the performance of official duties.

## §33-4. Access to Facilities and Information for Immigration Enforcement

(a) **Entrance to facilities.** A City employee may not consent to a law enforcement officer entering a non-public portion of a City facility for an immigration enforcement operation, unless there are exigent circumstances or the officer presents a signed judicial warrant or court order.
(b) **Access to information.** A City employee may not assist a law enforcement officer in accessing records, information, equipment, or databases for an immigration enforcement operation without a signed judicial warrant. A warrant received is forwarded to the employee's designated supervisor; any approved access is limited in scope and duration to the warrant's parameters and targets.
(c) **No interference.** Nothing in this section authorizes a City employee or contractor to interfere with or impede a law enforcement officer once access has been denied or consent withheld.

## §33-5. Use of City Property

(a) **Prohibition.** City property will not be used for an immigration enforcement operation, including surveillance, staging, processing, detention, or vehicle storage, unless required by law or authorized by a valid judicial warrant.
(b) **Exceptions.** Does not apply to (1) any temporary access necessary to address an immediate threat to public safety, or (2) any portion of a City facility open to the general public.
(c) **Reporting.** A City employee who becomes aware of unauthorized use of City property should immediately report it to their designated supervisor, including time, location, and a description of what was observed.

## §33-6. Inquiries

Applies to any inquiry regarding an individual's immigration status, citizenship, country of origin, place of birth, or nationality, for any purpose, including a determination of eligibility for a federal benefit or program administered by the City. A City employee will make such an inquiry only if required by law to do so.

## §33-7. Immigration Enforcement Guidance

(a) **Guidance.** The Legal Department provides City employees with guidance on responding to inquiries or actions by federal immigration authorities, including requests for information about employees or the public and requests to access City facilities or property.
(b) **Other actions.** The Mayor ensures each City director (1) designates one or more supervisors to assist employees under this article, (2) where applicable, identifies non-public areas of City facilities and posts signage per §33-12, and (3) establishes any additional department-specific measures needed to manage interactions with law enforcement officers conducting immigration-enforcement operations.

## §33-8. Regulations

The Mayor may establish any regulations and standards necessary to carry out the purposes and intent of this ordinance.

## §33-9. Compliance with Federal Law

Nothing in this ordinance may be interpreted to prevent a law enforcement agent from sending or receiving information to or from any local, state, or federal agency regarding an individual's citizenship or immigration status, consistent with applicable federal or constitutional law.

## §33-10. Penalties

The City Council does not intend for a City employee who acts in contravention of this section to be cited, fined, or otherwise civilly or criminally penalized. An employee who fails to comply with the direction given by their supervisor may be subject to disciplinary action in accordance with applicable employee policies and procedures.

## §33-11. Reporting

(a) **Incident reporting by department directors.** Each City director submits a written report to the Legal Department within 30 days after each calendar quarter's close, documenting any incident in which a City employee or contractor was required to respond to an action, request, or inquiry by a law enforcement officer implicating this article. Each report includes: (1) date and general location; (2) which provision was implicated (§33-4 facility/record access, §33-5 property use, or §33-6 inquiry); (3) how the employee or supervisor responded; and (4) whether a judicial warrant was presented and, if so, whether access was granted.
(b) **Quarterly report to City Council.** The Legal Department, coordinating with the Mayor or designee, compiles the incident reports into a consolidated written report to the City Council no later than 45 days after each quarter's close. The report is transmitted to the Council President, distributed to all Council members, and posted publicly on the City's website upon submission.
(c) **Annual report.** No later than March 31 each year, covering the prior calendar year: a summary and analysis of incidents and any patterns or trends; an assessment of whether department protocols under §33-7(b)(3) are functioning as intended; a description of guidance issued or updated under §33-7(a); and any recommendations for amendments to this article or to departmental protocols.
(d) **Confidentiality.** Nothing in this section requires disclosing information that would identify any individual whose immigration status was implicated in an incident, or any City employee who reported or responded to one. The Legal Department redacts identifying information before submission, consistent with applicable law.

## §33-12. Designation and Notice of Areas Not Open to the General Public

(a) **Designation required.** The Mayor requires each department director to identify and submit a written inventory of all City-owned or City-controlled facilities under that department's jurisdiction, designating each discrete area as open or not open to the general public. The inventory is filed with the Office of the City Attorney and updated within 30 days of any material change in use or access configuration.
(b) **Notice requirement.** Conspicuous signage is posted at all primary points of ingress at each City-owned or City-controlled facility, identifying which areas are open to the public and which are not, and at any point of transition between a public and non-public area.
(c) **Sign content.** Public areas: "This area is open to the general public." Non-public areas: "This area is not open to the general public."
(d) **Applicability.** Applies to all facilities owned by the City or controlled through lease, management agreement, or similar arrangement. Does not apply to property owned by the federal government and operated by or leased to the City.

## §33-13. Federal Immigration Enforcement Cost Recovery

(a) **Purpose.** The City may seek to recover costs incurred from federal immigration enforcement activities conducted within its jurisdiction, to protect local resources and ensure City taxpayers do not bear the financial burden of federal actions.
(b) **Cost Recovery Authority.** The Mayor, or the Mayor's designee, is authorized to identify, document, and pursue reimbursement from the federal government, including but not limited to DHS, ICE, and CBP, for any costs incurred by the City arising from or related to federal immigration enforcement activities.
(c) **Recoverable Costs** include, but are not limited to: (1) personnel costs, including salaries, overtime, and benefits associated with diverted staff time; (2) public safety response costs, including police, fire, and emergency services; (3) administrative and legal costs, including documentation, compliance, and interagency coordination; (4) costs associated with detention, transportation, or facility use; (5) community impact mitigation costs, including outreach, translation, and service provision; and (6) any other direct or indirect costs reasonably attributable to federal immigration enforcement activities within the City.
(d) **Documentation and Billing.** The City may establish procedures to track and document all eligible costs. The Mayor, or designee, may submit invoices, claims, or other formal requests for reimbursement to the appropriate federal agencies and may take any administrative action necessary to pursue payment.

## Section III. Effective Date

This ordinance takes effect on the date it is approved by the Mayor. All other ordinances or parts of ordinances inconsistent with this ordinance are repealed to the extent of that inconsistency as of that date.

## Notes on what this resolves

This is the enacted, signed text, not the February 16 draft or the March 19 as-introduced summary. Comparing it against those:

- It confirms the vote: approved 7-0 on April 2, 2026, per the ordinance's own printed header (distinct from the harder-to-read handwritten signature date on the same page) and per César's direct account as a member present for the vote.
- FPD is not carved out. Every operative restriction (§33-4, §33-5, §33-6) applies to "a City employee," full stop, and is scoped by *purpose* (whether the activity is "for the purpose of, or in furtherance of, an immigration enforcement operation"), not by which department the employee belongs to. FPD's ordinary criminal enforcement work is untouched because it is not itself immigration enforcement, not because FPD is exempted from the article.
- The cost-recovery provisions are §33-13 of this ordinance itself, not a separate amendment. The categories match the March 20 draft amendment almost exactly. That means the cost-recovery language was folded into G-26-1 sometime between the March 20 draft and the April 2 vote, rather than passed later as a standalone amendment. No vault note documents when or how that merge happened.
- Section-by-section, the enacted text tracks the March 19 as-introduced provision list almost exactly. The only substantive addition is §33-13. No other amendments between introduction and passage are evident from this document alone.
- None of the February 16 pre-introduction draft's discarded elements reappear here: no centralized ICE-interaction log, no translated public-summary requirement, and no explicit civil-detainer no-detention clause (the closest equivalent is the general judicial-warrant standard in §33-4).
- What this document does *not* resolve: preemption exposure relative to the Community Trust Act litigation (a legal question about a different statute, not answered by this ordinance's own text), and the implementation record (whether Legal Department guidance, department protocols, signage, or any §33-11 reports have actually been produced since passage).
