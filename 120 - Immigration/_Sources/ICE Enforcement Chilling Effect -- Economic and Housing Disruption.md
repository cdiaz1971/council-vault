# ICE Enforcement Chilling Effect -- Economic and Housing Disruption

## Summary

Aggressive ICE enforcement creates a chilling effect that extends well beyond the immigrant community. In Minnesota, families who stopped working out of fear fell behind on rent, triggering thousands of eviction filings and a historic rent strike movement. Memphis saw a parallel dynamic under the National Guard-backed Memphis Safe Task Force. Both cities show how immigration enforcement destabilizes housing markets and local economies in predictable, documentable ways.

## Key Points

In Minnesota, Operation Metro Surge drove tens of thousands of low-income immigrant households to stop going to work by January 2026. HOME Line, a statewide tenant advocacy nonprofit, tracked over 2,400 eviction filings in Minneapolis and St. Paul in the first weeks of 2026. Estimates placed lost wages at over $47 million and additional rental assistance needs at $15.7 million among affected households.

Five labor unions and the newly formed Twin Cities Tenants union threatened a March 1 rent strike -- organizers called it the largest such action in the US in over a century -- demanding an eviction moratorium and $50 million in rent relief from Governor Tim Walz. The coalition represented nearly 26,000 workers. The strike was structured as an escalating pledge drive: 10,000 tenant commitments required before a strike authorization vote, with the logic that solidarity from tenants who could pay would create a protective buffer for those who could not.

In Memphis, Tennessee Governor Bill Lee requested the Memphis Safe Task Force in September 2025, deploying the National Guard and 30+ federal agencies under 287(g) agreements -- a federal program with a 916% increase in county participation since January 2025. Critics documented racial profiling, with pretextual traffic stops disproportionately targeting Black and Latino residents. Local elected officials filed a lawsuit challenging the National Guard deployment as unconstitutional under state law. Vecindarios 901, a volunteer-based immigrants' rights coalition, built a rapid-response observer network to document ICE activity.

Both cities illustrate the same pattern: enforcement-driven fear suppresses labor participation, reduces consumer spending, increases eviction pressure, and strains local housing systems -- with effects falling on low-income renters regardless of immigration status.

## Why This Matters for César

Frederick County is experiencing its own ICE-related fear, as documented in WYPR reporting from March 2026. As Council President, César has a direct policy interest in understanding how other cities have responded -- what municipal protections were enacted, what advocacy coalitions formed, and what the measurable housing and economic effects were.

The Minnesota rent strike model and the Memphis community observer network (Vecindarios 901) are both worth tracking as peer examples of organized civic response. The 287(g) agreement pattern is also directly relevant: Frederick County's relationship with federal immigration enforcement is a live policy question.
