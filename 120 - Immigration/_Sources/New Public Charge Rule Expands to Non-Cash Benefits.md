# New Public Charge Rule Expands to Non-Cash Benefits

## Summary
New USCIS guidance taking effect September 18 lets immigration officers weigh enrollment in non-cash public benefits, including Medicaid, CHIP, and SNAP, against applicants seeking green cards, certain visas, or admission to the United States, reversing a 2022 rule that excluded those programs from public charge consideration.

## Key Points
The rule affects people applying for adjustment to lawful permanent residency, including spouses, siblings, parents, and children of citizens, certain workers, and foreign medical graduates.

Asylees, refugees, trafficking victims, VAWA self-petitioners, and several other protected classes are explicitly exempted from public charge consideration.

DHS itself projected in its proposed guidance that the rule could reduce federal and state benefit spending by nearly $9 billion annually due to disenrollment, and separately acknowledged downstream revenue effects for hospitals, pharmaceutical manufacturers, grocery retailers participating in SNAP, and landlords in federally funded housing programs.
