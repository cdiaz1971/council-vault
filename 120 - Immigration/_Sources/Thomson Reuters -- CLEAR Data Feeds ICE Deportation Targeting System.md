# Thomson Reuters -- CLEAR Data Feeds ICE Deportation Targeting System

## Summary

Thomson Reuters operates a data brokerage product called CLEAR that sells names, addresses, car registrations, Social Security numbers, ethnicity details, and household information to government clients. Investigative reporting by 404 Media has established that CLEAR feeds directly into Palantir's ELITE system, which ICE uses to map deportation targets and generate confidence scores on current addresses. Over 200 Thomson Reuters employees have signed internal letters protesting the company's ICE and DHS contracts.

## Key Points

ELITE is a Palantir-built tool used by ICE's Enforcement and Removal Operations division. It populates a map with potential deportation targets, pulls up dossiers on individuals, and assigns a "confidence score" on their current address. Internal ICE documents obtained by 404 Media name CLEAR as one of ELITE's address sources.

CLEAR is also integrated into Motorola's Mobile Companion app, which allows ICE agents to cross-reference license plate reader data with Thomson Reuters' personal data in the field. DHS procurement documents describe CLEAR as "vital to the mission-essential, time sensitive investigative work of several DHS Components."

Thomson Reuters employees in Eagan, Minnesota -- a major CLEAR development hub -- say they are concerned about their work's role in immigration enforcement operations, including Operation Metro Surge, which resulted in the deaths of Renee Good and Alex Pretti. Company leadership has responded by stating CLEAR prohibits use to locate undocumented immigrants who have not committed crimes, but did not comment on the specific allegations when contacted by 404 Media.

A minority shareholder, the BC General Employees Union, has engaged Thomson Reuters on its ICE contracts and stated that the findings cut against the company's public claims about limiting CLEAR to criminal investigations.

## Relevance

This is directly relevant to Frederick's immigration protection work and the City Values Act. If Frederick ever considers using any Thomson Reuters products (including Westlaw, which shares the parent company), this reporting surfaces the data-sharing architecture upstream of those tools. The Palantir/CLEAR/ICE pipeline is also directly applicable to any council discussion about surveillance technology governance and data broker relationships -- a gap in Frederick's current policy framework.
