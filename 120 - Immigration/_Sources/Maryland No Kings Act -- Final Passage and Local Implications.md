# Maryland No Kings Act -- Final Passage and Local Implications

## Summary
The Maryland House of Delegates gave final approval to the No Kings Act on April 11, 2026, the final Saturday of the 2026 General Assembly session. The bill allows Maryland residents to sue federal officials who violate their constitutional rights while acting under color of federal authority. It passed overwhelmingly in the House and had already cleared the Senate.

## Key Points

**What the bill does:** The No Kings Act creates a state-level civil cause of action against federal officers who violate the constitutional rights of Marylanders. It is designed to give state and local prosecutors -- and private citizens -- a legal mechanism to identify and hold accountable federal agents operating in Maryland whose actions exceed constitutional authority.

**Legislative context:** The bill is part of a broader package of legislation the Maryland General Assembly advanced in 2026 in direct response to Trump administration federal enforcement actions. Related legislation includes the Community Trust Act (limiting local cooperation with ICE) and ending Maryland's 287(g) program, which had allowed local law enforcement to partner with ICE for immigration detentions.

**No Kings rallies:** The bill drew its name and energy from a statewide protest movement. Three rounds of "No Kings" rallies filled cities across Maryland, including a notable event in Hagerstown where plans to convert a nearby warehouse into an immigration detention center had galvanized advocates.

**Passage timing:** Final House vote came April 11, 2026, the last Saturday before the legislature's April 13 adjournment. The bill had already passed the Senate.
