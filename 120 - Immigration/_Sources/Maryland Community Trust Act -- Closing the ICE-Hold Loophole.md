# Maryland Community Trust Act -- Closing the ICE-Hold Loophole

## Summary
The Community Trust Act is an emergency bill pending Gov. Moore's signature that would prohibit local and state police from holding people for ICE except in limited cases involving judicial warrants. It closes a loophole that became apparent after the 287(g) ban, when some sheriffs signaled they would cooperate with ICE informally anyway.

## Key Points
The bill, sponsored by Sen. Clarence Lam (D-Anne Arundel and Howard), prohibits the use of administrative warrants from ICE to hold a person -- only a judicial warrant would obligate compliance. Exceptions cover people convicted of felonies in the US, registered sex offenders, those who served 12-18 months in state prison, or committed offenses in another state with 5+ years served.

Lam framed it as the "critical bookend" to the 287(g) ban: after that bill passed, several Maryland sheriffs indicated they might continue informal cooperation with ICE. All nine sheriffs with 287(g) agreements ultimately dropped them, but some are now assessing whether the Community Trust Act constrains their communications with federal officials.

The bill is an emergency measure that would take effect immediately upon signing. If Moore lets it become law without his signature, it goes into effect June 3, 2026. Washington County Sheriff Brian Albert has said he hopes the governor vetoes it and is "exploring legal actions."

More than two dozen immigrant advocacy and civil rights groups -- organized by We Are CASA -- wrote to Moore urging signature before the final scheduled bill signing of the year, which is approaching next week.

## Relevance
This is directly consequential for District 2. The Community Trust Act is one of the strongest state-level protections for the immigrant community César represents, and its fate hinges on a gubernatorial decision in the next two weeks. Frederick County has its own sheriff dynamics that mirror the statewide pattern. Worth tracking the signing deadline closely and considering a public statement of support once Moore acts -- or before, if timing warrants.

The companion Data Privacy Act (blocking sale of personal data for immigration enforcement purposes) remains unsigned as well.
