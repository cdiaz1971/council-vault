## Summary

As of late May 2026, both the Community Trust Act and the Maryland Data Privacy Act have become law without Governor Moore's signature. Progressive Maryland reported this outcome in its May 26 weekly memo as a major organizing win after sustained pressure from advocacy organizations including Progressive Maryland, We Are CASA, and allied groups.

## Key Points

The Community Trust Act focuses local government resources on community safety rather than ICE collaboration. It restricts how local law enforcement agencies can cooperate with federal immigration enforcement -- a structural equivalent at the state level of what Frederick has been attempting through the [[City Values Act]] and the [[Frederick Rapid Response Network]].

The Data Privacy Act protects personal information from being used in immigration enforcement contexts. One of the primary vectors for immigration enforcement escalation has been data sharing -- across agencies, with private databases, and with federal partners. A state law limiting this creates significant leverage.

Governor Moore's decision to let both bills become law without signing them reflects political calculation rather than full endorsement. The coalition pressure was sufficient to prevent a veto but not sufficient to generate active executive championing -- worth tracking.

Progressive Maryland is also mobilizing around the Maryland primary election one month out, with phonebanks targeting Crystal Jackson Parker's race in District 40 (the delegate who was the only Democrat on the Judiciary Committee to vote against the Community Trust Act).

## Why This Matters for Cesar

Maryland's statewide immigrant protection framework now strengthens the ground Frederick has been building locally. The [[City Values Act]] and Frederick's 287(g) termination advocacy operate within this state context -- the passage of these bills creates favorable legal terrain and potential preemption protection for local sanctuary policies.
