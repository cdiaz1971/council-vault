# ICE Buying Immigrant Tax Identifiers -- Data Broker Loophole in Action

## Core Finding

A $9,968,353.56 contract signed June 5, 2026, between ICE's Homeland Security Investigations (HSI) and Thundercut Technology LLC is for "ITIN data subscription and analytics for HSI agents in fraud investigations." ITINs -- Individual Taxpayer Identification Numbers -- are the identifiers the IRS issues to undocumented people who cannot obtain a Social Security number. Tens of millions of undocumented people use them to pay taxes.

Two federal courts have already blocked ICE from obtaining ITIN data directly from the IRS, ruling the arrangement unlawful and at risk of "significantly raising the risk of misidentification of taxpayers." The IRS has admitted it inappropriately shared information with ICE. This contract appears to achieve the same outcome through a data broker intermediary, sidestepping the court orders entirely.

Senator Ron Wyden's assessment: the contract "looks for all the world like Trump is trying to skirt the law and a court order to fuel his mass-deportation campaign." He notes the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau was on the verge of closing the data broker loophole before the Trump administration dismantled the agency.

## The Data Broker Loophole

ICE has repeatedly purchased data it cannot legally compel or obtain under court order -- smartphone location data, phone and utility records, neighborhood-level phone monitoring (Webloc). The pattern is consistent: when legal mechanisms are blocked or constrained, the federal government routes around them via private data markets. The ITIN contract is the clearest example yet because a court had already specifically prohibited the underlying data transfer.

## What This Means for Immigrant Protection Work

The "fraud investigation" framing is a pattern. Nearly 90% of HSI agents -- over 6,000 officials -- have been reassigned from their normal responsibilities (money laundering, child exploitation) to immigration enforcement. Fraud framing was also used in the large-scale Minnesota operation targeting Somali immigrants.

For Frederick's immigrant protection infrastructure, this reinforces the argument that ITIN data -- long considered a firewall because it was tax-protected -- is now a targeting vector. Communities using ITINs to participate in the formal economy are exposed.
