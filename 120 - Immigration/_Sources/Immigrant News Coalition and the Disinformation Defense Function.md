# Immigrant News Coalition and the Disinformation Defense Function

## Summary

Four nonprofit newsrooms serving immigrant communities -- Conecta Arizona (Phoenix), El Tímpano (Bay Area), Documented (New York), and Sahan Journal (Minneapolis) -- describe their editorial model in a Next City podcast. Their shared framework: publish in multiple languages across platforms, deliver actionable information rather than awareness content, and run disinformation defense workshops. The newly-formed Immigrant News Coalition is their coordinating structure.

## Key Points

The distinction these newsrooms draw is between journalism that explains immigrant communities to outsiders and journalism that serves immigrant communities directly. As Vanan Murugesan of Sahan Journal put it: traditional coverage of minority communities is typically produced for someone else's awareness and understanding -- their model adds a layer by answering "what does this actually mean for your life?"

Madeleine Bair of El Tímpano identifies disinformation as the critical vulnerability: "When people don't have a place to go for trusted information, that's when fear sets in. That's when people are very vulnerable to misinformation and disinformation." Disinformation defense workshops are an active component of these newsrooms' programming, not just a side project.

The podcast also covers Memphis organizers resisting 287(g) contracts that deputize local police as ICE agents -- the same mechanism that Maryland's Community Trust Act is designed to prohibit.

## Relevance

Frederick does not have a Spanish-language or multilingual civic information infrastructure equivalent to what these newsrooms provide. Centro Hispano de Frederick and SSCM play some of this role in the community, but neither operates as a journalism organization with disinformation defense programming.

As the enforcement climate intensifies and the Community Trust Act faces legal challenge, the question of where Frederick's immigrant residents get accurate, actionable, and trusted information becomes urgent. This model is worth understanding as context for any communications strategy around the CVA, immigrant legal services, or the IAC resource guide revision (which has already surfaced a gap: the current guide does not adequately serve undocumented residents).

The disinformation defense workshop model could be something Frederick partners on with Centro Hispano or SSCM -- a low-cost civic infrastructure intervention.
