# Federal Contract Collapse Leaves Unaccompanied Immigrant Children Without Legal Counsel

## Summary

More than 20,000 unaccompanied immigrant children nationwide are at risk of losing legal representation after the Trump administration let its federal legal-services contract with the Acacia Center for Justice expire July 31, 2026, with the eventual replacement contractor lacking any history of immigration representation.

## Key Points

- Acacia Center for Justice coordinated roughly 100 nonprofit providers -- including the Amica Center for Justice, based in Baltimore and DC -- representing unaccompanied children in immigration court. Most providers have kept representing existing clients unpaid since the contract lapsed; Amica executive director Michael Lukens calls that "not a sustainable model," adding the field feels "both ethically and morally obligated to continue as long as we can."
- Children newly placed into federal custody since July 31 are largely going without attorneys. Without a lawyer, fewer than 10% of immigrant children win the right to remain in the US while their case proceeds.
- HHS first tried handing the $150M replacement contract to a small Texas firm (two immigration attorneys, run by a former Trump official) that backed out. It then awarded a $158M no-bid contract, running August 7, 2026 to February 6, 2027, to Our Rescue -- formerly Operation Underground Railroad, founded by Tim Ballard (who left after sexual-misconduct allegations) -- a group with no apparent immigration attorneys on staff or history representing children in immigration court.
- As of early September, neither Acacia's communications director nor the Florence Immigrant & Refugee Rights Project (representing 600 children in Arizona) had heard from Our Rescue about a case-transition process.
- Underlying dispute: HHS has withheld $65 million in congressionally appropriated legal-representation funding since December 2025, after Acacia's attorneys refused to hand over sensitive case data (medical records, types of relief sought) to the administration.
