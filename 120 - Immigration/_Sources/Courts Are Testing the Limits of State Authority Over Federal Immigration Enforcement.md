# Courts Are Testing the Limits of State Authority Over Federal Immigration Enforcement

## Summary
A California law banning masked immigration enforcement was blocked in court for exempting state law enforcement, and a Colorado detention facility health inspection law is being challenged by a private prison operator, both showing courts actively testing how far state pushback laws can go.

## Key Points
A California law banning federal and state agents from wearing masks was blocked by a judge earlier this year specifically because it exempted state law enforcement, which the court found discriminated against federal agents. Colorado expanded its health department's authority to inspect immigration detention facilities and require compliance with state health and safety standards. That law is now being challenged by a private prison operator as state officials seek more access and information over at least one tuberculosis case among detainees. Virginia's governor vetoed a similar courthouse and school protection bill over concern it would put security guards and local law enforcement in the position of choosing between state and federal law.

## Relevance
This is the same legal terrain Frederick's Community Trust Act exposure sits on. The pattern across California and Colorado, courts scrutinizing even carefully drafted state pushback laws for equal protection or federal preemption problems, is directly relevant to how the DOJ's Supremacy Clause challenge against the Community Trust Act might play out.
