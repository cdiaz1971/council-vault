# Ghost Cars -- ICE Arrests Leave Families Scrambling for Abandoned Vehicles

## Summary

A Howard Center for Investigative Journalism investigation documents the "ghost car" phenomenon across Maryland: vehicles abandoned at the site of car-based ICE arrests, often with broken windows, keys confiscated or thrown on the ground, and no notification to local police or family members. More than 50 such cars have been documented in Prince George's and Montgomery counties since January by a single volunteer group. People Over Papers has logged 8,500 car-based arrests nationwide.

## Key Points

**The mechanism:**
ICE conducts surveillance on targets, then initiates a traffic stop and arrests the person from their vehicle. The vehicle is left at the scene. ICE policy (per their spokesperson) is to allow arrestees to call for pickup or to notify local police for towing -- but in practice, multiple Maryland police departments say they are not notified before arrests or before vehicles are abandoned.

**What happens to the car:**
Agents have been observed leaving cars with broken driver's side windows, with keys thrown on the ground, in tow-away zones, blocking fire stations, and on private property. When on private property, the business owner arranges the tow -- not ICE, and not the police. Tow fees run $200+ for the initial tow and $50/day for storage. If the detained person is the titled owner, many tow lots will not release the car to family members.

**Scale documented:**
- Immigrant Rights Collective: 50+ abandoned cars in PG and Montgomery counties since January
- People Over Papers: 8,500 car-based immigration arrests nationwide since inauguration; 800+ abandoned car reports since May of the previous year

**Community response:**
Volunteers locate vehicles by driving likely routes, looking through windows for identifying documents, and using registration paperwork to reunite cars with families. Organizations like CASA describe cars that are also work vans with thousands of dollars of tools, or the family's only transportation for children.

**Policy response:**
Montgomery County passed an ordinance allowing tow lots to release cars to household members using documents like a lease or utility bill -- addressing the title-holder bottleneck.

## Relevance

This is a direct operational dimension of the ICE enforcement environment in Maryland, and it connects to the Frederick Rapid Response Network's work. The City Values Act governs Frederick's own response to ICE activity on city property. The ghost car phenomenon is what happens on public streets and private lots outside the city's jurisdiction -- but it is happening to Frederick residents too.

The community volunteer coordination model also points to organizational infrastructure questions: does Frederick Rapid Response Network or another organization have the capacity to track and respond to ghost cars in the Frederick area?
