# Maryland 287(g) Ban -- Implementation and Compliance After Governor Signature

## Summary

Maryland's ban on 287(g) agreements between local law enforcement and ICE took effect immediately upon Governor Moore's signature on February 17, 2026. Despite months of vocal opposition from sheriffs who vowed to fight the legislation, seven of the nine covered counties terminated their ICE agreements the same day or the day after the signing. The rapid compliance is politically significant -- it demonstrates that state preemption of local-federal immigration enforcement cooperation works and that sheriff resistance was largely posturing.

## Key Points

Seven counties -- Allegany, Carroll, Cecil, Frederick, Harford, St. Mary's, and Wicomico -- formally notified ICE of their termination. Washington County's sheriff said the agreement is "pretty much null and void" though it remained on ICE's website. Garrett County did not respond to press inquiries and remained listed as active on ICE's national participant list as of the article's publication.

Frederick County was among the seven that complied immediately. Cecil County Sheriff Scott Adams's termination letter thanked ICE for the partnership since 2019 while acknowledging the legal obligation to exit.

The remaining fight has shifted to the Community Trust Act (SB 791), which prohibits local law enforcement from holding individuals for ICE except in narrow circumstances (felony conviction, sex offender registration, significant prior prison time) and requires a judicial warrant rather than an administrative ICE detainer. Several sheriffs are consulting attorneys about potential legal challenges and Harford County Sheriff Gahler has publicly urged Governor Moore to veto it. Moore had not yet signed it as of the article's publication, with one more signing event scheduled for May 26.

A companion bill, the Data Privacy Act (HB 711), would prohibit businesses from selling personal data for immigration enforcement purposes and is also awaiting the governor's signature.

## Relevance

Frederick County was one of the seven that complied immediately -- this is a direct win for the immigrant protection framework the City Values Act was designed to complement. The CVA was built on the premise that local governments have authority to limit cooperation with federal civil immigration enforcement; the 287(g) ban confirms that state law can go further and mandate compliance.

The Community Trust Act is the more consequential pending piece. If Moore signs it, Frederick County law enforcement will be prohibited from holding people on ICE administrative detainers -- closing the primary mechanism through which 287(g) termination could be circumvented. The judicial warrant requirement is the same standard the City Values Act uses for non-public city facility access.

The Data Privacy Act matters for District 2 constituents who may be targeted through data broker pipelines -- directly connects to [[Palantir ELITE -- ICE Builds a 20 Million Person Target List]] and the surveillance infrastructure concerns already documented in the vault.

Watch the May 26 signing event for Community Trust Act and Data Privacy Act status.
