# Immigrant News Coalition -- Multilingual Journalism as Community Infrastructure in ICE Era

## Summary

The Immigrant News Coalition is a newly formed collaboration of four mission-driven nonprofit newsrooms -- Conecta Arizona (Phoenix), El Tímpano (San Francisco Bay Area), Documented (New York City), and Sahan Journal (Minneapolis) -- built to coordinate immigrant-serving journalism during the federal enforcement surge. Their shared model distinguishes between journalism produced for outside audiences and journalism produced in service to the communities being covered. Key offerings: multilingual publishing, actionable information for daily decisions, and disinformation defense workshops.

## Key Points

The Coalition's framing challenge is a useful diagnostic for any local immigrant support infrastructure. When communities lack trusted information sources, fear fills the vacuum -- and fear makes people vulnerable to misinformation. El Tímpano's founder Madeleine Bair put it directly: the absence of trusted information is not neutral; it is a condition that bad actors exploit.

Sahan Journal's Vanan Murugesan articulated the distinction that separates community-serving journalism from traditional coverage: traditional journalism about minority communities is typically produced for someone else's awareness. Community-serving journalism answers a different question -- what does this actually mean for your life? That operational framing -- not awareness, but actionability -- is what makes journalism into infrastructure.

The Coalition's practical outputs include: publishing in multiple languages and on multiple platforms (print, digital, social, broadcast), providing workshops that help community members identify and resist disinformation campaigns, and producing content that gives people specific, usable information rather than framing their situation for external consumption.

The episode also touches on Memphis organizers fighting new 287(g) contracts -- a direct parallel to the battle Frederick already won through the City Values Act and Maryland's 287(g) ban.

## Relevance

The Immigrant News Coalition model is directly relevant to the gap in Frederick's immigrant protection ecosystem. The CVA and the Frederick Rapid Response Network address enforcement-side protections. What the Coalition model points to is the communications infrastructure gap: who is producing trusted, multilingual, actionable information for Frederick's immigrant community at a scale and in formats that actually reach them?

Centro Hispano and Spanish Speaking Community of Maryland are the closest local analogs, but neither is a journalism operation. The model worth watching is whether a local analog to El Tímpano or Conecta Arizona exists or could be seeded in Frederick -- a small, mission-driven outlet that produces information in Spanish and other languages specifically oriented toward daily decision-making for immigrant residents.

This is also relevant framing for how César communicates publicly about immigration policy. The distinction between awareness-oriented communication and actionable communication maps onto how council communications can be designed -- not just announcing what passed, but explaining what it means for a family's specific situation.
