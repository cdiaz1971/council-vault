# Immigration Enforcement Fear Compounding Maryland Childcare Access Crisis

## Summary
Immigration enforcement fear is driving falling enrollment at immigrant-serving childcare centers like Arco Iris Bilingual Children's Center in Laurel, compounding an already strained childcare access landscape after Maryland's 2025 scholarship program freeze.

## Key Points
Arco Iris director Carolina Reyes said enrollment dropped from consistently full, with a waitlist every year for thirteen years, to about half capacity over the past two years. She traces part of the shift to a December incident in which ICE agents parked eleven cars in the lot just outside the center, after which several families stopped coming out of fear. A parent separately asked Reyes whether the drop was partly because the center's Spanish name might draw extra attention from immigration agents, a possibility she said she had not previously considered. Layered on top of this is the state's May 2025 freeze of its childcare scholarship program, which hit low-income immigrant families hard. The legislature restored $20 million in funding this year and scholarships resumed July 1, but a waitlist remains and Reyes said she has not yet seen improvement on the ground.
