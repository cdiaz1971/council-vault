# ICE Detainers Are Requests, Not Mandates -- Fact-Checking DHS Secretary Mullin's New York Claims

## Summary

DHS Secretary Markwayne Mullin's claim that Gov. Hochul agreed to help ICE target 100 New York individuals was denied by her office and appears to conflate state prisoner-notification law, New York City's 177-felony ICE detainer exception, and the fact that detainers are voluntary requests a jail can decline absent a judicial warrant.

## Key Points

- After an ICE agent shot Oscar Belgal in Manhattan's Marble Hill neighborhood (Oct. 8, 2026), DHS Secretary Markwayne Mullin claimed in press remarks that Gov. Kathy Hochul had agreed in a phone call to help ICE target "over 100 different criminals -- or crimes," giving inconsistent versions of the number within the same remarks.
- Hochul spokesperson Alexia Nizhny called Mullin's account "inaccurate": "The Governor did not agree to cooperate with ICE on a list of 100 individuals or on any individual cases." Hochul herself has not directly rebutted Mullin but posted on X that she expressed "outrage" to Trump and Mullin, called for a halt to ICE enforcement in New York, and asked for release of body camera footage of the shooting.
- New York Correction Law section 147 does require the state prison system (DOCCS) to notify federal immigration authorities about noncitizens serving state prison sentences -- but this applies only to people sentenced to DOCCS custody, not a general cooperation agreement. Separately, this year's FY2027 state budget included the Local Cops, Local Crimes Act, which bans 287(g) agreements between local police and ICE.
- Documented NY's theory: Mullin's "100" figure may be a garbled reference to NYC's detainer policy, not a state-level number. Under a 2014 law (expanded in 2019), NYC carved out 177 felonies for which the city is permitted to honor ICE detainers -- a figure Mayor Zohran Mamdani and Public Advocate Jumaane Williams have both cited publicly (including in Mamdani's November 2025 Oval Office meeting with Trump and a January 2026 ABC News interview) to rebut "sanctuary city shields criminals" claims.
- Mullin separately claimed ICE filed 14,766 detainers in New York State between Jan. 20, 2025 and Sept. 2026, implying they were being ignored. Legal Aid Society immigration supervising attorney Hasan Shafiqullah confirmed detainers are not mandatory: "These are not mandatory... New York City in 2014 said, 'Thanks, but no thanks, we're no longer honoring these.' However, there was an exception for folks who have certain criminal convictions." A judicial warrant, not a detainer request alone, is required for a locality to hold someone for ICE transfer.

## Relevance

This is useful background for Frederick's own non-cooperation posture, which rests on the same legal distinction the article documents: an ICE detainer is a voluntary request, not a mandate, and a jurisdiction can set its own policy on which (if any) detainers it honors without violating federal law. That distinction -- and the pattern of a federal official either conflating or misstating state and local cooperation mechanisms after a high-profile enforcement incident -- is directly relevant to César's CVA advocacy and non-cooperation framing (see [[City Values Act]] and [[Local Authority and Federal Preemption]]), and to anticipating how the City's own non-cooperation record might be mischaracterized publicly after any local incident.
