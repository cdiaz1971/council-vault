# County-Level Trust Acts Proliferate as Community Trust Act Faces DOJ Preemption Challenge

## Summary
Montgomery County passed its own County Values Act layered on top of Maryland's Community Trust Act, part of a wider push for local trust ordinances. Advocates say it is too early to judge how much protection any of these laws actually deliver while DOJ's Supremacy Clause suit against the state law remains unresolved.

## Key Points
An education director with We Are CASA characterized the pre-Trust Act landscape bluntly: virtually every Maryland county was already informally collaborating with ICE to some degree, just without a formal agreement, which is the gap these local ordinances are meant to close. Montgomery residents rallied in Gaithersburg this month demanding their county pass its own trust law after a mother of three was detained there on July 28, even though Montgomery already has the County Values Act. Advocates interviewed for the piece described the overall effectiveness of this legal layer, state Community Trust Act plus county-level trust acts, as "a mixed bag" given the DOJ's ongoing Supremacy Clause challenge to the state law.

## Relevance
This is the same legal exposure question sitting under Frederick's own City Values Act. Both the state Community Trust Act and the CVA rely on a warrant-based cooperation restriction, the same structure already flagged in [[Warrant-Based ICE Restrictions Create Legal Exposure Not Prevention]] as slowing enforcement without preventing it. If the DOJ suit succeeds against the state law, the reasoning would likely extend to any local ordinance built the same way, including the CVA. Montgomery's County Values Act is also a useful naming and structure precedent if Frederick considers strengthening or amending the CVA later. Worth a quiet check with Rachel Gray on how exposed the CVA actually is if the state case goes against Maryland.
