# Salvadoran TPS Termination Threatens Deportation for Nation's Second-Largest Population Center

## Summary

Temporary Protected Status for roughly 200,000 Salvadorans nationwide was scheduled to expire September 9, 2026, after 25 years in effect. Congressional Democrats pushed legislation to extend it 18 months for the more than 170,000 Salvadorans it would directly cover, but that legislation had not passed as the deadline hit. The D.C. area holds the nation's second-largest Salvadoran population, behind only Los Angeles.

## Key Points

USCIS notified Salvadorans last month that their TPS protection was set to expire September 9. Rep. Suhas Subramanyam (D-Va.) and other Democratic lawmakers backed legislation extending the protection 18 months for more than 170,000 people, framing an end to TPS as economically disruptive given Salvadoran contributions to the regional workforce and tax base. Under federal law, DHS is required to decide on extension or termination at least 60 days before expiration. The administration's stated position is that TPS has been treated as an unintended amnesty program rather than a genuinely temporary status.

Daysi Gonzalez, a 31-year-old TPS holder who arrived at age 5, spoke at a Capitol press event describing the prospect of being forced to leave the only home she has known. The D.C. area's Salvadoran population is second nationally only to Los Angeles, which puts this deadline closer to home for Maryland's immigrant community than a typical TPS story elsewhere in the country.

## Relevance

District 2 has a foreign-born population 1.4 times the county rate, and Frederick's immigrant community organizations, Centro Hispano, RISE Coalition, SSCM, are the network through which a Salvadoran TPS termination would land locally. This sits alongside the existing City Values Act and immigrant-protection track, and belongs with the vault's broader ICE enforcement documentation as evidence of the federal posture immigrant residents are operating under. No council action is implicated directly, this is a federal deadline, but it is relevant background for any City Values Act communications, constituent outreach, or RISE Coalition conversations touching TPS specifically.
