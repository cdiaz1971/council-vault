# Maryland Community Trust Act -- Governor Allows Law Without Signature

## Summary

Governor Wes Moore declined to sign the Community Trust Act (SB 791) and the companion Data Privacy Act into law, but allowed both to take effect without his signature. Moore stated he supports the bills' goals but cited "real implementation challenges" around joint federal-state law enforcement coordination. Both bills passed on the final day of the 2026 legislative session and were priorities of immigrant advocacy groups including We Are CASA.

## Key Points

The Community Trust Act ends local law enforcement's practice of accepting administrative (non-judicial) warrants from ICE to hold detainees. Under the law, Maryland local law enforcement is only obligated to honor a judicial warrant from a federal immigration agent. The act was framed as a complement to the earlier emergency bill banning 287(g) agreements between ICE and local police -- eight of nine Maryland counties with 287(g) agreements quickly dropped them; Washington County said it would no longer enforce its agreement even while keeping it on the books.

Moore's implementation concern centers on "ambiguities around joint investigations" -- the worry that the law creates confusion when local and federal agencies are working together on criminal cases. He said his office is working with the Attorney General to clarify those provisions through executive action and potentially next year's legislative session.

The Data Privacy Act closes loopholes in the state's Public Information Act to prohibit businesses from selling personal data for the purpose of immigration enforcement. The Senate version, sponsored by Sen. Clarence Lam, did not advance in the House; the House version sponsored by Del. Lorig Charkoudian (D-Montgomery) is the one becoming law.

The Maryland Legislative Latino Caucus welcomed the outcome while noting that what matters is the policy, not the signing ceremony.

## Relevance

This is the state-level anchor for Frederick's local immigration protection work. The CVA (City Values Act, G-26-1) operates in the same policy space -- it governs Frederick Police Department cooperation with ICE at the city level. Moore's non-signature posture creates a politically useful precedent: a governor who agrees with the goal but hedges on implementation is not the same as opposition. The Frederick Council can cite the CTA as state backing for the CVA's underlying principle.

The companion lawsuit filed today by 17 Maryland sheriffs -- including Frederick County Sheriff -- challenging the CTA creates a live legal environment. Monitor the federal case (Gahler v. Moore, No. 8:26-cv-02057) for any preliminary injunction that could delay implementation. See [[Frederick County Sheriff Joins Lawsuit Challenging Community Trust Act]].
