# Maryland Community Trust Act -- SB791 Passage and ICE Detainer Limits

## Summary

On the final day of the 2026 legislative session, the Maryland General Assembly passed the Community Trust Act (SB791) and the Data Privacy Act (HB711), two landmark bills limiting law enforcement cooperation with ICE. The Community Trust Act was designated an emergency bill and takes effect immediately upon the Governor's signature. Both bills were championed by We Are CASA and a coalition of partner organizations.

## Key Points

The Community Trust Act does two things. First, it requires a judicial warrant before any correctional facility can detain or hold a person for transfer to ICE -- ending the practice of honoring ICE administrative detainers that lacked judicial oversight. Second, it places strict limits on police and correctional officers proactively sharing information about individuals with ICE, cutting off a key pathway into the deportation pipeline.

The Data Privacy Act (HB711) passed alongside SB791, though CASA's communication did not detail its specific provisions.

Several additional bills were advanced in the same session with CASA support:

- HB1017: prohibits and regulates private immigration detention in Maryland
- SB810 (Maryland Values Act): expands protections at sensitive locations
- SB792: expands limits on ICE access to hospitals
- HB1350: expands in-state tuition eligibility for noncitizen students
- HB963: allows noncitizens to serve as personal representatives of estates
- SB1: limits law enforcement's ability to wear facial coverings during enforcement

The emergency designation on the Community Trust Act means it bypasses the standard 90-day waiting period and goes into effect as soon as Governor Wes Moore signs it.

## Relevance

This is directly relevant to Frederick's [[City Values Act]] and [[Municipal ID]] legislation work. Maryland has now established a statewide floor on ICE cooperation that local policy can build on or align with. The Community Trust Act's warrant requirement and prohibition on proactive information-sharing are provisions Frederick has been considering at the municipal level -- the state legislation now provides both a model and political cover. Worth tracking how implementation rolls out, particularly for correctional facilities in Frederick County. Also relevant to [[Spanish Speaking Community of Maryland]] and [[Centro Hispano de Frederick]] as community partners tracking these outcomes.
