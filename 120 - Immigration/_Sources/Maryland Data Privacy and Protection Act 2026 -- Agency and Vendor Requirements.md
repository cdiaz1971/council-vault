## What the Law Does

The Maryland Data Privacy and Protection Act of 2026 (MDPPA) does two primary things:

1. **Limits agency data collection and retention:** State agencies must have a documented legal basis for collecting resident data. Retention periods are now constrained -- agencies cannot hold data indefinitely.

2. **Expands vendor privacy requirements:** Third-party contractors who receive, process, or have access to resident data must now meet expanded privacy obligations. This includes how they store data, who they share it with, and what happens to data at contract end.

The law applies to state agencies. City governments like Frederick are not directly bound, but the vendor requirements create a de facto floor: major GovTech vendors doing business with Maryland state agencies will need to update their data handling practices, and those updated practices will carry over to their municipal contracts as well.

## Why This Matters for Procurement

When Frederick evaluates technology vendors -- for permitting software, payment processing, public engagement tools, or any system that touches resident data -- the MDPPA framework is now the right benchmark. Even if Frederick is not legally required to enforce state-level rules, using the MDPPA as a procurement checklist aligns the city with Maryland's evolving expectations and reduces risk.

Questions to ask vendors:

- What resident data do you collect and for what purpose?
- How long is data retained?
- Who do you share data with (including subprocessors)?
- What happens to data when the contract ends?
- Do you comply with Maryland's data privacy requirements?

## Connection to City Values Act

Frederick's City Values Act limits cooperation with federal authorities on immigration enforcement. Data privacy is an extension of the same logic: the city should not be inadvertently sharing resident data through vendor pipelines in ways that could be accessed by federal agencies. Vendor data governance is an immigration protection issue as much as a privacy issue.

## Why This Matters for Cesar

As Council President, oversight of city technology contracts is an indirect but real governance responsibility. The MDPPA creates a Maryland-specific standard that Frederick can adopt as a vendor evaluation framework without waiting for state preemption. This also connects to the Municipal ID program -- any system that stores Municipal ID holder data needs rigorous data governance terms.
