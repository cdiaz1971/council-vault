# ICE at Military Graduations -- Chilling Effect on Immigrant Families

## Summary

Immigration enforcement presence at a Marine Corps graduation ceremony at Parris Island, South Carolina created widespread fear among immigrant families, causing some parents to skip their own children's graduation. The incident illustrates how ICE enforcement posture radiates beyond active enforcement operations into ordinary civilian and community life.

## Key Points

The Marine Corps posted a notice that federal law enforcement would conduct "lawful immigration status inquiries" at base entry points -- described as the first time in recent memory this had occurred at a military graduation. DHS denied that ICE would make arrests.

Despite the denial, news spread rapidly and immigrant families made the decision not to attend. Newly graduated Marines celebrated without their parents present. Marine veterans joined protesters outside the base.

The incident generated significant national reaction. The chilling effect was real and measurable even absent any confirmed arrests: the announcement alone was sufficient to separate families at a military milestone.

## Relevance

This is a concrete, high-visibility example of the chilling effect that immigration enforcement posture creates -- even when enforcement action doesn't occur.
