# Federal Agencies and Local Government Data -- ICE Data Requests

## Summary

Federal agencies, including ICE, are requesting access to state and local government data for immigration enforcement purposes. GovTech's coverage highlights the tension between federal data requests and the public trust obligations of local governments, and notes that technical and policy protections can be implemented to limit exposure.

## Key Points

The requests involve data that local governments routinely collect: driver records, utility accounts, benefits enrollment, court records, and similar administrative data. The concern is that sharing this data with federal immigration enforcement undermines the trust of immigrant and mixed-status communities in local government services, potentially reducing utilization of services people are entitled to use.

Experts cited by GovTech argue that local governments are not required to proactively share all data on request and that they can implement technical controls (data minimization, access logging, legal review processes) and policy controls (formal data sharing agreements, legal counsel review before disclosure) to protect residents while remaining compliant with applicable law.

The framing from GovTech is that this is a governance and policy decision, not just a legal one -- and that cities which establish clear policies in advance are better positioned than those responding ad hoc.

## Relevance

Directly relevant to the [[Proposed City Values Act Amendment]] (federal immigration enforcement cost recovery) and to Frederick's broader obligations to its immigrant community, including populations served by [[Centro Hispano de Frederick]], [[Spanish Speaking Community of Maryland]], and [[Asian American Center of Frederick]]. A city data governance policy addressing federal data requests would complement the cost recovery amendment and signal a coherent posture on immigration enforcement. Worth discussing with the City Attorney.
