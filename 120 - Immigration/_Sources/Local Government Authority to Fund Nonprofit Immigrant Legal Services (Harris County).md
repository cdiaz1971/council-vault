# Local Government Authority to Fund Nonprofit Immigrant Legal Services (Harris County)

## Summary

The 15th Court of Appeals rejected Texas AG Paxton's challenge to Harris County's immigrant legal aid program, finding no evidence of harm to residents. The ruling affirms that county funding of nonprofit immigrant legal services is legally defensible even under hostile state-level pressure.

## Key Points

Texas AG Ken Paxton sued Harris County in November 2025 after the county allocated taxpayer funds to nonprofits providing legal assistance to immigrants navigating immigration proceedings. Paxton's theory: the program was an unconstitutional gift of public funds to a private entity. He filed a parallel suit against Bexar County.

Both district court judges rejected the lawsuits. Paxton appealed the Harris County ruling to the 15th Court of Appeals -- a court created in 2023 with an all-Republican bench specifically to hear cases involving state government actions. That court still ruled against Paxton, finding he had not provided any proof the immigrant legal aid program harmed residents.

This is the same appellate court that sided with Paxton on blocking San Antonio's abortion travel funding. The distinction is significant: the court found a legitimate public purpose in immigrant legal aid that it did not find in abortion travel support. That is the legal line Paxton's theory could not cross.

Harris County Attorney Jonathan Fombonne: the ruling made clear the county has authority to serve residents through this program. The program has continued throughout the litigation without interruption.

The governance structure matters: county funds the nonprofit, the nonprofit provides services, the public benefit (legal representation for community members facing removal) is the legitimate purpose. That structure is exactly what a Frederick appropriation through a nonprofit intermediary would look like.

## Relevance

Maryland does not have a Paxton equivalent. But the Harris County case provides a tested legal framework and factual record -- including the public benefit argument that prevailed even before a hostile court -- that Frederick could draw on if a legal challenge ever arose to city funding of immigrant services.

The county-to-nonprofit funding model is directly replicable. Frederick's existing partnership with RISE Coalition, Centro Hispano, and SSCM establishes the nonprofit intermediary relationships. A modest city appropriation flowing through one of those organizations for legal services would structurally mirror what Harris County did -- and what the court upheld.
