# Refugee Admissions on Track for Record Low Under Trump's Afrikaner-Prioritized Cap

## Summary
US refugee admissions are on pace to hit a record low this fiscal year, 10,258 in ten months against 100,034 two years prior, after Trump capped the program at 17,500 and issued an executive order prioritizing white South African Afrikaners.

## Key Points
Pew Research Center analysis of State Department data shows the US admitted 10,258 refugees in the first ten months of fiscal 2026, nearly all of them white South Africans, compared to 38,102 in fiscal 2025 and 100,034 the year before that under Biden. The Trump administration capped fiscal 2026 admissions at 17,500, the lowest cap in 25 years, well below the roughly 50,000 annual average the US maintained from 2001 to 2026.

The Afrikaner prioritization stems from a February 2025 executive order asserting that Black-led South African leadership has discriminated against Afrikaners, descendants of Dutch, German, and French colonial settlers who were the primary architects of apartheid, including through land seizure without compensation. South African leaders dispute that Afrikaners face systemic persecution. Historically, most US refugees have come from the Democratic Republic of Congo, Syria, Ukraine, Bhutan, Iraq, Afghanistan, Sudan, Venezuela, Eritrea, Myanmar, and Guatemala, populations largely locked out under the current cap and admissions pattern.
