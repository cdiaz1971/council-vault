# Comite de 100 Letter Shows Bipartisan Latino Demand for Work Permits

## Summary
More than 250 Latino leaders from MAGA to progressive signed a Comite de 100 letter demanding work permits and legal protections for long-term undocumented immigrants. Polling cited in the letter shows 63% of Latino voters rank treatment of immigrants as a top issue and 75% would favor a candidate backing work permits.

## Key Points
The Comite de 100 is a bipartisan coalition of 100 Mexican American and Latino business leaders. At a virtual press conference on September 29, 2026, more than 250 signers from across the political spectrum released a letter calling on President Trump and Congress to provide work permits and legal protections for long-term undocumented immigrants who have worked and paid taxes for years.

The letter cites two July killings by ICE officers: Lorenzo Salgado Araujo, a long-term undocumented father of three with no criminal record, in Houston, and Johan Sebastian Duran Guerrero, a young father with a legal work permit, in Biddeford, Maine.

Co-chair Massey Villarreal, a former chair of the Republican National Hispanic Assembly, argued that deportations are draining the workforce and that Republican Latino voters are turning against the mass deportation agenda.

## Relevance
This is evidence that the work-permit position is not a partisan fringe among Latino voters, including business-minded Republicans. Useful framing for City Values Act talking points and for coalition conversations that need to reach beyond progressive allies: the economic and workforce argument is coming from the right as well as the left.
