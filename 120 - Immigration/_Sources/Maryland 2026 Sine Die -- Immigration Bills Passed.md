# Maryland 2026 Sine Die -- Immigration Bills Passed

## Summary

On Sine Die (April 13, 2026), the final day of Maryland's 448th legislative session, the General Assembly passed two major immigration-related bills and sent them to Gov. Wes Moore. Both target the expansion of ICE enforcement under the Trump administration. These are the most significant state-level immigration protections passed in Maryland this session.

## Key Points

**Community Trust Act** passed the Senate 32-13 on the evening of April 13. The bill prohibits law enforcement from detaining or transferring individuals to ICE without a valid judicial warrant. It builds on emergency legislation Moore signed earlier in the session banning local sheriff departments from entering 287(g) agreements with ICE. Senate President Bill Ferguson cited local officials who were "outright discussing" cooperation with ICE as a reason to move the bill forward.

**Police Identification / Face Covering Bill** also received final approval on Sine Die. It mandates that officers wear identification while on duty and prohibits face coverings during duty, with exceptions for weather and personal health. A similar California law was recently overturned; Maryland Democrats crafted theirs as a civil offense rather than criminal to address those constitutional concerns.

Both chambers spent large portions of Sine Die day on immigration legislation, reflecting the urgency legislators felt about the pace of federal enforcement activity in Maryland communities.

## Relevance

Both bills directly inform Frederick's local [[City Values Act]] work. The Community Trust Act's judicial warrant standard is the state-level precedent that Frederick's own policy should align with or exceed. The face-covering bill is relevant if Frederick ever revisits police conduct policies.

The 32-13 Senate vote on the Community Trust Act is notable -- it passed but with significant opposition. Worth tracking which senators voted no as Frederick-area relationships are built.

Moore still needs to sign both bills. If signed, most new laws take effect October 1, 2026, which sets a natural milestone for local implementation alignment.
