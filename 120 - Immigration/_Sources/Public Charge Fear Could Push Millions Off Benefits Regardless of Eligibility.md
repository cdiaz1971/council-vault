# Public Charge Fear Could Push Millions Off Benefits Regardless of Eligibility

## Summary
Health researchers estimate the new public charge rule's chilling effect, not just direct ineligibility, could cause between 1.4 million and 4.1 million Medicaid and CHIP enrollees in mixed-status households to disenroll, including U.S. citizen children whose own eligibility is unaffected by their family members' status.

## Key Points
KFF's estimate covers people who remain legally eligible for benefits but disenroll anyway out of fear that a family member's use of the program will jeopardize an immigration case.

Prior research from the first Trump administration's public charge rule found families became less likely to apply for food assistance or seek healthcare even when eligible, a pattern the American Academy of Pediatrics says worsens childhood chronic disease risk and delays care for children with disabilities.

The Autism Self Advocacy Network specifically flagged immigrants with disabilities as disproportionately affected, since they rely on public benefits at higher rates than other immigrants.

## Relevance
This is the same chilling effect mechanism already documented for childcare and schools in [[Immigration Enforcement Fear Compounding Maryland Childcare Access Crisis]] and [[Immigration Enforcement Chilling Effect Compounds Across Schools Childcare and Legal Protections]], now extended specifically to Medicaid, CHIP, and SNAP. Strengthens the case that Frederick's own outreach and Municipal ID work needs to address benefits chilling effects directly, not just enforcement chilling effects, when the Affordability Beyond Housing thread resumes.
