# MPI Estimates 980,000 Deportations in First 18 Months of Second Trump Term

## Summary

An October 2026 MPI fact sheet, built on a new methodology to fill gaps left by slowed federal reporting, estimates about 980,000 ICE and CBP deportations from January 20, 2025 to July 31, 2026, many of long-term residents and most with no criminal conviction.

## Key Points

Retrieval note: the MPI page blocked direct fetching. The figures below come from MPI's published page excerpts and NewsNation's report on the release. The fact sheet's total-departures estimate (deportations plus "self-deportations") was not retrieved and should be pulled from the PDF before citing a self-departure number.

- **Total**: about 980,000 deportations by ICE and CBP between January 20, 2025 and July 31, 2026.
- **Composition**: roughly 580,000 formal removals and roughly 410,000 people granted voluntary departure or permitted to withdraw an application for admission.
- **Border share**: an estimated 500,000 were turned around at the border without entering or were deported within a year of arrival.
- **Residence and record**: the deportations include large numbers of people with two or more years of U.S. residence, and sizeable majorities had no criminal conviction. Among ICE deportations that began with a border arrest, 62 percent were of people who had been in the country more than a year.
- **Method**: MPI built a new methodology (described in the fact sheet appendix) because official federal reporting has slowed. It also uses public survey data to estimate self-departures. One co-author is a former DHS statistics official; MPI says none of the estimates draw on internal DHS records.
- **Context**: the rate exceeds both the Biden administration and Trump's first term, but the total remains below deportation levels of the late 1990s under Clinton. DHS has claimed more than 3 million combined deportations and self-departures. Border czar Tom Homan said validated deportation data will not be available until December at the earliest.

## Relevance

This is the most credible independent count available while federal data is withheld, and it separates enforcement fact from administration messaging: about 980,000 deportations, not 3 million departures. The finding that most people deported had no criminal conviction and many were long-term residents is the national evidence behind the City Values Act's premise that enforcement reaches settled families and neighbors, not only recent arrivals or people with records. Useful for the UMD advisory panels in October and December, for public remarks, and as a number to hold steady when DHS figures circulate.
