# Data Privacy Act -- Blocking Personal Data for Immigration Enforcement

## Summary
Maryland's Data Privacy Act would amend the Public Information Act to prohibit businesses from selling personal data of individuals "for the purpose of immigration enforcement." It is the companion bill to the Community Trust Act, and both remain unsigned by Gov. Moore as of May 19, 2026.

## Key Points
Sponsored by Del. Lorig Charkoudian (D-Montgomery) in the House and Sen. Clarence Lam in the Senate, the bill passed the Senate but not the House in its Senate form. The House version passed with over 100 witnesses testifying, the vast majority in support.

Opponents worried the bill could weaken the Public Information Act more broadly and would make state officials liable for determining whether a given records request was intended for immigration enforcement. These are legitimate implementation concerns that didn't ultimately block passage.

If signed, the Data Privacy Act takes effect July 1, 2026. It does not have the emergency designation of the Community Trust Act, so it cannot take effect immediately upon signing.

The two bills together, per We Are CASA, form a "one-two punch": the Community Trust Act addresses the law enforcement side of cooperation with ICE, while the Data Privacy Act addresses the data pipeline through which immigrant families can be targeted.

## Relevance
Frederick County's immigrant community in District 2 is directly affected by data-sharing practices that enable immigration enforcement targeting. Even if Frederick itself has no direct role in the Public Information Act, César can use this bill as a reference point for local data governance conversations -- particularly around what data the city collects, retains, and shares with federal agencies. The July 1 effective date makes this a near-term legislative milestone to track.
