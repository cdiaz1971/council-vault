# Trump Administration Appeals Dismissal of Vindictive Prosecution Charges Against Abrego Garcia

## Summary
The Trump administration appealed a federal judge's May dismissal of human smuggling charges against Kilmar Abrego Garcia, which the court had called vindictive prosecution retaliating against his successful deportation challenge.

## Key Points
US District Judge Waverly Crenshaw dismissed the two-count human smuggling indictment against Abrego Garcia in May, ruling the administration reopened a years-old 2022 Tennessee traffic stop investigation only after Abrego won his Supreme Court-backed challenge to his wrongful deportation to El Salvador. Crenshaw, an Obama appointee, wrote that absent Abrego's successful lawsuit, the government would not have brought the prosecution, and found the presumption of vindictiveness had not been rebutted, though he stopped short of finding actual vindictiveness.

The administration filed its opening appeal brief in the Sixth Circuit this week, arguing the vindictiveness presumption is improper because prosecutor Rob McGuire had no personal stake in Abrego's removal suit, and that Crenshaw's ruling represents an improper judicial intrusion into prosecutorial discretion. Crenshaw's opinion had focused scrutiny on Associate Deputy AG Akash Singh, who worked under now-Attorney General Todd Blanche and called charging Abrego a "top priority" in an April email. Abrego remains free while his separate immigration case continues in Maryland.
