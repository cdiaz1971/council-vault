# ICE Enforcement in Maryland and Frederick

Federal immigration enforcement is carried out by federal agencies. The City of Frederick doesn't direct it, and the state and City laws that limit local cooperation are covered on [[Maryland Community Trust Act]] and [[City Values Act]]. This page covers how enforcement has actually unfolded.

## Frederick incidents

- **Early response (2025).** By August 2025, the Frederick Rapid Response Network had a leadership team, tiered notification protocols, and canvassing capacity, and treated its response at Katherine Drexel as a positive case study ([[Immigration Policy Brief -- 2026-03-22]]).
- **Hillcrest (early 2026).** At the March 5, 2026 regular meeting, César made a public statement on an ICE action in Hillcrest about 200 yards from a school while children were walking to school. It entered the official record shortly before he introduced the City Values Act ([[Hearing Brief 2026-03-19]]).
- **Taskers Chance (September 3–4, 2026):**
  - **Thursday, Sept 3, about 6 a.m.** ICE vehicles arrived at Schaffer Drive and Stoneybrook Court. It followed a vehicle stop in which the man agents were seeking fled. Agents staked out a house for about 40 hours ([[ICE Raid Press Coverage Brief -- 2026-09-09]]; [[ICE Confrontation in Frederick's Tasker's Chance Neighborhood]]).
  - **Friday, Sept 4.** Roughly 100 residents and supporters gathered to observe, including members of Indivisible Frederick Forward. Some locked arms around the house.
  - **Friday night, Sept 4.** Frederick Police were called about 9 p.m. to de-escalate. They hadn't been told about the federal operation and didn't assist it. Around 9:30 p.m. agents drove vehicles onto sidewalks toward observers, pushed through the line, struck at least one person on the ground with a baton (on Hagerstown Rapid Response video), and dragged a demonstrator from a porch. Agents searched the property. **No arrests were made.**

  ([[ICE Raid Press Coverage Brief -- 2026-09-09]]; [[ICE Confrontation in Frederick's Tasker's Chance Neighborhood]])
- **Responses:**
  - **Mayor and Council President.** Mayor O'Connor and César issued a joint statement objecting to "federal enforcement tactics that unnecessarily intimidate residents or disrupt an entire neighborhood." It affirmed residents' right to observe and record, and it cited the City Values Act's inquiry clause ([[ICE Raid Press Coverage Brief -- 2026-09-09]]).
  - **César on record.** In at least six outlets, César described agents driving at observers, baton use, and reported threats: "we know where you live, we have your license plate" ([[ICE Raid Press Coverage Brief -- 2026-09-09]]).
  - **Council review.** NBC4 reported the Council would formally review the incident ([[ICE Raid Press Coverage Brief -- 2026-09-09]]).
  - **Governor Moore.** He said ICE's operation is now larger than the FBI, DEA, and ATF combined ([[ICE Confrontation in Frederick's Tasker's Chance Neighborhood]]).
  - **Rep. April McClain Delaney and Del. Kris Fair.** McClain Delaney demanded a transparent investigation, and Fair called on Congress to use its funding power over ICE. Protesters asked the Maryland Attorney General to investigate ([[ICE Confrontation in Frederick's Tasker's Chance Neighborhood]]).
  - **The warrantless-arrest lawsuit.** Its attorneys cited Taskers Chance as illustrating ICE's approach, but it isn't part of the case ([[Frederick Named in Statewide ICE Enforcement Pattern]]).

## Frederick County's 287(g) record and the Sheriff

- **The agreement.** Frederick County's 287(g) agreement ran from 2008 under Sheriff Chuck Jenkins. It used the Jail Enforcement Model, asking about immigration status at booking. It was described as the oldest in the U.S., and it ended when the state banned such agreements in February 2026 ([[Immigration Enforcement -- Frederick County WYPR]]; [[Maryland Community Trust Act]]). Jenkins claims about 2,000 referrals to ICE ([[Frederick County Immigration Enforcement -- Jenkins, Community Harm, and the Limits of Reform]]).
- **Jenkins's warning.** He said ending the agreement could increase direct ICE activity in the county ([[Immigration Enforcement -- Frederick County WYPR]]).
- **An accountability case: Sara Medrano.** A 2018 traffic stop led to immigration questioning and an attempted hold for ICE. The ACLU of Maryland's 2021 account of the settlement lists $25,000 to Medrano, a written apology, deputy training, public meetings on 287(g), public information on biased policing, and $100,000 in fees and costs. *That's the account of a party to the case, and the case ended in a settlement, not a verdict* ([[Frederick County Immigration Enforcement -- Jenkins, Community Harm, and the Limits of Reform]]).
- **After the ban.** Bolts' September 2026 reporting describes continuing fear, reluctance to seek police help, reliance on mutual aid, and more direct federal enforcement. It frames the November 2026 sheriff race as Jenkins, who won in 2022 by 2.8 points and defends cooperation, against Karl Bickel, who proposes monitoring ICE operations and publishing reports ([[Frederick County Immigration Enforcement -- Jenkins, Community Harm, and the Limits of Reform]]; [[Karl Bickel]]). At the March 19 joint City-County meeting, by contrast, Centro Hispano's Manuel Casiano said City police and the Sheriff's office had kept positive relationships with the immigrant community ([[Immigration Enforcement -- Frederick County WYPR]]).
- **Local impact reported March 19.** Immigrant Affairs Commission chair Claudia Hernandez reported a drop of more than 50% in people using services starting in June 2025, and heavy concentration of the community along the Route 40 Golden Mile. Centro Hispano reported demand rising sharply over the prior six to eight months ([[Immigration Enforcement -- Frederick County WYPR]]).

## Maryland arrest patterns and the warrantless-arrest lawsuit

- **The suit.** In September 2026, We Are CASA, the Amica Center for Immigrant Rights, the ACLU of Maryland, and the National Immigration Project filed a 56-page Fourth Amendment suit in U.S. District Court in Baltimore. It targets an "arrest first, ask questions later" practice of warrantless ICE arrests based on perceived status and names DHS, ICE, DOJ, and their leadership. The plaintiffs are:
  - **Samuel Guzman**, a 24-year-old U.S. citizen handcuffed outside a 7-Eleven despite showing ID
  - **K.M.**, pulled from her car near Salisbury by agents in six unmarked vehicles who threatened to shoot her, then released on an ankle monitor

  ([[Maryland ICE Warrantless Arrest Lawsuit]])
- **The numbers in the suit.** 80% of people ICE arrested in Maryland since 2025 had no criminal conviction. Of about 1,300 arrests in Operation Safe Community, a two-week August 2026 surge across Maryland and Virginia, fewer than a third had convictions. Of nearly 7,000 Maryland street arrests since January 2025, 93% were of citizens of Latin American or Caribbean countries ([[Maryland ICE Arrest Data Shows Pattern Beyond Criminal Enforcement]]). *Figures as cited by the plaintiffs.* An earlier note records 55% of Maryland ICE arrestees in June 2025 as having no criminal record ([[percent of Maryland deported]]). *No source recorded.*
- **Collateral arrests.** Deportation Data Project figures, analyzed by Stateline, show that about 64,000 of 253,000 ICE arrests from August 2025 to early March 2026 (~25%) were "collateral": based on appearance or proximity during sweeps, not a preexisting warrant. Fewer than 2% of those people had violent convictions, and 18% had any conviction, versus 33% for warrant-based arrests. **Maryland's collateral share was above 30%, among the highest in the country.** ICE formalized after-the-fact warrants in January 2026. Courts have pushed back, including an Illinois consent decree requiring reasonable suspicion and a DC injunction against warrantless arrests without probable cause ([[ICE Collateral Arrests -- Scale, Pattern, and Legal Challenges]]).
- **National detention.** TRAC counted 65,765 people in ICE detention on July 11, 2026, of whom 70.6% had no criminal conviction ([[Frederick County Immigration Enforcement -- Jenkins, Community Harm, and the Limits of Reform]]).

## Enforcement in everyday public places

- **Schools.** On June 11, 2026, agents followed a car into the lot of Commodore John Rodgers Elementary/Middle School in Baltimore and detained both parents in front of their daughters. A federal judge later found no probable cause and ordered the father released. Maryland and Baltimore laws keeping ICE out of schools didn't prevent it, because such laws mostly govern what local officials may do, not what federal agents do in a parking lot or on an adjacent street. At least 17 ICE encounters on school grounds have been tracked nationally since the 2025 reversal of the sensitive-locations policy ([[ICE Enforcement at Schools Shows Limits of State and Local Sensitive-Location Restrictions]]; [[Warrant-Based ICE Restrictions Create Legal Exposure Not Prevention]]).
- **Airports.** In March 2026 the administration began deploying ICE agents to a list of major airports, including JFK, LaGuardia, Newark, Atlanta, O'Hare, Philadelphia, Phoenix, and San Juan. NDLON organized "ICE Out" observer and know-your-rights actions ([[ICE Enforcement -- Expansion into US Airports]]).
- **Military ceremonies.** Federal agents announced immigration status inquiries at base entry for a Marine Corps graduation at Parris Island. DHS said no arrests would be made, but some parents skipped their children's graduations ([[ICE at Military Graduations -- Chilling Effect on Immigrant Families]]).

## After a car-based arrest

- **Ghost cars.** When ICE arrests someone from a vehicle, the car is often left at the scene, sometimes with a broken window, keys confiscated or thrown down, or in a tow-away zone. Maryland police departments say they often aren't notified. Volunteers counted 50+ abandoned cars in Prince George's and Montgomery counties since January 2026, and People Over Papers logged 8,500 car-based arrests nationally ([[Ghost Cars -- ICE Arrests Leave Families Scrambling for Abandoned Vehicles]]).
- **The cost.** Families face $200+ tow fees, $50/day storage, and one reported $925 bill for a work truck and trailer. Many tow lots release a car only to the titled owner, who is detained. Work tools inside can be lost, and at least one car was repossessed. Those costs compete with legal fees ([[Vehicle Seizure as Secondary ICE Harm -- Financial and Logistical Cascade]]).
- **Montgomery County's response.** It passed an ordinance letting tow lots release a detained owner's car to household members who present a lease, utility bill, or similar documentation ([[Ghost Cars -- ICE Arrests Leave Families Scrambling for Abandoned Vehicles]]; [[Vehicle Seizure as Secondary ICE Harm -- Financial and Logistical Cascade]]).

## Cases that became symbols

- **Kilmar Abrego Garcia.** He was a Maryland resident, detained in March 2025 despite a 2019 court order and deported to El Salvador's CECOT prison. After a Supreme Court ruling ordering his return, he was returned, charged, and came home over the 2025 holidays. At May Day 2026, his wife Jennifer Vasquez Sura credited the labor-immigrant coalition ([[Abrego Garcia -- One Year Later Movement Reflection and May Day 2026]]). In May 2026, Judge Waverly Crenshaw dismissed the human-smuggling indictment against him. He found a presumption of vindictive prosecution tied to Abrego's successful challenge to his deportation, though he made no finding of actual vindictiveness. The administration appealed to the Sixth Circuit in August. Abrego remains free while his immigration case continues in Maryland ([[Trump Administration Appeals Dismissal of Vindictive Prosecution Charges Against Abrego Garcia]]).
- **Rodney Taylor.** A Liberian-born barber and double amputee who came to the U.S. at age 2, he has been held since January 15, 2025, despite a work permit, a green card in process, and a 2010 Georgia pardon for his only charge. Twenty-one members of Congress documented denied medical care. His wife pointed to Georgia's growth from 5 to 40+ 287(g) jurisdictions, in contrast to Maryland's ban ([[Rodney Taylor -- Double Amputee Barber Detained by ICE Since January 2025]]). *This note and the Abrego Garcia May Day note report the same rally.*
