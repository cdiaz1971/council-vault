# Portland Advances $150K in City Funds for Immigrant Legal Services

## Summary

Portland City Council advanced $150,000 in city funds to cover legal services for roughly 75 immigrants and refugees through SOAR Immigration Legal Services, recommended by the city's New Portlanders Policy Commission. The action came in response to intensified federal immigration enforcement in the region.

## Key Points

Portland City Council advanced the proposal on February 27, 2026, introduced by Councilor Elana Pirtle-Guiney. The funds would come from the city's Legal Priorities Reserve Fund -- a pot of approximately $850,000 set aside for unexpected legal expenses -- and flow to SOAR Immigration Legal Services, a nonprofit providing legal assistance to refugees and immigrants seeking asylum, citizenship, or other protections.

Pirtle-Guiney framed it directly: having sanctuary city principles means acting in a time of crisis, not only stating values. Portland has the largest immigrant and refugee population in Oregon, with nearly 14 percent of residents being immigrants. The policy echoes a similar agreement the city adopted in 2018 during Trump's first term.

The governance structure behind the proposal is worth noting. The New Portlanders Policy Commission -- a standing volunteer advisory board that advises the city on how policies impact immigrant and refugee communities -- recommended SOAR as the recipient. Pirtle-Guiney asked them specifically what practical, low-cost steps the council could take. The commission's recommendation then became the council action.

The vote was not without internal tension. Councilor Candace Avalos wanted more time to consult with additional immigrant rights organizations and stakeholders in the rapid response network, arguing that the council should be creating resources for that broader work, not channeling funds through a single nonprofit. Councilor Angelita Morillo, the only immigrant on council, raised concerns about prioritizing one nonprofit over others. Councilor Eric Zimmerman proposed advancing it as an emergency ordinance for immediate effect, but that required nine votes and fell short.

## Relevance

The New Portlanders Policy Commission model is directly replicable in Frederick. District 2 has the city's highest concentration of immigrants, and the council currently has no standing advisory body with a specific mandate to advise on how city policies affect that population.
