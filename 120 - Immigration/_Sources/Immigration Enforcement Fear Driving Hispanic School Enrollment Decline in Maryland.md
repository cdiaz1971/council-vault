# Immigration Enforcement Fear Driving Hispanic School Enrollment Decline in Maryland

## Summary
Maryland's Hispanic K-12 enrollment fell for the first time in years after growing 4.7% annually since pandemic lows, a decline concentrated in Montgomery and Prince George's counties and paired with a steeper graduation rate drop for Hispanic students. It is the first hard statewide data documenting the chilling effect of stepped-up immigration enforcement on school participation.

## Key Points
Immigration arrests in Maryland nearly tripled between the last year of the Biden administration and the first year of Trump's second term, from around 1,300 to almost 4,700, after the administration rescinded the policy directing agents to avoid "sensitive locations" like schools. Of Maryland schools with at least 500 Hispanic students, 12 of the 20 sharpest enrollment declines last school year happened in Montgomery County, with drops of 6 to 11 percent at individual schools and an average decline of more than 3 percent across the county's public schools overall. The Hispanic graduation rate fell 4.4 percent between the 2023-24 and 2024-25 school years, and 5.5 percent for English learners specifically, against a 1.2 percent decline overall. School international enrollment offices report new-student intake down to what one education director called unprecedented levels, particularly in Montgomery and Prince George's counties, which together enroll more than half the state's roughly 210,000 Hispanic students.
