# Maryland ICE Arrest Data Shows Pattern Beyond Criminal Enforcement

## Summary
Statistics cited in the We Are CASA lawsuit show the large majority of people ICE has arrested in Maryland since 2025 had no criminal record and were overwhelmingly Latin American or Caribbean, evidence advocates call racial profiling rather than targeted criminal enforcement.

## Key Points
Eighty percent of people arrested by ICE in Maryland since 2025 had no criminal convictions, per the lawsuit. Of roughly 1,300 arrests made during Operation Safe Community, a two-week ICE enforcement surge across Maryland and Virginia last month, less than a third had criminal convictions.

Of nearly 7,000 street arrests in Maryland since President Trump's second inauguration, 93 percent of those arrested were citizens of Latin American or Caribbean countries. We Are CASA legal director Shana Kader described the pattern as agents "stalking primarily brown and Black neighborhoods" without warrants or probable cause, citing incidents from the Eastern Shore to Park Heights to Washington County.

## Relevance
Gives a documented, citable statistical basis for the racial profiling concern that has come up repeatedly in Frederick's own immigration enforcement tracking, useful alongside [[ICE Enforcement -- Expansion into US Airports]] and [[ICE Enforcement at Schools Shows Limits of State and Local Sensitive-Location Restrictions]] as evidence that enforcement in Maryland is not narrowly targeted at people with criminal records.
