# H.R. 1 Ends Medicaid for Refugees and Asylees in Maryland

## Summary

As of October 1, 2026, H.R. 1 removed Medicaid eligibility for refugees, asylees, humanitarian parolees, and trafficking victims, cutting off up to 4,500 Maryland residents. ACA marketplace subsidies for the same groups end in January 2027, and a parallel SNAP cost shift adds about $57 million to Maryland's bill.

## Key Points

The provision took effect October 1. Green card holders, Cuban and Haitian entrants, and migrants from the Compact of Free Association nations keep Medicaid eligibility. Refugees, asylees, humanitarian parolees, and trafficking victims lose it. These are groups the federal government itself admitted on humanitarian grounds.

The state estimates up to 4,500 Marylanders are affected, many in active treatment for cancer, diabetes, and other chronic conditions. Because they qualified for Medicaid, most cannot afford private coverage. The temporary fallback, subsidized coverage on Maryland's ACA marketplace, closes for the same populations after January 2027. State officials are pointing people to community clinics with sliding-fee scales, which CASA notes can handle preventive care but not comprehensive treatment for serious illness. The realistic outcome for many is going uninsured until an emergency room visit.

The same date shifted SNAP administrative costs from a 50-50 federal-state split to 75 percent state, 25 percent federal. Maryland now pays roughly $57 million more on top of the $115 million it already spends, with up to another $240 million in added cost possible in 2027. That squeeze lands on the state budget that funds the programs immigrant families might otherwise turn to.

## Relevance

Frederick's refugee and asylee families are among the people losing coverage, and the immigrant-serving organizations César works with (Centro Hispano, SSCM, AACF, CASA) will be fielding the fallout. This is a concrete local harm worth raising with HHS Department Head Stuart Campbell: does the City's Housing and Human Services referral network know where to send someone mid-treatment who just lost Medicaid, and is there a current list of local sliding-fee providers?

It also compounds the chilling effect already documented in the vault. Families who lose coverage and fear enforcement are less likely to seek care at all, which connects to the pediatric care survey cited for the UMD lecture.

The SNAP cost shift belongs with the food access work. State budget pressure from H.R. 1 is a reason to expect less state support for local safety-net programs over the next two years, not more.
