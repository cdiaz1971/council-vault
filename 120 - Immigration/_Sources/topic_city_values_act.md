# TOPIC: City Values Act (Ordinance G-26-___)
**Immigration Enforcement Guidance — Frederick City Code, Chapter 33, Article II**
**Status:** Introduced. Work session held March 12, 2026. No vote scheduled.
**Introduced by:** Council President César Díaz.

---

## What It Does

The ordinance makes immigration enforcement guidance permanent, enforceable City law by adding Chapter 33, Article II to the City Code. Three core prohibitions:

- City employees cannot assist ICE in accessing non-public City facilities without a signed judicial warrant or exigent circumstances.
- City property cannot be used for ICE enforcement activities — surveillance, staging, processing, detention, or vehicle storage — without a judicial warrant or as required by law.
- City employees cannot inquire about immigration status, citizenship, country of origin, place of birth, or nationality except when legally required to do so. Applies to benefit eligibility determinations and all other City purposes.

---

## Why It Matters

The stated rationale is service integrity. City services — public health, housing code, libraries, recreation, emergency response — depend on community trust. When residents fear that accessing City services may expose them to immigration enforcement, service delivery breaks down for everyone. The ordinance frames this as converting the City's stated values of equity, inclusiveness, dignity, and justice from aspirational language into operative legal standards.

---

## Scope and Limits

**Covers:** All City employees in all departments; all City-owned, leased, or managed property; all City facilities including non-public areas; City databases, records, and equipment; immigration status inquiries in any context.

**Does not cover:** Frederick Police Department criminal law enforcement activities. Law enforcement retains the ability to share immigration status information as required by federal or constitutional law (§33-9). Employee violations are subject to internal discipline only — no civil or criminal penalties. Exigent circumstances remain a recognized exception for facility access. Applies to civil immigration enforcement only, not criminal warrant activity.

---

## Implementation Framework

**Legal Department:** Issues guidance to all City employees on responding to ICE requests, warrants, and facility access — including the distinction between judicial warrants and civil detainers.

**Mayor:** Ensures each department director designates a supervisor ICE liaison, identifies and posts signage at non-public area boundaries, and establishes department-specific ICE interaction protocols. May also issue implementing regulations.

**Department Directors:** Designate supervisors, mark non-public areas, train staff, and establish procedures. Supervisors serve as point-of-contact for ICE interactions and receive reports of unauthorized use of City property.

**Council oversight:** Monitors to ensure any mayoral regulatory guidance is consistent with legislative intent.

## Risk Notes

**FPD carve-out will draw questions.** Have a brief public answer ready before content goes out: the ordinance addresses civil immigration enforcement by City civilian employees; FPD operates under separate legal authorities governing criminal law enforcement.

**"Sanctuary city" framing.** The ordinance does not use that language. If pressed, the accurate framing is that it codifies how City employees interact with federal civil immigration enforcement — it does not bar cooperation with criminal law enforcement or federal agencies acting under judicial warrants.
