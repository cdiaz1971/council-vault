# Immigration Enforcement -- Frederick County WYPR

## Summary

A joint meeting of the Frederick County and City councils heard directly from local nonprofits on March 19, 2026 about the documented impact of federal immigration enforcement on Frederick's immigrant community. Service access dropped by more than 50% starting in June 2025. Simultaneously, Frederick County's 18-year 287(g) agreement with ICE -- the oldest in the US -- was terminated in February 2026 when Governor Wes Moore signed legislation banning such partnerships statewide.

## Key Points

Claudia Hernandez, Chair of Frederick County's Immigrant Affairs Commission, reported a more than 50% drop in people accessing services beginning in June 2025, aligned with the timing of federal executive actions and increased enforcement activity. She described a community that has become hypervigilant, with many choosing to self-isolate. While numbers have slowly begun to rise recently, she noted outreach requires intentional reassurance and trust-building.

Manuel Casiano, board member of Centro Hispano de Frederick, reported the opposite dynamic for his organization: a dramatic increase in demand for services over the last six to eight months. He noted that Frederick City police and the County Sheriff's office have maintained positive relationships with the immigrant community, contrasting their approach with ICE's. He called for greater focus on emotional health support for community members.

Claudia Hernandez flagged that much of the immigrant community is concentrated along Route 40 -- the Golden Mile -- where more than 56% of clients walking into their office are Hispanic.

On the 287(g) front: Frederick's partnership with ICE dated to 2008 under Sheriff Chuck Jenkins, using the Jail Enforcement Model (asking about immigration status at booking, not in the field). Governor Moore signed a ban effective immediately in February 2026. Jenkins warned this could increase direct ICE activity in Frederick County rather than reduce it. Maryland is simultaneously considering legislation restricting private immigration detention facilities and requiring minimum conditions at any detention facility operating in the state.

## Why This Matters for César

This is primary documentation of the immigration enforcement situation in Frederick from a joint council meeting. The 50% service access drop is a measurable local impact that can anchor policy conversations about City Values Act protections, municipal ID, and city-level support for affected residents. The Golden Mile concentration connects to César's existing community organization work with Golden Mile Alliance and Centro Hispano. The 287(g) termination is a major local policy change that shifts the enforcement landscape but does not resolve the underlying fear in the community.
