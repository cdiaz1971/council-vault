# New Birthright Citizenship Orders Face a Venue Question

## Summary
After the Supreme Court affirmed birthright citizenship, Trump issued two narrower orders; a Maryland federal judge has already enjoined them, while a New Hampshire judge signaled the ACLU needs a new lawsuit rather than extending the old injunction.

## Key Points
The August executive orders would deny citizenship to children of parents designated as part of a terrorist organization, children of parents who came to the US to obtain citizenship ("birth tourism"), children of foreign diplomatic staff, and children born in US territories without a congressional citizenship statute, which in practice means American Samoa.

In Maryland, Judge Deborah Boardman, who handled the original 2025 challenge, issued a preliminary injunction in September barring the new orders. In New Hampshire, Judge Joseph Laplante told the ACLU he considers the Barbara case over and that the new orders cover a different class, so relief likely requires a new suit. He also said he was uneasy about the appearance of the same judge handling closely related follow-on claims. The ACLU's position is that the new orders are a continuation of the same effort the Supreme Court already rejected.

## Relevance
Maryland is currently the jurisdiction providing protection, which matters for Frederick families. The pattern to watch is the administration responding to a loss with narrower orders that force new litigation, which buys time and creates uncertainty even when the orders are unlikely to survive. Useful context for community messaging through Centro Hispano and SSCM: the orders are blocked in Maryland right now, and the Supreme Court ruling on the original order stands.
