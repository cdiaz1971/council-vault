# Frederick Named in Statewide ICE Enforcement Pattern

## Summary
The statewide ICE lawsuit cites a recent Frederick neighborhood confrontation, in which agents staked out a block for 40 hours and were filmed pushing and striking protesters, as an example of the broader enforcement pattern the suit challenges, though attorneys say the Frederick incident is not itself part of the litigation.

## Key Points
Amica Center managing attorney Austin Rose, who was born and raised in Frederick, said ICE agents "staked out a neighborhood for 40 hours, apparently looking for someone," and that neighbors intervened when agents began detaining people.

Video circulated by Hagerstown Rapid Response shows an agent striking a person on the ground with a baton during the confrontation.

Rose was explicit that the federal lawsuit is not legally connected to the Frederick incident, but cited it as illustrative of ICE's broader approach of "creating problems for themselves by just randomly showing up in neighborhoods."

The incident is referenced only in passing in this article. Full detail, including the timeline and the range of officials calling for an investigation, is in [[ICE Confrontation in Frederick's Tasker's Chance Neighborhood]].
