# Trump Administration Policies Likely to Reduce Unauthorized Population From 2026 Peak

## Summary

MPI's projection is that the unauthorized population will begin declining from the 2024 peak of 15.8 million as the second Trump administration's enforcement actions compound: deportation campaign, revocation of Biden humanitarian parole programs, repurposing of the CBP One app as a self-deportation tool, termination of Temporary Protected Status for hundreds of thousands, and near-zero border entry levels.

## Key Points

The key enforcement mechanisms now in motion: the CBP One app, previously used for asylum appointment scheduling, has been converted into a voluntary departure tool. Humanitarian parole granted to Afghans, Cubans, Haitians, Nicaraguans, Ukrainians, and Venezuelans has been revoked or is in the process of being revoked. TPS terminations are underway for multiple nationalities. Border entries are at historically low levels.

MPI frames the projection conditionally: "should the administration succeed in its efforts to sizably ramp up deportations and if border entries remain at their remarkably low levels." Both conditions are uncertain -- deportation logistics are resource-constrained, and legal challenges to TPS and parole revocations are ongoing.

The population that arrived under Biden humanitarian programs -- people who believed they had legal status, obtained work permits, established roots -- now faces retroactive enforcement. This is a distinct legal and human situation from long-term undocumented residents.

## Relevance

This is the enforcement context César's community protection work operates inside. The people most at risk in Frederick are not the long-established undocumented community but those whose legal status has been revoked mid-residence -- a category that didn't exist at this scale before 2025. The City Values Act's non-cooperation provisions and the rapid response infrastructure are specifically protective of this population. The conditional nature of MPI's projection also matters for advocacy framing: the administration's enforcement campaign is not a fait accompli, and legal challenges are live.
