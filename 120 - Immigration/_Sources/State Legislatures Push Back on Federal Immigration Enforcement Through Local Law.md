# State Legislatures Push Back on Federal Immigration Enforcement Through Local Law

## Summary
Democratic state lawmakers introduced roughly 242 immigration enforcement pushback bills across 32 states in 2026, with 53 passed in 17 states, covering anti-masking rules for officers, protections in sensitive locations, and detention facility zoning.

## Key Points
State Futures, a nonprofit coordinating Democratic state lawmakers, tracked 242 immigration related bills introduced across 32 states in 2026, with about a fifth passing so far. Most respond directly to the ramped up federal enforcement of Trump's second term. Common approaches include banning immigration officers from masking, protecting sensitive locations like schools and hospitals from enforcement action, and applying zoning regulation to private detention facilities. Some Democratic governors have vetoed similar bills despite supporting the goal, citing concerns about litigation risk and putting local officials in the position of choosing between state and federal law. Oregon signed eight such laws in April. Rhode Island signed a law letting people sue over arrests made without a judicial order or warrant.

## Relevance
Useful national context for where Frederick's own sanctuary infrastructure sits relative to other jurisdictions. The City Values Act and Community Trust Act both fit within this broader wave of state and local pushback legislation.
