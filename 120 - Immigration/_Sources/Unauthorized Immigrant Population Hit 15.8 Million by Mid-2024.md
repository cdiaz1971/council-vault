# Unauthorized Immigrant Population Hit 15.8 Million by Mid-2024

## Summary

MPI's analysis of Census ACS data puts the unauthorized immigrant population at 15.8 million by mid-2024, the highest on record, up from roughly 11 million where it had held since 2010. The increase reflects a combination of Biden-era humanitarian parole programs for six national groups, strong post-COVID economic pull, and large-scale displacement from gang violence, political repression, and economic collapse across the Americas.

## Key Points

The 5 million net increase between 2019 and 2024 represents an average growth rate of 8 percent per year -- far faster than the prior decade of stability. The biggest single-year surge was 2022 to 2023, adding 1.6 million.

The national origin composition has shifted significantly. Mexico, which represented 62 percent of the unauthorized population in 2010, now accounts for 35 percent. Ecuadorians, Colombians, Venezuelans, Guatemalans, and Hondurans have driven the change. This is not the same population as prior immigration waves -- it skews more toward South and Central America, includes significant numbers from political crisis contexts, and arrived under different legal frameworks.

Biden's CBP One app, which enabled appointment-based asylum entry, contributed to the growth. The programs creating the 2024 peak -- humanitarian parole for Afghans, Cubans, Haitians, Nicaraguans, Ukrainians, Venezuelans, and parole via CBP One -- covered roughly 1.4 million people in total.

## Relevance

This is baseline data for immigration context arguments. When Frederick's community protection work is challenged on grounds that the problem is overstated or that the local immigrant population is small, this report provides the national scale. More directly: the shift in national origin composition means the Frederick community César works with through Centro Hispano, SSCM, and AACF likely includes people who arrived under Biden-era humanitarian programs now being terminated -- making the City Values Act's protections specifically relevant to people with recently-revoked or precarious legal status, not just long-term undocumented residents.
