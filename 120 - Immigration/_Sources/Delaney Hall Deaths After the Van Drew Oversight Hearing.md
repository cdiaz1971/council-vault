# Delaney Hall Deaths After the Van Drew Oversight Hearing

## Summary
Two weeks after a July 2026 House oversight hearing where Rep. Jeff Van Drew dismissed reports of medical neglect at Delaney Hall, two more detainees died. The ACLU-NJ uses the deaths to press for enforceable standards of care, unannounced inspections, and the Dignity for Detained Immigrants Act.

## Key Points
- **Hearing, July 2026.** House Judiciary Subcommittee on Oversight chair Jeff Van Drew and his witnesses called reports of medical neglect, inedible food, unsanitary conditions, and abuse at Delaney Hall (GEO-run ICE facility, Newark NJ) exaggerated or fabricated. He questioned the legitimacy of a weeks-long hunger strike and said detainees "get 24-hour emergency care."
- Amol Sinha testified that detainees were denied life-sustaining medication, left without wheelchairs, lost dramatic weight, and had serious conditions ignored. Named Jean Wilson Brutus, 41, Haitian, who died one day after arriving at Delaney Hall in December 2025.
- **Edwin Jovanny Lopez Cornejo**, 39, Plainfield NJ resident 20+ years, construction worker, father of a 12-year-old U.S. citizen. Managed diabetes, hypertension, and epilepsy with medication; family doubts he received it during six weeks at Delaney Hall. Getting Tylenol took paperwork and days. Rep. Rob Menendez reported nurse practitioners handled his emergency because the facility's only physician was neither present nor on call. Pronounced dead at University Hospital August 1, 2026.
- **Jose Chajon-Raxon** had a seizure around the time of the hearing, was granted temporary medical release, and died afterward. Because he was technically released, his death is not counted as an in-custody death. This is a counting gap worth remembering when citing official totals.
- Other 2026 deaths cited: Jesus Manuel Arenas Silva, 45 (Georgia, July, family says officers kept him from bringing medications); Prisciliano Trejo, 29 (acute leukemia, treatment allegedly delayed).
- **Scale:** nearly 60 deaths in ICE custody since January 2025. The custody death rate has more than doubled in the second Trump term and is the highest in over a decade.
- **Asks:** enforceable standards of care and accountability; bipartisan, unannounced, uncensored congressional inspections; direct conversations with current and former detainees; compelled testimony from ICE and private prison executives; passage of the Dignity for Detained Immigrants Act (H.R. 6397, 119th Congress), which would phase out for-profit detention.

## Relevance
Extends the Delaney Hall thread already in the vault from organizing and hunger strikes to a documented death toll. The medical-release loophole (deaths after release are not counted) is a useful talking point when official statistics are cited against advocates.
