## Summary

NDLON's Director of Organizing Jorge Torres sent this update marking approximately two weeks of sustained resistance at Delaney Hall, a detention facility in New Jersey. Detained immigrants organized hunger strikes, labor strikes, and public letters that drew widespread community, faith, and media attention. NDLON announced the launch of a new hub site -- ICE Out of New Jersey (iceoutofnj.com) -- as a centralized resource for updates, actions, and ways to engage with the ongoing campaign.

## Key Points

- Detained individuals at Delaney Hall conducted hunger strikes, labor strikes, and issued public letters over approximately two weeks, exposing conditions inside the facility
- Community mobilization was broad: families, faith leaders, advocates, and grassroots journalists sustained public attention throughout
- NDLON launched the **ICE Out of New Jersey** website as a coordinating hub for the ongoing campaign
- Coalition organizations involved include: AFSC New Jersey Immigrant Rights Program, CATA, Cosecha NJ, DIRE, El Pueblo Unido, New Labor, NDLON, Semilla Roja NJ, Estamos Unidos NJ, Resistencia en Accion NJ
- NDLON is encouraging continued support for grassroots media outlets Radio Cosecha and Radio Jornalera NJ, which have been documenting the struggle from the beginning
- The email closes with "Porque solo el pueblo salva al pueblo" -- framing mutual aid and collective power as the organizing principle

## Why This Matters for Cesar

This connects directly to the [[City Values Act]] and Frederick's [[Municipal ID program]] threads. The Delaney Hall campaign is a model of what sustained community organizing looks like in the face of expanding federal immigration enforcement. NDLON is a key national partner, and their escalation strategy -- detainee-led, faith and media amplified, coalition-built -- may be instructive for Frederick's rapid response infrastructure conversations with [[Frederick Rapid Response Network]] and [[Centro Hispano de Frederick]].

The ICE Out of NJ coalition structure is also worth tracking as a model for how municipalities and community organizations coordinate when federal enforcement pressure escalates.
