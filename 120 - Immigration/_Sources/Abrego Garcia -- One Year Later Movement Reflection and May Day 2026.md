# Abrego Garcia -- One Year Later, Movement Reflection and May Day 2026

## Summary

One year after Kilmar Abrego Garcia was wrongfully deported to El Salvador's CECOT prison despite a standing judicial order blocking his removal, his wife Jennifer Vasquez Sura spoke at Washington DC's May Day rally alongside We Are CASA and labor groups. She credited the immigrant-labor movement with his eventual return and called on supporters to keep fighting for families still separated.

## Key Points

Abrego Garcia's case traced the full arc of Trump-era immigration enforcement gone wrong: detained in March 2025 in violation of a 2019 court order, swiftly deported to CECOT, the subject of a Supreme Court ruling ordering his return, then detained again on new charges before finally returning home over the 2025 holidays. His case became a national symbol of ICE overreach and the vulnerability of long-term residents with prior legal proceedings.

Vasquez Sura's remarks at May Day 2026 explicitly framed his return as a movement victory, not just a legal one. The coalition of labor and immigrant rights groups that showed up repeatedly over the course of a year was, in her framing, what made the difference alongside the courts.

Also speaking: Mildred Danis-Taylor, wife of Rodney Taylor, a double-amputee barber from Georgia detained by ICE since January 2025 despite a 2010 pardon and an in-process green card. Taylor's case illustrates that ICE is still using decades-old criminal records that have been formally expunged or pardoned as pretexts for detention.

Danis-Taylor called for elimination of 287(g) agreements, noting Georgia has grown from 5 jurisdictions in 2024 to more than 40. She said Maryland's ban made her "jealous." CASA Executive Director George Escobar framed the next phase as redirecting federal military spending toward healthcare, housing, and living wages.

## Relevance

The Abrego Garcia case and Maryland's 287(g) ban are directly connected to Frederick's City Values Act work and the local immigrant protection ecosystem. The contrast between Maryland's legislative response and Georgia's 287(g) expansion is a useful frame for why local and state sanctuary-adjacent policy matters concretely. The "movement made this possible" framing is also relevant for how César talks about coalition work publicly.
