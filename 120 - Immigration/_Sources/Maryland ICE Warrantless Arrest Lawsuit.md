# Maryland ICE Warrantless Arrest Lawsuit

## Summary
We Are CASA, the Amica Center for Immigrant Rights, the ACLU of Maryland, and the National Immigration Project sued the Trump administration over an "arrest first, ask questions later" policy of warrantless ICE arrests that sweep up people based on perceived immigration status alone.

## Key Points
The 56-page suit, filed in U.S. District Court in Baltimore, alleges Fourth Amendment violations by ICE agents conducting arrests without warrants or individualized cause.

Named plaintiff Samuel Guzman, a 24-year-old U.S. citizen and Maryland native, was handcuffed and detained by ICE outside a 7-Eleven in June despite presenting identification and stating his citizenship, then released without explanation after a second document review. He still takes the same route to work and fears re-arrest.

A second plaintiff, identified only as K.M., was pulled from her car near Salisbury by agents in six unmarked vehicles who threatened to shoot her, then held and interrogated for hours before release on an ankle monitor.

Defendants include the Department of Homeland Security, ICE, and the Department of Justice, along with their current leadership. The suit follows a separate incident, captured on video, in which ICE agents staked out a Frederick neighborhood for 40 hours and were seen pushing and striking protesters who intervened.
