# Palantir ELITE -- ICE Builds a 20 Million Person Target List

## Summary

At the Border Security Expo in Phoenix in May 2026, Matthew Elliston, ICE's assistant director of Law Enforcement Systems and Analysis, described how Palantir's technology has transformed the agency's enforcement capacity. ICE agents now carry a list of 20 million potential targets on their iPhones. The system aggregates 30-40 disparate datasets into a unified query tool, including data from HHS and Thomson Reuters' CLEAR product. The arrest location success rate has reportedly increased from around 27 percent to nearly 80 percent. As of April 2026, 70.8 percent of people held in ICE detention -- over 42,000 people -- have no criminal conviction.

## Key Points

**The ELITE system.** Palantir's Enhanced Leads Identification and Targeting for Enforcement tool populates a map with potential deportation targets, generates a dossier on each person including personal information, and provides a confidence score on current address. It was revealed by 404 Media in January 2026 and has since become a "more mature partner" relationship between Palantir and ICE.

**Scale and speed.** ICE officials claimed investigative work that previously took hours now takes 10-15 minutes. Agents can identify a target's address, then check whether lower-priority targets live nearby and arrest them opportunistically. This is not targeted enforcement -- it is dragnet operations enabled by data aggregation.

**Mobile Fortify.** Elliston also described Mobile Fortify, ICE and CBP's facial recognition app, which has reportedly been used 200,000 times. 404 Media previously documented a case where Mobile Fortify misidentified a woman twice. Elliston claimed a 0 percent mismatch rate.

**The data sources.** Palantir does not generate its own datasets -- it aggregates disparate government and commercial data. The inclusion of HHS data is particularly significant: people who sought healthcare, applied for benefits, or interacted with social services may now be in the target pool. This is a direct chilling effect on healthcare access and social service utilization.

**Who is detained.** April 2026 data shows that 70.8 percent of ICE detainees have no criminal conviction. The rhetoric of targeting "criminals" is not matched by the reality of who is being arrested.

## Relevance

This is the most concrete piece of infrastructure documentation to date for the threat environment Frederick's immigrant community faces. The City Values Act, Municipal ID program, and Frederick Rapid Response Network exist precisely because this kind of surveillance-enabled dragnet enforcement is the operating reality.

Key implications for Frederick's work:
- The chilling effect on healthcare, schools, and social services is not hypothetical -- it is driven by the documented reality that HHS data feeds ICE targeting.
- Municipal ID cannot provide data to federal enforcement. Any city ID program must be designed with this in mind.
- Community education on what data ICE can and cannot access is a public safety issue.
- The rapid response infrastructure must account for the speed of enforcement operations that now take 10-15 minutes rather than hours.
