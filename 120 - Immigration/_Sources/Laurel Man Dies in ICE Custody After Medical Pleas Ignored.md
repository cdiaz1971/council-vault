# Laurel Man Dies in ICE Custody After Medical Pleas Ignored

## Summary
Julián Curo-Churata, a 47-year-old Laurel father who lived in Maryland for 17 years, died September 29 after his transfer out of Richwood Correctional Center in Louisiana, and his family's attorney says guards ignored his urgent requests for medical care.

## Key Points
Curo-Churata died September 29, 2026 at Ochsner LSU Health Monroe Medical Center after being transferred from Richwood Correctional Center in Monroe, Louisiana. ICE says he was found in "medical distress" the night before and received proper care. Cause of death is undetermined pending autopsy.

Attorney Max Schoening of Qureshi Law, representing his wife Verónica Ramírez and their 6-year-old daughter, says he is investigating "credible information that guards at Richwood Correctional Center ignored urgent requests for medical attention." ICE did not respond to the claim. Sen. Chris Van Hollen called for a full independent investigation.

Richwood has a documented record. In April 2026 a detained Guatemalan-born woman reported pregnancy complications and later delivered a stillborn fetus at about 23 weeks, with records obtained by The Intercept showing a lack of basic prenatal care. In October 2025 a federal jury awarded more than $40 million to the family of Erie Moore Sr., who died in 2015 after Richwood guards body-slammed him and fractured his skull. Schoening represented that family too.

Two GoFundMe campaigns support the family (funeral costs, and his son Luis Curo's architecture degree). A vigil was held at the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints in Laurel.

## Relevance
A Maryland resident of 17 years transferred more than a thousand miles to a private Louisiana facility with a known neglect record is the detention pipeline made concrete. Arrests in Maryland feed out-of-state detention where families, lawyers, and Maryland officials have little reach. That is a useful framing for why local non-cooperation and rapid-response legal support matter even when a city cannot stop an arrest, and it is a concrete case for the UMD advisory panel and any public statement on detention conditions.
