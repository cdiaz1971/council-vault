# Frederick County Sheriff Joins Lawsuit Challenging Community Trust Act

## Summary

Seventeen Maryland county sheriffs filed a federal civil suit (Gahler v. Moore, No. 8:26-cv-02057) in U.S. District Court in Greenbelt to block the Community Trust Act from taking effect. The lead plaintiff is Harford County Sheriff Jeffrey Gahler. Frederick County is among the 17 plaintiff counties. The Federation for American Immigration Reform (FAIR) filed the suit on behalf of the sheriffs, naming the State of Maryland, Governor Moore, and Attorney General Brown as defendants.

## Key Points

The suit was filed on May 26, 2026 -- just days after Governor Moore declined to sign the CTA while allowing it to become law. The sheriffs claim the law "intentionally obstructs federal law enforcement" and would prevent them from fulfilling their obligations to uphold the Constitution. The suit asserts the CTA amounts to "blatant defiance of federal immigration law" that "jeopardizes the public safety of all Americans."

The 17 plaintiff counties are: Allegany, Calvert, Caroline, Carroll, Cecil, Dorchester, Frederick, Garrett, Harford, Kent, Queen Anne's, St. Mary's, Somerset, Talbot, Washington, Wicomico, and Worcester.

The suit comes despite the fact that eight of nine Maryland counties with 287(g) agreements quickly dropped those agreements after the earlier emergency bill banning them. Some sheriffs indicated they planned to continue informal ICE cooperation even without formal agreements, which was one of the drivers for the Community Trust Act's passage.

## Relevance

Frederick County Sheriff is a plaintiff in this suit, which means that the question of local law enforcement cooperation with ICE in Frederick -- a thread that runs directly through the CVA and the IAC -- is now also a federal litigation question. The Frederick County Sheriff's position is in direct tension with the City of Frederick's City Values Act.

As Council President, this creates two distinct tracks to monitor: the legal track (does the CTA survive a preliminary injunction challenge?) and the political track (what does the Sheriff's participation signal about county-level immigration enforcement posture?). The Sheriff's office is a county, not city, function -- but the practical overlap with FPD and Frederick residents is real.

This also lands the night of the IAC meeting, where public comment is likely to surface it. Be prepared to articulate the distinction between the City's CVA (which governs FPD) and the County Sheriff's jurisdiction.
