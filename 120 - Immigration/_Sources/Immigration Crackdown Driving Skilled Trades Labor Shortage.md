# Immigration Crackdown Driving Skilled Trades Labor Shortage

## Summary
Documented's October 9 roundup leads with an aging workforce plus the immigration crackdown producing skilled trade shortages, alongside an ICE order for 1,000 more arrests a day, a proposed $70,000 fee on international student work authorization, and a study finding Minnesota lost 32,000 jobs to Operation Metro Surge.

## Key Points
Economic impact. An aging workforce combined with enforcement is producing nationwide shortages of skilled trade workers (New Jersey Monitor). The Immigration Research Initiative estimates Minnesota now has 32,000 fewer jobs because of the economic impact of Operation Metro Surge.

Enforcement escalation. ICE leadership told agents to increase arrests by 1,000 more people per day (CNN, Local10). ICE has arrested nearly 40,000 people across New York and New Jersey this administration (Gothamist). NPR reports ICE used a warehouse in Roxbury, New Jersey as a detention site despite being legally barred from doing so. An Army study of the electric shock gloves ICE has bought in the thousands found they caused "intense pain," fatigue, soreness, and lasting apprehension (Mother Jones).

Federal policy. VP JD Vance announced a J-1 visa fraud investigation at nine universities including Harvard, Yale, and MIT, and suspension of a green card program for tech workers. DHS proposed a $70,000 fee, payable by schools, for international students seeking U.S. work authorization. The administration is building its own team of lawyers for unaccompanied children after cutting the Acacia Center for Justice contract, which managed 24,000 cases. More Republican lawmakers are publicly worried the enforcement push will hurt them in the midterms (The Hill).

Local pushback and accountability. A Utah police department is reconsidering its 287(g) agreement after ride-alongs led to detentions at a Moroni turkey plant. The family of Silverio Villegas González, killed by an ICE agent in Franklin Park, Illinois, is seeking federal damages. Eighteen activists completed a 100-mile march against detention centers ending in Los Angeles. Due process concerns are mounting at the Concord, California immigration court after San Francisco's court was closed.

## Relevance
The labor-shortage and Minnesota job-loss findings give an economic argument for local non-cooperation that does not depend on the moral case, which matters with business-minded swing votes like Adkins. Frederick's construction and housing production goals depend on trades labor, so enforcement-driven shortages cut directly against Resolution 26-15's 4,000-unit target. The Utah 287(g) reversal is a reminder that Maryland's 287(g) ban already took that tool off the table locally. The 1,000-more-arrests directive suggests Taskers Chance-style operations will get more frequent, not less.
