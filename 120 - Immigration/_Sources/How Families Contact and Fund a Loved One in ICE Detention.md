# How Families Contact and Fund a Loved One in ICE Detention

## Summary
Practical guide for families of people in ICE detention: find the person through ICE's Online Detainee Locator, call the facility for the A-Number and communication vendor, fund separate phone and commissary accounts, and assume every call is recorded. NYLAG advises that a citizen or LPR provide billing information where possible.

## Key Points
- **Scale.** More than 65,000 people were in ICE detention as of July 11, 2026 (TRAC), across more than 200 facilities nationwide.
- **Step 1, locate.** ICE's Online Detainee Locator System (ODLS) finds people held by ICE, or by CBP for more than 48 hours. Search by country of birth plus nine-digit A-Number, or by full name, date of birth, and country of birth. Hyphenating multiple last names can fix a failed search. The ODLS excludes anyone under 18; for unaccompanied minors call the Office of Refugee Resettlement at 1-800-203-7001 or the local ICE field office.
- **Step 2, call the facility.** The locator returns the facility name and phone. Staff can confirm the person is there, give their A-Number (useful for court monitoring), and say which communication vendor the facility uses. Don't guess the vendor.
- **Step 3, communication account.** Detainees cannot receive incoming calls. Each facility's "Contacting a Detainee" tab on ice.gov lists methods. At least 48 ICE facilities provide tablets for messaging and video. Vendors vary, for example myViaPath (formerly GettingOut) at Delaney Hall in New Jersey and iWebVisit at Washoe County Jail. Adding a contact usually needs the full name and A-Number or booking number, sometimes date of birth and country of origin.
- **All calls are recorded.** Do not discuss private matters, immigration case details, or anything tied to a criminal investigation.
- **Step 4, commissary.** Trust accounts are separate from phone accounts and pay for food, hygiene items, and sometimes clothing. Deposits may go through Connect Network, Western Union, or Access Corrections. Most accounts cap deposits, and some apps charge card processing fees. Some facilities use their own account numbers, so confirm with the person or staff.
- **Risk guidance (Melissa Chua, NYLAG Immigrant Protection Unit).** NYLAG knows of no enforcement action tied to paying into these accounts, but it is potentially safer for a U.S. citizen or LPR to provide billing information. NYLAG knows of no case where recorded family calls were used against someone, but calls are not confidential. NYLAG still generally advises against visiting a detention center without secure immigration status.
- The guide is current as of October 2026 and is not legal advice.

## Relevance
This is ready-made constituent and rapid-response material. When a Frederick family has someone picked up, these are the first practical questions, and the answers belong alongside the referral information in [[Immigrant Legal Services and Local Support]]. The billing and visiting guidance matters most for mixed-status households, which is exactly who the CVA and the Data Privacy Act are meant to protect. It could be adapted into a Spanish-language one-pager with RISE or Centro Hispano. Background on the facilities themselves is in [[ICE Detention Infrastructure]], and Delaney Hall appears in several existing sources.
