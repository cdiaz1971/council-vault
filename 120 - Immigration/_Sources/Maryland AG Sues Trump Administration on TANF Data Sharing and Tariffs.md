# Maryland AG Sues Trump Administration on TANF Data Sharing and Tariffs

## Summary

Maryland joined 24 other jurisdictions suing to block a Trump administration policy that would let federal agencies -- including DHS -- access personal data on TANF recipients including immigration status, set to take effect August 11. Maryland also joined a separate 25-jurisdiction suit challenging recent tariffs as unlawful. Together these represent two more fronts in what is now nearly three dozen legal actions Maryland AG Anthony Brown has led or joined against the Trump administration this year.

## Key Points

The TANF lawsuit targets a new Administration for Children and Families policy that would give federal agencies access to data on TANF recipients including Social Security numbers, marital history, and immigration status. The stated use includes cross-referencing with DHS and U.S. Citizenship and Immigration Services to verify citizenship or immigration status. The policy goes into effect August 11.

Maryland receives approximately $228 million annually under TANF. In fiscal 2026, more than 38,000 participants received assistance monthly -- about 27,000 children and nearly 12,000 adults.

The lawsuit argues that states, not the federal government, are responsible for verifying applicant eligibility; that the ACF's claimed authority to share recipient data across agencies is not supported by the TANF statute; that the policy violates the Administrative Procedure Act; and that it imposes new conditions on federal funding in violation of the Spending Clause.

AG Brown's statement frames this explicitly as protecting families from having their data used to target them for immigration enforcement -- accurate given the DHS cross-referencing purpose stated in the federal notice.

The tariff lawsuit was filed in the U.S. Court of International Trade, following a May 2026 2-1 ruling that the president's 10% global tariff was unlawful. The administration has continued pursuing new tariffs despite that ruling.

## Relevance

The TANF data sharing policy is a direct immigration enforcement escalation using a poverty assistance program as a data collection mechanism. For the immigrant community in Frederick -- where 28% of Golden Mile residents are foreign-born and District 2's foreign-born population is 1.4x the county rate -- this creates a chilling effect on TANF participation for mixed-status families that extends well beyond undocumented individuals. Families with legal status may avoid TANF to protect other household members.

This connects to the infrastructure that the City Values Act was designed to protect. Worth flagging to Centro Hispano and SSCM as a concrete new federal threat to monitor. The August 11 effective date is immediate.
