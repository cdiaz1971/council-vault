# ICE Confrontation in Frederick's Tasker's Chance Neighborhood

## Summary
ICE agents staked out a house in Frederick's Tasker's Chance neighborhood for two days before a Friday night confrontation with protesters turned violent, with agents pushing protesters and striking one with a baton, prompting elected officials from the governor on down to demand an investigation.

## Key Points
Residents noticed ICE agents staking out a house on Thursday. Protesters, including members of Indivisible Frederick Forward, gathered and by Friday were blocking the street and locking arms to form a human barrier around the house. Video from Hagerstown Rapid Response shows agents moving in, grabbing and pushing protesters, and one agent striking a person on the ground with a baton, the scene lit by ICE vehicle headlights. Indivisible Frederick Forward member Tatiana Santos said residents were "terrorized" for two days, and questioned why ICE showed up at all given that no arrests were made.

Frederick Police were called to the scene at 9 p.m. Friday for escalating tensions but made no arrests and were not notified of the federal operation in advance or involved in planning it. City spokesperson Jennifer Gerlock said FPD's role was limited to maintaining public safety and preventing further escalation, not assisting ICE.

Governor Wes Moore said his office monitored the situation over the holiday weekend and voiced concern about the scale of the current ICE operation, which he said is now larger than the FBI, DEA, and ATF combined. Rep. April McClain Delaney called the confrontation deeply concerning and demanded a transparent investigation. Del. Kris Fair, whose district includes the neighborhood, called the agents' conduct erratic and argued Congress should use its funding power over ICE to rein in the agency. Santos and fellow protester Soolah Kemp of Hagerstown Rapid Response called on the Maryland Attorney General's Office to investigate; the office declined to comment. ICE and DHS did not respond to requests for comment.
