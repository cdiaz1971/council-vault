# Community Trust Act -- CTA Sheriffs Lawsuit Synthesis

## The Convergence

Three things happened on the same day, May 27, 2026:

1. The Maryland Community Trust Act is becoming law without Governor Moore's signature.
2. Seventeen Maryland sheriffs -- including the Frederick County Sheriff -- filed a federal civil lawsuit to block it.
3. César attends the Frederick County Immigrant Affairs Commission meeting tonight at 5pm at Winchester Hall.

These are not coincidentally concurrent. They are the same political conflict playing out at three institutional levels simultaneously.

## The Structural Pattern

Maryland has built a layered state-level immigration protection framework over the past several months: first the 287(g) ban (emergency bill, signed), then the Community Trust Act (judicial warrant requirement, becoming law without signature), then the Data Privacy Act (restricts data sales for immigration enforcement, also becoming law without signature). Each layer closes a gap that the previous layer left open.

The county sheriffs -- who are independently elected constitutional officers, not city employees -- are the enforcement layer that this framework most directly constrains. Their lawsuit (Gahler v. Moore, No. 8:26-cv-02057) argues the CTA obstructs federal law enforcement and violates their constitutional obligations. The Federation for American Immigration Reform (FAIR) filed it on their behalf. Frederick County Sheriff is among the 17 plaintiffs.

The City of Frederick operates its own layer through the City Values Act (G-26-1), which governs Frederick Police Department -- not the Sheriff's office. FPD and the Sheriff's office are separate agencies with separate jurisdictions and separate command structures. The CVA controls FPD; it does not and cannot control the Sheriff.

## What This Means Tonight

At the IAC meeting tonight, public comment is likely to surface both the CTA becoming law (a partial win for the community) and the sheriff's lawsuit (a threat to that win). Community members who came to celebrate the CTA's passage may now face questions about whether the lawsuit will undo it.

As Council President, the useful distinction to have ready: the City's CVA governs the agency the City controls. The Sheriff's lawsuit is a county matter. The City's position is clear and documented in the CVA. The Sheriff's decision to join the lawsuit is his constitutional prerogative as an independent elected official -- but it does not change the City's posture.

The more politically productive framing: the CTA and CVA together represent a coherent framework for protecting immigrant residents through the agencies and laws that are available. The federal lawsuit is the resistance to that framework, not its undoing. The framework has survived the sheriff's threat before -- eight of nine Maryland counties with 287(g) agreements dropped them quickly even after threatening litigation.

## The Open Question

The federal lawsuit's most significant risk is a preliminary injunction that delays the CTA's implementation while the case proceeds. If Judge grants a preliminary injunction, law enforcement agencies that wanted to comply with the CTA will have legal cover to continue honoring administrative ICE detainer requests. Monitor the docket. The CVA remains operative regardless of what happens to the CTA in federal court.

## Synthesis Implication

The Immigrant Legal Services thread (research complete, proposal stage) acquires urgency here. The legal services model addresses what comes after enforcement contact: representation, know-your-rights, community support. It operates independently of whether the CTA survives litigation. Even if the CTA is enjoined, a Frederick immigrant legal services appropriation is structurally sound and politically differentiated from the enforcement debate. It is an affirmative investment in community resilience rather than a defensive posture against federal overreach.
