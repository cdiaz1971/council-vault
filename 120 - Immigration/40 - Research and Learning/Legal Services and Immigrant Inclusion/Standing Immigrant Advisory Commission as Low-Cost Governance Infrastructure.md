# Standing Immigrant Advisory Commission as Low-Cost Governance Infrastructure

## Summary

Portland's New Portlanders Policy Commission -- a standing volunteer advisory body advising the council on how policies affect immigrant and refugee communities -- provided the recommendation that became the $150K legal services appropriation. Frederick has no equivalent body despite District 2 being the city's highest-concentration immigrant community.

## Key Points

Portland's New Portlanders Policy Commission is a volunteer advisory board with a standing mandate to advise the city council on how policies affect immigrant and refugee communities. When Councilor Pirtle-Guiney needed to know what practical, low-cost steps the council could take in response to intensified federal enforcement, she asked the commission. The commission recommended SOAR Immigration Legal Services. That recommendation became the council action.

This is the governance model: a standing body with a specific mandate, connected to the community it represents, able to convert community knowledge into actionable council recommendations. It costs almost nothing to establish -- a resolution creating the commission, a defined mandate, an appointment process -- and it provides an ongoing input channel that does not depend on individual councilmembers having personal community relationships.

Critically, the commission's recommendation gave Pirtle-Guiney political cover to act. She was not channeling funds to a nonprofit based on her own judgment; she was implementing the recommendation of an advisory body that represents the community being served.

## Relevance

Frederick has no standing body with a mandate to advise on how policies affect its immigrant community. District 2 has the city's highest concentration of immigrants. The council's relationship with that community currently runs through individual councilmember relationships (primarily César's), the RISE Coalition, Centro Hispano, SSCM, and AACF -- all valuable, but none of them a formal city governance structure.

A Frederick analog to the New Portlanders Policy Commission would be a low-cost, high-signal governance move. A resolution establishing an Immigrant and Refugee Advisory Commission -- volunteer, advisory-only, with a mandate to advise the council on how city policies affect immigrant and refugee residents -- would create a formal input channel, provide political cover for future actions, and send a visible signal to District 2.

This is worth developing into a concrete proposal. A commission resolution could be drafted without significant staff resources and introduced at any regular council meeting.
