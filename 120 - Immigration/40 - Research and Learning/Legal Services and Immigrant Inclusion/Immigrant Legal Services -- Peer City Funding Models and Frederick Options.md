# Immigrant Legal Services -- Peer City Funding Models and Frederick Options

## Summary

Three peer city cases -- Harris County's legally upheld nonprofit appropriation, Portland's $150K reserve fund draw, and New Jersey's statutory codification -- together define the available design space for Frederick to fund immigrant legal services. The common structure: city funds a nonprofit intermediary; the public benefit is the legitimate purpose.

## Thesis

Frederick has the legal authority, the nonprofit infrastructure, and the political rationale to fund immigrant legal services. The peer city record answers the legal question; what remains is a design choice about structure, timing, and scale.

## The Three Models

**Harris County -- Legal Precedent:** Texas AG Paxton challenged Harris County's appropriation to immigrant legal services nonprofits as an unconstitutional gift of public funds. The 15th Court of Appeals -- an all-Republican bench created specifically to hear state government cases -- rejected the challenge. The court found a legitimate public purpose in immigrant legal representation that it did not find in abortion travel funding. This is the strongest available legal precedent for the county-to-nonprofit appropriation structure. The public benefit test is the operative legal standard, and legal representation for community members facing removal proceedings passes it.

**Portland -- Operational Model:** Portland City Council drew $150,000 from its Legal Priorities Reserve Fund -- a pot set aside for unexpected legal expenses -- and directed it to SOAR Immigration Legal Services, a nonprofit, to cover legal services for roughly 75 immigrants and refugees. The action was recommended by the New Portlanders Policy Commission (a standing volunteer advisory body) and introduced by a councilor who had specifically asked the commission what practical, low-cost steps the council could take. The internal tensions (act fast vs. consult broadly; fund one nonprofit vs. resource the whole network) are real governance trade-offs that Frederick should plan for rather than encounter in crisis.

**New Jersey -- Durability Model:** Governor Sherrill's codification of the Immigrant Trust Directive converted an AG policy into permanent statute -- much harder to reverse under future political conditions. This is the state-level model: lock protections into law rather than leaving them as administrative policy. Maryland has not done this. Frederick's council could support Maryland legislative allies in advancing equivalent codification.

## The Common Structure

All three models use the same basic design: government entity allocates funds to a nonprofit intermediary, the nonprofit delivers services, the public benefit (legal access for community members facing legal proceedings) is the legitimate governmental purpose. The nonprofit intermediary structure matters because it insulates the city from direct operational responsibility for legal services delivery while maintaining the council's role as the funding authority.

## Frederick's Design Space

Frederick's existing nonprofit infrastructure -- RISE Coalition, Centro Hispano, SSCM -- provides the intermediary layer. A city appropriation flowing through one of those organizations for immigration legal services would structurally mirror what Harris County did and what the court upheld.

Scale options: Portland's $150K for ~75 clients is a useful benchmark. At Frederick's population scale, a $50-75K appropriation in a supplemental budget or through a legal priorities reserve might cover a meaningful number of cases.

Governance option: establish a standing Immigrant and Refugee Advisory Commission (see [[Standing Immigrant Advisory Commission as Low-Cost Governance Infrastructure]]) before introducing the appropriation. The commission's recommendation provides political cover and demonstrates that the council is acting on community-defined needs, not unilateral council judgment.

Timing: proactive planning is better than crisis-response appropriation. Portland's inability to reach the supermajority for emergency action is the cautionary example. A regular appropriation through the normal budget process is more defensible and more durable.

## Open Questions

- Does Frederick have a legal priorities reserve fund or equivalent appropriation mechanism that could be used without waiting for a budget cycle?
- Which nonprofit is best positioned to receive and deploy these funds -- RISE Coalition, Centro Hispano, or a dedicated legal services organization?
- What is the Maryland Attorney General's posture on local immigrant services funding? Has any Maryland jurisdiction faced a legal challenge to this type of appropriation?
