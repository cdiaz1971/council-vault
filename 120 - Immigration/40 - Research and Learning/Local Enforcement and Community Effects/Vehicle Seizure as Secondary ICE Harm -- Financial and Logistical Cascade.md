# Vehicle Seizure as Secondary ICE Harm -- Financial and Logistical Cascade

## Summary

Beyond detention itself, a car-based ICE arrest triggers a documented financial and logistical cascade: tow fees, daily storage charges, tow lots that require the detained person's signature, loss of work tools in vehicles, potential vehicle repossession, and the compounding cost of legal fees alongside vehicle recovery. This is a secondary harm that local governments can partially address through ordinance.

## Key Points

**The financial cascade:**
- Tow ordered by Prince George's County police: $200+
- Daily storage: $50/day
- One local business was charged $925 for a work truck and trailer
- Storage fees accumulate while the detained person is held, sometimes for weeks
- Legal fees for bond hearings compete with vehicle recovery costs -- families cannot do both

**The title-holder problem:**
Many tow lots will release a car only to the titled owner. If the titled owner is detained, family members -- even household members with documented connection to the vehicle -- are turned away. This is not legally required; it is tow lot policy. Montgomery County passed an ordinance requiring tow lots to accept alternative proof of household membership (lease, utility bill) to bypass this restriction.

**Work tools:**
For many detained immigrants, the vehicle is also a work van with thousands of dollars of tools and materials. Loss of tools means loss of income for the family left behind.

**Repossession:**
At least one documented case: a family could not afford both vehicle payments and other costs during detention. The car was repossessed from the tow company by the lender.

**What Montgomery County did:**
Passed a local ordinance allowing tow lot release to household members with alternative documentation.

## Relevance

This maps directly onto the ALICE affordability framework. ALICE households operate at the margin: a $925 tow bill and $50/day storage, combined with $5,000+ in immigration legal fees, exceeds what most affected families can absorb. The vehicle loss then eliminates transportation to work and school.

More broadly: the ghost car phenomenon and its financial cascade is part of the lived ALICE experience in Frederick's immigrant communities. The affordability agenda and the immigration protection agenda are the same agenda for many District 2 households.
