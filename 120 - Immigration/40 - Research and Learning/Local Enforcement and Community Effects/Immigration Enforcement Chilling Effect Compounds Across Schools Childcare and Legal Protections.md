# Immigration Enforcement Chilling Effect Compounds Across Schools, Childcare, and Legal Protections

## Summary
Three data points from the same reporting converge on one dynamic. Immigration enforcement fear is producing measurable, population-level withdrawal from schools and childcare, while the legal protections meant to blunt that fear, Frederick's City Values Act included, are proliferating at the local level even as the state law they're modeled on faces a DOJ preemption suit that could expose them all.

## The Pattern
Your vault has documented the chilling effect anecdotally for months, through individual case notes like Ghost Cars and ICE Collateral Arrests. This week's reporting turns that into hard statewide numbers for the first time: Hispanic K-12 enrollment reversing a five-year growth trend, concentrated in Montgomery and Prince George's counties, and childcare centers serving immigrant families losing roughly half their capacity, as at Arco Iris in Laurel. Both effects are happening to families who never have a direct enforcement encounter at all. Fear alone is doing the work.

At the same time, the legal tool meant to counter that fear is spreading and weakening in the same breath. Montgomery County passed its own County Values Act on top of the state Community Trust Act, and residents elsewhere are demanding their counties do the same, evidence the model is seen as worth replicating. But the state Community Trust Act it's layered on is under a live DOJ Supremacy Clause challenge, and, per [[Warrant-Based ICE Restrictions Create Legal Exposure Not Prevention]] and [[ICE Enforcement at Schools Shows Limits of State and Local Sensitive-Location Restrictions]], even where these laws hold up in court they only restrict local cooperation, not what a federal agent can do in a school parking lot or a childcare center's parking lot. The CVA is built on the identical warrant-based structure. So the harm being measured is accelerating, the tool built to prevent it is legally contested, and even where it survives, it doesn't reach the actual encounter families are afraid of.

## Implications for César's Work
The UMD URSP708 guest lecture on the CVA (September 29) now has hard statewide enrollment and childcare data to open with instead of leading on anecdote. Worth a quiet check with Rachel Gray on whether a DOJ win against the state Community Trust Act would extend the same preemption reasoning to the CVA, given they share the same warrant-based design. Know-your-rights guidance from Frederick Rapid Response Network, Centro Hispano, and SSCM should explicitly extend past formal enforcement encounters into school drop-off zones and childcare parking lots, since that is where the documented harm is concentrating. And it may be worth asking Stuart Campbell or Lakisha Wade whether Frederick has any visibility into local Hispanic school enrollment or childcare impact comparable to what this data shows statewide.
