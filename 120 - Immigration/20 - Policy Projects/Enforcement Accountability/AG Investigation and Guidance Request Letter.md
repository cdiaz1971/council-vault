# AG Investigation and Guidance Request Letter

## Summary
September 21, 2026 letter co-signed by Cesar and the Mayor asking the Maryland Attorney General to investigate ICE conduct during the Tasker's Chance operation and to issue written guidance on whether and how Frederick police may intervene when federal agents use apparently unlawful force.

## What Happened
The letter describes a roughly 40-hour federal immigration operation in a Frederick neighborhood earlier in September that produced zero arrests, several First Amendment demonstrations, and multiple uses of force by federal agents. Documented conduct includes a federal vehicle driven onto a public sidewalk toward demonstrators, force against people who appeared to be passively resisting, baton use, forcible removal of people from a residence entrance, and statements to elected officials that agents knew their names, plates, and home addresses. FPD addressed actual obstruction when it occurred, and demonstrators complied. An FPD supervisor verbally intervened when an ICE agent escalated force against a passive demonstrator, and the agent stopped.

## The Legal Gap
Public Safety Section 3-524 requires a police officer to intervene to stop excessive force by another police officer. The statute defines "police officer" by reference to Section 3-201, which leaves it unclear whether the duty reaches force used by federal agents, what intervention is permitted, and what protection an officer has if the federal government retaliates. The letter's core point: officers cannot resolve that question in seconds during a confrontation with armed federal personnel.

## Requests
1. An independent investigation of federal conduct, covering the sidewalk incident, each use of force, force against passive resisters, the statements to elected officials, the residence entry and its legal basis, and whether any state criminal or civil rights law was implicated.
2. Preservation and production of warrants, body-worn and vehicle video, radio traffic, operational plans, use-of-force reports, and any claimed consent to enter.
3. Written guidance on whether Section 3-524 or any other authority requires or permits intervention, what forms are allowed up to detention or arrest of a federal agent on probable cause, what to do if an agent refuses a verbal intervention or turns force on the officer, how supremacy, immunity, and removal doctrines apply, and what reporting should follow.
4. A statement of what legal and institutional support Maryland will give an officer who acts reasonably and in good faith and then faces federal investigation, prosecution, litigation, or retaliation.
5. An expedited meeting with City leadership, FPD, the City Attorney's Office, the Maryland Police Training and Standards Commission, and other state officials to produce guidance that can go into FPD policy and training before the next operation.

## Relevance
This is the most concrete institutional response so far to the Tasker's Chance confrontation, and it is a rare joint action with Mayor O'Connor, whose relationship with Cesar is otherwise adversarial. It moves the question from local non-cooperation, which the [[City Values Act]] already addresses, to the harder one of what local officers may do when federal conduct happens in front of them. The AG's answer, or silence, will shape FPD policy and is worth tracking against the Community Trust Act litigation. Full signed text is in the PDF.
