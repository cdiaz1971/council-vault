# Golden Mile Small Area Profile Data and Cross-Thread Notes

## Summary
Data points and cross-thread connections pulled from the Golden Mile Small Area Profile, the existing-conditions document preceding the forthcoming Golden Mile Small Area Plan under FOCUS Frederick.

## Document Context
The Small Area Profile is the existing-conditions half of a new two-part planning process (Profile, then Plan) that the Planning Department is piloting with the Golden Mile as the first entry in the FOCUS Frederick series. The Profile itself carries no legislative action. It is meant to kick off the community engagement phase that precedes the actual Golden Mile Small Area Plan, which will contain policy recommendations. Plan Area is roughly 2,100 to 2,138 acres along the Route 40 corridor, running from the Catoctin foothills to Route 15, home to about 27,315 residents per the 2024 ACS estimate, roughly one third of the citywide population, across 10,029 households.

## District and NAC Boundaries -- Confirmed Error in the Profile
The Profile states the Plan Area spans District 1 and District 2, and describes the two Neighborhood Advisory Councils covering it as "NAC 8 to the north and NAC 5 to the south" of Route 40. This is wrong. Comparing the City's own NAC base map (Base_NACWall2023) against Route 40 and Patrick Street confirms NAC 5 sits north of Route 40 and NAC 8 sits south of it, the reverse of the Profile's wording. César attends NAC 8 meetings and lives in NAC 8 territory south of the corridor, consistent with the base map and inconsistent with the Profile. The district picture holds up better: the City's 2025 Election Districts map shows Route 40 running entirely within District 2 in this stretch, with the wider Plan Area reaching north into District 1 neighborhoods (Stonegate, Lake Coventry, Willowdale Park, Taskers Chance Park) and south into District 2 neighborhoods (Golf View, Monarch Ridge, Emerald Farm) around it. So "spans District 1 and District 2" in the Profile is accurate, but the Profile's NAC 8/NAC 5 north-south assignment is backwards and should be flagged to Planning Director Gabrielle Collard's office as an error in an official published document.

## Housing and Tenant Data
Renter cost burden in the Golden Mile is 63 percent, compared to 52.5 percent citywide and 47.8 percent in the Washington-Arlington-Alexandria MSA, higher than renter cost burden in the New York and San Francisco metro areas. Rental units make up just over half the area's housing stock, versus roughly 60 percent owner-occupied citywide. Median gross rent is $1,860 and median home value is $356,484. Golden Mile census tracts produce the city's highest share of ALICE households per the United Way's 2025 State of ALICE in Frederick County report.

As of July 2026 there were 694 units in the Golden Mile's residential pipeline across 9 named projects (273 single-family, 200 townhomes, 221 multifamily), 11 percent of the citywide pipeline and only fourth among the city's small areas despite the area housing roughly a third of the city's population. The Westridge Shopping Center redevelopment (MCB Real Estate, purchased 2022) is planned to add 119 residential units in a mixed-use format. The Prospect Center, a new Frederick County hub for public health, aging, and family services, is planned to include roughly 150 units of affordable housing plus a 911 call center. None of these three production figures currently appear reflected in the Resolution on Affordable Housing Action's tracking of the 4,000-unit floor.

Over 30 commercial parcels along the corridor qualify as underutilized by land-to-improvement value ratio (1.03 to 5.07), including 3 surface-parking-only parcels and 8 vacant parcels. The average Golden Mile commercial building covers only 30 percent of its parcel even though the LMC allows up to 90 percent impervious coverage in General Commercial zones, suggesting real headroom for redevelopment.

## Demographics and Equity
28.2 percent of Golden Mile residents are foreign-born and 33.7 percent of all residents identify as Hispanic or Latino, with Salvadoran and Mexican residents making up the two largest diaspora communities. Nearly a third of the population speaks Spanish at home. The area has the youngest population and the highest concentration of youth citywide as of 2024.

13.6 percent of Golden Mile youth live below the poverty level, concentrated in one census tract (7505.04) where over a third of children are in poverty. This accounts for roughly one fifth of countywide and over 40 percent of citywide childhood poverty. In March 2025, Frederick County received a $300,000 Maryland ENOUGH Initiative grant to produce a neighborhood action plan to end child poverty along the Golden Mile. Worth tracking as a parallel county effort that any city equity or budgeting work should coordinate with rather than duplicate.

## Transportation and Safety
From 2020 to 2024 there were 576 car accidents, 42 non-motorist accidents, and 25 severe or fatal injuries in the Plan Area. Pedestrian accidents concentrated on Hillcrest Drive and Key Parkway, both already designated High Injury Network corridors, which is why the Key Parkway High Injury Corridor Study and the Hillcrest Drive traffic calming project (CIP 340009) are already active.

The Golden Mile Multimodal Access Plan (CIP 380009, design phase) will add a 10-foot bi-directional shared-use path and a 12-foot bus-only or right-turn lane along westbound Route 40. Separately, the Maryland State Highway Administration selected the Route 40 corridor from Old Camp Road to Route 15 as one of the state's first six Vulnerable Road User Safety Assessment corridors, with reduced speed limits already in effect and a site-specific design plan still to come.

## Economic and Community Context
The corridor is home to 555 businesses and 5,151 employees, and generated over $1 billion in 2025 sales, 13 percent of citywide commercial revenue. There are roughly 50 restaurants and bakeries and 17 international grocers spanning more than 20 cuisines. The Red Horse Restaurant, a legacy business operating since 1968, was significantly damaged by a fire in early 2026, with its return to business described as uncertain. The West Frederick Library (new Frederick County Public Libraries branch, former Hillcrest Orchard Park site) and the Westside Regional Park Phase 1 community center ($620,000 design contract with Knu Design, approved June 2025) are both in progress and are positive constituent-facing capital projects in the district.
