## How to use the wiki

1. Begin with a core topic to understand the problem, authority, evidence, options, and metrics.
2. Follow links to active projects when Council is considering a concrete action.
3. Use case pages for property-specific facts and procedural history.
4. Use law and legislation pages to verify current authority.
5. Use research and meeting notes as supporting material, not automatically as current policy.
6. Use `_Sources` for dated snapshots and retained source material.

## Page types

| Type | Purpose | Expected maintenance |
|---|---|---|
| `map-of-content` | Navigation and relationships | Update whenever pages or folders change |
| `council-decision-reference` | Durable, sourced subject reference | Review sources periodically and before major decisions |
| `policy-project` | Specific proposal, strategy, or deliverable | Update while active; close with outcome and date |
| `development-case` | Property- or application-specific record | Update at procedural milestones |
| `research-note` | Source analysis or conceptual exploration | Preserve date and source; promote verified findings upward |
| `meeting-note` | Historical record of a meeting or event | Keep dated; do not silently rewrite history |
| `source-snapshot` | Superseded page or retained source | Treat as immutable |

## Maintenance rules

- Keep only this page and [[Housing -- In-Progress Plans]] as working files in the Housing root.
- Give durable core pages undated filenames; put review dates in frontmatter.
- Date strategy briefs, meetings, cases, and snapshots when time matters.
- Preserve superseded versions in `_Sources`; do not erase the research trail.
- Distinguish enacted policy, pending proposals, staff recommendations, personal strategy, and outside examples.
- Recheck time-sensitive law, funding, program rules, officeholders, and statistics before relying on them.
