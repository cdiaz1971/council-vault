# Policy Analyst Position as Alternative to Affordable Housing Commission

## Summary
Instead of standing up the Affordable Housing Commission named in the Housing Production Target Resolution, César is considering hiring a planning and zoning focused policy analyst to manage LMC input and pursue targeted zoning text amendments over a full code overhaul.

## Key Points
Rather than establishing the Affordable Housing Commission recommended in Section 4 of the Housing Production Target Resolution, the plan is to hire a policy analyst position, still titled "policy analyst" but explicitly planning and zoning focused, to help manage the city's input on Land Management Code changes. The analyst would not necessarily draft legislation directly, but would drive LMC changes through targeted zoning text amendments rather than a comprehensive code overhaul, approached through an affordability lens without being limited to it.

The targeted-amendment approach is framed as more effective, more cost-effective, and more efficient than a full rewrite, producing more actual changes in less time. A secondary benefit is that pursuing amendments one at a time is likely to surface staffing and internal process gaps, not necessarily LMC-specific, that a full-overhaul approach might not reveal until much later. The position is meant to sit inside a broader affordability agenda rather than stand alone.

## Relevance
This is a direct alternative to a named recommendation in the Housing Production Target Resolution, which is still in draft, so it's worth reviewing against the resolution's full Section 4 list before the job description is finalized. It may functionally replace or overlap with other listed priorities like the Affordable Housing Navigator, which is worth resolving one way or the other rather than leaving both in the draft.

It also gives an institutional mechanism, dedicated staff capacity, for actually advancing the four targeted amendments in [[LMC Zoning Amendments -- Personal Strategy Brief (2026-05-13)]] (by-right missing middle in R4/R6, parking minimum reform, ADU owner-occupancy elimination, ADU cap removal in planned developments), which currently exist only as a personal strategy document with no staffing plan behind them.
