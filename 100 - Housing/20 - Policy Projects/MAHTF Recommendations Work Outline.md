# MAHTF Recommendations Work Outline

**1. MPDU Reform**
Raise the in-lieu fee, deepen affordability targets to 60% and 30% AMI, and drop the development trigger from 25 units to 10. Move all three together.

**2. City Housing Fund Capitalization**
The fund is structurally undercapitalized. Use the task force's own numbers to build the case for dedicated revenue streams and a Housing Trust Fund.

**3. LMC Targeted Amendments -- Missing Middle and Parking**
Missing middle housing and parking minimum reduction are rated high urgency. Move them without waiting for the comprehensive LMC review.

**4. LMC Targeted Amendment -- Faith-Based Land**
By-right zoning approval for housing on faith-based land. Virginia's YIGBY law is the peer model. Introduce as a standalone LMC text amendment.

**5. SRO and Co-Living Legalization**
The task force explicitly endorses single-room occupancy and co-living as affordability tools. The mayoral endorsement creates a political opening that didn't exist before. Pursue as a targeted LMC amendment.

**6. ADU Reform**
Expand and streamline ADU development. Rated high urgency by the task force. Move as a targeted LMC amendment on its own track.

**7. APFO Reform**
Tiered AMI weighting that reduces or eliminates affordable units' impact on school capacity tests. The highest-urgency council-actionable item in the report. Introduce as an ordinance amendment.

**8. Affordable Housing Commission**
If the Mayor creates this by executive action, the council loses oversight. Push for ordinance constitution with council appointment authority before he moves first.

**9. Affordable Housing Navigator**
A new HHS staff position to package developer incentives and convert stalled projects. This will show up as a budget ask. The council should condition it on reporting to both branches.

**10. Permitting Streamlining**
The administration owns the process changes, but the council can attach reporting requirements and tie funding to compliance with the 8-10 month target.

**11. TIF and Alternative Finance**
The council has direct authority here. A resolution directing the CFO to evaluate TIF feasibility for housing corridors is concrete and low-friction.

**12. Tenant Protection Gap**
The report is supply-side only. Go on record publicly that production and tenant protection are parallel tracks, not substitutes.

**13. The 4,000-Unit Shortfall**
The task force projects 4,000 units. The documented need is 13,774. Name that gap publicly so the report doesn't get treated as sufficient.

**14. Public Messaging Frame**
The task force recommends a workforce-stability and generational-opportunity narrative. Adopt it publicly before the Mayor controls the frame.
