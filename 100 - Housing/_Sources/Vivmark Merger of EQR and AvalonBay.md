# Vivmark Merger of EQR and AvalonBay

## Summary
Equity Residential and AvalonBay have merged into a new $69 billion REIT called Vivmark, realizing the consolidation risk flagged in earlier FTC pre-merger review research.

## Key Points
Equity Residential (EQR) and AvalonBay, two of the largest corporate apartment landlords in the country, have combined into a single entity rebranded as Vivmark. Debt Collective's housing team, which had been watching for this possibility since at least May 2026, is treating the deal as confirmation of the consolidation risk they had been tracking. They interviewed René Moya, their Housing Team Lead, about what the merger means for organizing strategy going forward. Moya brings experience from the Los Angeles Tenants Union and from directing the 2020 Proposition 21 rent control ballot campaign in California, along with a background in pandemic-era emergency tenant protections and social housing advocacy.

Debt Collective is framing the merger as a shift in organizing terrain rather than an organizing dead end, and is holding a townhall for tenants living in EQR or AvalonBay buildings to discuss strategy under the new corporate structure.

## Relevance
This merger is the real-world materialization of the scenario already flagged in [[REIT Merger Exemption from FTC Pre-Merger Review -- Corporate Landlord Consolidation Risk]] and [[FTC REIT Merger Loophole -- The Accountability Gap in Corporate Landlord Policy]]: large REITs combining without triggering meaningful antitrust review, consistent with the pattern described in [[REITs as the Apex Predator of the Rental Housing Market]]. Worth checking whether either EQR or AvalonBay (now Vivmark) has a footprint in Frederick, since that would give this merger direct local relevance to housing affordability and tenant protection work rather than only national policy interest. This also strengthens the case for the corporate landlord accountability angle already being developed in [[Policy Levers to Counter Private Equity in Housing]].
