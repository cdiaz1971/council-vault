# ROAD to Housing Act Innovation Fund Rewards Local Zoning Reform Only If Congress Funds It

## Summary
The ROAD to Housing Act creates a HUD innovation fund that would pay localities $10,000 to $250,000 for demonstrated housing supply growth through reforms like plexes and parking elimination, but only if Congress appropriates the money. Other provisions trim NEPA review and raise the bank public welfare investment cap from 15% to 20%.

## Key Points
Localities would apply with data showing objective improvement in housing supply and affordability, plus reforms such as legalizing duplexes through quadplexes, eliminating parking requirements, and shortening regulatory steps. Congress may appropriate up to $200 million a year through 2031, but nothing is funded yet.

The law exempts several categories from environmental impact statements, including rehabilitation of one to four unit buildings, new construction up to four units, and acquisition of public land. It also drops the chassis requirement for manufactured homes.

Raising the bank public welfare investment cap from 15% to 20% of capital could free more bank money for LIHTC equity, since banks supply about 80% of LIHTC investment.

Implementation is the weak point. HUD has fallen from about 8,800 staff to 5,800, and analysts doubt the agency can stand up new programs without restaffing.

## Relevance
Frederick's missing middle and parking reforms are exactly the kind of record this fund would reward. If the fund is ever appropriated, a documented production baseline puts the City in position to apply. Until then it is an argument to cite, not money to count on.
