## Summary

State coalitions promoting community land trusts (CLTs) and real estate cooperatives have grown significantly in recent years, providing durable policy infrastructure for community ownership of housing as an alternative to speculative markets. This Shelterforce piece documents the coalition-building strategies, legislative vehicles, and organizational models enabling this shift.

## Key dynamics (from article excerpt)

State-level CLT coalitions are not just advocating for individual projects -- they are building the policy infrastructure (enabling legislation, funding mechanisms, preservation tools) that makes community ownership scalable at the state level. The model draws on both Canadian experience and domestic precedents.

The coalition approach mirrors what's already documented in the vault: CLTs function simultaneously as housing policy, civic infrastructure, and economic democracy. The state coalition framing is the missing piece -- local CLT advocacy gains leverage when it can point to a state-level scaffolding.

## Connection to Frederick work

The Housing Production Target Resolution and the Real Rent Ordinance both address affordability at the market level. CLTs address it at the ownership structure level. Neither substitutes for the other -- the gap note on income concentration driving housing demand (no current Frederick policy initiative) maps directly to what CLTs address. A Frederick CLT initiative with state coalition backing would close that gap.
