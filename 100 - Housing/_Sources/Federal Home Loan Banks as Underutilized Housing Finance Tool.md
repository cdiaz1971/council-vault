# Federal Home Loan Banks as Underutilized Housing Finance Tool

## Summary

The eleven regional Federal Home Loan Banks run targeted lending programs -- including 0% interest advance programs -- that collectively rival CDBG and LIHTC in scale but are almost entirely unknown outside the banking industry, representing a significant underutilized tool for local housing and community development finance.

## Key Points

The Federal Home Loan Bank system is a network of eleven regional wholesale banks created by Congress in 1932 to support housing and community development. Their members include over 6,300 banks, credit unions, insurance companies, and community development lenders. The system's primary function is making low-cost advances (loans) to member institutions to fund eligible lending.

The 0% Development Advance program, created by the Federal Home Loan Bank of New York in 2022, offers zero-interest advances to members for loans in five categories: small business, climate resilience or energy efficiency, infrastructure, housing, and tribal development. Since 2022, the program has supported $460 million in eligible loans across New York, New Jersey, Puerto Rico, and the U.S. Virgin Islands. The housing category was added in 2025 and supported $9.8 million in pre-development and acquisition loans in its first year.

Across all eleven regional banks, targeted lending programs supported nearly $8 billion in loans in 2024 -- comparable to or larger than CDBG ($3B/year), New Markets Tax Credits ($5B/year), and Opportunity Zone investments ($12B/year), yet largely invisible to municipal policymakers.

Key limits: per-institution subsidy caps ensure smaller institutions like CDFIs can access the program, but the annual cap means a first-come, first-served scramble when application windows open. Community bank consolidation is shrinking the pool of institutions with local lending relationships. Minority depository institutions -- the most likely to lend in majority-minority neighborhoods -- represent only 130 of 3,909 community banks.

The program is not a grant program. Advances must be repaid, but the zero-interest period (typically structured around the loan term) functions as a significant subsidy that can make otherwise marginal deals viable.

## Relevance

At a moment when federal housing dollars are being cut and Frederick is actively working on [[Housing Production Target Resolution]] and searching for financing tools that don't require new municipal spending, the FHLB system represents an underexplored lever. The Atlantic Community Bankers or local credit unions serving Frederick may already be FHLB members and eligible to access these advances. This is worth a specific inquiry to the city's financial staff or to community lenders operating in Frederick.

Also relevant to the [[FY2027 Budget -- Work Session 2 April 9 Notes]] conversation: if the city is looking for ways to support affordable housing production without direct budget expenditure, facilitating FHLB program awareness among local lenders could be a low-cost, high-leverage move.
