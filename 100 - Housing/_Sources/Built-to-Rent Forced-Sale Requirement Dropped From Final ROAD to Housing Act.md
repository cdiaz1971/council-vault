# Built-to-Rent Forced-Sale Requirement Dropped From Final ROAD to Housing Act

## Summary
An early Senate provision that would have forced institutional investors owning more than 350 single-family rentals to sell built-to-rent homes to individual buyers after seven years was stripped from the final 21st Century ROAD to Housing Act, leaving the law's institutional-investor guardrails weaker than earlier drafts.

## Key Points
The Senate's original version of the ROAD to Housing Act included a seven-year forced-sale requirement for covered built-to-rent single-family homes owned by large institutional investors. The stated goal was to discourage institutional buyers from outcompeting individual homebuyers for single-family housing stock. The built-to-rent industry argued the rule would instead choke off construction and shrink supply, and pointed out it could have swept in some LIHTC-financed and opportunity-zone-financed properties, especially in rural and tribal areas. The provision froze BTR industry activity for several weeks even before it was decided. The House dropped the requirement from its version, and it did not survive conference. The final law markets itself as limiting institutional investors from outcompeting individual buyers, but this specific enforcement mechanism is gone.

## Relevance
This directly complements the existing critique note on the ROAD to Housing Act's supply-only framing. That note argues the law helps middle-income renters and builders but does nothing for households below the poverty line. This detail sharpens that argument on a second front: even the law's one concrete institutional-investor guardrail did not survive negotiation, so the "limiting institutional investors" framing in NLC's celebratory coverage overstates what the final text actually does. Useful if César wants a specific, citable example when pushing back on supply-only narratives in council remarks or public communications, and it reinforces why local tools (tenant protections, MPDU reform) still carry the weight federal policy did not pick up.
