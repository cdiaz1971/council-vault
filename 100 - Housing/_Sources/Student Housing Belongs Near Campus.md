# Student Housing Belongs Near Campus

## Summary

A student urbanist argues that near-campus housing is essential infrastructure, not a luxury, pointing to 20-30% rent increases around NC universities since 2020 and the compounding access barriers of long commutes.

## Key Points

Written by Ka'Mya Cooper, a student fellow at CityBuilder and undergraduate at NC Central University, from direct experience. Key data points:

- Rents near NC State have risen 30.5% since 2020; rents near UNC Chapel Hill up 23.3%
- Student rents rising at roughly the same rate as or faster than overall market-rate apartments
- Students who can't get on-campus housing are pushed into competitive rental markets, driving prices higher for both students and local residents
- Proximity matters beyond convenience: shorter commutes reduce barriers to attending class, campus activities, and support resources
- The core argument: building more near-campus housing relieves pressure on the broader rental market, because students and local renters compete for the same units when campus supply is inadequate

**Structural framing:** Student housing is part of the infrastructure of student success, not an amenity. Treating it as optional creates cascading costs for both students and communities.

## Relevance

This piece provides clean, student-voiced framing for a housing supply argument that transfers directly to Frederick. When transit-oriented development or density near Hood College, Frederick Community College, or other institutions comes up in LMC reform or housing production conversations, this framing is useful: market pressure from students competes with local renters, and adding supply near institutions benefits both populations. Relevant to the [[Housing Production Target Resolution]] and the ongoing [[LMC Review]] work.
