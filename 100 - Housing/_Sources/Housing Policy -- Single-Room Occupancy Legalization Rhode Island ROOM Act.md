# Housing Policy -- Single-Room Occupancy Legalization Rhode Island ROOM Act

## Summary

Rhode Island is considering the ROOM Act, which would legalize single-room occupancy (SRO) housing statewide in all residential and commercial zones. The bill was heard by the Housing Committee on March 31, 2026 and held for further study. SROs represent the lowest rung of market-rate housing and have largely been banned across the country since mid-20th century zoning reforms.

## Key Points

SROs are exactly what the name implies: single rooms with access to shared kitchens and bathrooms, often available for rent on daily or weekly terms. They are the cheapest form of housing available on the private market and historically functioned as a critical buffer against homelessness -- providing affordable shelter for low-income workers, people in transition, and those who couldn't qualify for or afford traditional apartments.

The mass elimination of SROs through zoning reforms is widely cited by housing researchers as a major factor in the rise of homelessness in American cities. Rhode Island's ROOM Act would reverse that by making SROs a by-right use in both residential and commercial zones statewide.

The bill was held in committee after the March 31 hearing -- meaning it didn't advance but wasn't killed either. The same issue of CITYBUILDER Brief also covered a close vote on Durham, NC's rezoning for increased density, and noted upcoming Parking Lot Reform events.

## Relevance

Relevant to Frederick's [[Housing Production Target Resolution]] and the broader housing supply strategy. SRO legalization is an underexplored tool in Maryland's housing toolkit -- most local housing conversations focus on MPDU, multifamily zoning, and accessory dwelling units, but SROs address the lowest-income tier of the market more directly. Worth flagging in the context of the LMC review and any conversation about housing types Frederick's code currently prohibits or discourages.
