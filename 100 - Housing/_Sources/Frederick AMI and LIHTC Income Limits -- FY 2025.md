# Frederick AMI and LIHTC Income Limits -- FY 2025

## Summary

FY 2025 HUD income limits for Frederick County across the full LIHTC stack (30% through 80% AMI), for 1-, 2-, and 4-person households. Base AMI is $163,900, derived from the HUD Washington MSA. These figures apply to affordable housing program eligibility, LIHTC project underwriting, and policy analysis.

## Key Figures

**Base AMI: $163,900**

> **Correction (2026-09-25):** The 40% and 60% rows originally read $54,590 / $62,390 / $77,930 and $63,160 / $72,190 / $90,180. Those didn't follow from the 50% limits. The old 40% figures sat only a few thousand dollars below the 50% limits, and the old 60% figures were too low. They are recalculated here with HUD's multifamily tax subsidy formula: 40% = 0.8 × the 50% limit and 60% = 1.2 × the 50% limit. The FY2025 60% limit for four, $98,340, matches HUD's published figure. The 30% and 80% rows are unchanged and weren't separately verified. **FY2026 limits took effect July 1, 2026:** 50% AMI is $58,150 (1 person), $66,450 (2), and $83,050 (4), per the [Frederick County Division of Housing FY 2026 table](https://www.frederickcountymd.gov/DocumentCenter/View/256909/Income-Limits-Table---All-Programs). See [[Housing Finance and Preservation]].
Source: HUD Washington Metropolitan Statistical Area (MSA). Frederick is pooled with DC, Arlington, and Alexandria, which drives limits significantly higher than Frederick's local income reality.

| AMI Level | 1-Person | 2-Person | 4-Person |
|---|---|---|---|
| **30% (Extremely Low)** | $34,450 | $39,350 | $49,150 |
| **40%** | $45,920 | $52,480 | $65,560 |
| **50% (Very Low)** | $57,400 | $65,600 | $81,950 |
| **60% (LIHTC Standard)** | $68,880 | $78,720 | $98,340 |
| **80% (Low / Moderate)** | $74,800 | $85,450 | $106,800 |

## Policy Notes

30% AMI is the HUD "extremely low income" threshold and the floor for the deepest subsidy programs, including Emergency Solutions Grant and Section 8 homelessness prevention.

40% AMI is not widely used in statute but matters when developers stack subsidies or negotiate set-asides below the standard LIHTC ceiling.

50% AMI is the HUD-published "very low income" tier and the income limit for Housing Choice Vouchers and most HUD-assisted programs.

60% AMI is the standard LIHTC ceiling -- what most affordable housing projects are built to serve. For a family of four, this is $98,340.

80% AMI is HUD's "low income" or moderate threshold, used in homebuyer assistance programs and some workforce housing definitions.

The gap between 30% and 60% AMI for a family of four is $49,190. This is where the hardest-to-serve households fall and where market-rate LIHTC production routinely fails to reach them.

## Relevance

These figures are the baseline for evaluating any affordable housing proposal that comes before the council -- whether a rezoning, a density bonus, a LIHTC project, or a set-aside ordinance. When a developer says a project serves "affordable" households, the first question is which AMI level and which household size. The 60% AMI standard is common but does not reach Frederick's lowest-income residents, particularly in District 2 where the poverty rate runs 1.5x the county rate.
