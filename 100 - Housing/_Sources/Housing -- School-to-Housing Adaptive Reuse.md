# Housing -- School-to-Housing Adaptive Reuse

## Summary

School-to-housing conversions are now the fastest-growing sector of adaptive reuse in the United States, driven by a nationwide decline in school enrollment. In 2024, nearly 2,000 apartments were completed in former school buildings -- four times the number from the prior year.

## Key Points

Enrollment drops have left school districts holding surplus property. Brookings Institution data shows that between 2018 and 2022, 12% of elementary schools and 9% of middle schools lost at least one-fifth of their student enrollment. RentCafe's analysis counts 74 school conversion projects currently underway nationally.

Atlanta's school system is repurposing eight former school sites into housing, with teachers receiving priority access to the resulting units. That model -- district as developer, with affordability tied to workforce housing -- is worth noting as a replication opportunity.

The sector's explosive growth (4x year over year) suggests this is no longer an experimental niche but an emerging standard approach to both surplus property management and housing production simultaneously.

## Relevance

Directly relevant to Frederick's housing strategy. Frederick City Schools occasionally closes or consolidates buildings. If surplus school sites exist or emerge, the city has standing to advocate for housing reuse -- especially affordable or workforce housing -- rather than sale to the highest commercial bidder. The teacher-priority model from Atlanta also aligns with workforce housing goals.
