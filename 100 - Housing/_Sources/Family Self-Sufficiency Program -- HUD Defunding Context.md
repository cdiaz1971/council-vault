# Family Self-Sufficiency Program -- HUD Defunding Context

## Summary

The Family Self-Sufficiency (FSS) Program -- a 30-year-old, bipartisan federal program rewarding Section 8 households with escrow savings when they increase their earnings -- is being defunded by the Trump administration. The justification relies on a methodologically flawed HUD evaluation that does not reflect how the program actually operated. This is a specific case of the broader pattern of using incomplete research to dismantle programs serving low-income renters.

## Key Points

The FSS Program was created in the early 1990s as an alternative to dependency-focused welfare arguments. Participants who increase their earnings while receiving federal rental assistance have a portion of what would otherwise be reduced subsidies deposited into an escrow account. Upon graduating from the program, they receive those accumulated savings as a lump sum -- often their first significant financial asset.

HUD commissioned a national impact evaluation in 2012. The final report found no significant earnings increase for participants relative to a comparison group. However, the author -- who has followed the program for most of its history -- identifies a critical methodological flaw: Congress changed the program parameters while the evaluation was underway, and HUD amended program rules mid-study. The treatment and comparison groups were therefore operating under different rules, undermining the comparison.

The Trump administration cited this evaluation to argue for defunding FSS in favor of block grants to states -- part of a 2025 proposal to restructure federal housing assistance broadly. HUD is simultaneously using the negative finding to promote alternatives like work requirements and time limits, approaches with a poor track record for low-income families facing structural employment barriers. A growing practice-based field outside the formal evaluation has produced more encouraging results that HUD leadership is choosing to ignore.

## Why This Matters for César

Frederick has residents in federally assisted housing. Understanding what FSS does, why it is being cut, and what the evidence actually shows is useful context for any conversation about the federal housing policy environment and what local governments may need to fill as federal programs are eliminated or block-granted to states. The FSS model -- savings-based, incentive-aligned, asset-building -- is also worth understanding as a policy design concept for local housing programs.
