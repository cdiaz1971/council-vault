# Housing -- NIMBYism Cycle and the YIMBY Counter-Strategy

## Summary

A retrospective on Kim-Mai Cutler's 2014 essay "How Burrowing Owls Lead to Vomiting Anarchists," framed as a 12-year-anniversary reading for North Carolina's emerging housing crisis. The piece argues that the same dynamics that produced San Francisco's housing collapse -- homeowner political dominance, restriction of supply, and the moralizing of opposition -- are now visible in North Carolina, and that the YIMBY response playbook is the right answer.

## Key Arguments

The SF housing crisis was produced by decades of restrictive zoning and organized homeowner opposition. Homeowners have a direct financial incentive to restrict supply because scarcity supports home value appreciation. This dynamic means roughly 80% of a city like San Francisco is not structurally oriented toward adding housing.

North Carolina is earlier in the same cycle, but the pattern is already visible: HOA-dominated suburbs, single-family zoning protections, and demand outpacing supply in the Triangle region.

The YIMBY movement's early wins came partly from transparency -- publishing the factually inaccurate and exclusionary arguments made by NIMBY groups during public comment. This kind of citizen journalism has barely begun in the South.

For young people, housing is existential. It determines whether they can stay in communities they care about, build stability, and plan ahead. The article argues that NIMBYism is backed by trillions in home equity, but that pressure is unsustainable against the lived experience of younger residents.

The correct policy response is pragmatic, supply-focused, and relentlessly accountability-oriented -- same as it was in SF in 2014.

## Relevance

Directly relevant to the [[Housing Production Target Resolution]] work. The YIMBY framing and the critique of "preservationist" NIMBYism are useful for shaping Frederick's supply-side housing argument. The dynamic of homeowners using political organizing to protect scarcity is visible in Frederick's LMC Review debate and in resistance to upzoning near the city center. The transparency tactic -- documenting and publishing opposition arguments -- is worth considering as a communications approach. See also [[LMC Review]] and [[Libby Taylor moratorium proposal]].
