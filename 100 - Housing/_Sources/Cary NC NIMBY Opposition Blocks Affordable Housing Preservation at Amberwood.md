# Cary NC NIMBY Opposition Blocks Affordable Housing Preservation at Amberwood

## Summary
A Cary, North Carolina town council hearing on allowing the Raleigh Housing Authority to purchase and preserve Amberwood at Lochmere, a 340 unit naturally occurring affordable housing complex, turned into public testimony stereotyping existing low income residents as a crime threat rather than a debate about the proposal's terms.

## Key Points
Amberwood at Lochmere is not subsidized housing. It is naturally occurring affordable housing, an older complex that already rents below market to households near 52 percent of area median income, and it was already 96 percent occupied. When the owner put the property up for sale, the risk was a private buyer renovating and raising rents, displacing current tenants. The Raleigh Housing Authority proposed buying the property and preserving all 340 units as affordable for at least 30 years. Because RHA is based outside Cary, it needed town council authorization to operate there, which is what the July hearing was about.

Much of the public opposition was not about financing structure, governance, or the rushed timeline. Speakers invoked violent crime, drugs, and neighborhood fear tied to the mere presence of housing assistance, despite the residents in question already living there. One resident's plea, quoted in the piece's title, asked council not to put affordable housing in his neighborhood, when the housing already existed and the vote was only about who would own and preserve it. A councilmember and another resident who had once relied on affordable housing herself pushed back, framing the proposal as preserving existing neighbors rather than importing new ones.

## Relevance
This is a preservation fight, not a production fight, which is the same distinction that runs through your Real Rent Ordinance and Housing Production Target work. Naturally occurring affordable housing disappearing to renovation and rent increases is a live risk in Frederick too, and this gives a concrete, well documented example of how NIMBY opposition reframes preservation as a threat narrative even when no new population is being introduced. Worth having on hand if similar rhetoric surfaces around any Frederick preservation proposal.
