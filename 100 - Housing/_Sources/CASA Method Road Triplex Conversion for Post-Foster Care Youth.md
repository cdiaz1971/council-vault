# CASA Method Road Triplex Conversion for Post-Foster Care Youth

## Summary

CASA owned a deteriorating 1960s triplex at 601 Method Road in Raleigh and was preparing to sell it. Raleigh's Missing Middle 2.0 text changes (summer 2021) turned the R-4 parcel into a nine-unit opportunity because it sat in a frequent-transit area. The finished building houses young adults aging out of foster care, with a supportive-service partner on site, and it never went through a rezoning hearing.

## How the Zoning Worked

- Base zoning R-4 offered nothing useful alone. The transit-area allowance permits roughly one unit per 1,800 square feet of qualifying area, which yielded nine units on a flat 0.43-acre corner lot.
- A tenth space was built as community office, which does not count against the unit cap.
- Raleigh's citywide elimination of parking minimums meant no waiver was needed. CASA built zero parking.
- The parcel predated 2001, so it was exempt from stormwater controls as long as impervious surface stayed under the limit. Skipping parking kept it under.
- CASA learned the rezoning was unnecessary almost by accident, when city staff pointed it out during a pre-application for a different property.

## Financing

Total development cost was $2.7 million.

- City of Raleigh bond funds, $700,000 (through a small-scale rental RFP)
- Wake County HOME funds through the CHDO set-aside, $760,000, the maximum at $95,000 per unit for households at or below 30% AMI
- Federal Community Project Funding grant secured by Rep. Deborah Ross, $1,000,000
- Other grants, $250,000

No contingency was drawn, no change orders, no deferred developer fee. The biggest risk was Congress failing to pass a budget, so CASA lined up a construction loan as a backstop that never had to be drawn. The CPF grant qualified for a categorical exclusion from full environmental review, and a Part 58 review was already underway for the HOME funds, which kept the schedule moving.

## Partnership and Residents

Hope Center at Pullen, connected through Raleigh's Continuum of Care, handles referrals, staffs the office, and provides case management. CASA owns and operates the building. Eight units go to Hope Center referrals and one to a resident advisor. Six units carry Foster Youth to Independence vouchers. A youth advisory board chose the building's name and finishes. More than 40% of youth aging out of foster care experience homelessness by 21.

Because housing authorities will not inspect before a certificate of occupancy, CASA used a two-stage lease, income-based at move-in and voucher-based after the CO, so residents could move in immediately.

## What Went Wrong

- The delivery schedule slipped, a real cost for residents who needed housing fast.
- An old sewer meter that should have been removed early was not.
- A city-requested right-of-way dedication beyond what the site required forced redesigns that shrank the building, plus a six-month encroachment agreement.
- Four rounds of site plan review for a half-acre parcel with no parking and no stormwater requirement, with substantive comments arriving late.
- Environmental review rules barred contractor selection early, so the builder could not give pre-construction input.

## CASA's Recommendations

Bring the general contractor on before locking the funding stack, hold twice-monthly site meetings with the architect, and refuse optional right-of-way dedications. The broader policy ask is that density reforms like Missing Middle must come with staffing and review timelines that catch design conflicts in the first round, not the fourth, so small developers do not sink tens of thousands into plans the review process has not caught up to.

## Why This Matters for Frederick

This is a working template for the Missing Middle and Parking LMC Package. It shows that transit-area density, parking elimination, and a supportive-service partner can produce deeply affordable units on a small existing lot by right. It also shows the gap CASA names directly, that a by-right reform underdelivers if staff review still behaves like discretionary review. Any Frederick LMC change should be paired with review-process expectations.
