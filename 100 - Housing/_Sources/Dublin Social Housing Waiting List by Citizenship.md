# Dublin Social Housing Waiting List by Citizenship

## Summary
Dublin City Council data shows non-Irish households are 42% of the social housing waiting list but receive 32% of offers. The release triggered calls for citizen-only allocation, and the author warns that restricting access by status would build a permanent racialised underclass.

## Key Points
This is a follow-up to the author's earlier post on the politics of allocation. Dublin City Council released waiting list and allocation data by nationality to independent councillor Gavin Pepper, and right-wing outlet Gript framed it as shocking and previously hidden. In fact national figures by citizenship status had been published all along in a Housing Agency report.

The Dublin numbers: Irish citizens are 58% of the list but about 68% of accepted offers. Non-Irish households are 42% of the list and 32% of offers, up from 28% of offers in 2020. Nationally, about 29% of the list is non-Irish, split roughly evenly between EU citizens and non-EU residents (mostly refugees and people with permission to remain). Most local authorities sit between 20% and 35%.

The context the author adds is that being on the list measures housing need. About 21% of Dublin residents are non-Irish citizens, so overrepresentation reflects higher need. 77% of Irish-born people own their home versus 37% of non-Irish-born households, 60% of immigrants rent privately versus 13.8% of the Irish-born, and more than a third of migrants spend over 30% of income on housing compared with under one in ten Irish-born residents.

Politically, the Taoiseach falsely told Fox News that only citizens are eligible and that citizens get priority. Minister Browne dodged the question on budget day. Simon Harris gave a clear defense of eligibility for long-term residents who contribute, though he called multilingual outreach "bizarre." Councillors from Independent Ireland and Fianna Fáil requested the same data, and some called for citizen priority.

The author predicts the governing parties will not touch EU citizens' rights because of EU law, but may reweight allocation toward time on the list or time resident, or restrict refugees. He calls that cynical "smart politics" that baits the left and splits Sinn Féin's vote, at the cost of a permanent racialised underclass.

## Relevance
This is the real-world test of the earlier note's warning: when data is released without a ready normative defense of need-based allocation, nativist framing fills the gap. For Frederick, the lesson applies to any eligibility rules for city-funded affordable housing and to how the city publishes demographic data on program participants. Have the need-based rationale ready before anyone asks for the breakdown.
