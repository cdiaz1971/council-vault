# Municipal Grocery Ownership Models -- From Vision to Operation

## Summary

Local Progress hosted the second session of its municipal grocery series on March 19, 2026, focused on implementation and ownership models. The session covered the range of institutional structures cities can use to create or sustain grocery access in food deserts: full-service municipal stores, nonprofit operators, cooperatives, and hybrid models. The framing is that government needs to get back into the business of service delivery, not just goal-setting.

## Key Points

The session was led by Alex Birnel (Local Progress), Rial Carver (Rural Grocery Initiative, Kansas State), and Erion Malasi (Economic Security Project).

The core argument is that food access gaps require governments to build "right fit" institutions rather than simply mandate outcomes. Ownership model options include:

**Full municipal operation** -- city owns and operates the store directly. Highest control, highest political and operational risk.

**Nonprofit operator** -- city provides space, subsidy, or anchor support; nonprofit runs operations. Tested model in several mid-size cities.

**Cooperative** -- community-owned, member-governed. Strong equity alignment, slower to scale, requires organizing capacity.

**Hybrid** -- mixed ownership/operator arrangements. Most flexible, most complex governance.

Peer examples referenced: Atlanta's Azalea Market (city-backed) and Illinois/Venice grocery development (state-backed groundbreaking under Gov. Pritzker).

## Why This Matters for César

Food access is an equity issue that intersects directly with Frederick's housing and community development work. The Golden Mile corridor has historically lacked full-service grocery options. This session gives a concrete policy menu -- not just advocacy goals -- for what the city could actually do. The cooperative and nonprofit operator models are likely the most viable for Frederick's scale and political context. The Economic Security Project policy background paper is worth reading before any council discussion on this topic.
