## Core Idea

The care affordability crisis and the care worker quality crisis are the same problem. Demos makes the case that public investment in child care, elder care, and disability care is insufficient without parallel policies that build worker power. Care workers -- disproportionately women, Black, and brown workers -- face low wages, high turnover, and limited workplace voice. These are not market failures; they are policy choices.

The explainer identifies four outcomes that flow from combining public investment with worker power: expanding the care workforce, improving care quality, securing democratic voice for marginalized workers, and building lasting political coalitions for public care funding.

## State Models Referenced

Demos points to Michigan, California, and Minnesota as jurisdictions that have expanded labor rights, set stronger wage and quality standards, and improved job conditions for care workers. These models are worth tracking as Frederick examines its Housing and Human Services budget commitments.

## Why This Matters for César

This connects directly to the FY2027 Housing and Human Services budget session (April 9 as key leverage point). Frederick's care infrastructure -- child care access, disability support, elder care -- shapes housing stability outcomes. If care workers can't afford to live in Frederick, the care system fails. The Demos framing gives language for linking workforce conditions to housing affordability in budget arguments.
