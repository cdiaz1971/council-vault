# Maryland Tenants' Bill of Rights - October 2026 Edition

## Summary
The Office of Tenant and Landlord Affairs (OTLA) in DHCD must publish this document and update it every year. Landlords must attach the latest version to every residential lease. This edition took effect October 1, 2026. It summarizes significant rights under Maryland law, notes that local jurisdictions may add protections, and states that it is general information, not legal advice. Statutory citations are given at the end of each section and are kept below. A copy is in Google Drive under City council / 02 - Legislation / Tenant Protection.

## Key Points

**1. Rental applications** (RP Subtitle 8-2A, §§ 8-210, 8-213, 8-218; 42 U.S.C. 3604; SG § 20-705.1)
- Landlords with five or more units may keep no more than $25 of an application fee unless actual costs, such as credit checks, are higher. Any unused amount over $25 must be refunded within 15 days of move-in or of written notice that the applicant will not rent.
- Landlords with five or more units must disclose in writing, before taking a fee, whether they screen criminal history.
- Applicants may request an itemized list of application expenses with amounts.
- If a landlord chooses to accept a tenant-supplied screening report (prepared by a consumer reporting agency within 30 days and paid for by the tenant), it may not charge an application fee.
- **Criminal history screening:** screening is optional, but a landlord who screens anyone must screen everyone. Landlords with more than five units who screen must make a **conditional offer before checking criminal history**. Before the offer they may only ask about a sexual offense, child pornography, first- or second-degree murder, human trafficking, or any crime requiring sex offender registration (plus methamphetamine production in federally assisted housing). An offer may be withdrawn only for a substantial, legitimate, nondiscriminatory reason tied to one of those crimes or a felony in the previous five years, with written reasons. Applicants may ask for reconsideration with evidence of inaccuracy, rehabilitation, or mitigating factors. The landlord must weigh nature and severity, age at offense, time elapsed, whether it happened in a residence, risk to other tenants, and evidence of rehabilitation.
- Landlords with more than five units may not advertise that they will not consider applicants with arrests or convictions, unless federal law requires it.
- **Housing subsidy holders:** a landlord may not refuse an applicant paying with an income-based subsidy because of income, credit score, lack of credit score, or negative credit history from a period without the subsidy. Income may be checked only against the tenant's share of rent, using the same rent-to-income ratio applied to other tenants.
- Pet policy (breed and weight limits, number, all fees and deposits, vaccination and insurance requirements) must be included in the application.
- Assistance animals are not pets. No added application fee or deposit may be charged for them, though damage may be charged.

**2. Leases and tenancies** (RP §§ 8-5A-01 to 8-5A-03, 8-208, 8-210, 8-402; Baltimore City PLL § 9-16)
- Landlords with five or more units must use a written lease. Without a written lease, tenancy is presumed to be one year.
- A written lease must include a statement of the property's condition, who pays which utilities and repairs, a security deposit receipt, the current Bill of Rights, and contact information for the landlord or agent. **Local jurisdictions may require additional lease content.**
- A lease may not include confession of judgment, waiver of legal rights or jury trial, shortened notice periods, or other terms barred by § 8-208.
- Automatic renewal terms must be separate and separately signed.
- Notice to end tenancy: landlord gives 60 days for fixed-term and month-to-month, 90 days for year-to-year. Tenant gives 30 days, or 90 for year-to-year (30 in Baltimore City).
- **Abuse:** a tenant may end the lease early with notice and a copy of a final protective or peace order or a report from a qualified third party. The tenant has up to 30 days to move and owes rent only until move-out, with a notarized notice required if leaving in under 30 days.

**3. Rent** (RP §§ 8-205, 8-205.1, 8-208, 8-209)
- **Late fees are capped at 5% of the late amount, and only on the unpaid portion** of a partial payment.
- Receipts are required for cash payments or on request.
- Rent may only be raised at renewal, with 90 days' written notice in most cases. There is no statewide cap on renewal increases. Electronic notice is valid only if the tenant agreed to it.

**4. Utilities** (RP §§ 8-122, 8-208, 8-211, 8-212; Env. § 9-4A-04)
- Units must have heat, light, electricity, hot and cold water, and sewage disposal.
- **Private wells:** landlord must share the latest test at lease signing and test every three years. If contaminated, the landlord must supply drinkable water and within 60 days fix the problem permanently or let the tenant end the lease.
- **Air conditioning, June 1 to September 30:** required in buildings with at least 10 rental units where the landlord provided AC after June 1, 2025, the lease requires AC, or the unit is renovated with electrical or heating systems replaced. Must be able to cool habitable space to 80°F measured three feet above the floor.
- If the tenant pays utilities to the landlord, the landlord must provide monthly bills or, at the start of each term, the prior year's totals broken out by water, sewer, gas, and electricity.
- RUBS must be disclosed before signing, with the last two bills and the allocation method.

**5. Security deposits** (RP §§ 8-203, 8-203.1)
- Maximum one month's rent in most cases. Up to two months only for leases signed before October 1, 2024, or for tenants on DHS utility assistance who pay utilities to the landlord.
- A tenant may voluntarily offer more, but the landlord may not ask for or suggest it, or change requirements to induce it.
- Pet deposits count as security deposits.
- Up-front payment is limited to first month's rent plus the deposit.
- Deposit must be held in an interest-bearing account, with a written receipt in the lease and notice of rights.
- The tenant may attend the move-out inspection by sending a certified letter at least 15 days before leaving.
- Itemized deductions with supporting documents, and return with interest, within 45 days. Allowed deductions: damage beyond normal wear and tear, unpaid rent or utilities, and eviction judgment costs. Failure to comply can forfeit the right to withhold and expose the landlord to up to three times the amount withheld.

**6. Condition of units** (RP §§ 8-204, 8-211, 8-212, 8-215, 8-220)
- Units must be fit for human habitation. A code citation or violation notice counts as written notice to the landlord.
- **Mold:** assessment within 15 days of written notice. If found, remediate and fix the cause within 45 days if possible, otherwise within a reasonable time, keeping the tenant informed.
- **Entry: at least 24 hours' notice** except in emergencies.
- Remedies for serious unrepaired conditions: suit for damages and rent reduction, defense to a rent claim, or rent escrow.
- Lead paint: possible relocation payments and legal action.
- Right to quiet enjoyment and to occupy on the lease start date.

**7. Evictions** (RP §§ 8-401, 8-402, 8-402.1, 8-407, 8-902)
- Eviction requires a District Court judgment of possession and a warrant of restitution. **Lockouts, utility shutoffs, and similar self-help are prohibited.**
- Access to Counsel in Evictions: free attorney for tenants at or below 50% of Area Median Income (211 or legalhelpmd.org), available as soon as the tenant receives notice.
- 10 days' written notice before filing a failure-to-pay-rent case. **"Rent" in those cases means base rent only, not utility charges, maintenance charges, or penalties.** Subsidized tenants may be owed longer notice.
- After judgment the tenant has seven days before the landlord can seek a warrant.
- Right of redemption by paying past-due rent and court costs, unless the tenant had three or more judgments in the prior 12 months (four in Baltimore City) and the landlord objects.
- Written notice before the scheduled eviction: six days under state law. **Local jurisdictions may set between 4 and 14 days.**

**8. Constructive eviction.** If the landlord fails to fix a major problem after notice and reasonable time, and the tenant moves out within a reasonable time, the lease may be treated as terminated and the tenant may recover moving costs, rent differences, and attorney fees.

**9. Tenant right of first refusal** (RP § 8-119). Named tenants who have lived at least six months in a property with one to three units get notice and 30 days to match a sale offer. Excludes transfers to family, the landlord's business, or by court order. Local jurisdictions may add their own purchase rights.

**10. Prohibited acts** (42 U.S.C. 3601 et seq.; SG §§ 20-101, 20-704, 20-705; RP §§ 2-115, 8-2A-07, 8-204, 8-208.1, 8-208.2; Crim. Law § 3-701)
- Protected classes: race, color, religion, sex, disability, marital status, familial status, sexual orientation, gender identity, national origin, **source of income**, and military status. Local jurisdictions may add more.
- Retaliation is prohibited for good faith complaints, lawsuits, tenant organizing, calling police or emergency services, and lead paint reports. Retaliation includes suing or threatening to sue, **threatening to report immigration status**, raising rent, cutting services, failing to repair, and terminating tenancy.
- **It is a crime for anyone, including a landlord, to try to obtain money, property, labor, or services by threatening to report someone's immigration status.**
- Entry: written notice at least 24 hours ahead, **Monday through Saturday, 7 a.m. to 7 p.m.**, except emergencies, unless the tenant gives written permission for shorter notice.

**11. Resources.** OTLA (855-511-3423, otla.dhcd@maryland.gov, phone support in the caller's language), MCCR for discrimination complaints, ACE through 211 or LegalHelpMD.org, District Court Self-Help Centers (410-260-1392), and the People's Law Library.

## Relevance
- **Update trigger for [[Tenant Protections]].** That page lists "DHCD publishes a new Bill of Rights" as a reason to update, and its footnotes cite the 2025 edition. This is the replacement.
- **Corrections to [[Tenant Protections Survey Policy Brief - 2026-10-08]].**
  - Entry notice is already state law: 24 hours, written, Monday to Saturday, 7 a.m. to 7 p.m. The brief listed it as a possible local tool.
  - Air conditioning is partly state law for larger buildings. A local rule would only matter for smaller buildings or units outside the triggers.
  - Criminal-history screening already has a statewide conditional-offer process for landlords with more than five units. A local fair chance rule would extend it to smaller landlords or go further, as Montgomery County's Housing Justice Act does.
  - Private well testing is already required. The question Clara raised about wells is covered.
  - The late fee cap (5%, unpaid portion only) is confirmed. The domestic violence early termination is in RP Subtitle 8-5A, confirming the brief's correction of Clara's citation.
  - Self-help eviction, lockouts, and landlord utility shutoffs are confirmed prohibited.
- **Real Rent.** "Rent" in a nonpayment case is base rent only. That supports Real Rent's approach of making the advertised and charged total rent honest, and it means fees folded into "rent" by lease language cannot drive a failure-to-pay-rent eviction under state law.
- **Immigrant renters.** The retaliation and criminal-law provisions on immigration threats are directly relevant to the [[City Values Act]] and to Frederick's outreach materials.
- **Local room the document itself identifies:** additional lease content, additional anti-discrimination protections, local right-of-first-refusal or purchase programs, and the 4-to-14-day pre-eviction notice window. Who exercises that last option (county or municipality) needs checking.
- **ACE eligibility wording:** this edition says 50% of Area Median Income. The Tenant Protections page and Rockville's February 2026 staff report describe it as 50% of state median income. Confirm which is current before using it in public materials.
- **Handbook use.** This is the right baseline document for a Frederick renter landing page and handbook, with a Frederick-specific layer on top.
