# NC Statewide Parking Minimum Repeal as Peer City Model

## Summary

NC Governor Stein signed the Parking Lot Reform and Modernization Act (H162), making North Carolina the first state to eliminate nearly all parking minimums statewide, effective January 2027. The bill covers 90% of the state's population and includes an environmental stormwater incentive provision.

## Key Points

The Parking Lot Reform and Modernization Act takes effect January 1, 2027. It eliminates parking minimums across housing and commercial projects for roughly 90% of North Carolina's population. Coastal counties retain parking minimums. Prior to this, some cities had already removed parking mandates, and some states had limited minimums near transit or in housing projects, but no state had gone statewide across both housing and commercial.

Three distinct wins packaged together in the bill: lower construction costs (builders choose their own parking levels), reduced environmental harm from surface parking lots (stormwater runoff, flooding, pollution), and more land available for housing in walkable areas. The Catawba Riverkeepers was a named coalition partner -- it demonstrates that environmental organizations were embedded in the reform coalition, not fighting it. The bill also gives municipalities the option to offer incentives for stormwater protection improvements when properties are redeveloped.

The Raleigh section of the brief was behind a paywall and not available for capture.

## Relevance

This is the most significant peer city precedent in the parking reform space since Dallas. NC is now ahead of Maryland on this. Frederick's LMC reform includes parking minimum changes, and the NC model is now available as a "first in the nation" citation for advocacy. The stormwater-housing linkage framing also maps cleanly onto Frederick's flooding and stormwater infrastructure challenges in the Golden Mile and East Frederick corridors.

The environmental coalition piece is worth noting for Frederick strategy: bringing Catawba Riverkeepers-equivalent partners into a parking reform fight reframes it as a multi-benefit environmental bill, not just a housing production bill. That shifts the political terrain.
