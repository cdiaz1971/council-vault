# Maryland Housing Certainty Act -- Developer Vesting Rights 2026

## Summary
The Maryland Housing Certainty Act (HB548/SB325) passed both chambers of the General Assembly and is headed to Governor Moore for signature. The bill gives developers vested rights in an approved housing project for a minimum of five years from the time a complete application is submitted, locking in the laws and regulations in effect at that moment. It is the centerpiece of Moore's 2026 housing package and revives a proposal that failed in the 2025 session.

## Key Points

**What the bill does:** Once a local authority approves a housing development application, the developer's right to build is locked in under the laws and zoning regulations in effect at the time of submission -- not the time of construction. The vesting period lasts at least five years, or longer if the local regulatory authority determines. This is called "early vesting," as opposed to the current Maryland standard where rights vest only late in the process.

**Legislative history:** A similar vesting rights provision was part of Moore's 2025 housing package. That bill did not receive a hearing until March 2025, was heavily rewritten, and ultimately died in the Senate Energy, Education and Environment Committee. Moore's team started working with county officials as early as August 2025 to build the coalition needed to pass it in 2026.

**Vote counts:** House passed 124-7. Senate passed 34-12. Strong bipartisan margins.

**Key opposition resolved:** The Maryland Association of Counties (MACo) was the primary opponent in 2025, arguing that early vesting intruded on local land use authority and removed communities' ability to update standards as needs change. MACo's executive director Michael Sanderson signaled openness to compromise heading into 2026 and ultimately supported SB325 in committee testimony. The final bill includes coordination language addressing MACo's concerns about ambiguous overlaps with existing local processes.

**Sponsors:** Sen. Malcolm Augustine (D-Prince George's) and Del. Dylan Behler (D-Anne Arundel).

**Context -- Maryland housing shortage:** State comptroller reported approximately 100,000 unit shortage statewide as of October 2025, with a need for 590,000 new units by 2045 to meet growth projections.

## Relevance
This bill directly affects Frederick's development pipeline. If signed, developers who have submitted complete applications can lock in current zoning and fee structures for at least five years -- limiting the city's ability to update standards mid-process. This has direct implications for the LMC review: changes produced by that process would not apply to projects where a complete application is already on file. Worth tracking closely alongside [[Government Operations Committee -- LMC Review and Consulting Moratorium 2026-04-06]] -- Libby Taylor's proposed moratorium on new building during the LMC review takes on different weight if state law simultaneously locks in vested rights on applications already submitted. Also connects to [[Housing Production Target Resolution]] and the broader state-local tension around housing supply.
