# Pensacola Uses Community Land Trust to Guarantee Permanent Affordability on City-Donated Land

## Summary
Pensacola donated the former Malcolm Yonge Gym site to the Northwest Florida Community Land Trust to build nine homes with annual cost capped at one-third of a qualified buyer's income, its first project structured for permanent rather than point-in-time affordability.

## Key Points
The gym had sat vacant and deteriorating until the city determined repair costs would exceed $3 million and voted to demolish it despite organized community pushback to save the building. Rather than sell the cleared site outright, the city routed it through the Northwest Florida Community Land Trust, created in 2022 by Pensacola Habitat for Humanity, so the land itself stays under the trust's ownership while homes on it are sold to income-qualified buyers. That structure is what makes the affordability permanent: because the trust retains the land, resale price and appreciation stay capped by formula rather than reverting to market rate the first time a home resells, the failure mode of most one-time subsidized affordable units.

## Relevance
This is a live peer example of exactly the mechanism named in Section 4 of the Housing Production Target Resolution, using city-owned or underutilized public land as a production lever, paired with the community land trust framework already laid out in [[Frederick Community Land Trust -- Policy Brief]]. It is a concrete, recently completed case to cite when building council support for the resolution: an underused municipal property (here, a demolished gym site) converted into permanently affordable homes instead of a one-time sale that eventually filters back to market rate. Worth checking whether Frederick has a comparable underutilized city-owned parcel that could pair with the CLT model the same way.
