# An Even Better Way to Zone -- Book Reference (Donald Elliott)

## Summary

A Planetizen review of Donald Elliott's updated zoning framework book, which builds on his 2008 work by focusing on practical application rather than theory. Key reframe: zoning is a governance system that defines legal rights to use property, not a wish list -- and it needs to anticipate ongoing change, especially in built-out places.

## Key Points

Elliott advocates for administrative flexibility for redevelopment: allowing staff to approve small, justified deviations to standards like setbacks or parking for constrained infill sites, rather than requiring full variance processes. This makes the code more responsive without sacrificing legal defensibility.

The book addresses zoning's exclusionary roots directly and argues for using a tool designed to exclude to produce more inclusive outcomes -- a frame directly relevant to missing-middle and MPDU reform conversations.

Environmental sustainability is integrated as a core pillar, reflecting how far the zoning conversation has moved since 2008.

The audience is practitioners on the front lines of zoning who need to balance legal defensibility with creative community design.

## Relevance

Worth reading or assigning to staff as a reference for Frederick's LMC rewrite process. The "governance system, not a wish list" framing is useful for council communications when explaining why LMC reform requires sustained engagement rather than one-time adoption. The infill and redevelopment focus is directly applicable to East Frederick and the missing-middle work. See [[LMC Reform Frederick -- Overview]].
