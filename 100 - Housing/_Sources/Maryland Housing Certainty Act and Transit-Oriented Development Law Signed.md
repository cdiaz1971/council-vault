# Maryland Housing Certainty Act and Transit-Oriented Development Law Signed

## Summary

Governor Moore signed two major housing bills at his final bill signing of the 2026 session on May 26, 2026. The Maryland Housing Certainty Act creates vesting rights for housing developers -- locking in local regulations at the time of project application rather than allowing changes mid-construction -- and delays certain fee payments to counties for new housing development. The Maryland Transit and Housing Opportunity Act encourages housing near transit hubs by restricting parking minimums and prohibiting collection of certain county taxes until near project completion. Together they respond to what Moore described as a 94,000-unit housing shortage in Maryland.

## Key Points

**Housing Certainty Act (HB 548 / SB 325).** The core mechanism is vesting rights: once a housing project application is complete, the developer is protected from changes in local regulations that would alter the project or add costs mid-stream. Senate President Ferguson framed it as a promise that "the rules don't move out from under" approved projects. The law also delays fee payments to county officials for new development, intended to reduce early-stage cost burdens. The vested rights protection also constrains the ability of local governments to course-correct after approval if community concerns emerge post-approval but pre-construction.

**Fee payment deferral.** Impact fees and similar charges due at permitting are a significant upfront cost; deferring them until projects are underway improves cash flow for smaller developers who cannot easily access construction financing.

**Transit and Housing Opportunity Act (HB 894).** Restricts parking minimums and certain local decisions near transit hubs in specific situations. Prohibits collection of certain county taxes until a project is close to completion. The stated goal is to unlock 300+ acres of underutilized transit-adjacent land and produce at least 7,000 new Maryland homes.

**Starter and Silver Homes Act.** The third element of Moore's housing package -- encouraging smaller units for new families and older residents looking to downsize -- died in committee without a vote in either chamber. This is the missing piece of the supply-side agenda most directly relevant to entry-level affordability.

**Legislative context.** This is Moore's second attempt at state housing legislation; a similar push for developer certainty was rejected in 2025. The session opened with Comptroller Brooke Lierman naming housing unavailability as a primary drag on economic growth.

## Relevance

**LMC timing risk.** The Housing Certainty Act's vesting rights mechanism means developers who apply before LMC modernization is complete lock in current (potentially less restrictive) standards. This creates a timing incentive to apply early. Monitor whether legal staff identifies this as a factor in the LMC timeline.

**Affordable housing conditions.** If the Act limits mid-project rule changes, affordable housing conditions need to be locked in at approval -- not imposed later. This affects how Council structures approval conditions for the Real Rent Ordinance and MPDU reform.

**Parking reform backing.** The parking minimum restrictions in the Transit and Housing Opportunity Act align with the LMC's direction on parking reform and may provide state backing for Frederick's own parking minimum reductions near the transit station and Carroll Creek corridor.

**Missing-middle gap.** The death of the Starter and Silver Homes Act means the missing-middle and small-unit supply gap remains unaddressed at the state level. Frederick's own LMC reforms -- allowing ADUs, reducing minimum lot sizes -- carry more weight in that context.
