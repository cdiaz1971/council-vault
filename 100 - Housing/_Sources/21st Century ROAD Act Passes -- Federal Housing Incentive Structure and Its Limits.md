# 21st Century ROAD Act Passes -- Federal Housing Incentive Structure and Its Limits

## Summary

The 21st Century ROAD to Housing Act passed the U.S. Senate on June 22, 2026, and the House on June 23, heading to President Trump for signature. The National League of Cities celebrated it as federal recognition of local housing leadership. The bill uses incentive structures rather than mandates, which limits its reach but also its political exposure.

## Key Points

NLC framed the bill as capitalizing on "local leadership cities nationwide are embracing to confront the housing attainability crisis." That framing is notable -- the federal posture here is to reward local action rather than require it. The bill does not override local zoning. It does not mandate production targets. It creates an incentive architecture that cities can opt into.

The vault already contains a research note on this bill's structural weaknesses: see [[Federal Housing Bills ROAD Act and H21 -- Incentive Structures and Their Weaknesses]]. The core concern documented there is that voluntary incentive structures produce uneven uptake -- cities with the most restrictive zoning and the greatest need are often also the ones least positioned to meet the conditions required to receive incentive funding.

That said, the bill's passage is a political signal worth tracking. Federal housing legislation of any kind has been difficult to move; the bipartisan passage of a production-focused bill indicates at least nominal consensus that supply is part of the answer.

## Relevance

For Frederick's Housing Production Target Resolution, the ROAD Act is not a resource -- the resolution is a local commitment, not a federal grant application. But the bill's passage provides useful external validation that production targets and local housing action are the direction of federal policy, not contrary to it. That framing helps with the introduction case.

Watch for whether the bill's final text includes any reporting or planning requirements that Frederick would need to satisfy to access incentive funding. If it does, the Housing Production Target Resolution could serve as the qualifying framework.
