# Eviction Prevention as Local Housing Stability Policy

Preventing evictions is cost-effective and politically defensible housing policy at the local level -- it keeps housed people housed without the infrastructure cost of new development, and avoids the political and fiscal weight of homelessness response after the fact.

Shelterforce's April 2026 overview identified several policy levers available to state and local governments. Right-to-counsel guarantees in eviction proceedings (as in San Francisco and other jurisdictions) dramatically reduce eviction rates by evening out the legal imbalance between tenant and landlord in housing court. Eviction prevention funds provide one-time rental assistance that resolves arrearages before they reach the courthouse. Good cause eviction protections -- which guarantee lease renewal when tenants are current and compliant -- reduce retaliatory and pretextual evictions without demonstrably suppressing new construction, per recent empirical work including a study endorsed by 54 housing economists. Mediation and payment plan requirements, including programs like New York City's housing stability model, keep disputes from escalating to court filings.

The article was framed around preventing unnecessary evictions at a moment when federal homelessness support is contracting -- making the local cost-avoidance argument more urgent.

## Why This Matters for César

The Libby Taylor renter-protection request during the Housing Production Target Resolution discussion reflects real constituent pressure. This Shelterforce overview is background material for what "stronger renter protections" actually looks like in peer-city practice. Frederick does not currently have right-to-counsel, a formal eviction prevention fund, or good cause protections. Any of these could be introduced as companion policy to the production resolution and would address the affordability-without-displacement gap the resolution leaves open.
