# Philadelphia Housing Dashboard Cut Zoning Appeals From 78 to 12 Days

## Summary
Philadelphia tied its 30,000-unit housing goal to 39 tracked implementation steps and a public dashboard, and used performance management to cut Zoning Board of Adjustment appeal times from 78 days to 12, a direct model for the housing dashboard in Frederick's affordable housing resolution.

## Key Points
Mayor Cherelle Parker set a target to build and preserve 30,000 units, based on an analysis of local housing gaps. Her team broke the goal into 39 discrete steps and tracks them on a public PhillyStat 360 dashboard to keep urgency. The measurable win so far is procedural: performance management on the Zoning Board of Adjustment brought appeals from a high of 78 days down to 12.

The column, from What Works Cities, also points to San Jose using AI to prescreen housing permit applications and Pittsburgh automating voucher scanning so staff can spend time helping residents find housing. Housing, infrastructure, and economic development top the NLC's 2026 State of the Cities priorities.

## Relevance
The Housing Production Target Resolution names a housing dashboard among its nine priority recommendations, and this is the strongest peer example of what that dashboard should do: track implementation steps and process times, not just units. Frederick's 4,000-unit floor would be more defensible if paired with a short list of city-controlled steps (permit times, APFO decisions, MPDU recalibration) that can be reported the same way. It also fits the Rorty note's point about pairing an output measure with a visible account of what the city actually delivered.
