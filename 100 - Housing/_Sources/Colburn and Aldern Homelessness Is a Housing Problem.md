# Colburn and Aldern Homelessness Is a Housing Problem

## Summary
Former Raleigh Mayor Pro Tem Nicole Stewart reviews Colburn and Aldern's Homelessness Is a Housing Problem, whose data shows regional homelessness rates track housing supply and cost, not poverty, addiction, mental illness, weather, or generous services. The fix is zoning and development policy plus funding and faster system exits.

## Key Points
The book's first half tests and rejects the common explanations for why some places have more homelessness than others. Individual factors such as poverty, unemployment, mental illness, drug use, and race, and place factors such as climate, local politics, and an abundance of services, do not explain the regional variation once the data is laid out. Housing market tightness does.

Because supply is shaped by government choices, the problem is solvable. Zoning and development rules either stimulate or restrict the growth that keeps housing in line with population. The authors close with three moves for communities: change how the public understands the problem, fund it adequately at every level of government, and fix the response system so people exit homelessness faster or never enter it.

Stewart writes from experience. After leaving Raleigh City Council in 2022 she became active in the local homeless response system and now serves as Past Chair of the Wake Continuum of Care. She uses the book to rebut claims that homelessness cannot be ended.

## Relevance
This gives Frederick a citable, data-backed frame that ties homelessness directly to the land use agenda rather than treating it as a separate social services problem. It supports linking the [[Homelessness]] page to the supply arguments in [[Starter Homes Over $1M in 242 Cities -- Supply Restriction Is the Variable]] and [[NYT Housing Cost Map -- Supply Permissiveness Predicts Affordability]], and its third recommendation lines up with [[Systems Thinking in Homelessness Prevention -- The Threshold Framework]]. Worth having on hand when anyone frames homelessness in Frederick as an addiction or public safety issue. The book itself may be worth adding to the reading list.
