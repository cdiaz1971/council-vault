## Summary

Raleigh is embarking on its first full Comprehensive Plan rewrite in over 15 years. CITYBUILDER, a pro-housing advocacy publication by Jenn Truman, frames this as a values question: not where growth belongs, but how every neighborhood can help welcome it.

## Key Concepts

**Comp Plan vs. UDO:** The Comprehensive Plan is the city's long-term vision document -- not binding law, but the lens through which all rezoning decisions, infrastructure investments, transit priorities, and capital planning are evaluated. The UDO (Unified Development Ordinance) is the rulebook; the Comp Plan is the vision the rulebook is meant to serve.

**History:** Raleigh adopted its current Comprehensive Plan in 2009. The city adopted a new UDO in 2015, completed a citywide rezoning (largely downzoning) in 2016, and made significant amendments in 2019 to embrace more housing choices and walkability. This update is the first clean-slate rewrite since the original.

**Reflecting Raleigh:** The city's planning department ran a Phase 1 public engagement initiative with over 12,000 resident participants -- surveys, pop-up events, a civic assembly. CITYBUILDER encourages engagement with those materials but is explicit that its own role is advocacy, not neutrality.

**CITYBUILDER's position:** Pro-housing in every neighborhood. Transportation choices beyond car dependency. Infrastructure that scales with population. Explicitly backs ADU homeowners, small neighborhood businesses, carpenters, nonprofits building affordable housing, local fourplex builders, and large development teams alike as all being "city builders."

## Relevance to Frederick

Raleigh's Comprehensive Plan rewrite is a peer-city case study in how a growing city frames the vision question before it starts adjusting rules. Frederick does not currently have a comparable comprehensive plan rewrite underway, but the framing here -- moving from managing growth to welcoming it -- maps directly onto the housing production target work and LMC reform efforts. The argument that Comp Plan consistency is the primary question in rezoning decisions is directly applicable to how Frederick evaluates incoming projects against its existing vision documents.
