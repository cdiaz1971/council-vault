# New Jersey FAIR Act Bans AI-Driven Rent Setting

## Summary

New Jersey became the first state to ban AI-powered rent-setting software under the FAIR Act, framing algorithmic pricing as an antitrust violation that drives coordinated rent increases across properties and markets.

## Key Points

Governor Mikie Sherrill signed the FAIR Act, which prohibits landlords from using AI-powered algorithms to set rent prices. The state framed the problem as a market competition issue: software like RealPage aggregates data from landlords and recommends near-identical rent increases across different properties and neighborhoods, effectively functioning as coordinated price-fixing even without explicit collusion.

The law makes using rent-setting software an antitrust violation. New Jersey is also pursuing a separate lawsuit against RealPage directly.

Bill co-sponsor Assemblywoman Katie Brennan noted the core harm: the software "recommends identical increases across different properties and neighborhoods, driving up rents and pricing people out of their communities." Governor Sherrill connected the practice directly to the state's affordability crisis and sharp rent increases.

The mechanism -- software that aggregates landlord data and generates uniform pricing recommendations -- reduces actual market competition while creating the appearance of independent pricing decisions by individual landlords.

## Relevance

This is a direct policy model for Frederick. The Real Rent Ordinance addresses rent increases after the fact; the FAIR Act addresses a root cause mechanism that coordinates increases in the first place. If algorithmic rent-setting software is operating among Frederick's larger landlords -- and given the presence of institutional property managers in the market, it likely is -- that is a structural driver of the affordability crisis that local rent stabilization cannot fully address alone.

Two angles worth tracking: (1) whether Maryland could pursue a parallel state-level ban, which would be worth raising with allies in Annapolis; (2) whether Frederick could reference the antitrust framing in strengthening the Real Rent Ordinance's justification section. The antitrust framing also gives the ordinance a less politically fraught entry point than "rent control."

The RealPage lawsuit and similar DOJ actions provide legal infrastructure that could support advocacy at both the state and local level.
