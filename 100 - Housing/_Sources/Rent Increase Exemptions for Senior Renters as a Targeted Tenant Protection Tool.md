# Rent Increase Exemptions for Senior Renters as a Targeted Tenant Protection Tool

## Summary

New York's Senior Citizen Rent Increase Exemption (SCRIE) freezes rent for income-eligible tenants 62+ in rent-regulated units, funded by property tax abatement. Los Angeles is developing a similar program. Both offer a peer model for targeted senior tenant protection that sidesteps broader rent control politics.

## Key Points

New York's SCRIE program is the most developed peer model:
- **Eligibility**: Tenant or spouse must be 62+, live in a rent-regulated unit, have household income under $75,000, and spend more than one-third of income on rent.
- **Mechanism**: Rent is frozen at current level; further increases and eviction protections apply. The landlord receives a property tax abatement in lieu of the rent increase.
- **Political structure**: The tax abatement mechanism frames this as landlord-neutral -- the city absorbs the cost rather than requiring landlords to forgo revenue, which reduces opposition.

Los Angeles is developing a companion program, the Rent Increase Exemption for Senior Renters (RIESR), modeled on SCRIE. Key differences from the NY model:
- Funded either through property tax abatement or through Measure ULA (a transfer tax on high-value property sales).
- Broader income threshold: eligibility at 80% AMI rather than a fixed dollar cap.
- Estimated reach: 3,000 to 4,000 older adult tenants per year in LA alone.
- A parallel Rent Increase Exemption for Disabled Renters (RIEDR) is being developed simultaneously.

A significant limitation of both programs: they apply only to tenants in rent-regulated units. Older adult renters in unregulated buildings -- like a 13-year-old affordable complex -- may not qualify, leaving the most precarious cases unprotected.

The economic argument for rent increase exemptions is fiscal as well as humanitarian: preventing homelessness among older adults is substantially cheaper than the public cost of sheltering and providing services to people who have lost housing.

Chicago's Senior Safety Ordinance (co-written by tenant organizers) takes a different approach -- additional habitability requirements for senior buildings during declared public health emergencies -- and offers another model for age-targeted protection.

## Relevance

The Frederick tenant protection package is the immediate application. A SCRIE-style exemption is politically defensible even in moderate-landlord environments because the cost is borne by the city (tax abatement) rather than the landlord directly. It also targets exactly the population most harmed by displacement: low-income older adults on fixed incomes with nowhere to go. Frederick's existing MPDU framework and the ongoing Rental Licensing Ordinance rewrite create an opening to layer in an income-based senior rent protection provision. The 80% AMI threshold used in the LA proposal maps well to Frederick's affordability definitions. This is worth raising with Sarah Hempel Irani as the Rental Licensing rewrite moves through committee.
