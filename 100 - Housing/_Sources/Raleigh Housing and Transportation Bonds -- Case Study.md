## Why this matters for César

Direct policy precedent for your work on the Housing Production Target and CIP. Raleigh is treating housing and transportation as a unified affordability problem — not "build housing here, add transit later." This integrated framing is stronger politically and more effective operationally. Their bond structure and public hearing process are worth studying for Frederick's next CIP/housing bond cycle.

## Core idea

**Transit is affordability infrastructure.** A household's total housing cost is housing cost + transportation cost. If you build housing far from jobs/services, you shift affordability burden from rent to transportation. Raleigh's bond treats these costs as a unified problem: housing bonding + transportation bonding as complementary, not competing, priorities. This is the "missing middle" framing applied to community infrastructure.

## Questions for Frederick

- What's Frederick's current capital budget split between housing and transit?
- Have you considered a unified "affordability infrastructure" bond proposal?
- What's the political appetite for tying housing production targets to transit/walkability standards?
- How would data center infrastructure (community benefit agreements) fit into this frame?
