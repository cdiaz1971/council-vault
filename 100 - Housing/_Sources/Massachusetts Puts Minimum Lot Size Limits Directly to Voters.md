# Massachusetts Puts Minimum Lot Size Limits Directly to Voters

## Summary
Massachusetts Question 7, on the November 3, 2026 ballot, would bar municipalities from requiring more than 5,000 square feet and 50 feet of frontage for a single-family home on lots with public water and sewer, the first zoning reform put directly to a statewide vote.

## Key Points
Large minimum lot sizes have long worked as a quiet exclusion tool, limiting how many homes fit on land and keeping neighborhoods expensive. Question 7 would cap what a municipality can require at 5,000 square feet on serviced lots, with the state housing office writing and enforcing compliance rules. Massachusetts needs an estimated 222,000 units; the MBTA Communities Act has produced about 7,000 units in development and the state has approved more than 2,000 ADUs, which advocates see as not enough.

The Legalize Starter Homes campaign, chaired by Andrew Mikula of the Pioneer Institute, says the point of going to the ballot is to show legislators that zoning reform is popular. The campaign is funded largely by real estate interests and the state realtors association. The Massachusetts Municipal Association opposes it on local-control grounds. Supporters note a state commission already recommended eliminating minimum lot sizes outside environmentally sensitive areas, and that the measure mainly affects village-like areas where many existing homes already sit on lots this size. Other states have moved through legislatures instead: Idaho capped minimum lot sizes, while Colorado struggled to pass the same idea.

## Relevance
Lot size minimums belong on the list of LMC barriers to missing middle and starter homes, alongside owner-occupancy and parking rules already addressed in the ADU work. The political lesson is the more useful one: a clear, narrow, popular reform can shift a legislature by demonstrating public support, which is the same strategy question facing the Housing Production Target Resolution. The funding mix (realtors, investment firms) is also a reminder that pro-housing coalitions are not automatically tenant coalitions.
