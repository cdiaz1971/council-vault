## Summary

North Carolina Governor Josh Stein signed Executive Order 36 on May 19, 2026, titled "Increasing Housing Opportunities for All North Carolinians." The order is notable less for any single provision than for its premise: state agencies have a role in solving the housing crisis, and they must work together. The analysis by Conor Mulderrig (CITYBUILDER) -- a former Climate Change Policy Advisor to Governor Cooper -- is the primary lens here.

## Key Points

Section 1 creates a Senior Advisor for Housing in the Governor's Office, filled by Janneke Ratcliffe (former VP at the Urban Institute). This is the structural innovation: for the first time, someone is explicitly tasked with seeing how all the pieces fit together across agencies that have historically operated in silos. NC lacks a muscular state housing entity beyond the NC Housing Finance Agency, which does not have a mandate to address cross-cutting policy drivers.

Section 2 is the substantive core. Key provisions: agencies will work with NCHFA to increase efficiencies for small-scale developers and small projects; an integrated housing information platform is directed linking zoning, infrastructure capacity, transportation, permitting, and market feasibility data across jurisdictions; DOT is directed to explore metrics for transportation project impacts on housing availability and to incorporate Complete Streets guidance into project design; DEQ is directed to bring Flood Resiliency Blueprint data into conversation with housing development patterns.

Section 3 directs the Department of Commerce to weight housing creation in Opportunity Zone nominations -- a potentially significant tool for linking federal investment to local zoning reform.

Mulderrig's critical read: the DOT directive is the most exciting provision, because NCDOT has historically refused to see itself as an active shaper of community design. The DEQ directive should go further -- resilience planning should affirmatively require density in safe areas, not just restrict building in hazardous ones.

## Why This Matters for Cesar

The NC model is directly relevant to Maryland and Frederick advocacy. The cross-agency coordination structure -- a Housing Senior Advisor with authority to break through silos -- is something Maryland lacks and Frederick should advocate for at the state level. The DOT-housing link is relevant to Frederick's own transportation-housing interface questions around the Golden Mile and east-west connectivity.
