# AI for Housing Permitting -- Use Cases and Guardrails

## Summary

Cities using data and AI to streamline permitting and track housing production targets are seeing measurable results -- DC built 36,000 units ahead of schedule, Philadelphia cut ZBA appeal times from 78 to 12 days -- but AI success depends on trustworthy data, clear policies, and active human oversight.

## Key Points

The article, from Bloomberg Philanthropies What Works Cities, focuses on three lessons for city leaders trying to use data and AI to accelerate housing production.

**Set ambitious but achievable goals and track publicly.** Washington DC's Mayor Bowser set a target of 36,000 new units by 2025 and accomplished it five months early. The city published affordable housing targets by neighborhood and tracked progress through a public dashboard. The public commitment created accountability across agencies and with private partners.

**Identify what you can actually influence.** Local leaders cannot control interest rates or material costs. They can control permitting speed, zoning, and access to publicly owned land. Philadelphia's performance management process reduced the Zoning Board of Adjustment's appeal process from 78 days to 12 days over seven months. Denver's cross-departmental permitting tiger team used data to reduce review times by 30%.

**Use AI as a force multiplier, carefully.** San Jose is using AI to prescreen permit applications for required documentation -- shaving days off permitting times and freeing staff. Pittsburgh is using AI to scan housing voucher applications for errors so staff can spend more time helping applicants find housing. Both are narrow, well-defined applications with clear human oversight.

The guardrails the article stresses: trustworthy underlying data, clear policies, active human oversight, community feedback loops, and regular auditing. AI amplifies whatever the underlying system is designed to do -- if the system is designed to help people, AI helps more people; if the system is designed to screen people out, AI screens more people out faster.

## Relevance

The DC public dashboard model is directly applicable to Frederick's [[Housing Production Target Resolution]]. One of the unresolved tensions in that resolution is how to make targets meaningful rather than aspirational -- a public tracking dashboard tied to specific production goals by area or housing type would do exactly that. The Philadelphia ZBA timing improvement is also relevant if Frederick's permitting process is a bottleneck for developers.

The guardrails section should be read alongside the Amsterdam Smart Check cautionary tale: AI tools in permitting are only as good as the underlying process they're accelerating.
