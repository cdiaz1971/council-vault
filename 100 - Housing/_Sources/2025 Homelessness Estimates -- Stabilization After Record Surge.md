# 2025 Homelessness Estimates -- Stabilization After Record Surge

## Summary

Preliminary 2025 estimates project a modest 2% decline in national homelessness to around 755,300 after a record-high 771,480 in January 2024 -- representing stabilization rather than reversal, with official HUD counts not expected until late 2026 or early 2027.

## Key Points

HUD's annual Point-in-Time count recorded 771,480 people experiencing homelessness in January 2024, an 18% surge from 2023. The nonprofit Community Solutions analyzed data from 170 communities and projects the 2025 count will fall to approximately 755,300 -- a roughly 2% decline. The organization characterizes this as stabilization: entries into homelessness are now more closely balanced with exits, rather than the sustained surge of prior years.

For context: 912,807 people experienced homelessness for the first time in 2024, a slight drop from 967,134 in 2023 but still far above pre-pandemic baselines. The official 2025 HUD count won't be released until late 2026 or early 2027.

The pattern suggests the emergency expansion of housing and shelter capacity in recent years may be reaching its limits as a buffer -- the numbers are stabilizing at a high floor rather than declining meaningfully.

## Why This Matters

Frederick does not face urban-scale homelessness, but the national trend data is relevant context for housing policy conversations. When advocates and residents frame the need for affordable housing production, workforce housing, and anti-displacement measures, the macro picture of a system at near-capacity nationally strengthens the case for local action. Also relevant to any conversation about HUD program cuts and their downstream effects on Maryland communities.
