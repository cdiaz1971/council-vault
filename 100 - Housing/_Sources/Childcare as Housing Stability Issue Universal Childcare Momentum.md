# Childcare as Housing Stability Issue Universal Childcare Momentum

## Summary

Children under five are the most common demographic facing eviction partly because childcare costs consume income that would otherwise cover rent; New Mexico became the first state to offer universal childcare in November, saving families an estimated $12,000 per year.

## Key Points

Research from eviction courts shows that young parents, who are early in their careers and more likely to earn low wages, face a compounding burden: high childcare costs plus low wages equals housing instability. Children under five are the most evicted demographic in the U.S.

New Mexico launched universal childcare in November, the first state to do so. Estimated household savings: $12,000 per year. New York Governor Kathy Hochul and NYC Mayor Zohran Mamdani are pushing for a similar program. Four states and DC already offer universal pre-K.

The policy logic: childcare is a housing affordability intervention. Reducing childcare costs frees household income that stabilizes housing tenure.

## Relevance

Useful framing for Frederick's housing stability work and tenant protection conversations. If childcare costs are a driver of eviction, then childcare access is a legitimate housing policy lever -- even at the municipal level where local advocacy for state-level programs is possible. Connects to Katie Nash's water shutoff and fee waiver work as part of the broader housing stability portfolio.
