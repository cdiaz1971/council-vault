# Commercial CLT Acquisition Lowers Barrier for Mission-Aligned Small Businesses

## Summary

South Tower Community Land Trust in Fresno acquired its first commercial property and leased to an indie bookstore, demonstrating how CLT commercial ownership removes the rent pressure that forces mission-aligned small businesses out of the neighborhoods they serve. The model stabilizes tenants who share community values but cannot compete with market-rate rents, and keeps both the land and the mission embedded in the neighborhood permanently.

## Key Points

Judging by the Cover, an indie bookstore focused on authors of color, queer, self-published, and indie-press authors, had tried and failed to find affordable storefront space in Fresno's Tower District -- rents were too high or vacant spaces needed more work than a startup could afford. After opening in Chinatown, they became South Tower CLT's first commercial tenant when the trust acquired a 2,300 sq ft building at 617-619 North Fulton Street in December 2025.

The CLT model changes the tenant's situation in several concrete ways: no risk of displacement due to market rent increases, no absentee commercial landlord, and the trust's offices are in the same building -- making the relationship relational rather than transactional. The bookstore owner joined the trust's board in January.

The acquisition cost $295,000 for the property plus renovation, totaling $477,000. Financing came from a $350,000 CDFI loan (Community Vision), private foundation grants, a bank motivated by CRA compliance, and individual donations as small as $5. The trust was able to acquire below asking price because there were no competing buyers and the owner granted a three-month closing window.

South Tower also uses community organizing influence over lots it doesn't own -- through three years of engagement, it shaped a new Tower District neighborhood plan approved by Fresno City Council in fall 2025, adding green space protections and fresh food access requirements.

The modern CLT movement originated with Black farmers in the rural south (New Communities, Inc.) protecting farmland during the civil rights era. Most of the roughly 225 CLTs in the country today focus on housing, but a small number -- Champlain Housing Trust in Vermont, Rondo CLT and Partnership in Property Commercial Land Trust in the Twin Cities, Brooklyn's East New York CLT, Denver's Tierra Colectiva, Boston's Dudley Street Neighborhood Initiative, and Oakland's OakCLT -- have moved into commercial or mixed-use acquisition.

## Relevance

Frederick does not yet have an active CLT, but [[Frederick Community Land Trust -- Policy Brief]] lays the groundwork. This Fresno case adds a dimension that the Frederick brief likely does not fully develop: CLT commercial ownership as an anti-displacement tool for small businesses, not just housing. The Golden Mile corridor, with its concentration of immigrant-owned restaurants, grocers, and community-serving businesses, is exactly the kind of neighborhood where commercial displacement pressure from redevelopment is a real risk. A Frederick CLT with a commercial acquisition mandate could preserve that ecosystem in ways that zoning alone cannot.

The bookstore owner's framing -- "anti-capitalist in nature," not trying to scale to the moon, a natural fit for community-focused operators -- describes the exact profile of many Golden Mile businesses. Worth incorporating into any future Frederick CLT policy pitch as a case study.
