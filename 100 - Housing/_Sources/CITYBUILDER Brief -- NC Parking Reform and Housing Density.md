## Summary

This issue of the CITYBUILDER Brief covers NC parking reform legislation (HB369) currently before the Ag Committee, a notable speech by Australia's Housing Minister calling explicitly for density as the solution to the country's housing shortage, and an ADU tour and training event. The full content is paywalled -- this is a stub from the available preview.

## Key Points (from preview)

- **NC parking reform at a critical juncture**: HB369 is before the Ag Committee; the brief calls on readers to contact their state senator
- **Australia housing minister on density**: Minister Clare O'Neil stated at the National Press Club that Australia needs to "build more dense cities, full stop," and noted that people are beginning to connect local housing development with their children's ability to afford homes -- framing density as a generational equity issue
- **Anglosphere housing shortages**: The brief cites Financial Times data showing the US, UK, Australia, and other English-speaking countries have experienced lower housing growth rates than European and East Asian peers
- **ADU tour and training**: An event was scheduled for June 6

## Why This Matters for Cesar

The Australia density framing is directly usable in Frederick housing production conversations. The political framing -- connecting density near you to your children's ability to afford a home -- is the kind of narrative bridge that could move skeptical constituents. This sits alongside the [[Housing Production Target Resolution]] and [[LMC Reform Commission]] threads.

NC parking reform tracking is also relevant as a peer-state example of how parking minimums reform moves through a legislative process. Worth following for playbook comparisons.
