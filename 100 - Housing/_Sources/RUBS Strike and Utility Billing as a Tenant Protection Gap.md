# RUBS Strike and Utility Billing as a Tenant Protection Gap

## Summary

Ratio Utility Billing Systems (RUBS) are a method by which landlords allocate shared utility costs across tenants using a formula -- rather than metering -- that allows them to raise effective rents while evading direct accountability for building maintenance and energy efficiency. The Virgil Square Tenants Association in Los Angeles has maintained a RUBS rent strike for nearly a year and is pushing the city to ban the practice entirely. The Debt Collective has submitted comments to the FTC on a related corporate landlord accountability issue.

## Key Points

RUBS work by distributing a building's total utility bill among tenants based on a ratio (unit size, occupancy, or other formula) rather than individual metering. The landlord pays the utility company and then bills tenants their allocated share, often with a markup. Because the landlord doesn't pay the marginal cost of inefficiency, there's no financial incentive to fix leaking pipes, upgrade HVAC systems, or improve insulation. The tenant bears the cost; the landlord pockets maintenance savings.

From a tenant protection standpoint, RUBS functions similarly to junk fees: it's a mechanism to extract additional revenue from tenants outside of the stated rent. It's also opaque -- tenants typically can't verify whether the allocation formula is accurate or whether the total bill being divided has been marked up.

The LA campaign is pushing for a complete ban at the city level, not just regulation. This is the appropriate policy ask: RUBS regulation tends to be captured by landlord lobbying; a ban removes the mechanism entirely.

The Debt Collective's parallel FTC comment on REIT mergers (Equity Residential and AvalonBay merging into a potential largest-landlord-in-the-country) situates RUBS within the broader corporate landlord consolidation dynamic. As ownership concentrates, opaque billing practices proliferate and tenant organizing becomes harder.

## Relevance

Frederick's Real Rent Ordinance currently addresses prohibited fees and anti-circumvention provisions. RUBS is a category of utility billing practice that likely falls in a gray zone under existing definitions -- it may not be captured as a "junk fee" even though it functions like one. This is worth raising with the City Attorney during Real Rent Ordinance review: does the ordinance as drafted prohibit RUBS-style utility billing, or does it need a specific provision? The rental licensing ordinance's new anti-retaliation and tenant protection provisions are also relevant context. Frederick's immigrant tenant population -- especially in District 2 -- is particularly vulnerable to opaque billing practices they may not recognize as manipulative.
