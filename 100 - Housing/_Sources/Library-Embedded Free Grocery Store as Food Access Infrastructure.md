# Library-Embedded Free Grocery Store as Food Access Infrastructure

## Summary

Prince George's County opened a free grocery store inside the Fairmount Heights Branch Library through a partnership with Goodr, an Atlanta-based company with 34 similar stores nationally since 2021. The model serves 200+ families per month and is designed around dignity -- residents shop, not receive charity. It is the first Goodr location in the DC area and the first inside a library anywhere.

## Key Points

The Fairmount Five Market is funded through a county grant and stocked weekly. Residents apply in advance; nearly 200 households were accepted at launch with a waitlist already forming. The selection includes produce, meat, dairy, and staples. Families receive a reusable bag and shop on a set schedule.

The Goodr model was designed to close the gap between "access to meals" and "access to food." Its founder frames the distinction as one of agency: a mystery box of donated food is not the same as choosing what you cook. The PG County location specifically targets seniors who are above the income threshold for federal food programs but cannot afford grocery store prices -- a classic ALICE household profile.

The store is inside a public institution (the library), uses a county grant as the funding mechanism, and is run by a private nonprofit partner. This is a Tier 1 local government intervention on a Tier 2 social need.

A second store is being planned for District 7. Maryland's First Lady attended the opening and tied it to the state's new "Food is Medicine" initiative including a Medically Tailored Meals program reaching 3,000+ Marylanders.

## Relevance

This maps directly onto the open gap in Frederick's Affordability Agenda research: the food access landscape note has not yet been written, and the council has not yet mapped what levers exist for food access specifically. The Goodr model offers a concrete peer city example at the county (not state) level.

Frederick has libraries. It has a food desert problem concentrated in the Golden Mile corridor -- District 2. The ALICE data shows 76% of single mothers and 72% of children in rental units are ALICE/poverty households, many of whom face the same gap the PG County program targets: too much income for SNAP, not enough to cover food reliably.

The partnership model (county grant + private nonprofit operator) does not require the city to run a grocery program directly. It is a procurement and convening function, which is squarely within the council's authority.
