# REITs as the Apex Predator of the Rental Housing Market

## Summary

Real Estate Investment Trusts occupy the top of the corporate landlord hierarchy and are the primary structural driver of rental housing unaffordability, with a specific regulatory gap at the FTC enabling unchecked merger activity.

## Key Points

Alexander Ferrer, PhD candidate in Geography at UCLA and researcher at the Luskin Institute on Inequality and Democracy, joined Debt Collective to analyze the corporate landlord structure. His framing: REITs are the apex predator in the rental housing market -- not simply one category of landlord among many, but the organizational form that extracts the most rent, accumulates the most units, and faces the least regulatory friction.

The FTC merger loophole is a concrete policy target. REITs can consolidate through merger structures that do not trigger standard antitrust review, allowing portfolio growth that would be blocked for other corporate actors. Debt Collective is organizing a campaign demanding the FTC close this loophole (Bit.ly/FTCProtectTenants26).

Ferrer also references the book *Abolish Rent: How Tenants Can End the Housing Crisis* as a frame for the broader political project. The Debt Collective's housing team is primarily Los Angeles and Bay Area based, but the analysis applies to housing markets nationally.

## Relevance

The REIT framing matters for Frederick's housing production work in a specific way: when the council debates supply-side solutions (more units, faster permitting, LMC reform), the REIT analysis poses the counter-question of who captures the benefit of new supply. If institutional investors are the primary buyers and consolidators of new rental stock, production targets alone may not solve affordability at the neighborhood level.

The FTC loophole campaign is a federal advocacy angle worth tracking. The Real Rent Ordinance and tenant protection work at the local level and federal REIT regulation are complementary levers, not substitutes. As Council President, connecting to national tenant organizing on the REIT question could strengthen the political coalition around local tenant protection work.

The book *Abolish Rent* is worth adding to the reading list given how directly its framing connects to the Housing Production Target Resolution and the affordability agenda.
