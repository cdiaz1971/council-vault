# Private Sector Housing Impact Fund Preserves Affordable Housing in Charlotte

## Summary

Charlotte's business-backed Housing Impact Fund has raised $225 million from private investors to buy and preserve naturally occurring affordable housing (NOAH), acquiring nearly 2,000 units so far with a 20-year affordability commitment, in a county where over 77 percent of low-cost housing was lost between 2015 and 2024.

## Key Points

Mecklenburg County estimated that more than 77 percent of the area's low-cost housing disappeared between 2015 and 2024, mostly through investors buying unsubsidized apartments, renovating them, and raising rents. The Housing Impact Fund, founded in 2020 by businessmen Erskine Bowles and Nelson Schwab with real estate firm Ascent Housing as operating partner, targets exactly that gap: buying at-risk NOAH communities before they convert to market rate.

Structure: the fund buys properties near transit, schools, and services, caps rents at 80 percent of area median income (AMI) with a third of units reserved at or below 30 percent AMI, and commits to those terms for 20 years. Two mechanisms make the model work. First, Mecklenburg County and the City of Charlotte both agreed to rebate an amount equal to each property's property taxes back into the fund as a subsidy for its lowest-income units (560 units had received these subsidies as of April 2026). Second, the fund raises impact-investor capital willing to accept 20-year loan terms at returns as low as 6 percent, well below market-rate expectations.

Results to date: $225 million raised, 11 communities and nearly 2,000 units acquired, with plans to acquire, renovate, and protect another 1,500 units for roughly 5,000 more low- and moderate-income residents, for a combined reach of up to 10,000 residents by the end of 2028. The model draws on comparable NOAH preservation funds in Austin, the Twin Cities, and Washington DC, and Charlotte's version has raised the most money and imposes the strictest income caps among them.

Scale context: even at this size the fund is one part of the picture. Mecklenburg County had more than 108,000 cost-burdened renter households in 2024, over 52,000 of them severely burdened (spending more than half their income on housing).

## Relevance

A working peer-city model for preserving existing affordable stock through private capital plus a property-tax rebate mechanism, worth weighing alongside the Housing Production Target Resolution's financing recommendations (CDFI/TIF financing, city-owned land RFP) as an additional acquisition-based tool distinct from new production.
