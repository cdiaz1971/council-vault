## Thesis

Housing crises in growth metros are structural consequences of knowledge economy geography, not policy failures alone. Effective responses require supply-side tools that address the constraints specific to knowledge-economy cities and cross-agency coordination that treats housing as a system rather than a zoning problem.

## Source Notes

- [[Knowledge Economy Double Divide -- Housing Unaffordability and the Urban Wage Penalty]] (Byrne, May 29, 2026)
- [[NC Governor Executive Order 36 -- Cross-Agency Housing Strategy]] (Mulderrig, May 28, 2026)

## The Convergence

Byrne's analysis explains why the problem exists: knowledge industry clustering in urban cores drives demand where supply is most constrained, producing both intra-urban inequality (non-college workers face an urban wage penalty in real terms) and inter-regional inequality (left-behind places that couldn't reposition). This is not a market failure in the conventional sense -- it is the market working as designed.

NC Governor Stein's Executive Order responds with a structural mechanism: a cross-agency housing coordinator who can see how transportation decisions, environmental rules, permitting efficiency, and workforce development collectively shape what gets built and where. The order explicitly links DOT infrastructure decisions to housing access -- treating transportation as a housing policy tool.

The gap between the two: Byrne's analysis describes a dynamic no single executive order can resolve. The Stein EO is the right institutional architecture but its success depends entirely on whether agencies actually change behavior, requiring sustained political pressure.

## Implications for Frederick

Frederick sits in exactly the dynamic Byrne describes -- adjacent to the DC knowledge economy cluster, absorbing its demand pressure as the affordability relief valve. This framing has strategic value:

It makes the case for MPDU and deed-restricted production as a structural response to structural conditions. It frames employer-assisted housing as addressing a real market failure that harms workers who generate the service economy. It supports advocacy for a Maryland equivalent of the Stein EO -- a cross-agency housing coordination structure linking MDOT investments, MDE environmental review, and Commerce Opportunity Zone nominations to housing production goals.
