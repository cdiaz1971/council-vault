# AI Permitting Tools Are Promising but Not Sufficient to Solve the Housing Supply Crisis

## Summary

Urban Institute researchers and HUD officials are cautiously optimistic about AI tools for housing permitting backlogs but emphasize that technology adoption alone is not sufficient, and that AI readiness of housing policy documents is a prerequisite for effective implementation. A new $3 million HUD grant program funds pilots due July 13.

## Key Points

Judah Axelrod (Urban Institute): permitting is the "really popular and in-demand" AI use case, where tools are being used to clear case backlogs, expedite document reviews, and clarify complex rules. But "the jury's out on whether AI solutions can do these things" and most cities "are not really yet at the point where they can say AI is the solution to the problem."

The HUD grant program funds state, local, and tribal governments to test automated permitting platforms including AI-enabled solutions. The stated goals: generate empirical evidence on operational performance, workflow efficiency, staff roles, and applicant experience.

Will Curran-Groome (Urban Institute): AI adoption creates pressure to simplify and clarify housing policies because "pretty clear and objective standards in those policy documents could potentially enable some of the AI products to make sure that they're generating accurate, useful outputs." Boston began reviewing and revising its zoning code in 2023 partly because outdated language and piecemeal revisions made it impossible to process efficiently.

The broader lesson: AI can work, but it works best when the underlying policy is clear. If the zoning code has 200 overlapping provisions, an AI doesn't resolve that conflict, it inherits it. Code clarity is a prerequisite for permitting automation, not a byproduct of it.

## Relevance

This directly feeds the LMC Review Commission thread. One argument for LMC modernization that hasn't been fully developed: a modernized, legible LMC is also AI-ready, making Frederick's permitting process more automatable and potentially faster for housing production applications. The technology argument for code clarity is separate from the housing production argument but reinforces it.

The HUD grant deadline is July 13 (two days from now). This was not actionable for Frederick in this cycle, but the next round should be flagged when announced.

The "jury's out" framing from Urban Institute is important for César's AI procurement posture. AI permitting tools are not proven. Piloting them on a small, low-stakes application before deploying them on something consequential is the right sequence, and that pilot-first logic should be built into any Frederick AI procurement framework.
