# REIT Merger Exemption from FTC Pre-Merger Review -- Corporate Landlord Consolidation Risk

## Summary

Real estate investment trusts are currently exempt from FTC pre-merger review, allowing massive consolidations like the proposed Equity Residential and Avalon Bay merger to proceed without antitrust scrutiny -- a structural loophole that accelerates corporate landlord market concentration.

## Key Points

Over 100 million tenants pay rent monthly, but landlord ownership is concentrated among 11 million landlords -- and corporate consolidation is accelerating that concentration further. REITs combine the limited liability of corporate ownership with political lobbying power and the ability to grow rapidly through mergers, without facing the same antitrust review as comparably sized mergers in other industries.

The specific trigger here: Equity Residential and Avalon Bay announced a merger that would instantly create the largest corporate landlord in the country. The FTC is actively considering whether to close the REIT merger exemption, meaning this moment is a live regulatory inflection point.

The Debt Collective's framing positions this as a structural issue -- REITs can raise rents, neglect maintenance, charge junk fees, and push evictions as profit-extraction strategies, with limited accountability and no pre-merger scrutiny.

## Relevance

The federal angle (FTC exemption, REIT consolidation) is upstream of what Frederick can control directly, but it shapes the market conditions that drive Frederick's affordability crisis. Understanding corporate landlord concentration helps frame why local tenant protection policies -- just-cause eviction, rent stabilization, MPDU policy -- matter even when supply reform is also happening. The "corporate landlord as market structure problem" argument is distinct from the "not enough housing" argument and politically usable with different audiences.

Worth watching the FTC's decision on REIT pre-merger review as a policy development to reference in housing discussions.
