# Community Land Trusts as Civic and Political Infrastructure

## Summary

Community land trusts permanently remove land from the speculative market, creating affordable housing that requires no ongoing government subsidy and is immune to gentrification -- CLT residents are among the most civically engaged people in their neighborhoods because they have a real stake in community governance.

## Key Points

From Nakagawa's piece on democratic economic infrastructure, the CLT section is worth isolating:

A community land trust separates the ownership of land from the ownership of buildings. The trust holds the land permanently; residents own or rent the structures. Because the land is never resold into the speculative market, affordability is preserved across ownership transitions without perpetual government subsidy.

The Champlain Housing Trust in Burlington, Vermont is the oldest major model in the U.S. There are now over 300 community land trusts nationally. Growth is slow because land acquisition is capital-intensive, but CLTs create permanent compounding infrastructure -- each acre of land held is held forever.

The civic dimension: CLT residents tend to have unusually high rates of civic participation. Nakagawa's explanation is straightforward -- they've made a long-term investment in their community and have a real material stake in how it's governed. Housing security translates into political agency.

This is what makes CLTs distinct from conventional affordable housing programs: they are simultaneously a housing intervention and a democratic institution-building strategy.

## Relevance

Directly relevant to Frederick's housing production and affordability agenda. A CLT strategy could complement the [[Housing Production Target Resolution]] by creating permanently affordable units outside the market-rate cycle. The civic engagement dimension is also relevant to Frederick's organizing and constituent engagement work. Worth exploring whether Frederick has land or funding mechanisms that could seed a CLT, or whether there are existing Maryland CLTs to partner with.
