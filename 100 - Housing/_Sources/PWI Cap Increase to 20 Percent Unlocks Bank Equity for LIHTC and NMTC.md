# PWI Cap Increase to 20 Percent Unlocks Bank Equity for LIHTC and NMTC

## Summary
The 21st Century ROAD to Housing Act raises the statutory cap on bank public welfare investment authority from 15 to 20 percent of risk-adjusted capital, freeing additional private bank equity for LIHTC and New Markets Tax Credit deals as national equity pricing has been trending down.

## Key Points
Section 203 of the law increases the PWI cap that limits how much of their risk-adjusted capital banks can put into public welfare investments, including LIHTC and NMTC deals. Many banks with PWI authority were nearing the old 15 percent ceiling, which was constraining their ability to provide equity for new affordable housing and community development deals. The increase is expected to add investment demand and put upward pressure on LIHTC and NMTC equity pricing, offsetting recent downward trends in both national and regional pricing. The provision also requires the Office of the Comptroller of the Currency and the Federal Reserve to report to Congress on member banks' public welfare investments over the prior two years. The cap increase is not self-executing. The OCC and Fed still need to issue guidance, and some states will need to amend state banking statutes before state-chartered banks can use the higher cap.

## Relevance
This is a financing-side lever, not a local policy tool, but it matters for how César frames Frederick's Housing Production Target Resolution. The resolution's Section 4 recommendations already include CDFI and TIF financing options as a response to the affordable housing finance gap. A larger pool of bank equity chasing LIHTC and NMTC deals nationally could ease some of the financing pressure documented in the Affordable Housing Finance Model Under Structural Stress note, though the benefit depends on implementation guidance that has not yet been issued and won't be immediate. Worth a brief mention as federal tailwind context when the resolution goes to council member outreach, without overstating the near-term local effect.
