# Apex Infill Rezonings and Housing Advocacy Playbook

## Summary
CityBuilder's August 10 Rezoning Watch highlights two low-controversy Apex, NC infill rezoning cases and a citizen advocacy script for showing up to housing hearings, alongside an update on Raleigh's minor and exempt subdivision ordinance comment period, open through August 19.

## Key Points
Two Apex rezoning cases are up this week. Case 26CZ04 would split a 0.46-acre single-family lot into two single-family lots; staff and the Planning Board both recommend approval, with conditions on curb, sidewalk, and architectural standards. Case 26CZ03 would amend the Veridea Sustainable Development plan to site the future Wake Technical Community College Conlon campus.

The newsletter frames a small lot split like 26CZ04 as the kind of by-right infill that arguably should not need a legislative rezoning at all, and it encourages readers to tell Apex Town Council that directly.

Raleigh's TC-4-26 subdivision ordinance, already tracked in [[Raleigh Exempt Plat Ordinance]], goes before the Planning Commission Text Change Committee on August 19. This issue adds a concrete comment-period call to action ahead of that date.

The "how to show up" script is worth noting as a reusable format. Nonexperts are told to arrive early, wear a visual signal such as green or pro-housing gear, and keep spoken comments to one plain sentence of support. It is a low-friction turnout tactic, separate from the substantive policy content.

## Relevance
This is a live model of citizen advocacy tactics for pushing rezoning and subdivision reform, directly relevant to the Housing Production Target Resolution and LMC rewrite work. The by-right infill argument for small lot splits and the push to streamline minor subdivision review mirror the same reform lever Frederick is pursuing. The "show up" script is a reusable format worth adapting for Frederick housing hearings, including the Planning Commission's October hearing on ZTA PC25-626ZTA.
