# SNAP Payment Error Rate Compliance Cliff -- States Face Federal Cost-Sharing Penalty Above 6 Percent

## Summary
The Big Beautiful Bill signed by President Trump established new SNAP administrative requirements, including a 6% payment error rate ceiling. States above the threshold by October 2027 will pay an increased share of program costs starting in 2028. The national error rate in FY2025 was 10.62%, meaning most states face real compliance pressure.

## Key Points
SNAP is a jointly funded federal-state program. The new rule ties cost-sharing ratios to administrative performance -- states that cannot bring their payment error rates below 6% will absorb a larger fraction of total costs, potentially in the tens of millions of dollars annually per state.

In FY2025 Nevada had an error rate of 6.22% -- among the lower-performing states -- and still faces a potential $50 million additional cost in 2028 if it does not comply. States with higher error rates face proportionally larger exposure.

The compliance window closes October 2027. That gives states roughly 18 months to restructure their case review processes, quality control systems, and staff workflows. The pressure is producing real investment in administrative technology, as the Nevada SAS platform case demonstrates.

Errors generally fall into two categories: staff data-entry mistakes (transposing figures, entering wrong income or shelter costs) and beneficiary fraud (duplicate addresses, multi-case phone numbers). The distinction matters for solutions -- the first is an administrative quality problem, the second is a fraud detection problem.

The Maryland SNAP Penalty Trap note documents an additional wrinkle unique to Maryland: improving error rates can itself trigger a federal cost-sharing cliff under a different provision, meaning that Maryland has to thread a compliance needle that other states do not face in the same way.

## Relevance
This is the federal policy architecture that makes the food access thread more than a moral argument. Frederick's 45% ALICE rate means that instability in SNAP administration at the state level lands directly on the households César is tracking through the affordability agenda. If Maryland absorbs increased SNAP costs, the pressure flows into state budget negotiations, county allocations, and ultimately the community organizations -- Centro Hispano, SSCM, food pantries -- that operate as backstops when benefits are disrupted. The compliance deadline of October 2027 is an actionable date to monitor.
