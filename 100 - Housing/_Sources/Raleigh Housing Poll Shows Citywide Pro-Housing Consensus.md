# Raleigh Housing Poll Shows Citywide Pro-Housing Consensus

## Summary

A RaleighForward survey of more than 1,500 residents across all five council districts found 88% concerned about housing affordability, 71% personally affected by the shortage, and 67% saying the city lacks enough housing options. The results are positioned as political cover for pro-housing choices in Raleigh's 2027 Comprehensive Plan rewrite.

## Key Points

RaleighForward commissioned the survey ahead of Raleigh's November 2026 City Council election and the first draft of the new Comprehensive Plan, expected in early 2027. Fieldwork ran September 29 to October 7, 2026, covering all five council districts, with a citywide margin of error of plus or minus 2.5 points.

Residents broadly praise the city's parks, dining, and transit, but the housing findings show a consensus that crosses district lines. Nearly 88% are concerned about affordability, and 62.8% are extremely or very concerned. More than 71% say the shortage of affordable housing has affected them, a family member, or a close friend. Over 67% say Raleigh does not have enough housing options for residents across income levels and family sizes.

Open-ended responses describe households priced out despite above-average incomes, adult children staying home to save, and friends forced into long commutes from outside the city.

This release is a limited first look. The full analysis, with district-by-district breakdowns and policy crosstabs, will be presented at events co-hosted with WakeUp Wake County and CITYBUILDER. The author, Eric Braun, is a retired land use attorney who sits on the Raleigh Housing Authority and previously chaired the Raleigh Planning Commission.

## Relevance

The core move here is using district-level polling to show elected officials that pro-housing votes are safe in every district, not only the urban core. That directly addresses the dynamic in [[Raleigh Housing Vote Splits -- Election-Year Rezoning Dynamics]], where members soften on rezonings near elections. Frederick has no equivalent public-opinion baseline for housing production, which leaves the [[Housing Production Target Resolution]] argued mostly on supply numbers rather than demonstrated constituent demand.

The approach parallels the [[Tenant Protections Survey Policy Brief - 2026-10-08]]. A Frederick housing poll broken out by ward-equivalent area, run with an allied group the way RaleighForward partnered with WakeUp Wake County, could do for production what that survey did for tenant protections.

The "personally affected" framing (71%) is the most transferable message. It turns affordability from an abstract policy concern into something most constituents already live with.

Worth watching for the full crosstabs, which will show which specific policies (middle housing, density near transit, and so on) residents support, and how this feeds into the comp plan process tracked in [[Raleigh Comprehensive Plan 2026 Update CITYBUILDER Framing]].
