# Manufactured Housing Speed -- Broad Sustainable Building 26 Story Modular Tower in 5 Days

## Core idea

Broad Sustainable Building (China) assembled a 26-story, 208-unit residential tower in five days using modular construction. Workers did not mix concrete, weld beams, or cut lumber. Modules arrived on flatbeds -- 12m x 3m x 2.4m each, matching a 40-foot shipping container footprint -- pre-wired, pre-piped, pre-furnished. They locked together on site.

The CITYBUILDER newsletter frames this alongside the [[ROAD to Housing Act -- House Passes 396-13, Senate Negotiations Ahead]] as a test case for whether the US can move toward comparable deployment speed in manufactured housing.

## Why this matters for Frederick

The primary constraint on housing production in Frederick is not demand -- it is speed and cost of production. Factory-built modular construction sidesteps the labor bottlenecks that slow stick-built development. If federal policy through the ROAD to Housing Act removes zoning barriers to manufactured housing at scale, the Broad case shows what the supply ceiling could look like when production is untethered from site-built constraints.

This is not a replicable model for Frederick directly -- the zoning, land, and political economy are different -- but it is evidence for the "housing production is a solvable engineering problem" argument when pushing back on slow-walking development timelines.
