# New Mexico's Universal Child Care Win

## Summary
Demos and the State Revenue Alliance are hosting an October 14 webinar on how a coalition of parents, educators, and grassroots groups won universal child care in New Mexico in 2025, the first state to do so.

## Key Points
New Mexico became the first state to offer universal child care in 2025. Demos frames the win as an example of treating care as public infrastructure rather than a private cost, built through long-term coalition work rather than a single legislative push. The October 14 webinar will bring in Organizers in the Land of Enchantment (OLE) and the Center for Civic Policy to walk through the coalition strategy that got it done.

## Relevance
This lands directly in the child care and housing affordability thread already running in the vault. New Mexico is a live case study for the argument that care infrastructure is part of housing stability, not separate from it, a point already developed in [[Childcare as Housing Stability Issue Universal Childcare Momentum]] and [[Treating Child Care as Infrastructure Funding]]. Worth watching for a coalition-building model that could translate to Frederick's own affordability push.
