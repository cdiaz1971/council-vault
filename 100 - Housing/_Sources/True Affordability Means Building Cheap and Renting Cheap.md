# True Affordability Means Building Cheap and Renting Cheap

## Summary
Lubeck defines True Affordability as housing built inexpensively and then rented or sold inexpensively, usually unsubsidized and built by small developers taking personal risk, and contrasts it with expensive, heavily subsidized Capital-A affordable housing. Kronberg's La France Walk in Atlanta is the model.

## Key Points
Lubeck argues the word affordability is so politically worn that practitioners like Atlanta architect and developer Eric Kronberg now avoid it, preferring attainable or workforce housing. His own term, True Affordability (trademarked as a joke), has a single test: low cost to build, low cost to occupy. It is typically unsubsidized and unpoliticized. Conventional subsidized affordable housing, in his telling, tends to be complex, expensive per unit, subsidy-dependent, and built by actors carrying little personal risk. He estimates at most five percent of the affordability conversation addresses lowering the cost of building.

La France Walk in Edgewood, Atlanta is his example. About 25 units on 2.5 acres near a rail station mix small single-family homes, duplexes, and attached suites, with parking minimized in favor of walkability. Many homes include a roughly 200 square foot separate suite with a kitchenette and full bath that can house a relative, serve as an office, or be rented to help owners carry a mortgage. The project won a CNU Charter Award and an Atlanta Regional Commission Development of Excellence award, and Lubeck stresses the homes were both cheap and popular.

## Relevance
This is a useful counterweight inside the vault. It strengthens the case for the [[Missing Middle and Parking LMC Package]]: small lots, attached suites, and reduced parking are exactly the code levers that let small builders produce low-cost units without subsidy. It pairs with [[Incremental Developer Builds a 35-Unit Affordable For-Rent Pocket Neighborhood in Memphis]] as a second built example.

It also cuts against parts of César's framing. Lubeck dismisses subsidized affordable housing broadly, which sits in tension with [[Affordable Housing Cannot Pay for Itself The Math Behind the Gap]] and the power-based analysis in [[What We Mean When We Say Affordable -- Class War Framing and the Housing Model Choice]]. Market-rate small homes rarely reach the lowest incomes, so the equity question is who True Affordability actually serves. The defensible position is both: legalize cheap construction and keep subsidy for households the market will never reach.
