# Circular Architecture and the Case Against Single-Use Buildings

## Summary

Architect Rick Cook argues that the construction industry's reliance on cheap, disposable materials creates "single-use buildings" destined for landfills, and that circularity -- designing for disassembly, adaptive reuse, and material lifecycle -- is the structural alternative.

## Key Points

Cook's central concept: buildings should be understood as having a lifecycle that is a closed loop, not a linear path from construction to demolition. This "circularity" framework encourages:

- **Adaptive reuse** -- repurposing buildings and their materials rather than demolishing and rebuilding
- **Design for disassembly** -- using fasteners instead of adhesives, modular components, and material specifications that allow parts to be separated and reclaimed at end of life ("reversible architecture")
- **Strategic material specification** -- choosing materials that can be reclaimed or recycled, since "the most adaptable building is only as sustainable as its parts"

The analogy Cook uses: the Trader Joe's tote replacing the plastic bag. A cultural and design shift, not just a technical one.

## Relevance

This framing has potential value in Frederick's LMC modernization and zoning reform conversations. As the city evaluates how to increase housing production and adapt its building stock, design standards that require or incentivize circularity could reduce long-term demolition waste and lower lifecycle costs. The Vertical Mixed-Use Text Amendment currently moving toward Planning Commission is one near-term opportunity where building design standards might be considered.

The adaptive reuse angle connects directly to the housing production thread: one of the fastest and most cost-effective ways to add units is repurposing existing commercial or underutilized buildings, and circularity principles support that approach structurally.

Worth holding as background context for any future discussion of Frederick building codes, green building standards, or LMC updates that touch on building materials or design standards.
