# Maryland Housing Certainty Act ↔ Frederick LMC Reform Synthesis

## The Core Tension

Frederick is currently pursuing two parallel tracks that interact in ways that haven't been fully surfaced:

1. The **LMC review** -- a comprehensive revision of the city's Land Management Code, with Libby Taylor proposing that it include outside consulting services and a moratorium on new building during the process.
2. The **Maryland Housing Certainty Act** (HB548/SB325) -- passed both chambers with strong bipartisan margins and headed to Gov. Moore for signature -- which gives developers vested rights in approved housing projects for a minimum of five years from the time a complete application is submitted, locking in the laws and zoning in effect at that moment.

The collision: any zoning changes Frederick produces through the LMC review will not apply to projects where developers have already submitted complete applications. Developers who are aware the review is coming have a strong incentive to submit applications now, before any new standards take effect -- effectively front-running the reform. The vesting clock starts at application submission, not approval.

## The Moratorium Compresses the Timeline

Libby Taylor's proposed moratorium on new building during the LMC review appears, on its face, to create space for reform to take hold before new projects are locked in. But under the Housing Certainty Act, the moratorium would need to be structured carefully: stopping new approvals doesn't stop vesting rights from attaching to applications already submitted. If the moratorium only halts approvals while applications continue to be accepted, it may not achieve the intended effect.

Key questions that need answers before the moratorium proposal advances:
- Does the proposed moratorium halt new applications, new approvals, or both?
- How many complete applications are currently on file that would be grandfathered under the Certainty Act?
- What is the anticipated duration of the LMC review? (The longer it runs, the more applications pile up.)
- Is there a legal mechanism to pause the vesting clock during a city-initiated review, or does state law override that?

## The Broader State Context

The Housing Certainty Act is part of a broader pattern of Maryland using state law to constrain local land use authority in service of housing production goals. The NYU Furman Center and recent Shelterforce research document this shift nationally: states are increasingly preempting local discretion, capping review timelines, and limiting the ability of local governments to change standards mid-process.

Frederick's LMC reform is happening in that environment. The city retains significant local authority, but the Certainty Act narrows one of the primary levers -- post-approval standard revision -- that Frederick might otherwise use to ensure new development meets updated codes.

This also means the LMC review has a shrinking window of relevance for projects in the pipeline. Getting the review done efficiently and well matters more now than it did before the Certainty Act passed.

## Strategic Implications

The moratorium proposal and the Certainty Act need to be analyzed together before either advances. The instinct behind the moratorium -- don't lock in new development under outdated standards while you're in the middle of updating them -- is sound. But the mechanism needs to be compatible with what state law now permits.

If Frederick wants LMC reform to actually shape the next generation of housing development, the path is: move the review quickly, get new standards adopted, and let the Certainty Act's vesting provisions work with the new code rather than locking in the old one. A lengthy moratorium that stalls production while the review drags on is the worst outcome on both counts -- it neither improves standards nor produces housing.

There is also a political framing question: César is advancing a Housing Production Target Resolution while a council colleague is proposing a building moratorium. These are not compatible signals, and that tension needs to be resolved visibly and on the record.

## Open Questions

- What is the precise scope of Libby Taylor's moratorium proposal? Applications? Approvals? Permits?
- How many applications are currently in the pipeline that would vest under the Certainty Act?
- Has the city attorney reviewed the interaction between the proposed moratorium and HB548?
- What is the realistic timeline for the LMC review with outside consulting engaged?
- Is there a model from other Maryland jurisdictions that have navigated similar LMC reviews in light of state vesting requirements?
