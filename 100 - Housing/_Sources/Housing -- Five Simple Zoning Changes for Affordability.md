# Housing -- Five Simple Zoning Changes for Affordability

## Summary

Donald Elliott -- author of *A Better Way to Zone* and *An Even Better Way to Zone* -- argues in a Planetizen feature that zoning is a primary driver of housing cost and that cities can make meaningful affordability gains with a small set of targeted code changes. The piece is practical rather than theoretical: Elliott identifies specific zoning provisions that raise prices and names simpler alternatives.

## Key Points

The Readwise capture includes one direct highlight from the article: larger minimum lot sizes are among the provisions that studies have found drive housing costs upward. The broader argument from Elliott's body of work is that many zoning rules were written for a context that no longer exists, and that cities tend to layer new exceptions on top of outdated foundations rather than removing the bad rules at the base.

Elliott's general framework for affordability-oriented zoning reform centers on: removing or reducing minimum lot size requirements; allowing more housing types by right in residential zones (missing middle -- duplexes, triplexes, ADUs); reducing parking minimums, which inflate per-unit costs and consume land; streamlining or eliminating discretionary review for by-right conforming projects; and using density bonuses rather than payment-in-lieu as the primary affordability lever.

## Relevance

Directly applicable to the LMC modernization track and to the Housing Production Target Resolution under development. The argument that minimum lot sizes drive costs maps to Frederick's existing LMC provisions. Elliott's critique of payment-in-lieu as an affordability escape valve aligns with the MPDU reform concern around the $2.00/sq ft in-lieu fee. The "by-right" framing connects to the supply-side goals in the production resolution.
