# CITYBUILDER Brief: Asheville Ends Single-Family Exclusionary Zoning

## Summary
CITYBUILDER Brief reports Asheville has ended single-family exclusionary zoning citywide, alongside a Raleigh Planning Commission vote to legalize exempt plats and an active Raleigh Housing Authority rezoning fight for the Heritage Park affordable housing redevelopment.

## Key Points
Asheville legalized duplexes in residential zones, ending single-family exclusionary zoning citywide, the brief's national radar item. Closer to home for the newsletter's audience, Raleigh Planning unanimously recommended legalizing exempt plats, a story already tracked in this vault.

The more time-sensitive item was a call to action: Raleigh Housing Authority sought a rezoning from RX-3 to DX-12 for the Heritage Park redevelopment, adding height and flexibility for future phases beyond the already-under-construction Phase 1. The rezoning commits to at least 122 units affordable at or below 30 percent AMI for at least 30 years, near the planned Western Boulevard BRT corridor. The newsletter frames this as a case where affordable housing needs organized public support to pass, citing a similar RHA proposal in Cary that was recently defeated by organized opposition despite clear need.

## Relevance
Asheville is a useful peer-city precedent for ending single-family-only zoning, directly relevant to Frederick's LMC reform and Housing Production Target work. The Cary defeat is a caution: broad community need does not guarantee a housing vote survives organized opposition, worth keeping in mind when sequencing Frederick's own housing votes and lining up visible support in advance. See [[Raleigh Exempt Plat Ordinance]] for the exempt plats story and [[Housing Production Target Resolution]] for the Frederick-side parallel.
