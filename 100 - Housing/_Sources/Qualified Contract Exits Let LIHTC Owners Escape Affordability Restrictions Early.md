# Qualified Contract Exits Let LIHTC Owners Escape Affordability Restrictions Early

## Summary

A little-known LIHTC exit mechanism called the Qualified Contract (QC) lets some owners escape affordability restrictions years early by forcing the state housing agency to find a buyer within one year at a formula price that housing advocates say almost always exceeds market value as affordable housing, effectively pricing out preservation buyers.

## Key Points

- National Low Income Housing Coalition director Dan Emmanuel: "The formula almost always leads to a price exceeding market value as affordable housing," meaning a QC exit tends to convert subsidized units to market rate rather than transferring them to another affordable owner.
- Case study: Jami Johnson, a wheelchair user with spinal muscular dystrophy receiving Section 8 assistance at Rosewood Estates in Springfield, Missouri, faced likely displacement when the building's owner pursued a QC exit. After months searching for an accessible, affordable replacement unit, "the finding rate was zero." A July 2026 court ruling granted temporary protections while litigation continues.
- This sits inside the broader preservation landscape covered in [[440,000 Federally Assisted Homes Face Affordability Restriction Expirations by 2030]] -- QC is a specific, owner-initiated early-exit path distinct from the ordinary end-of-term expiration wave, and it disproportionately threatens accessible units for disabled tenants.

## Relevance

Worth knowing as a specific mechanism to watch for if Frederick or Maryland ever tracks its own LIHTC portfolio for preservation risk -- a Qualified Contract filing is an early-warning signal, not just a date on an expiration calendar.
