# US-Nordic Childcare Gap -- A Nordic System Would Cost 1.17 Percent of GDP Today

## Summary

People's Policy Project researcher Brian Dew analyzes how Nordic countries provide childcare benefits, compares US state-level programs to that standard, and calculates what a full Nordic-style system would cost the United States. The headline finding: due to changes in wages and fertility rates, it would cost 1.17 percent of GDP today -- less than at any point in the past, including far less than the 3.05 percent it would have cost in the 1970s. The fiscal objection to universal childcare is weaker than it has ever been.

## Key Points

Nordic countries provide near-universal subsidized childcare as a public benefit, with low family cost-sharing and high quality standards. The US has no federal equivalent, though some states have partial programs.

The cost calculation is driven by two dynamics: wage growth has increased the taxable base available to fund such a program, and declining fertility rates mean fewer children need to be served relative to the working population. Both trends have made a Nordic-style program structurally cheaper over time.

At 1.17 percent of GDP, this is comparable to many existing federal programs and well within the range of feasibility for a society that has chosen to fund far more expensive commitments. The paper argues this is a political choice, not a fiscal constraint.

## Relevance

Childcare access is a housing stability issue -- when childcare costs consume a significant share of household income, it directly affects housing affordability and labor force participation, particularly for single parents and lower-income families. This paper provides a clean fiscal frame for council conversations about why federal childcare investment matters locally, even when the policy lever is national.

If Frederick is ever in a position to advocate at the state or federal level on childcare -- through NLC, through Maryland municipal advocacy, or through direct testimony -- this 1.17 percent figure is a useful anchor.
