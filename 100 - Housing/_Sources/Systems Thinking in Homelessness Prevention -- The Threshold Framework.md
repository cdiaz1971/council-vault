# Systems Thinking in Homelessness Prevention -- The Threshold Framework

Threshold's argument: homelessness, particularly family homelessness, is not the result of a single event but of interacting structural, administrative, and social failures. Addressing it requires moving the policy focus from crisis response to upstream, preventative action -- and doing so requires systems thinking as a learning practice rather than a technical fix.

The framework identifies seven dimensions where systems thinking must be embedded.

## The seven dimensions

**Prevention as a systemic objective.** Homelessness prevention must be treated as a core outcome of the housing system, not a secondary activity. This requires shared statutory definitions of both homelessness and risk of homelessness, so early warning signals (rent arrears, eviction notices) trigger prevention interventions before crisis.

**Pathways and feedback loops.** Current data systems measure homelessness after it occurs. A prevention-led strategy tracks how insecurity in the private rental sector, inadequate income supports, and administrative barriers interact over time. Missed early interventions compound downstream costs across emergency accommodation, social services, and child welfare.

**Data, metrics, and learning systems.** Prevention cannot improve without meaningful measurement. Key metrics: tenancies sustained, evictions prevented, housing stability after intervention -- disaggregated by household type. The distinction between feedback loops (learning from past performance) and feedforward loops (acting on emerging patterns before problems occur) is critical. A rise in rent arrears in a specific area should trigger increased outreach before evictions spike.

**Upstream intervention in the private rental sector.** Eviction from the private rental sector is the primary pathway into family homelessness. Achieving alignment across government departments, statutory bodies, and NGOs -- extending beyond housing to Social Protection, Health, and related areas -- is essential. Delays in accessing HAP after job loss can trigger rent arrears and tenancy termination, illustrating how misaligned systems undermine prevention.

**Accessible advice and early support.** Independent housing advice services are leverage points that intercept risk before crisis. Sustained funding and embedded referral pathways through schools, health services, and community organizations make early engagement systematic rather than accidental.

**Inter-agency coordination and local authority leadership.** People at risk navigate multiple systems simultaneously while responsibility for prevention is fragmented. Local authorities are best positioned as system integrators -- aligning housing, welfare, family support, and community services around a shared prevention goal.

**Child-centered decision-making.** Housing instability harms children before homelessness occurs. A child impact lens generates benefits across education, health, and wellbeing systems -- recognizing that protecting housing stability for children reduces costs across multiple policy domains over the life course.

## The Allegheny County model

Allegheny County (Pennsylvania) uses an integrated human services data warehouse linking housing, child welfare, mental health, substance use, justice, employment, and income support data to identify households at heightened risk of eviction before they reach crisis. The model focuses on patterns of interaction across systems rather than single indicators. Predictive analytics are embedded in everyday decision-making, subject to external fairness reviews, and continually refined through learning loops. The data often reveals where financial assistance alone is insufficient, prompting differentiated responses.

## Why this matters for César

This framework is directly transferable to Frederick. The City has no integrated prevention-focused housing data system. Most of what Frederick does on homelessness is crisis response -- shelter referrals, emergency assistance -- rather than upstream intervention. The Allegheny County data model is a peer-city example worth flagging in housing policy discussions. The inter-agency coordination point maps onto the existing challenge of aligning City housing programs with County social services.

Connects to the active [[Housing Production Target Resolution]] work and the broader affordability framing -- prevention is the missing third leg alongside production and preservation.
