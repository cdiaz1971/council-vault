# Policy Levers to Counter Private Equity in Housing

## Summary

Rent stabilization, LLC transparency, carried interest tax reform, and social housing development are the four structural levers that can counter private equity's grip on rental housing -- each operating at a different point in the extraction chain.

## Key Points

**Rent stabilization disrupts the business model directly.** PE firms target properties where they project rent growth. Rent regulation caps that growth, reducing the projected return and making the acquisition less attractive. Evidence: Blackstone pulled out of Sweden after the country enacted good cause eviction protections. New York's 2019 Housing Stability and Tenant Protection Act closed multifamily rent regulation loopholes and imposed manufactured home lot rent increase limits. Rent stabilization is not just a tenant protection -- it is PE deterrence.

**LLC transparency removes the organizing barrier.** Without beneficial ownership disclosure, tenants cannot identify who actually owns their building. New York's LLC transparency law (applying to foreign LLCs) is the only state-level example. Gov. Hochul vetoed expansion in 2025. A Maryland LLC transparency bill would be a legislative priority worth pursuing through Local Progress or state advocacy partners.

**Carried interest tax reform removes the federal subsidy.** The carried interest loophole allows PE managers to classify their compensation (a percentage of fund profits) as capital gains rather than ordinary income, taxing them at rates lower than those paid by ordinary workers. This is a federal policy that effectively subsidizes PE's risk-taking in housing. State-level responses are limited but the framing is useful in public advocacy.

**Social housing as structural alternative.** Community land trusts, Tenant and Community Opportunity to Purchase Acts (TOPA/COPA), and adequately funded public housing remove units from the speculative market permanently. Patient capital pools that do not require PE-level returns can finance both preservation and new multifamily construction without the extraction pressure. This is where CLT development, CDFI financing, and city-owned land RFPs connect to the PE counter-strategy.

**The 21st Century ROAD Act context:** The article was published amid Congressional debate over the ROAD Act, which includes caps on single-family home purchases by institutional investors. Trump canceled a signing event, and the bill's fate is uncertain. This illustrates the limits of federal legislative action -- the durable interventions are at the state and local level.

## Relevance

Every lever in this note has a Frederick application. The algorithmic rent pricing ban in the Rockville Chapter 18 model is rent stabilization applied specifically to PE's key pricing tool. The Housing Production Target Resolution's CDFI/TIF financing recommendation is patient capital development. The Frederick CLT policy brief is social housing infrastructure. TOPA/COPA legislation would require state enabling authority but is worth flagging as a Maryland legislative priority. The carried interest framing is useful in public advocacy connecting local tenant protection to federal tax policy -- particularly relevant when César is speaking to progressive audiences about why local action matters even when federal policy fails.
