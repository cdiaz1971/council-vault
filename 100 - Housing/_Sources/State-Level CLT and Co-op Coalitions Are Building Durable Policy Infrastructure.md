# State-Level CLT and Co-op Coalitions Are Building Durable Policy Infrastructure

## Summary

State-level housing coalitions in TX, MT, VA, OR, and elsewhere are successfully advancing CLT and limited-equity co-op policy at the legislative level -- demonstrating that community ownership models require organized state coalition infrastructure to scale, not just individual project development.

## Key Points

**The pattern:** In multiple states, housing coalitions organized explicitly around community ownership models have achieved legislative wins that individual CLTs or co-ops could not achieve alone. The key variable is not the quality of individual projects but the presence of a coordinating coalition capable of shaping legislative language, building cross-sector support, and sustaining presence across multiple sessions.

**Oregon:** The Oregon Cooperative Housing Network coordinated passage of two bills amending the state's Low-Income Rental Housing Property Tax Exemption and Oregon Affordable Housing Tax Credit to be inclusive of limited-equity cooperatives (LECs). The result was a 4% mortgage interest rate reduction available to LECs -- a structural subsidy that changes pro forma math.

**Montana:** After the 2023 "Montana Miracle" (statewide ADU, duplex, and commercial-zone housing legalization), the state followed up in 2025 with parking requirement reductions. CLTs in Missoula are actively reviewing new project pipelines enabled by these zoning changes. Front Step CLT is a direct beneficiary of coalition-driven state reform.

**Virginia:** A YIMBY-oriented Commonwealth Housing Coalition won five housing bills in 2026, including parking reform, ADUs, and affordable housing on congregation land. Two further priorities (commercial-zone housing and housing targets) did not advance -- illustrating that even well-organized coalitions face multi-session timelines.

**Texas:** Texans for Reasonable Solutions coordinated a package of missing-middle housing bills with flexible advocacy strategy, accepting amendments to expand support while preserving core provisions.

**The coalition model features:**
- Multi-session staying power with organizational continuity
- Cross-sector membership (housing nonprofits, labor, faith, legal)
- Flexibility on bill language while holding core principles
- Explicit attention to racial equity as an organizing value
- Connection between state legislative wins and local CLT project pipelines

## Relevance

The [[Frederick Community Land Trust -- Policy Brief]] identifies a Frederick CLT as a viable housing strategy. This piece adds the missing ingredient: a state-level coalition is the mechanism that makes individual CLTs viable at scale. Maryland already has a housing advocacy ecosystem -- the question is whether Frederick's local work can plug into state coalition infrastructure the way Missoula CLT plugged into the Montana Miracle.

More broadly, this connects to [[Institutional Solidarity Pact Gap -- Frederick Civic Coalition]]: the same coalition architecture that wins housing legislation is the architecture that provides democratic resilience. The two threads are not separate.
