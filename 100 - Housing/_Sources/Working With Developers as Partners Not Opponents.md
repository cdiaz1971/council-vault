# Working With Developers as Partners Not Opponents

## Summary

Strong Towns speaker MK Lindsey argues that framing development debates as fights between communities and "greedy developers" produces worse outcomes than approaching them as negotiations. When communities enter with deal-making logic rather than opposition logic, they have more leverage and more room to shape outcomes.

## Key Points

The "evil developer" frame is emotionally satisfying but strategically weak. It positions the community as a defender with veto power, but veto power only produces "no" -- it does not produce affordable units, green space, or community benefits. Deal-making logic asks: what do we want out of this, and what can we trade to get it?

This does not require trusting developers or abandoning accountability. It requires clarity about what the community actually needs and the willingness to negotiate rather than simply resist.

The framing is especially useful in contexts where development is already likely to happen -- where the real question is not whether it happens but on what terms.

## Relevance

Directly applicable to Frederick's housing production conversations, particularly around LMC and the Housing Production Target Resolution. The council's posture toward developers shapes what the community actually receives. Lindsey's framing gives language for explaining to constituents why engagement beats resistance in contexts where development is the default outcome.
