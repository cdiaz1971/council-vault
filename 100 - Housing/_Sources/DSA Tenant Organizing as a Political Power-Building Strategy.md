# DSA Tenant Organizing as a Political Power-Building Strategy

## Summary
Shelterforce traces how DSA chapters nationally have made tenant organizing central to their work alongside labor organizing, treating housing as a natural entry point for political education because everyone needs it. The piece draws a direct line back to Shelterforce's own 1975 founding, when the Democratic Socialist Organizing Committee was doing tenant organizing in several cities.

## Key Points
In New Haven, where more than 70 percent of residents rent, DSA members activated a dormant local tenant protection, a fair rent commission that has existed since 1984 but went largely unused. Organizers went door to door asking who residents' landlords were and whether they were responsive, then pushed the city to enforce the existing commission rather than waiting for new legislation. The tactic treats underused local authority as low-hanging fruit for organizing, not just a policy gap to close.

Nicole Zimmerman, a member of DSA's Montgomery County, Maryland branch, frames housing as an easy way to connect people's everyday lives to larger economic systems, which makes it a natural entry point for both power building and political education. The 2008 housing crisis is cited as the moment that drove a resurgence of both tenant unions and DSA membership more broadly, suggesting the organizing model scales with material pressure on renters.

## Relevance
Frederick has its own dormant and underused tenant protections worth auditing the way New Haven's fair rent commission was, particularly as the tenant protection package (informed by Rockville's Chapter 18) takes shape. The Montgomery County DSA framing is geographically close and politically relevant given César's own DSA ties.
