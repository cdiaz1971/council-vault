# Erdmann's Contagion Cities and the 2008 Credit Crackdown

## Summary
Kevin Erdmann (Mercatus Center) argues that housing cost increases in both high cost and low cost cities trace largely to a post-2008 tightening of mortgage credit rather than zoning restrictions alone. He calls lower cost cities that absorbed displaced buyers from expensive metros Contagion Cities, Houston being the clearest example.

## Key Points
Between 2008 and 2019, lending to borrowers with credit scores above 760 nearly doubled while lending to borrowers scoring 720 to 760 dropped 58 percent, per Erdmann's research in "Building From the Ground Up." That credit tightening froze working class households out of homeownership and pushed them into the rental market, raising rental demand even in places where supply kept growing. Single family construction still has not recovered to its 2006 peak, while multi-family completions ran 64 percent higher, though both remain well below their 1973 peaks despite roughly 60 percent population growth since then. Lewyn's pushback: if the credit crackdown fully explains the pattern, Canada and Australia, which did not experience the same lending freeze, should show stable rents, but rents in both countries have also risen sharply.

## Relevance
This is a useful counterweight to Condon's land price capture argument in [[Condon's Land Price Capture Critique of Upzoning]] and gives a financing side explanation for the same phenomenon Condon attributes purely to zoning. Relevant background for the Housing Production Target Resolution's framing, since it suggests production targets alone may not move affordability without also addressing mortgage access, which sits outside the City's direct authority but is worth naming as a limitation in the resolution's findings section.
