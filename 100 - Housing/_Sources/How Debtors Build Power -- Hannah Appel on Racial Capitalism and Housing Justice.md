# How Debtors Build Power -- Hannah Appel on Racial Capitalism and Housing Justice

## Summary

Interview and essay featuring Hannah Appel (UCLA / Debt Collective co-founder) connecting debt abolition, racial capitalism, and the housing crisis -- with attention to organizing frameworks for debtors and a new report linking climate debt to climate justice.

## Key Points

- Hannah Appel's work spans debt abolition, housing justice, racial capitalism, climate justice, and anthropology; the conversation covers the structural roots of the housing crisis and organizing frameworks for debtors
- Her key argument: the goal is not just "free" education or housing, but "freeing" -- the distinction points to structural transformation vs. transactional relief
- Debt Collective recently published "From Climate Debt to Climate Justice: Debtor Organizing in the Fight for Climate Repair" -- connecting housing precarity, climate vulnerability, and debt as interlocking systems
- A related Jubilee School event addressed divesting universities from state violence (weapons, private prisons, surveillance technology) via endowment campaigns -- the UC Move Your Money campaign as a model
- The debtor organizing frame is relevant beyond students: renters, medical debt holders, and utility customers are all debtors in systems designed to extract rather than support

## Relevance

The racial capitalism framing gives Frederick's affordability work a deeper analytical anchor than affordability-as-price. The "freeing vs. free" distinction is useful for communicating what structural housing policy actually aims at. The surveillance technology divestment thread also connects to the Flock/ALPR governance work -- university endowments, police tech contracts, and municipal procurement share the same democratic accountability deficit.
