# Child Care as a Public Good -- Demos on the Case for Sustainable Federal Funding

## The Problem Frame

Demos frames child care unaffordability as a structural policy failure, not a market failure with private solutions. Key facts:

- Average child care cost: $13,128 per child per year nationally
- Providers operate on razor-thin margins despite high family costs
- Child care workers -- disproportionately Black and Brown women -- remain among the lowest-paid workers in the economy
- The cost and wage squeeze exist simultaneously because the sector is structurally underfunded

The argument: this is the result of policy choices, not economic inevitability. Different choices are possible.

## The Policy Case

State-level experiments demonstrate what is achievable when policymakers treat care as a public good. New Mexico and New York have stepped up in the absence of federal action. But Demos argues that access to affordable, high-quality care should not depend on zip code -- federal investment is the only mechanism that can achieve universality.

The federal government has the resources to build a care system that expands access, supports living wages for workers, and advances racial and economic equity. Without it, the current dynamics continue: spiraling family costs, providers struggling to survive, and care workers remaining underpaid for essential work.

## Relevance to Frederick Policy Work

Child care appears in the ALICE data as a significant cost burden for Frederick County working families. The Affordability Agenda Council Authority Tiering note maps where local government has direct levers vs. where federal action is required -- child care primarily sits in the latter category at the funding level, though local government can shape access and supplemental support.

The racial equity dimension is directly relevant: the workforce providing this essential labor is disproportionately Black and Brown women, making child care wages an economic justice and racial equity issue, not just a labor market question.

## Connection to Demos Synthesis Arc

This is part of the broader Demos policy frame appearing across multiple council notes -- the argument that structural economic problems require structural public investment, that the private market cannot solve what public policy created, and that racial equity is embedded in the economic analysis, not a separate track.
