# Ocean City vs St Pauls Homeless Shelter Zoning Case

## Summary

St. Paul's-by-the-Sea Episcopal Church in Ocean City filed a federal lawsuit against the town after it sought to shut down an overnight shelter in the parish hall, raising First Amendment free exercise and RLUIPA claims against the municipality's zoning enforcement.

## Key Points

The church operates an overnight shelter averaging 30-35 guests nightly and serves about 120 lunches per day during the summer. The shelter began after the town told the church to remove tents from its property and move unhoused people indoors -- then objected to the indoor shelter on zoning grounds.

The church filed suit claiming violations of:
- First Amendment Free Exercise Clause
- Religious Land Use and Institutionalized Persons Act (RLUIPA)

Ocean City filed a counterclaim seeking an injunction to close the shelter, arguing it does not comply with local zoning.

The town's position is that this is a zoning compliance issue, not a homeless services issue. The church's rector characterized the underlying goal as removing unhoused people from the island entirely, citing a town manager statement that the town should "look like Disneyland." The town manager disputes that characterization.

The church also faces permit obstacles installing a washer and dryer -- the electrical permit was approved, but the plumbing permit application was rejected for a code issue the town says can be resolved with a compliant submission.

## Relevance

RLUIPA is an underused tool in homelessness policy fights. If Ocean City loses this case, it sets useful precedent for other Maryland jurisdictions where municipalities are using zoning enforcement to deter or prevent religious organizations from operating shelters. Frederick has several faith communities actively engaged in homeless services -- UUCF among them -- and this case is worth monitoring for its precedential value.

The case also illustrates the political dynamic of resort economies trying to manage visible homelessness through displacement rather than services. The "Disneyland" framing -- whether accurately quoted or not -- captures an attitude that shows up in more polished form in many municipalities.
