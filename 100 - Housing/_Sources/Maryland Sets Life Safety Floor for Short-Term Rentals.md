# Maryland Sets Life Safety Floor for Short-Term Rentals

## Summary

The Jillian and Lindsay Wiener Short-Term Rental Safety Act (HB 1221 / SB 624), effective October 1, 2026, requires Airbnb and Vrbo-style rentals in Maryland to have working smoke detectors, carbon monoxide alarms, and fire extinguishers, plus posted evacuation routes and emergency numbers. It sets a state baseline relevant to Frederick's rental licensing rewrite.

## Key Points

The law is named for two Montgomery County sisters who died in a 2022 fire at a New York short-term rental that lacked working smoke detectors. As of October 1, 2026, every short-term rental in Maryland must have functioning fire extinguishers, smoke detectors, and carbon monoxide alarms. Operators must also post evacuation routes and emergency response numbers inside the unit.

Before this law, short-term rentals could fall between the cracks of residential and lodging regulation. Long-term rental units are typically covered by local licensing and inspection, and hotels by commercial fire code, while a whole-home Airbnb could escape both. The act closes that gap at the state level for basic life safety equipment.

## Relevance

The most immediate connection is Sarah Hempel Irani's Rental Licensing Ordinance rewrite in committee. The state now sets a minimum for short-term rentals, which raises practical questions for that rewrite. Does Frederick currently license or register short-term rentals at all, and if so, does its inspection checklist already cover these items? If Frederick does not track them, the state mandate exists without any local mechanism to know which properties are subject to it.

There is also a housing supply angle worth keeping in view. Whole-unit short-term rentals remove homes from the long-term market, and peer cities often pair safety requirements with registration that doubles as a data source on how many units have converted. A registration requirement justified on life safety grounds would give the City that count, which bears on the Housing Production Target Resolution and the broader affordability case.
