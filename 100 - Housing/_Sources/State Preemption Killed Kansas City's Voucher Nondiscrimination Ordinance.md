# State Preemption Killed Kansas City's Voucher Nondiscrimination Ordinance

## Summary

Kansas City, Missouri passed a source-of-income nondiscrimination ordinance in January 2024, but a Fourth Amendment lawsuit produced a preliminary injunction within a month, and a May 2025 Missouri state law then banned any locality in the state from enforcing source-of-income protections at all -- ending it through preemption rather than the courts.

## Key Points

- Sequence: local ordinance passed January 2024, landlord Fourth Amendment challenge filed, preliminary injunction blocking enforcement issued February 2024, then a May 2025 state law preempted the entire policy area statewide, foreclosing any future Kansas City ordinance or any other Missouri locality's.
- This is a distinct and arguably more durable failure mode than the New York litigation path covered in [[Fourth Amendment Challenge Threatens Source-of-Income Voucher Protections Nationwide]]: a constitutional ruling can still be appealed or narrowed over time, but a state preemption statute closes the door entirely regardless of how the underlying Fourth Amendment argument eventually resolves in court.
- Illinois is currently fighting a similar landlord challenge to Chicago's and the state's source-of-income laws (Moore v. Mac Property Management), which could go either direction.

## Relevance

A cautionary sequencing lesson for Frederick's tenant-protection package: even a well-drafted local ordinance can be erased by state preemption after the fact, independent of its own legal merits. Worth flagging to Rachel Gray specifically as a distinct risk from the constitutional exposure -- a "won the case, lost the war" scenario.
