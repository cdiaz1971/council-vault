# Portland Housing Production Strategy -- 35-Action Six-Year Plan

## Summary

Portland City Council unanimously adopted its first-ever Housing Production Strategy (HPS) in August 2024 via Resolution 37673. Required under Oregon state law (HB 2003, 2019), the strategy identifies 35 specific actions across eight categories to increase housing production and meet the city's Housing Needs Analysis targets. It is a six-year action plan with no direct budget impact at adoption -- funding flows through the city's annual budget process.

## Key Points

Oregon law requires cities over 10,000 population to complete a Housing Needs Analysis (HNA) every six years and adopt a Housing Production Strategy within one year of that HNA. Portland completed its 2045 HNA in December 2023 (Ordinance 191547), finding a need for more than 120,000 new housing units by 2045 -- roughly 6,000 units per year.

The 35 strategies span eight categories and are cross-bureau in nature: land use, transportation, infrastructure, economic development, and housing bureaus are all directed to coordinate on implementation. Actions range from zoning reform and permitting streamlining to expanding inclusionary housing program access for underrepresented communities and establishing new Tax Increment Financing (TIF) districts in the Central City and East Portland.

The strategy was led by Commissioner Carmen Rubio. Community engagement included 25 meetings with 250+ participants, plus six focus groups with communities of color and 16 developer interviews.

Key equity framing: rents and home prices have continued to climb and outpace incomes; over half of Portland renters were cost-burdened as of 2023. The strategy explicitly addresses gentrification and displacement risk alongside supply goals.

A related 2025 social housing resolution (Resolution 37703) directed the Portland Housing Bureau to study alternative ownership models including social housing, municipal ownership, and cooperative housing -- with a report due by May 31, 2026.

In 2025, Portland also passed a temporary System Development Charge (SDC) waiver (Ordinance 192082) to improve financial feasibility of housing development during a period of elevated construction costs.

## Relevance

Portland's HPS is a direct model for Frederick's deferred [[Housing Production Target Resolution]]. The structure -- a state-mandated HNA followed by a time-bound production strategy with specific, cross-departmental actions -- mirrors exactly what Frederick is considering.

Key transferable elements: the 35-action framework organized by category, the explicit coordination directive across city bureaus, the integration of equity and anti-displacement goals alongside production targets, and the social housing study as a parallel track to market-rate production. The SDC waiver approach is also relevant to Frederick's discussion of development incentives.
