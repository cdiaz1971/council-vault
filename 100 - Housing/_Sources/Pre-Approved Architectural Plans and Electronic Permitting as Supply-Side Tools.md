# Pre-Approved Architectural Plans and Electronic Permitting as Supply-Side Tools

## Summary

Cities pursuing housing supply growth in 2026 are pairing zoning liberalization with administrative modernization -- pre-approved architectural plan libraries cut design costs for small developers, and electronic plan review cuts permitting timelines. Both tools address cost and time barriers that zoning reform alone does not fix.

## Key Points

The two major administrative strategies converging in 2026 are pre-approved architectural plans and electronic plan review. Pre-approved plan libraries work by having a city's planning and permitting department certify a set of architectural designs (for ADUs, duplexes, small apartment buildings) in advance. A developer or homeowner can then select from that library and skip the design review process entirely, paying only for site-specific modifications. This materially reduces soft costs for small-scale projects -- the design and permitting burden that makes ADU development financially unviable for many homeowners.

Electronic plan review eliminates the physical submission and routing process that adds weeks to permitting timelines in most municipalities. Combined with pre-approved plans, it can reduce the time from project decision to permit issuance from months to weeks.

These administrative tools operate in parallel with zoning liberalization -- they do not require zoning reform to implement, but they multiply the impact of zoning reform when both are in place. A city that has legalized ADUs but has a six-month permitting timeline and no pre-approved plans will see much lower ADU production than one that has streamlined both.

## Relevance

Frederick's permitting process is an understudied lever in the housing production conversation. The council has focused heavily on zoning -- LMC reform, parking minimums, missing middle -- but the administrative side of production (permit timelines, design costs, staff review capacity) may be equally constraining for small developers and homeowners.

Asking Planning Director Gabrielle Collard specifically about pre-approved ADU plan availability and current electronic submission capacity would surface whether this tool is already available, partially available, or not yet pursued. This does not require council legislation -- it may be an administrative action within the department's existing authority.
