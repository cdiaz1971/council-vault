## Core Argument

In many NC communities (and most US cities), it is still illegal to build housing in commercially zoned areas -- even on aging strip mall parcels with full road, water, and sewer access already in place. The result is that land that could support housing sits underutilized while undeveloped greenfield land gets consumed instead.

Allowing housing by right in commercial zones does several things at once:

- **Reduces infrastructure cost:** Roads and utilities are already paid for.
- **Eliminates approval uncertainty:** By-right means no discretionary hearing, no neighborhood opposition veto.
- **Supports corridor revitalization:** Aging commercial corridors become mixed-use neighborhoods rather than declining retail deserts.
- **Enables incremental development:** Small builders can add housing without needing large land assemblies.

NC SB499 (2025 long session) proposed this. It did not pass but CITYBUILDER is pushing for its provisions to be incorporated into 2026 negotiations.

## The Infrastructure Argument

CITYBUILDER's framing is particularly useful: allowing housing on commercial corridors means "making better use of existing infrastructure that we've already built and paid for." This is a fiscal efficiency argument, not just a housing production argument. It reframes the conversation from "allowing density" to "not wasting public investment."

This framing works in a Frederick context. The Golden Mile corridor has significant commercial zoning with existing infrastructure. The LMC's treatment of mixed-use and commercial-to-residential conversion is directly implicated by this model.

## Why This Matters for Cesar

The Golden Mile is Frederick's primary aging commercial corridor -- exactly the type of land SB499 is designed to unlock. Frederick's LMC process is the right vehicle for a by-right residential allowance in commercial zones, particularly along high-frequency transit routes. This connects to the [[Maryland Transit Housing Plan -- 5,000 Homes Near Transit]] work and the [[Mobility vs Accessibility -- The Urban Planning Distinction]] frame.

The fiscal efficiency framing -- we already paid for this infrastructure, let's use it -- is durable for council communications because it doesn't require arguing about density for its own sake.
