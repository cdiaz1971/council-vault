# Single-Stair Building Reform Housing Production

## Summary

Single-stair building reform allows apartment buildings up to six stories to be built around one protected staircase rather than two, enabling smaller, more affordable multifamily housing that fits into neighborhoods -- 19 states have introduced bills since 2022 and seven passed legislation in 2025 alone.

## Key Points

Current building codes in most U.S. jurisdictions require two staircases in apartment buildings, which forces long double-loaded corridor layouts, limits daylight and cross-ventilation, and makes smaller mid-rise buildings economically difficult. Single-stair reform removes that constraint while retaining modern safety requirements: elevators, sprinklers, fire-rated doors and hallways.

The housing production benefits are significant. Single-stair buildings can be smaller and thinner, fitting into infill sites that double-corridor buildings cannot. They enable more family-sized two- and three-bedroom units because floor plates can be designed around a central core. They are particularly well suited to missing middle housing in existing neighborhoods.

National momentum is strong. As of 2026, 19 states and DC have introduced single-stair bills; seven states passed legislation in 2025. Virginia enacted a study law in 2024; Maryland enacted a law in 2025 requiring its Department of Labor to study the reform and report by December 2026. Tennessee passed reform in 2024 and Nashville, Chattanooga, Knoxville, Memphis, and Jackson have all moved forward locally.

North Carolina has SB 492 pending, stuck in the Senate Rules Committee. A citizen-led advocacy site launched at singlestairnc.com.

## Relevance

Maryland's study mandate means a report is due December 2026, potentially creating a legislative opening in the 2027 session. Frederick should monitor that report. This reform directly supports the goals of the [[Housing Production Target Resolution]] by enabling more diverse housing typologies in infill locations. Worth raising with the state delegation and tracking alongside LMC reform work.
