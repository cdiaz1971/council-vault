# Alexandria Eviction Diversion Program Lets Tenants Pay Back Rent in Installments

## Summary
Alexandria, Virginia's Office of Housing launched an eviction diversion program letting tenants behind on rent stay in their homes by paying 10 percent of what they owe upfront and the remainder in installments, aimed at renters set back by one-time expenses like car repairs or medical bills.

## Key Points
The program is run through Alexandria's Landlord-Tenant Division. Tenants falling behind on rent can avoid eviction by paying 10 percent of the total balance owed immediately and repaying the rest on an installment schedule. Mary Horner, Chief of the Landlord-Tenant Division, frames the target population as people dealing with a one-time financial shock rather than a chronic inability to pay. The city's stated rationale is mutual benefit: tenants keep their housing and landlords avoid the legal costs, vacancy risk, and delay of a formal eviction filing.

## Relevance
This is a lightweight, low-cost model worth having on hand for the tenant protection package discussion, alongside the Rockville Chapter 18 provisions already under evaluation. It is narrower than Rockville's repair-and-deduct or relocation assistance provisions and does not require new fee schedules or landlord obligations, which makes it a plausible low-friction addition if the phased approach needs another early-phase, small-landlord-friendly component. It also strengthens the existing case for eviction prevention as local housing stability policy with a live 2026 peer-city implementation to cite alongside Rockville.
