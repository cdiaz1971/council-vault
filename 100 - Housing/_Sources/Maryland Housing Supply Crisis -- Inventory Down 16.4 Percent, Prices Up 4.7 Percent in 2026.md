# Maryland Housing Supply Crisis -- Inventory Down 16.4%, Prices Up 4.7% in 2026

## Summary

Maryland Realtors May 2026 data shows statewide housing inventory fell 16.4% year-over-year to 15,395 units, new listings dropped 22.1%, and average sale prices rose 4.7% to $547,405 -- concrete evidence for the Housing Production Target argument that Maryland is not building fast enough.

## Key Points

**The data (Maryland Realtors, May 2026):**
- Active inventory: 15,395 units (May 2026) vs 18,402 (May 2025) -- down 16.4%
- New listings: 7,296 (May 2026) vs 9,368 (May 2025) -- down 22.1%
- Average sale price: $547,405 (May 2026) vs $522,664 (May 2025) -- up 4.7%
- Maryland's estimated housing unit shortage: ~96,000 units (Governor Moore's figure)

**The supply-demand dynamic:** Maryland Realtors director Lisa May's framing: "We're not building new homes." States where costs are improving are the ones building. People are not moving because they see no better option in the market -- a self-reinforcing inventory lock-in.

**Regulatory context:** Maryland is a highly-regulated state, which creates investment risk for developers. Maryland Realtors favor regulatory reform to allow different housing types. But renter advocates (Montgomery County Renters Alliance) push back: removing regulations could threaten existing protections like rent stabilization, and "housing shortage" in the real estate sense means market demand, not people without roofs -- a distinction with political weight.

**State legislative picture (2026):** Governor Moore passed two housing bills this session: the Housing Certainty Act (developer vesting rights, delays certain fees) and the transit-oriented development law (restricts parking minimums near transit). Neither takes effect until October 2026. The Silver and Starter Homes Act -- which would have prohibited local restrictions on small lot/home size -- stalled without a vote after fierce opposition from county and local governments claiming state overreach.

**What did not pass:** The Starter and Silver Homes Act faced what Maryland Realtors called "vitriol" in committee hearings and never reached a floor vote. Local control over zoning remains a hard political constraint at the state level -- the same tension that shapes Frederick's LMC reform work.

## Relevance

This data is direct ammunition for the Housing Production Target Resolution. Frederick's 1,400 units/year target through 2035 exists in the context of a statewide supply crisis. When the resolution is introduced, this statewide inventory data situates Frederick's production goal within a Maryland-wide failure pattern -- not just a local policy preference.

The renter advocate counterpoint (Matt Losak's "market demand vs people without homes" framing) is also worth holding. It is a preview of the political tension César will navigate when arguing for both supply production and tenant protections simultaneously -- the Real Rent Ordinance and the Housing Production Target are companion moves, not contradictory ones, but the political framing requires care.

The Housing Certainty Act's vesting rights provision (effective October 2026) is flagged in the LMC thread as creating a timing risk for Frederick's LMC reform work -- any LMC changes that could affect development applications should be completed before October to avoid locking in old standards.
