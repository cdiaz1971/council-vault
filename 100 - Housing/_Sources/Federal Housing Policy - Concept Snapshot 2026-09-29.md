## The 21st Century ROAD to Housing Act

- **Path to law.**
  - Spring 2026: an amended House version passed 396-13, dropping earlier anti-build-to-rent provisions that critics said would have cut about 70,000 homes a year ([[ROAD to Housing Act -- House Passes 396-13, Senate Negotiations Ahead]]).
  - June 22 and 23, 2026: the final bill passed the Senate and then the House ([[21st Century ROAD Act Passes -- Federal Housing Incentive Structure and Its Limits]]).
  - July 11, 2026: it became law at midnight without President Trump's signature, after he neither signed nor vetoed it ([[ROAD to Housing Act Signed Into Law Without Presidential Signature July 2026]]; [[ROAD to Housing Act Enacted Without Presidential Signature]]).
- **Incentives, not mandates.** The law rewards localities that permit more housing. It doesn't override local zoning or impose production targets. The National League of Cities framed it as recognizing local leadership ([[21st Century ROAD Act Passes -- Federal Housing Incentive Structure and Its Limits]]). Earlier coverage noted the bill directs HUD to develop best practices for boosting supply and requires reporting on removing land-use barriers ([[Housing Policy Trends  012926]]).
- **Main provisions in the vault's sources:**
  - community incentives and lower manufactured-home costs ([[ROAD to Housing Act Enacted Without Presidential Signature]])
  - an expanded federal definition of manufactured housing to include homes built without a permanent chassis
  - HUD guidelines and model code for single-stair buildings up to six stories, with pilot grants ([McGuireWoods Consulting section-by-section](https://mwcllc.com/2026/07/17/21st-century-road-to-housing-act-section-by-section-guide/))
  - permanent reauthorization of the HOME Investment Partnerships Program, whose authorization had lapsed since 1994 though Congress kept funding it, plus a HUD study of Build America Buy America effects on HOME projects ([[HOME Investment Partnership Program Permanently Reauthorized]])
- **What was dropped.** An early Senate provision would have required institutional investors owning more than 350 single-family rentals to sell build-to-rent homes to individual buyers after seven years. The House removed it and it didn't survive conference, leaving the law's institutional-investor guardrails weaker than it advertises ([[Built-to-Rent Forced-Sale Requirement Dropped From Final ROAD to Housing Act]]).

## Critiques

- **Incentives too small; HUD too thin.** Before enactment, the Urban Institute found the CDBG-linked bonus too small: the median recipient would get about $55,000 a year, not enough for one staff position or unit. It also noted the bills load new mandates onto a HUD undergoing deep staffing cuts. It recommended conditioning part of federal transportation aid on housing growth as a stronger lever. H21, the House companion, went further on single-stair guidance ([[Federal Housing Bills ROAD Act and H21 -- Incentive Structures and Their Weaknesses]]).
- **Supply won't reach the bottom.** Fran Quigley calls the law "mid."
  - It eases financing and building for middle-income renters but does nothing for the 7 million households below the poverty line who pay more than half their income in rent and can afford about $224 a month.
  - Only 1 in 4 eligible households gets a voucher.
  - The National Housing Law Project called it "progress but … no silver bullet."

  ([[21st Century ROAD to Housing Act Critique Supply Without Subsidy Won't Fix the Crisis]])

## HUD actions affecting renters

- **Work requirements and time limits (proposed).** A proposed HUD rule would let housing agencies and subsidized owners impose work requirements of up to 40 hours a week and time limits as short as two years. It would cover Housing Choice Vouchers, public housing, project-based vouchers, and project-based rental assistance.
  - CBPP estimates up to 3.7 million people, including 1.9 million children, are at risk under the strictest version.
  - It's framed as optional, but Arkansas and Wisconsin have trigger laws.
  - Research on past work requirements shows no gains in employment or income, only more paperwork and lost assistance.
  - The proposal drew nearly 2,000 comments, mostly opposed.

  ([[HUD Work Requirements and Time Limits Would Strip Rental Assistance From 37 Million People]])
- **Family Self-Sufficiency defunded.** The 30-year bipartisan Family Self-Sufficiency program deposits rising earnings into an escrow account that voucher households receive when they graduate. The administration is defunding it, citing a HUD evaluation that found no earnings gain. Program rules changed mid-study, so the treatment and comparison groups operated under different terms ([[Family Self-Sufficiency Program -- HUD Defunding Context]]).
