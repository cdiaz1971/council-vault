# Asheville Could Eliminate Parking Minimums

## Summary

Asheville, North Carolina is considering a package of zoning amendments that would remove parking minimums citywide, expand ADU size allowances, and permit duplexes in all residential districts. The changes are part of a broader overhaul of Asheville's Unified Development Ordinance.

## Key Points

The city council will vote on several amendments simultaneously. The proposed removal of parking minimums is the most significant -- it would eliminate mandatory off-street parking requirements across the city, freeing developers to determine parking provision by market demand rather than code mandate.

Other amendments in the package allow larger accessory dwelling units and expand duplex-by-right to all residential zones. The package also introduces a "vulnerability index" to measure displacement risk as a mechanism for tracking equity impacts of increased density.

City Planning Director Stephanie Monson-Dahl described the amendments as part of the city's long-term UDO overhaul, which also requires updating the comprehensive plan to align with the new development regulations.

## Relevance

Useful comparative case for Frederick's housing policy discussions. Parking minimums are rarely on the agenda locally, but this illustrates the full scope of what a UDO overhaul can address. The "vulnerability index" concept is directly applicable to Frederick's Golden Mile and LMC work -- a displacement-tracking mechanism is something worth asking staff about as density increases.
