# Condon's Land Price Capture Critique of Upzoning

## Summary
Patrick Condon (University of British Columbia) argues that upzoning fails to reduce housing costs because landowners capture the value of new density through higher land prices, offsetting the benefit of increased supply. Vancouver is his central case, despite tripling housing units since 1960, it has the third highest housing prices in the world.

## Key Points
Condon's theoretical claim is that when a city authorizes a doubling of allowable density, the price of the underlying land rises in response because developers are willing to pay more for land that can now hold more units. The net effect is little to no reduction in the cost per square foot of the finished housing. Planetizen columnist Michael Lewyn pushes back on several points without fully rejecting the argument. He notes Condon's assumption that land prices rise in exact proportion to density seems arbitrary rather than derived, and that land costs have also risen sharply in cities that did not upzone, like New York and San Francisco, which cuts against a purely supply side explanation. Lewyn also flags that Condon's Vancouver only focus ignores whether the surrounding suburbs and exurbs kept pace with the city's housing production, since growth management policies that block sprawl could explain regional undersupply independent of the city's own upzoning record.

## Relevance
This is the strongest version of the upzoning doesn't help argument you're likely to encounter, and it lands close to the same terrain as Marjorie Rosensweig's CRG letter and Kevin Sellner's covenant proposal on the ADU Ordinance, both of which argue that removing use restrictions mainly benefits absentee investors rather than lowering costs. Worth having Condon's argument and Lewyn's specific rebuttals in hand before the Housing Production Target Resolution goes to individual council member outreach, since a councilmember skeptical of the 4,000 unit production floor is likely to cite exactly this kind of land capture logic.
