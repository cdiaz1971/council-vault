# Rockville Chapter 18 Rewrite - Adopted Ordinance Text

## Summary
Rockville amended City Code Chapter 18, "Rental Facilities and Landlord-Tenant Relations," by ordinance adopted July 6, 2026, effective January 1, 2027. This note is read from the adopted redline itself, with struck text removed, rather than from press coverage. Section numbers are Rockville's. The clerk's certification block on the published copy is unsigned and undated. Copies of the ordinance, the fact sheet, six staff reports, and the tenant and landlord input presentations are saved in Google Drive under City council / 02 - Legislation / Tenant Protection.

## Key Points

**Scope and enforcement architecture**
- Applies to all rental facilities, including single-unit dwellings and ADUs, all licensed (§ 18-3, § 18-111). Multi-unit and hotel licenses renew annually, single-unit and ADU every two years (§ 18-114).
- Violations are municipal infractions (§ 18-5). State law prevails where the two conflict, and all provisions are severable (§ 18-4(c), (e)).
- The **Commission on Landlord-Tenant Affairs is a pre-existing body**, expanded by this ordinance, not created by it. Seven members (two landlords, two tenants, three public) plus three alternates (§ 18-37). Power to summon witnesses and documents (§ 18-39(3)). After a hearing it may order lease termination with return of deposit and rent, damages up to **$5,000 per affected unit** (raised from $2,000), or rent reductions for lost services (§ 18-80(b)). Unpaid awards are enforceable in court by the party awarded (§ 18-80(d)). Appeals go to Circuit Court (§ 18-82).
- Complaints go first to the City Manager for investigation and conciliation, then to the Commission (§ 18-56 to 18-60). Tenant organizations have standing to file complaints for tenants who authorize them (§ 18-161(d)).
- **City-authorized repair and deduct:** if a landlord misses a correction deadline after inspection, the City Manager may authorize a tenant to hire a licensed contractor and deduct up to two months' rent, or revoke the license (§ 18-115(g)).
- Licensing: mandatory denial for three violations in two years not cured within 30 days, or one serious health and safety citation (§ 18-118(b)). Enhanced inspection of at least 25% more units for buildings with prior failures (§ 18-21(5)b). Out-of-area owners need an agent in Maryland, Virginia, or D.C. (§ 18-113(c)).

**Algorithmic pricing (§ 18-148)**
- Landlords may not use, subscribe to, or pay for an "algorithmic device," engage in price coordination, or agree not to compete on rents, fees, or terms.
- An algorithmic device uses non-public competitor data under 90 days old from two or more landlords to recommend rents, fees, terms, or occupancy.
- Exempt: aggregated reports published no more than monthly that do not recommend prices, tools used for affordable-program rent or income limits, and landlords with only one rental unit in the City.

**Fees (§ 18-163)**
- Banned: fees to pay rent by any method (except credit card processing and bounced checks), fees for services previously included in rent, fees for repairs the landlord already owes, any charge not billed within 120 days, and utility administrative fees, including trash, not expressly allowed by Maryland law.
- Capped: lost key at cost plus $25, lockout at $25 or documented third-party cost, bicycle parking at one-sixth or one-tenth of the car parking fee. Application fees follow Real Property § 8-213.
- Opt-in only: parking, internet, cable. Internet and cable billed at provider cost split evenly among opting units, with documentation.
- Pets: one monthly fee per pet plus one escrowed pet deposit, returned within 45 days with itemized proof of any deduction.
- Other fees allowed only after written, informed opt-in following a disclosure of the service, price, and how to cancel. Regulated and opt-in fees may rise once per 12 months with 90 days' notice. Landlord must refund anything collected above actual rent and required fees.

**Disclosure and pricing transparency**
- Every advertised price must disclose all mandatory fees and whether utilities are included, except on sites the landlord does not control (§ 18-180(b)).
- Before signing and at renewal: every fee with whether optional, basis, amount, and frequency, plus estimated utility costs (§ 18-180(c)). The lease must state the total monthly charge including mandatory fees (§ 18-147(g)).
- **Three years of rent history** before signing: increases and lease terms for the unit, and average year-over-year increases across the facility (§ 18-180(d)).
- Renewal offer rent must match the renewed lease (§ 18-147(e), § 18-180(a)).

**Leases and tenancy terms (§ 18-146, § 18-147, § 18-149)**
- Landlord must **offer** a two-year initial term. The tenant may decline. A shorter offer needs a stated "reasonable cause" addendum the tenant can challenge. Any increase during a two-year term must be disclosed in the lease up front.
- One rent increase per 12 months for a continuing tenant, with 90 days' written notice.
- Late fees capped at 5% of delinquent rent after a 10-day grace period. Entry on 24 hours' written notice, except emergencies.
- No jury-trial waiver, no court costs above actual costs, no attorney fees unless court-awarded and found reasonable. A tenant attorney-fee clause must be reciprocal and those fees are not rent for redemption purposes.
- Early termination on 30 days' notice, charge capped at one month's rent, for: involuntary job relocation, unit unfit for 60 days, license suspended or revoked within 60 days, unrepaired health and safety violations, domestic violence against the tenant or a dependent, death of a primary wage earner, change in disability status, entry into inpatient substance use or mental health treatment, or other cause beyond the tenant's control.
- Executed lease to the tenant within three days. Proposed lease to applicants at least two days before signing. Written reason required when a guarantor is demanded. Full rent payment history within 14 days of request.
- No eviction-history question on applications, though landlords may still consider eviction history as otherwise permitted by law.
- Household defined to allow up to five unrelated people, one household per unit (§ 18-1, § 18-96).

**Relocation assistance (§ 18-178)**
- Temporary (under six months, unit unfit): hotel, per diem, or comparable housing, plus moving and storage. Owed before the landlord takes possession for code work or within 24 hours of a City unfit notice, including when the tenant is behind on rent or facing eviction.
- Permanent: triggered by license suspension or revocation when the landlord stops renting, displacement of six months or more, a City finding the unit will be unfit six months or more, or demolition, redevelopment, substantial renovation, or change of use that forces out a household under 50% AMI before lease end.
- Permanent payment: deposit with interest, prorated rent, three months of HUD small area fair market rent, actual moving and storage, and an added amount set by the City Manager for households with someone over 62, a person with disabilities, or a minor child. Paid regardless of arrears.
- Exceptions: natural disasters and incidents beyond the landlord's control, tenant-caused conditions, legal eviction, or transfer to a comparable unit.
- City relocation costs are billed to the landlord and, if unpaid after a month, become a lien collected like property taxes (§ 18-178(d)).
- Displaced tenants have a right of first refusal to return on substantially similar lease terms (§ 18-178(e)).

**Tenant organizing (§ 18-161, § 18-181)**
- Listed rights to self-organize, act collectively, and distribute information. Free use of meeting rooms for tenant organization meetings. Landlords may not bar tenant organization activity on the premises.
- Local anti-retaliation section covering eviction, threats, privacy violations, harassment, service cuts, and unreasonable increases.

**Radon (§ 18-182)**
- Ground-contact and basement units only. Test within two years before the lease, tenant may test, landlord follow-up test within 14 days and mitigation within 90 days, tenant may terminate without penalty if the landlord fails to mitigate.

**Rental data and voluntary guidelines (Article V)**
- At every license issuance or renewal, landlords report a standard lease, owner contacts, building age and size, amenities, included utilities, a fee schedule, and unit-level current rent, increase offers, increases, **evictions**, and rent restrictions (§ 18-195(a)).
- Landlords keep utility costs and operating expenses by category for City review on request (§ 18-195(b)).
- Rent increase notices must include the City's voluntary rent guidelines and the landlord-tenant program contact (§ 18-194). The housing director reports annually, with some data published online (§ 18-195(d)).

**Not in the ordinance**
- No rent cap, good cause eviction, lease translation requirement, screening-report right, fair chance or first-in-time rule, climate control standard, or private right of action.

## Relevance
Rockville is the closest Maryland municipal peer for Frederick's tenant protection work and the model behind In-Progress Plans tenant protection item 1. Four points matter for Frederick:

1. **Real Rent.** § 18-163 and § 18-180 are an adopted Maryland municipal fee and disclosure template. The 120-day stale-charge rule, informed opt-in standard, once-a-year fee increase limit, and three-year rent history disclosure are not in Frederick's Real Rent draft.
2. **Coverage.** Rockville licenses every rental. Frederick's Ch. 12.5 exempts buildings with certificates of occupancy eight years old or newer, the gap the Real Rent revisions are addressing.
3. **Enforcement without a private lawsuit.** City-authorized repair and deduct, Commission damage awards, municipal infractions, license denial, and relocation costs recovered by lien form an enforcement stack that does not depend on a local private right of action, the issue raised by *Aleti v. Metropolitan Baltimore*. Whether Frederick's Charter supports the same structure is an open question.
4. **Post-revocation displacement.** § 18-178 is a working answer to [[What Happens After License Revocation -- The Missing Piece in Frederick's Tenant Protection Stack]].

**Correction for the Tenant Protections page:** describe Rockville's Commission as an existing body expanded by Chapter 18, and the lease rule as a required two-year **offer**, not a required two-year term.
