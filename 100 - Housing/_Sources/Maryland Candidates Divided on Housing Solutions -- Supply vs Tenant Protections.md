# Maryland Candidates Divided on Housing Solutions -- Supply vs Tenant Protections

## Summary

Maryland Matters primary voter guide surfaces the candidate split on housing: supply-side deregulators vs tenant protection advocates, with most candidates agreeing the crisis is real but diverging sharply on solutions -- useful for tracking General Assembly relationships and framing Frederick's dual-track approach.

## Key Points

**The consensus:** Among 141 General Assembly candidates surveyed, broad agreement that Maryland's housing costs are too high. Personal stakes are visible -- candidates citing homelessness, families leaving the state, workers priced out of their districts.

**Supply-side camp (bipartisan but skews Republican):** Increase production by simplifying permitting, reducing regulatory burdens, and reforming zoning. Del. Chris Tomlinson (R-Frederick and Carroll) specifically named: market-based approaches, permitting simplification, regulatory reduction. This is the Frederick County adjacent voice on housing -- worth tracking for LMC battles.

**Tenant protection camp (Democratic):** Rent stabilization legislation, stronger tenant protections, banning private equity ownership of single-family homes. Representative quote: "Simply removing zoning regulations may get us more houses, but it will not get us more affordable houses." The argument that supply-side reform without affordability mandates serves developers more than workers.

**Broader economic frame (some Democrats):** Root cause framing -- wages stagnant, rents rising, housing instability is a symptom of economic insecurity. "We must address root causes including low wages and lack of access to economic opportunity." This is the Affordability Is a Power Problem framing already in the vault.

**Small/alternative housing models:** Tiny home communities, cottage-style developments cited as entry points for first-time buyers and seniors downsizing -- connects to the Starter and Silver Homes Act that failed this session.

**The Frederick connection:** Del. Chris Tomlinson (R-Frederick and Carroll) is named explicitly as a supply-side, market-based voice. He represents a district overlapping Frederick County. His framing -- permitting simplification, regulatory reduction, market responses -- is the dominant Republican counter-argument César will face when advancing LMC reform, the Housing Production Target, and any inclusionary zoning work.

## Relevance

This is a political landscape note more than a policy note. Its value is in mapping the General Assembly candidate field heading into the June 23 primary -- understanding who holds what housing position helps anticipate which delegates and senators will be allies or obstacles on state housing preemption issues, the LMC reform, and any state-level rent stabilization moves.

The dual-track argument -- production targets AND tenant protections -- is politically coherent but requires explicit framing to avoid being captured by either camp. The Real Rent Ordinance and the Housing Production Target Resolution together are that dual-track. The Abundance Model vs Vienna Model note ([[Abundance Model vs Vienna Model -- Housing Politics Typology]]) is the theoretical frame; this note is the current Maryland political terrain.
