# Frederick County Childcare Landscape -- Background Reference

Summary of the Frederick County early childhood education and childcare report. Background reference for any future council work on childcare access or workforce development.

## The Supply Picture

Frederick County has 260 registered family child care providers and 116 licensed child care centers. A real-time mapping tool identifies Frederick County as a childcare desert -- meaning supply is substantially below demand relative to the child population. Most children currently served are school-age (6 and older); the underserved populations are disproportionately infants, toddlers, and preschoolers, where licensed care is most scarce and most expensive to provide.

## The Demand Picture

The majority of families seeking childcare do so because of work demands or because they are seeking employment. This makes childcare access a workforce participation issue, not just a family services issue. The report identifies high demand for full-time care specifically, with part-time care representing a smaller share of unmet need.

## Key Program Gaps

Child Care Scholarships (state subsidy program) have significant unmet demand. Both family providers and licensed centers in Frederick County have indicated willingness to serve scholarship families, but funding availability limits enrollment. Public pre-kindergarten and Head Start programs are also under-expanded relative to eligible population.

## Data Sources

The county's childcare data comes from the American Community Survey (ACS) and Census of Population and Housing. This means the underlying numbers lag by 1-3 years and should be treated as baseline estimates rather than current-state precision.

## Why This Matters for César

Frederick County is officially a childcare desert. If the council moves toward any childcare-related legislation -- employer partnerships, zoning for childcare facilities, or advocacy for state scholarship funding -- this report is the baseline evidence. The desert designation and the infant/toddler gap are the two most compelling data points for any public argument.
