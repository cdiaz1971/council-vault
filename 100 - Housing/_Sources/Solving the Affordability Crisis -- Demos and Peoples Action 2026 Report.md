## Summary

Demos and People's Action released a joint report in June 2026 arguing that the affordability crisis across housing, energy, food, health care, and child care is not the product of market forces but of specific, deliberate policy choices. The report, *Solving the Affordability Crisis: A Plan for a People-Powered, Racially Just Economy*, frames the crisis as reversible through different policy choices and outlines a five-pillar agenda.

## Key Points

**The findings by sector:**
- Housing: Half of all renters are priced out of the market; for Black renters, 56%. The private market will not deliver affordable homes for low- or moderate-income households without intervention.
- Energy: Investor-owned utilities raised electricity rates 49% above inflation while delivering billions in shareholder profits.
- Food: Corporate consolidation gave a small number of companies outsized pricing power. Roughly one in seven households lacked enough food in 2024; for Black households with children, nearly one in three.
- Health care: Insurance companies deny or delay care 850 million times per year. Most denials are overturned when challenged, indicating the system is built for profit extraction, not care.
- Child care: Average annual cost is $13,128 per child. No state meets the federal affordability benchmark.

**Five-pillar policy framework:**
1. Robust public investment in housing, child care, energy, and health care
2. Higher wages and stronger worker power through unions, living wages, and labor standards
3. Accountability for corporate power: break up monopolies, stop price gouging, enforce fair competition
4. Racially just taxation that makes the wealthy pay their share and invests in disinvested communities
5. Democracy as economic policy, because the same forces blocking affordable housing and higher wages are spending billions to shape elections

**Core framing:** These outcomes were caused by policy. They can be changed by policy. The report explicitly names democratic capture as the mechanism that sustains the affordability crisis.

## Relevance for Frederick

The Demos framing reinforces the structural argument underlying César's housing and affordability work. The racial disparity in housing cost burden (50% of all renters vs. 56% of Black renters priced out) maps directly onto Frederick's demographic context. The fifth pillar connecting economic policy to democratic capture is relevant to the City Values Act and anti-authoritarian organizing threads. The corporate power accountability pillar has direct application to data center and utility rate discussions.
