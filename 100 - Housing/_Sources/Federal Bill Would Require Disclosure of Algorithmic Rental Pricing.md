# Federal Bill Would Require Disclosure of Algorithmic Rental Pricing

## Summary
Rep. Kathy Castor (D-Fla.) introduced the Housing Price Transparency Act, requiring firms that create or influence rental pricing to disclose when algorithms are involved in setting housing and rental costs, enforced through the FTC's unfair-and-deceptive-practices authority.

## Key Points
The bill applies the Federal Trade Commission Act's Section 18(a)(1)(B) provisions on unfair or deceptive acts and practices to algorithmic rental pricing. Firms overseeing rental rates must report their use of pricing algorithms rather than operate them without disclosure. Enforcement runs two tracks: the FTC can act directly, and states can bring civil actions on behalf of tenants seeking damages, restitution, or other compensation. This is a transparency mandate, not a ban on algorithmic pricing itself, so it stops short of the stronger local measures some cities are pursuing.

## Relevance
Rockville's Chapter 18 rewrite, the primary peer model for Frederick's broader tenant protection package, already includes an algorithmic rent pricing and price collusion ban, a provision not yet in Frederick's Real Rent Ordinance draft. A federal disclosure requirement wouldn't replace a local ban, but it would establish baseline transparency nationally and could surface the kind of data that makes a local ban easier to enforce and defend. Worth tracking as this bill moves through Congress, since it strengthens the empirical case for adding an algorithmic pricing provision to the phased tenant protection package.
