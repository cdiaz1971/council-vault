# ROAD to Housing Act Signed Into Law Without Presidential Signature, July 2026

## Summary

The 21st Century ROAD to Housing Act became law at midnight on July 11, 2026, without President Trump's signature, after nearly a year of negotiations -- enacting a package of federal housing reforms including manufactured home price reduction and community housing incentives.

## Key Points

The bill became law on Friday, July 11, 2026, via the constitutional default (10 days without presidential action when Congress is in session). Trump neither signed nor vetoed it.

CITYBUILDER's brief is focused on North Carolina implementation -- the full provisions are paywalled -- but the key confirmed elements include:

- **Manufactured home price reduction** mechanisms (consistent with prior reporting on the bill)
- **Community housing incentives** to encourage localities to allow more housing
- A broad package of federal housing reforms described as potentially reshaping "how America builds"

This is the bill that passed the House 396-13 in spring 2026 after years of bipartisan negotiation. The lopsided House vote reflected genuine cross-partisan alignment on supply-side housing reform.

CITYBUILDER notes the NC Senate is also advancing a separate regulatory reform bill in the same period.

**What's not confirmed from this snippet:** The exact scope of the enacted provisions versus what was negotiated out. Prior vault notes on this bill (see related) contain more detail on the incentive structure and its limits.

## Relevance

This is a significant milestone for the housing production track. Federal law now incentivizes communities to allow more housing -- which creates both carrots (for jurisdictions that comply) and a national policy context that makes local obstruction harder to defend publicly.

For Frederick's Housing Production Target Resolution, the ROAD Act's enactment strengthens the argument that federal policy is moving in the direction of supply reform. It also ties directly to the manufactured housing conversation: if price reduction mechanisms are enacted, manufactured homes become a more viable missing-middle tool for the Golden Mile corridor.

The Maryland Housing Certainty Act and this federal action together represent a two-layer policy environment pushing in the same direction. Frederick's LMC reform process now operates against a backdrop of both state and federal supply incentives.
