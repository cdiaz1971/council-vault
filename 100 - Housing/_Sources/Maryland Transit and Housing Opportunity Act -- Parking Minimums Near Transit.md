# Maryland Transit and Housing Opportunity Act -- Parking Minimums Near Transit

## Summary

Maryland's Transit and Housing Opportunity Act restricts local governments from imposing parking minimums and density limitations in areas near transit, encouraging transit-oriented development. Signed by Governor Moore in May 2026, the law establishes a state-level framework for TOD that local zoning codes must accommodate.

## Key Points

Parking minimums near transit are widely recognized by planners as counterproductive: they increase development cost, consume land that could be used for housing, and undercut transit ridership by making it easier to drive. The Planetizen / Maryland Matters summary notes the Act restricts both parking minimums and "other density restrictions" near transit, suggesting it functions as a form of state preemption of local exclusionary zoning in transit zones.

The Act is part of the same Moore housing package as the Housing Certainty Act. A third bill in the package -- supporting smaller homes for younger and older households -- died in committee, which signals that the legislature was willing to go this far but not further.

The specific definition of "near transit" -- the distance threshold, mode types covered (MARC, bus, etc.) -- will determine how much of Frederick falls under the Act's coverage. Frederick is served by MARC commuter rail at the Frederick station and by Frederick County Transit buses. If MARC stops and major bus corridors qualify, portions of downtown Frederick and transit corridors could be in scope.

## Relevance

This Act is immediately relevant to Frederick's LMC modernization and Housing Production Target work. If state law now restricts parking minimums near transit, Frederick's LMC needs to be audited against that standard. Any LMC provisions that impose parking minimums or density caps in qualifying transit zones may be preempted. This also strengthens the case for transit-oriented density as a production strategy: the state has now explicitly endorsed it at the level of law, which gives Council political cover to push for it locally. The [[LMC Review Commission]] process should incorporate a TOD overlay analysis as part of its scope.
