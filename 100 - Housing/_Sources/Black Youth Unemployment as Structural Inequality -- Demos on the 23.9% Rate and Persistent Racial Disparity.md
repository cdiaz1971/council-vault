# Black youth unemployment as structural inequality -- Demos on the 23.9% rate and persistent racial disparity

From a Demos email update (June 25, 2026), summarizing their blog post "The Labor Market is Leaving Black Teens Behind."

## The finding

Black teens ages 16-19 searching for work face an unemployment rate of **23.9%** -- nearly twice the rate of their white peers. This disparity is not cyclical. White teenagers reach comparable unemployment levels only during severe economic downturns (the Great Recession, COVID-19). For Black youth, that level is the persistent baseline even when the broader economy appears strong.

## Structural causes

Demos frames this as the product of systemic barriers, not individual behavior:

- Discrimination in hiring
- Unequal access to professional networks
- Obstacles that limit opportunity before young people enter the workforce

A first job builds skills, opens future career pathways, and often provides critical income support for families. Unequal access to that entry point compounds disadvantage across a lifetime.

## Policy framing

Demos calls for targeted policy and investment to address the structural drivers -- not just demand-side labor market interventions but attention to the network and institutional access gaps that precede the formal job search.

## Frederick relevance

This is background framing for the economic equity dimension of housing and workforce policy. Several active threads in Frederick touch adjacent terrain:

- The care economy / HHS intersection (workforce stability for caregivers and low-wage workers)
- The Real Rent Ordinance's underlying premise that housing instability constrains workforce participation
- The Housing Production Target Resolution's argument that affordability gaps create downstream economic harm

This Demos data point -- that Black youth unemployment is structurally elevated regardless of broader economic conditions -- is useful context when making the case that housing affordability and workforce investment are inseparable from racial equity outcomes.
