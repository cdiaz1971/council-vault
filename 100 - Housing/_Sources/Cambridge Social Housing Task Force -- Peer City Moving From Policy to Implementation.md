# Cambridge Social Housing Task Force -- Peer City Moving From Policy to Implementation

## Summary
Cambridge, MA is establishing a Social Housing Task Force composed of elected officials, city staff, and housing advocates to evaluate feasibility and make recommendations. This follows a September 2025 policy order, a December 2025 Housing Committee meeting, and an April 2026 Finance Committee vote making social housing a budget priority. The city has over 20,000 families on the affordable housing waitlist.

## Key Points
Social housing in the Cambridge model means publicly financed, publicly owned, mixed-income housing with permanent affordability -- at least 40% of units affordable at or below 80% AMI, with a significant portion for very-low income households. The financial structure depends on three elements: low-interest government revolving fund loans, cross-subsidy from market-rate units to lower-income units, and reinvestment of surplus income to repay loans and fund future development.

Cambridge points to Montgomery County, MD's $100 million revolving social housing fund (requiring 30% affordable in residential developments) as the closest domestic model. Vienna's 40% social housing share of total units is the international benchmark -- rents in Vienna are significantly lower than comparable European cities, attributed in part to social housing supply and strong tenant protections.

Massachusetts has established several relevant funding mechanisms: the Boston Acquisition Fund (revolving low-interest loans), the $50 million Momentum Fund (mixed-income housing), and a social housing pilot embedded in the most recent Housing Bond bill. Cambridge also has $51 million in its FY2027 budget for affordable housing including social housing planning.

The task force starts work in June 2026. It will evaluate land the city already owns, financing options, feasibility, and governance models including tenant and community control.

The practical critique: social housing projects often do not qualify for LIHTC and may need to be financed more fully through public mechanisms, which limits how many units can be produced. Cambridge councillors themselves acknowledged this is not low-hanging fruit.

## Relevance
This is a direct peer city reference for Frederick's housing work. Cambridge is at the feasibility study stage -- which is roughly where Frederick could be in 18-24 months if César advances the Housing Production Target Resolution and a companion social housing exploration. Montgomery County's revolving fund model is already cited as a Maryland-specific precedent, which makes the argument to Frederick City more grounded. Worth monitoring the task force's findings.
