# Louisiana Homelessness Criminalization Bill -- Unpaid Labor and Jail Time

Louisiana House Bill 211 would make "unauthorized camping" a criminal offense, exposing unhoused people found sleeping outside to up to six months in jail and $500 fines on a first offense, and up to two years in prison plus unpaid labor on subsequent convictions. The bill allows defendants to avoid jail by entering a 12-month treatment program -- but those programs can charge participants for their costs, and people unable to pay can be required to perform unpaid labor to offset them. The National Homelessness Law Center called it one of the most punitive anti-homelessness bills in the country.

The bill is advancing through the Louisiana legislature as of late April 2026.

## Why This Matters for César

This is the policy opposite of Frederick's values on housing and immigration. As a peer-city and state-level negative example, it is useful context for the argument that homelessness requires housing policy responses, not criminalization. The unpaid labor provision is particularly relevant to any immigration or labor rights framing -- it creates a coerced labor mechanism targeting the most economically vulnerable.
