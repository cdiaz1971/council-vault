# Tenant Organizing Against Private Equity Landlords

## Summary

Effective tenant organizing against PE landlords requires piercing the corporate veil to identify the actual owner, then escalating pressure to the PE firm's pension fund investors and federal lenders -- the parties whose capital actually funds the operation.

## Key Points

PE firms and their housing investments operate through layered corporate structures deliberately designed to obscure accountability. The on-the-ground management company may be a wholly owned subsidiary or a contracted firm; neither is the decision-making entity. Organizers must trace ownership back to the PE firm itself.

**Research targets:**
- Size and geographic scope of the firm's housing portfolio
- Track record of similar practices at other properties
- Identity and wealth of PE executives
- Which pension funds and institutional investors hold stakes in the fund

**Escalation levers:**
- Existing habitability and code enforcement regimes are the most accessible tool; if the PE firm can be held liable (not just the management LLC), enforcement has more teeth.
- Pension fund pressure: tenants at Southern Towers broke CIM Group's refusal to negotiate by reaching out directly to the San Francisco Employees' Retirement System and Pennsylvania's Public School Employees' Retirement System -- CIM's institutional investors. Workers whose pension funds finance extractive landlords have both moral standing and practical leverage.
- Federal lender complaints: African Communities Together filed a complaint with Freddie Mac, which had guaranteed $346.7 million of CIM's Southern Towers acquisition loan. This brought FHFA scrutiny and ultimately produced the first-ever FHFA tenant protection requirements for multifamily finance (though these were gutted by Trump in April 2025).
- Algorithmic pricing: Providence, RI tenants organized to pass a law banning algorithmic rent pricing and generated public pressure to enforce it.
- Minnesota: Inquilinxs Unidxs por Justicia organizing prompted AG Keith Ellison to sue Pretium Partners/HavenBrook Homes; outcome included transfer of 345 homes to two area nonprofits.

**Ownership transparency as a prerequisite:** None of this organizing works if tenants can't identify who actually owns their building. LLC transparency laws -- requiring disclosure of true beneficial owners -- dramatically reduce the research burden. New York passed an LLC transparency bill (foreign LLCs only; Hochul vetoed expansion in 2025). This remains a major gap nationally.

## Relevance

Frederick does not currently have significant PE-owned multifamily presence at the scale of Alexandria or Providence, but the organizing frameworks here are directly applicable to any large corporate landlord. The pension fund pressure tactic is underused and potentially powerful in Maryland -- state employee pension funds invest in PE, and state legislators are responsive to arguments about public pension money funding tenant displacement. The LLC transparency gap is worth raising as a Maryland legislative priority. The Mamdani receivership model -- transferring ownership of poorly-maintained buildings to nonprofits -- connects to César's interest in community land trust development as a stabilization mechanism.
