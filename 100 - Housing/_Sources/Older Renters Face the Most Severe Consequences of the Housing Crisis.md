# Older Renters Face the Most Severe Consequences of the Housing Crisis

## Summary

One in five older adults is a renter, and most are severely cost-burdened -- spending more than half their income on housing. Fixed incomes, accessibility needs, social isolation, and the practical impossibility of relocation make their housing precarity qualitatively different from that of younger renters.

## Key Points

The dominant cultural narrative -- renting as a temporary state on the way to homeownership -- obscures the reality that older adult renters are the most vulnerable renter cohort in the country. About half of older renters are cost-burdened (spending more than 30% of income on housing and utilities); most of those are severely cost-burdened (spending more than half).

What makes their situation distinct from younger renters facing similar costs:

- **Fixed income vulnerability**: Social Security and pension income does not keep pace with rent increases. Undocumented older adults pay into Social Security but cannot collect it.
- **Accessibility requirements**: A broken elevator, malfunctioning HVAC, or unresponsive maintenance isn't just inconvenient -- it can be a health emergency. The article documents an elder who had to call the fire department to be carried downstairs to make a dialysis appointment.
- **Immobility**: Transportation constraints, proximity to medical services, and the need to be near support networks make moving difficult or impossible even when conditions deteriorate.
- **Social isolation**: Many older adult renters have no family nearby. Tenant advocates report: "If they lose this housing, that's it -- it's either this housing or a nursing home or the streets."
- **Fear of retaliation**: Tenants in poor conditions often won't report violations for fear of eviction, and the stress of eviction proceedings can directly exacerbate underlying health conditions.

Ageism compounds all of this. A "you're not paying much so be grateful" attitude from landlords is documented at Section 8 properties. Older adult renters on subsidized income are doubly targeted -- by poverty stigma and by age.

Those aged 50 and older are currently the fastest-growing demographic experiencing homelessness, and many will enter homelessness for the first time after retirement.

## Relevance

The Frederick tenant protection package is being scoped right now. This piece is a reminder that "tenant protections" can't be treated as a generic category -- the stakes are materially higher for older renters, and a well-designed package should include provisions that address their specific vulnerabilities. The ALICE data already shows District 2 has elevated poverty rates, high residential mobility, and a large foreign-born population, which means undocumented older adults who cannot access Social Security are a real local constituency. The relocation assistance provisions in the Rockville Chapter 18 model are particularly relevant here -- permanent displacement assistance triggered by demolition or substantial renovation protects exactly the people described in this article.
