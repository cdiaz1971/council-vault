# FTC REIT Merger Loophole -- The Accountability Gap in Corporate Landlord Policy

## Summary

A specific gap in FTC merger review rules allows REITs to consolidate rental housing portfolios without triggering antitrust scrutiny -- a federal policy target that complements local tenant protection work.

## Key Points

The core regulatory failure: standard antitrust merger review does not apply to REIT consolidation transactions in the same way it applies to other corporate mergers. This structural gap allows institutional landlords to grow their portfolios through acquisitions that would otherwise face federal scrutiny. The result is accelerating concentration of rental housing ownership without a regulatory check.

Debt Collective is running a direct campaign targeting the FTC to close this loophole (campaign link: Bit.ly/FTCProtectTenants26). The ask is specific and regulatory in nature -- not a legislative fix but an agency enforcement action or rule change. This makes it potentially faster-moving than congressional housing bills.

The distinction between production and ownership is central here. A city can increase housing supply while simultaneously watching that supply get absorbed by REIT portfolios -- in which case new units serve investor returns more than affordability. This is the structural argument for why tenant protection, rent stabilization, and CLT models must accompany production targets rather than treat them as sufficient on their own.

## Relevance

For Frederick's council work, this note pairs directly with the Housing Production Target Resolution and the Real Rent Ordinance. The REIT loophole is a federal argument that supports the local case: if the federal government won't regulate corporate landlord consolidation, local tenant protection ordinances become even more critical as a line of defense.

Worth watching whether the FTC acts on REIT merger rules under the current administration, and whether this becomes a state legislative issue (Maryland tenant protection coalition may pick this up). Connect to NLC federal advocacy tracking if that channel is active.
