# State Housing Joint Authorities as HUD Hedge

## Summary

With federal housing support at risk under Project 2025-aligned proposals, Shelterforce proposes that states create multi-state joint authorities to build housing together -- pooling resources, bonding capacity, and administrative infrastructure to reduce dependence on HUD and federal financing channels.

## Key Points

The piece argues that the threat of HUD dismantling (dodged so far, but still present) should prompt states to develop independent housing production infrastructure rather than remaining dependent on federal systems. Joint authorities spanning multiple states could issue bonds, administer programs, and finance development at a scale no single state could sustain alone.

The proposal is policy-generative rather than a description of existing programs. It represents a strand of thinking among housing advocates: if federal housing infrastructure is unreliable, what does a state-based alternative look like?

The article content was paywalled; this note is based on the available abstract and summary.

## Relevance

Maryland is an active housing policy state, and the conversation about state-level housing authority capacity is directly relevant to Frederick's relationship with DHCD and to the Housing Production Target Resolution. If Maryland were to pursue joint authority structures with neighboring states, it could affect the financing tools available for Frederick's affordability goals. Worth tracking as the federal housing funding picture becomes clearer.
