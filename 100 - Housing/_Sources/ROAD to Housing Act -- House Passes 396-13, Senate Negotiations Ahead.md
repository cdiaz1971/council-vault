# ROAD to Housing Act -- House Passes 396-13, Senate Negotiations Ahead

## Summary

The House passed an amended version of the ROAD to Housing Act on a 396-13 vote, stripping the anti-Build-to-Rent provisions that were a major flaw in the earlier version. Those provisions would have eliminated roughly 10% of the nation's annual housing stock increase -- about 70,000 homes. The bill now moves to the Senate, where bipartisan sponsors Tim Scott and Elizabeth Warren have signaled that further negotiations lie ahead before a version can pass and reach the president's desk.

## Key Points

The anti-Build-to-Rent removal is the headline change. The prior version of the ROAD Act drew criticism from housing advocates precisely because those provisions would have reduced housing supply in the name of promoting homeownership -- a contradictory outcome for a bill nominally designed to boost production. Their removal makes the bill substantially cleaner.

The 396-13 vote is a remarkable margin and reflects broad House consensus that federal action on housing supply is needed. It also gives Senate negotiators a strong signal that the House can pass a housing bill.

The Senate path remains uncertain. Scott and Warren's joint statement -- that "there's still work to be done" -- is diplomatic language for ongoing disagreement. The White House's posture on the bill is also a variable. CITYBUILDER notes that Raleigh separately voted to place housing and transit bonds on its November ballot, a peer city signal worth tracking.

## Relevance

The ROAD Act's federal incentive structure -- rewarding localities that permit more housing with infrastructure and grant dollars -- is directly relevant to Frederick's position. Frederick is already moving on the Housing Production Target Resolution and LMC reform; if the ROAD Act passes the Senate, Frederick would want to be positioned as a compliant jurisdiction to capture any federal benefit.
