# Food Access and SNAP

## Purpose

Use this page to decide whether a proposal addresses **food affordability, physical access, transportation, retail viability, benefit access, or emergency need**. Those problems overlap, but they are not interchangeable.

## A practical definition of food access

| Dimension | Question | Example measure |
|---|---|---|
| Affordability | Can households pay for adequate food after housing and other essentials? | Food hardship, SNAP participation, household budget gap |
| Proximity | Is an appropriate retailer reasonably near? | Network distance/travel time to a SNAP-authorized or full-service retailer |
| Mobility | Can people reach it safely and reliably? | Transit span/frequency, walkability, vehicle access, delivery coverage |
| Retail quality | Are healthy, culturally appropriate, and affordable foods consistently stocked? | Price basket, inventory, hours, acceptance of SNAP/WIC |
| Stability | Will access persist through shocks and business turnover? | Closures, margins, supply reliability, emergency capacity |
| Administrative access | Can eligible residents obtain and retain benefits? | Application/renewal completion, churn, processing time |

## Frederick baseline: what is known and what must be updated

The older concept page cited eight low-income/low-access tracts using the USDA **2019 Large Retailer Access Map**. That is a historical screening result, not a current count of “food deserts.” USDA’s current Food Access Research Atlas now includes a **2025 SNAP-authorized Retailer Access Map** based on June 2025 authorized retailers, the 2020 Census, LandScan 2020, and the 2020–24 ACS. The 2019 large-retailer layer still relies on 2019 stores, the 2010 Census, and 2014–18 ACS. [USDA ERS, Food Access Research Atlas documentation](https://www.ers.usda.gov/data-products/food-access-research-atlas/documentation)

For the next City analysis, map both layers and add:

- current retailers, prices, hours, SNAP/WIC acceptance, and closure risk;
- transit routes, walking barriers, vehicle access, senior/disabled mobility, and delivery;
- City Food Bank and nonprofit distribution sites, hours, eligibility, and utilization;
- housing cost burden and benefit participation; and
- resident experience, including culturally appropriate food.

Do not infer household food security from distance alone.

## SNAP: current policy context

SNAP is federally authorized and state-administered; the City does not determine eligibility or benefit amounts. Eligibility is not accurately summarized by a single “130% of poverty” cutoff. Household composition, gross and net income tests, deductions, categorical eligibility, immigration status, and work rules can matter. Direct residents to [MarylandBenefits.gov](https://mymdthink.maryland.gov/home/#/) or Maryland DHS for a current determination.

Maryland DHS reports that H.R. 1, signed July 4, 2025, expanded work-reporting rules, changed some exemptions and immigrant eligibility, changed utility documentation, ended SNAP-Ed funding, and shifted more administrative and potentially benefit costs to states. DHS states that up to **80,000 additional Marylanders** may need to report work hours. Updated work rules took effect November 1, 2025 for new applicants and at recertification for existing customers. [Maryland DHS, Important Changes to SNAP Benefits](https://dhs.maryland.gov/important-changes-snap-benefits/)

For City planning, the relevant risks are:

- benefit loss or interruption increasing emergency-food demand;
- additional documentation and recertification assistance needs;
- transportation and digital-access barriers;
- loss of nutrition-education capacity; and
- State budget pressure affecting outreach and administration.

Payment error rates include both overpayments and underpayments and are not a fraud rate. Do not use the metric as evidence of recipient misconduct.

Nevada's Division of Social Services offers one model for meeting the error-rate threshold without expanding enforcement broadly: a SAS Institute AI platform, live since April 2026, cross-references case data against income and rent databases to generate a monthly risk-ranked list, so caseworkers triage the cases most likely to contain errors rather than reviewing the full caseload at random. Nevada reported about $330,000 saved across 3,000 case reviews and 45 fraudulent cases identified in the platform's first month, while its own official cautioned it is "way too soon" to know whether the approach will meet the federal deadline. [Route Fifty, "Nevada official 'cautiously optimistic' about AI solution to reducing SNAP error rates," June 30, 2026](https://www.route-fifty.com/finance/2026/06/nevada-official-cautiously-optimistic-about-ai-solution-reducing-snap-error-rates/414544/). The tool changed what caseworkers look at first; it did not replace them or make eligibility determinations. That narrow, auditable, workflow-augmenting design, not a decision-replacing one, is the relevant model if Frederick or Maryland DHS considers similar tools under the H.R. 1 compliance pressure described above.

## Maryland retail financing

Maryland’s FY2027 **NourishMD Round 2** opened September 22, 2026, with Tier 1 applications due November 2, 2026. Awards range from $25,000 to $150,000. Eligible uses include refrigeration, shelving, build-out, SNAP/EBT technology, inventory/working capital, delivery vehicles, and certain energy improvements. Applicants must meet the current requirements, including serving an area with a **Fresh Food Gap Score of 65 or higher**. [Maryland DHCD, NourishMD Round 2 notice](https://dhcd.maryland.gov/news-stories/public-notices/nourishmd-round-2-grant-program-applications-open-september-22-2026)

This corrects the older page’s pilot-era threshold and status. The City can identify eligible areas, convene applicants, align permitting and infrastructure, and provide technical or matching support; it does not control State awards.

## Cash acceptance for essential goods

Maryland's HB 191, effective October 1, 2026, requires businesses to accept cash for in-person purchases of food, fuel, and health and hygiene products under $300 between 6 a.m. and 10 p.m., with no cash surcharge. [Maryland Matters, "New laws: No Kings, no trying youths as adults, no 'dynamic pricing' and a prehistoric shark," October 1, 2026](https://marylandmatters.org/2026/10/01/new-laws-no-kings-no-trying-youths-as-adults-no-dynamic-pricing-and-a-prehistoric-shark/); [bill page](https://mgaleg.maryland.gov/mgawebsite/Legislation/Details/hb0191/?ys=2026rs). This is a state consumer-protection law, not a City program, but it bears directly on food access for unbanked households, a population concentrated among immigrant residents and ALICE households without reliable access to mainstream banking. It addresses the same gap as the Municipal ID work from the opposite end: Municipal ID aims to get residents into systems that require identification, while HB 191 protects residents who remain unbanked in the meantime. Whether the City or County has any role in fielding noncompliance complaints, or whether enforcement sits entirely with the state, is unresolved and should be checked before referring residents anywhere.

## Local option matrix

| Option | Best fit | Main risk | Measure of success |
|---|---|---|---|
| Benefits navigation and recertification help | Administrative barriers/churn | Duplicating County/State role | Applications, renewals, prevented closures |
| Emergency food capacity | Immediate hardship or benefit disruption | Becoming permanent substitute for adequate income/benefits | Households served, wait, repeat need, referral completion |
| Mobile market/delivery | Mobility or sparse demand | High operating cost, weak continuity | Repeat customers, price, route coverage, subsidy per household |
| Retail capital/technical support | Viable operator lacks equipment/build-out | Subsidizing a business that cannot persist | Opening/retention, healthy inventory, prices, hours |
| Transit/walking improvements | Store exists but route is unsafe/unreliable | Access gains may be too small | Travel time, service span, pedestrian safety, ridership |
| City-site or procurement partnership | Public asset can fill a defined gap | Procurement/operating complexity | Reliable service, local sourcing where lawful, cost per use |
| Community food production | Education, community, supplemental supply | Overstating volume | Plots/users, harvest, participation, tenure stability |

## Decision test

Before approving a food-access initiative, answer:

1. Which access dimension is failing, for which population and geography?
2. Is the evidence current enough to locate the gap?
3. Does an existing provider already have the mandate and capacity?
4. Is the intervention recurring service, one-time capital, or emergency response?
5. What price, transportation, benefit, and cultural-access assumptions underlie it?
6. What happens when grant funding ends or a retailer closes?
7. How will residents be referred without creating a confusing parallel system?

## Minimum dashboard

- current retailer openings/closures and SNAP/WIC acceptance;
- price basket and hours at supported retailers;
- emergency-food demand, repeat use, unmet requests, and referral completion;
- SNAP application/renewal assistance and known benefit disruptions;
- travel time and transportation barriers in priority areas;
- NourishMD and other capital applications/awards;
- outcomes by neighborhood and priority population; and
- public cost per household or customer, with operating subsidy shown separately.

## Council checklist

- [ ] Identify whether the problem is affordability, geography, mobility, retail quality, stability, or administration.
- [ ] Date every map and distinguish 2019 LRAM from 2025 SRAM data.
- [ ] Verify current SNAP rules with Maryland DHS; do not publish a single cutoff as universal eligibility.
- [ ] Check provider capacity and avoid duplicative intake systems.
- [ ] Test retail projects for long-term operating viability.
- [ ] Define outcome measures beyond food distributed or grants awarded.
- [ ] Include a continuity plan for federal or State policy changes.
