# Homelessness

## Purpose

Use this page to separate four questions that are often collapsed in public debate:

1. **How many people are experiencing homelessness, and what does the count miss?**
2. **Which intervention fits which household?**
3. **Which level of government controls the relevant lever?**
4. **How will Council know whether an appropriation, contract, or ordinance worked?**

This is a decision reference, not a claim that one program model fits every person or that a one-night count describes the full local need.

## Definitions that matter

| Term | Working meaning | Decision caution |
|---|---|---|
| Sheltered homelessness | A person staying in emergency shelter, transitional housing, or a safe haven on the count night | Capacity limits can suppress the observed number. |
| Unsheltered homelessness | A person staying in a place not meant for human habitation | Visibility varies by weather, outreach coverage, and counting method. |
| Chronic homelessness | A federal eligibility category combining disability with a specified duration or recurrence of homelessness | It is narrower than “long-term housing instability.” Verify current HUD rules when designing eligibility. |
| PIT count | A one-night census conducted in January | Useful for a comparable snapshot, not an annual unduplicated total. |
| HMIS | The service system’s administrative database | Better for flows and service use, but it excludes people who never enter participating programs. |
| Continuum of Care (CoC) | The federally recognized regional planning and funding system | The City is a partner, not the sole system owner. |

## What the counts tell us—and do not

HUD reported **745,652 people experiencing homelessness on one night in January 2025**, including **266,320 people counted as unsheltered**. That was 3% below the 2024 one-night estimate, but 27% above 2013. These figures establish national scale; they do not diagnose Frederick’s causes or measure every person who loses housing during a year. [HUD, 2025 AHAR release](https://www.hud.gov/news/hud-no-26-037)

For local decisions, use three views together:

- the Maryland Balance of State CoC PIT count for a comparable annual snapshot;
- HMIS inflow, exit, return, and length-of-stay data for system performance; and
- school, health, eviction, outreach, and provider data for people the homelessness system may miss.

Do not quote a local PIT number without recording the count year, geography, sheltered/unsheltered split, and whether the source is final. The [Maryland Balance of State CoC](https://dhcd.maryland.gov/HousingDevelopment/Pages/BoSCoC/Point-in-Time-Count.aspx) should be the controlling source for the current Frederick County count.

## Frederick’s current service system

The City’s Housing and Human Services department operates federal and local programs including PATH outreach, SOAR disability-benefit assistance, and permanent supportive housing. Its public program page reports **23 scattered-site Housing First units serving 29 people** and states that this particular permanent-supportive-housing program serves chronically homeless single adults with disabilities; families and adults with children are not eligible for that program. [City of Frederick, Homeless Assistance Program](https://hhs.cityoffrederickmd.gov/149/Homeless-Assistance-Program)

That inventory is a program fact, not a complete system map. Before a funding decision, update the following table with County/CoC data:

| System component | Capacity / annual reach | Current wait or unmet need | Primary operator | Funding expiration |
|---|---:|---:|---|---|
| Outreach and coordinated entry | Verify | Verify | City/County/CoC partners | Verify |
| Emergency shelter | Verify | Verify | Provider network | Verify |
| Rapid rehousing | Verify | Verify | CoC grantees | Verify |
| Permanent supportive housing | At least 23 City scattered-site units / 29 people on City page | Verify | City HHS and partners | Verify |
| Prevention and diversion | Verify | Verify | City/County/nonprofits | Verify |

## Match the intervention to the problem

| Household situation | Primary response | Supporting response | Core outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| Imminent eviction or utility shutoff | Prevention assistance and legal/problem-solving support | Benefits and income stabilization | Homelessness avoided |
| Safe alternative available with modest help | Diversion or flexible assistance | Mediation, transport, document help | Shelter entry avoided |
| Short-term housing crisis with moderate barriers | Emergency shelter plus rapid rehousing | Landlord engagement and time-limited subsidy | Permanent exit, short stay |
| Long homelessness plus disabling condition | Permanent supportive housing | Voluntary services and health care | Housing retention and reduced crisis use |
| Acute behavioral-health or medical crisis | Clinical/crisis response | Housing navigation | Safety first; connection to appropriate housing |

The policy choice is therefore not “Housing First or services.” It is whether housing and services are sequenced and matched to need, whether participation conditions create avoidable barriers, and whether the system can document durable exits.

### Shared housing as a low-cost addition, not a substitute

Shared housing, two or more unrelated adults splitting an existing market-rate unit and its costs, is a tool for the "short-term housing crisis" and "safe alternative available" rows above because it uses existing inventory rather than new construction, which makes it fast to stand up with limited funds. Camden County, New Jersey built a shared-housing pilot system entirely from existing resources: the county paid for roughly 80 local providers to complete a Shared Housing Institute training program, then required master leasing, where a nonprofit rather than the individual tenant holds the lease and absorbs tenant-matching risk, as a funding condition for its highest-acuity populations. Landlord participation depended more on guaranteed rent and reduced turnover costs than on cash incentives. [Shelterforce, "How Camden County Is Using Shared Housing to Tackle Homelessness Without New Funding," April 10, 2026](https://shelterforce.org/2026/04/10/how-camden-county-is-using-shared-housing-to-tackle-homelessness-without-new-funding/)

This is a New Jersey county's reported program design, not an evaluated outcome study, and it is not evidence that shared housing works for every household; it is best suited to people who can safely cohabit and benefit from reduced isolation alongside reduced cost. For Frederick, the transferable question is whether the City's housing trust fund terms could seed a small landlord incentive pool and whether a local nonprofit (reentry-serving organizations are the most likely candidates) has the capacity to serve as master lessee. That is an unanswered local capacity question, not a recommendation that the City adopt the model.

## Authority map

| Level | Principal levers |
|---|---|
| City | HHS operations; local grants and contracts; land/building use; public-space rules; police practice; City-owned sites; coordination and data requirements |
| County | Human services, behavioral health, public health, corrections/reentry, broader housing programs, and regional coordination |
| State | Homeless-services funding; Medicaid and behavioral-health policy; landlord-tenant law; housing finance; statewide CoC support |
| Federal | HUD definitions, CoC/ESG funding, vouchers, fair housing, benefits, and program rules |

Council should avoid promising outcomes controlled by another level of government without a named intergovernmental partner and implementation commitment.

## Enforcement and public-space decisions

Enforcement may address a specific unsafe act or obstruction, but a fine does not create shelter, treatment, income, or housing. Before changing camping, panhandling, or related penalties, require:

- the exact prohibited conduct and the public interest being protected;
- current shelter availability and outreach protocol;
- constitutional and state-law review by counsel;
- expected enforcement, court, collections, and jail costs;
- a plan for people unable to pay;
- a comparison with non-penal responses; and
- reporting by location, disposition, repeat contact, and connection to services.

Treat proposed fine amounts, pending court matters, and federal grant conditions as time-sensitive. Recheck the ordinance file and controlling law immediately before a vote.

## Decision matrix

Score each proposal before funding or adoption.

| Test | Questions |
|---|---|
| Target population | Who is eligible, who is excluded, and how was the need estimated? |
| Fit | Is the intervention matched to the household’s housing and service needs? |
| Net capacity | Does it add beds/units/assistance or merely rename existing capacity? |
| Flow | Will it reduce entry, shorten homelessness, increase permanent exits, or reduce returns? |
| Equity | Are families, youth, veterans, people with disabilities, and people avoiding systems visible in the design? |
| Cost | What is the full local cost per household, including administration and match? |
| Sustainability | What happens when a grant or temporary subsidy ends? |
| Evidence | Are claims based on local administrative data, a comparable evaluation, or an anecdote? |

## Minimum dashboard

Report at least quarterly:

- first-time homelessness and returns to homelessness;
- shelter entries, exits, average and median length of stay;
- exits to permanent housing and 6/12-month retention where measurable;
- diversion and prevention outcomes;
- unsheltered outreach contacts and successful connections;
- units/slots available, utilization, and wait time by intervention type;
- racial, disability, household-type, age, and veteran disparities; and
- spending by intervention and funding source.

## Council checklist

Before action, confirm:

- [ ] The local baseline is dated and comes from PIT/HMIS/provider records, not a national statistic.
- [ ] The proposal names the target population and intervention type.
- [ ] The implementing agency and authority are clear.
- [ ] Capacity, operating cost, funding expiration, and staffing are disclosed.
- [ ] The contract defines outcomes, reporting cadence, and data access.
- [ ] Legal review is current for enforcement or eligibility restrictions.
- [ ] A review or sunset date is included.

## Source hierarchy

1. Adopted law, HUD/DHCD rules, grant notices, budgets, and executed contracts.
2. Final PIT/HMIS and local administrative data with definitions.
3. Independent evaluations and peer-reviewed research.
4. Provider reports and comparable-city cases.
5. News, advocacy materials, testimony, and anecdotes as leads—not dispositive evidence.
