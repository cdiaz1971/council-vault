# Frederick Housing Policy Baseline

## Summary

This note documents what Frederick's Land Management Code (LMC) and City Code Chapter 19 (Affordable Housing) currently say on seven housing policy domains: missing middle typologies, minimum lot sizes, ADUs, parking minimums, MPDU requirements and affordability depths, the City Housing Fund, and confirmed gaps. It is the reference baseline for the Frederick Ledger section of the housing policy digest.

The code figures come from LMC Supplement No. 13 (March 2025) and Chapter 19 as re-enacted by Ordinance G-23-20 (November 2, 2023), which supersedes the version in the base City Code docx for MPDU purposes. Local ordinances and Maryland laws enacted since then that change these rules are noted in each section and in section 8. For a parcel-specific determination, check the current published LMC and recent ordinances on the City's code page.

---

## 1. Missing Middle Typologies — What the LMC Currently Allows

Frederick's LMC is more permissive than a pure single-family code, but missing middle typologies are zone-dependent rather than citywide by right.

**Duplex dwellings** are permitted in R8, R12, R16, R20, DBO, DB, and DR districts, with minimum lot sizes ranging from 1,500 sq ft (downtown) to 3,500–4,000 sq ft in residential districts. Not permitted by right in R4 or R6.

**Townhouses** are permitted in R8, R12, R16, R20, DBO, DB, and DR districts, with a minimum lot size of 1,400 sq ft per unit in most residential districts. Not permitted by right in R4 or R6.

**Multifamily dwellings** are permitted in R12, R16, R20, DBO, DB, DR, and mixed-use zones. Minimum lot sizes range from 6,534–16,335 sq ft depending on district. Not permitted in R4, R6, or R8.

**Triplexes, fourplexes, sixplexes** are not listed as defined dwelling types in the LMC's dimensional tables. There is a passing reference to "quadraplex/sixplex" in the context of site plan thresholds, but no dedicated by-right permission structure for these types in residential zones. This is a notable gap relative to peer cities and the state's missing middle push.

**Key implication:** R4 and R6, Frederick's lowest-density residential zones, permit only single-family detached dwellings. No state mandate will change that. The Starter and Silver Homes Act (HB 239/SB 36), which would have required townhouse permissions in these zones, failed in the 2026 session, so opening R4 and R6 to missing middle is a local decision. See [[Maryland Housing Law and Policy]].

**Mixed-use zones:** Ordinance G-26-10 (approved August 20, 2026, effective August 30) allows first-floor residential in certain MU-1 and MU-2 mixed-use buildings with at least 35,000 square feet of first-floor area. See [[Zoning Reform]].

## 2. Minimum Lot Sizes

Per LMC Table 405-1 (Dimensional Regulations), current minimum lot sizes by district and dwelling type:

| District | Single-Family (sq ft) | Duplex (sq ft) | Townhouse (sq ft) |
|---|---|---|---|
| R4 | 8,000 | not permitted | not permitted |
| R6 | 7,000 | not permitted | not permitted |
| R8 | 6,000 | 4,000 | 1,400 |
| R12 | 5,000 | 4,000 | 1,400 |
| R16 | 5,000 | 3,500 | 1,400 |
| R20 | 5,000 | 3,500 | 1,400 |
| DB/DBO | 2,000 | 1,500 | 1,500 |
| DR | 2,000 | 1,500 | 1,500 |

**Key implication:** No state lot-size cap applies. The Starter and Silver Homes Act would have capped minimum lots at 5,000 sq ft where water and sewer are available, but it failed in 2026. Frederick's R4, R6, and R8 single-family minimums (8,000, 7,000, and 6,000 sq ft) stand unless the City changes them.

## 3. Accessory Dwelling Units (ADUs)

ADU regulations are in LMC Section 802, substantially revised by Ordinance G-21-15 (effective October 18, 2021).

**Where ADUs are permitted by right:** On lots with a single-family detached dwelling, duplex, or townhouse in the RO, R4, R8, R12, R16, R20, DBO, and DR districts. ADUs are permitted by right even in R4 — they are not restricted to higher-density zones.

**Key restrictions:**
- Only one ADU per lot.
- Owner-occupancy required: the property owner must live in either the primary residence or the ADU.
- Minimum 90-day lease term for the non-owner-occupied unit.
- Integrated ADUs (within the principal structure) cannot exceed 35% of gross floor area.
- Detached ADUs in new accessory structures capped at 800 sq ft, must be in the actual rear yard, and must be built on a foundation.
- Exterior materials must match the principal structure unless modified by Planning Commission.
- In TNDs, PNDs, and MU zones, no more than 10% of single-family lots in a development may include ADUs.
- One additional parking space required if existing parking is at or below the code minimum.

**Note:** The owner-occupancy requirement and the 10% cap in planned developments are restrictions that more progressive ADU codes have eliminated, and they limit production compared to peer models.

**Pending and required changes:** Maryland's 2025 ADU law (HB 1466) requires compliant local ADU rules by October 1, 2026. The City's pending ADU ordinance, PC25-614ZTA, would remove owner-occupancy and change ADU parking; it has not been adopted. Check the [[Council Tracker]] for its current status and [[Zoning Reform]] for the State requirements.

## 4. Parking Minimums

Parking requirements are set in LMC Section 607, Table 607-1. Key residential minimums:

| Use | Minimum Spaces | Maximum Spaces |
|---|---|---|
| Single-family | 2 per unit | no maximum |
| Duplex | 1.5 per unit | 2.5 per unit |
| Townhouse | 2 per unit | 3.0 per unit |
| Multi-family | 1.5 per unit | 2.5 per unit |

**ADU parking:** One additional space required only if existing parking is at or below the code minimum — more flexible than a blanket per-ADU requirement.

**Downtown:** Certain parking requirements are relaxed in the Carroll Creek Overlay (Section 420) and the downtown DB district. No citywide elimination of parking minimums exists outside downtown.

**Key implication:** Frederick requires per-unit parking minimums in all residential districts outside downtown, and no local parking reform has been enacted. State law does not change that. The Transit and Housing Opportunity Act (HB 894, Chapter 592 of 2026, effective October 1, 2026) bars parking minimums only near rail stations with at least hourly weekday service from 8 a.m. to 6 p.m. Frederick and Monocacy are Brunswick Line MARC stations without that service, so the law gives Frederick no parking relief. Parking reform here is a local ordinance question. See [[Parking Reform]].

## 5. MPDU Requirements and Affordability Depths (Chapter 19)

Chapter 19 (re-enacted G-23-20, November 2, 2023) is the substantive affordable housing chapter. Key provisions:

**Threshold:** Applies to all residential development projects of 25 or more dwelling units, and mixed-use projects with 25 or more residential units. No MPDU obligation below 25 units.

**Baseline requirement:** 12.5% of all units must be MPDUs. LMC Table 405-3 allows up to a 22% density bonus for developers who increase MPDU provision up to 15%.

**Affordability depths — for-sale units (§19-8):**
- One-third of MPDUs sold to households at or below 70% FCAMI
- One-third at or below 80% FCAMI
- One-third at or below 90% FCAMI
Pricing set so total monthly mortgage payment does not exceed 30% of threshold income.

**Affordability depths — rental units (§19-9):**
- One-third of rental MPDUs at rent equivalent to 30% of monthly income for households at or below 60% FCAMI
- One-third at or below 70% FCAMI
- One-third at or below 80% FCAMI

**Control period:** 40 years from original sale or rental. Resale prices capped at original price plus CPI adjustment plus fair market value of improvements. Covenants run with the land, senior to all permanent financing instruments.

**After control period:** First right of refusal to designated housing agencies before open-market sale.

**Payment in lieu (§19-6.1):** $2.00 per square foot of each dwelling unit in the development as of November 2023. Fees deposited into the City Housing Fund. Formula to be recalculated after three years of square footage and affordability gap data, which falls in November 2026.

**Housing agency set-aside:** Up to 20% of MPDUs in any development may be purchased or leased by designated nonprofit or government housing agencies, with a 45-day exercise window.

**Key implications:**
- Frederick's MPDU baseline (12.5%) is below the 15% target in the housing production target resolution.
- The rental affordability depth structure already reaches 60% FCAMI for one-third of rental MPDUs — consistent with the resolution's 33% sub-target at 60% AMI.
- The 40-year control period is solid but falls short of the permanent affordability that CLT models provide.
- The $2.00/sq ft payment-in-lieu rate is structurally low relative to actual affordability gaps. This is the clearest near-term legislative lever in the housing ordinance sequence.

## 6. City Housing Fund (Chapter 19, §19-18)

**The City Housing Fund exists in law**, established by §19-18 as re-enacted in 2023.

**Funding sources:** MPDU payment-in-lieu fees and MPDU foreclosure sale proceeds.

**Authorized uses:** Operating the MPDU program; purchasing/reselling MPDUs; addressing needs of households below MPDU income thresholds; supportive housing; homebuyer education and foreclosure counseling; MPDU accessibility and energy efficiency modifications; emergency loans to prevent mortgage defaults; health and life safety improvements in owner-occupied housing; financing LIHTC and other affordable housing projects.

**Key implication:** The fund's legal authority is broad but its capitalization depends entirely on the $2.00/sq ft payment-in-lieu fee and MPDU foreclosure proceeds — both structurally modest. There is no general fund appropriation or transfer fee revenue stream. The gap is capitalization, not legal existence.

## 7. Confirmed Gaps — Not in Current Code

**Tenant protections (local):** Frederick licenses rental units ($240 per unit for a two-year license, with registration and inspection provisions), and a rewrite of the rental licensing ordinance is under review. There is no just cause eviction ordinance, relocation assistance requirement, or rent stabilization beyond Maryland state law. May 2025 rent stabilization drafts in Google Drive are early research, not introduced policy. See [[Tenant Protections]].

**Junk fees ordinance:** Not enacted. The Real Rent Ordinance, César's all-in rental pricing proposal, is pending; check the [[Council Tracker]] for its status. Preemption risk and covered-unit definitions need City Attorney review.

**Community land trust:** No CLT relationship or city-CLT partnership in city code. No public land disposition policy directing surplus parcels to affordable housing use.

**Missing middle by-right in R4/R6:** No ordinance extending duplex, triplex, or townhouse permissions to the R4 and R6 districts. César's proposed amendments are in [[Housing -- In-Progress Plans]].

**Parking reform:** No ordinance eliminating or reducing residential parking minimums outside the downtown overlay.

**Funded inclusionary zoning:** No tax credit or subsidy mechanism offsetting below-market costs for MPDU-producing developers. Baltimore's 2024 property tax credit model is the direct peer comparison.

## 8. State Law Now Affecting These Rules

- **Housing Certainty Act** (HB 548, Chapter 591 of 2026, effective October 1, 2026): locks in the rules that apply to a substantially complete development application and, after approvals, vests rights for the longer of five years or a locally set period, with conditions and exceptions. Frederick's implementing ordinance G-26-11 passed September 17, 2026. Any LMC change affects only applications filed after it takes effect, so delay has a cost.
- **Transit and Housing Opportunity Act** (HB 894): see section 4. No effect on Frederick's parking minimums today.
- **Starter and Silver Homes Act** (HB 239/SB 36): failed in 2026. No statewide lot-size or housing-type mandate.
- **ADU law** (HB 1466, 2025): local compliance due October 1, 2026. See section 3.

Details and citations are in [[Maryland Housing Law and Policy]].
