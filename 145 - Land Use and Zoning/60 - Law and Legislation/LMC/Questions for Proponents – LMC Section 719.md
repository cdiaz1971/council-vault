> Version context: this note concerns the December 2025 Section 719 proposal. The April 2026 draft analyzed in [[Brownfield Text Amendment Review -- LMC Sec. 744]] uses Section 744 and a different process. Preserve this as an earlier reference; confirm which draft is under consideration before applying its requirements or questions.

[[LMC Section 719 – Site Soil, Water, and Air Characterization Ordinance Summary]]
## Scope & Purpose
1. Can you clarify which types of properties will be subject to this ordinance, and how the City will verify historical uses or contamination potential?  
2. Why is Fort Detrick specifically excluded, and are there any potential gaps in public health protection due to this exemption?  
3. How does this ordinance align with existing state regulations and voluntary programs like MDE-VCP? Are there redundancies or gaps?

## Process & Implementation
4. Who is responsible for reviewing Phase I and Phase II assessments at the City level? Will additional staffing be required?  
5. What is the expected timeline for MDE-VCP reviews, and how might this impact development schedules?  
6. How will the City ensure that applicants do not begin construction or other site activities before MDE clearance?  
7. How will public input be incorporated in the review of Response Action Plans? Are there formal procedures proposed for public notice or hearings?

## Technical & Compliance Questions
8. How will the City track contaminants identified on sites? Will there be a public database or reporting system?  
9. If Phase II identifies contaminants requiring mitigation, how will the City enforce adherence to the RAP?  
10. Are there specific thresholds for contaminants that would automatically require a RAP, or is it left to MDE’s discretion?  
11. What liability protections exist for the City and for developers who comply with this ordinance?

## Fiscal & Resource Considerations
12. Has there been an estimate of the fiscal impact on City resources for oversight, inspections, or public engagement?  
13. Will developers bear all costs of assessments and remediation, or are there potential City costs?  
14. Are there plans to provide technical assistance to smaller developers who may not have resources for Phase I/II studies or RAP implementation?

## Strategic & Policy Considerations
15. How will this ordinance affect redevelopment incentives and economic development goals in Frederick?  
16. Are there comparable ordinances in other Maryland municipalities, and what lessons were learned from them?  
17. How will the ordinance handle properties where contamination is discovered after development begins?
