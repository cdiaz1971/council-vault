[[LMC Section 719 – Site Soil, Water, and Air Characterization Ordinance Summary]]

Questions: [[Questions for Proponents – LMC Section 719]]
## 1. Scope & Coverage
- **Groundwater/Surface Water Flow:** Ordinance applies to sites “receiving surface or groundwater from nearby sites” but does not define thresholds or assessment methodology for neighboring contamination.  
- **Fort Detrick Exemption:** Excluding federal property may leave nearby residents or workers unprotected if contamination migrates offsite.  
- **Post-Development Discovery:** No clear mechanism if contamination is discovered **after construction begins**.

## 2. Process & Timing
- **MDE-VCP Dependency:** City processing halts entirely until MDE issues NFRD or COC. This may create **long delays** without contingency for time-sensitive developments.  
- **Phase I/II Assessment Standards:** References MDE guidance, but **does not specify who determines sufficiency** of assessments if discrepancies arise.  
- **Public Review:** RAP public review is mentioned, but no **timeline, notice requirements, or appeal process** is defined.

## 3. Enforcement & Oversight
- **City Staffing/Responsibility:** Ordinance assumes MDE oversight, but the City Planning Department must track submissions and enforce “no activity” rules. Staffing/resource requirements are undefined.  
- **Compliance Monitoring:** RAP implementation is overseen by MDE, but the ordinance does not **explicitly define City authority** to enforce or penalize violations.  

## 4. Legal & Liability Considerations
- **Developer Withdrawal:** Ordinance states that if an applicant withdraws from VCP, the application is invalid. Could **create disputes** if partial remediation or assessment was completed.  
- **Threshold Ambiguity:** Contaminant levels are referenced from MDE tables, but there’s no guidance on **how mixed or emerging contaminants** are handled.  

## 5. Equity & Access
- **Small Developers/Property Owners:** No provision for technical or financial support for smaller applicants, potentially favoring well-resourced developers.  
- **Community Engagement:** Public input is required only at RAP stage; **earlier engagement** is not mandated, limiting transparency for initial assessments.

## 6. Technical Clarifications Needed
- **Air Quality Assessment:** Section title includes air quality, but procedural steps focus on soil and water; **air monitoring methodology is unclear**.  
- **Contaminant List Updates:** MDE list is from 2018 — ordinance does not specify **automatic updates** as new contaminants are regulated.  
- **Adjacent Site Impact:** No clear procedures for **cumulative impact** of multiple contaminated sites in proximity.

---

**Summary:**  
The ordinance provides a strong framework for environmental protection, but gaps in **timing, enforcement, air quality assessment, public engagement, and support for smaller developers** could limit effectiveness or create procedural challenges. Council may want to request clarifications or amendments before adoption.
