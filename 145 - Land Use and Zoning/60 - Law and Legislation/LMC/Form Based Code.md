# Form-Based Code (FBC)
## One-Page Summary for City Council

### What It Is
**Form-Based Code (FBC)** is a zoning approach that prioritizes the **physical form of development**—building placement, height, frontage, and relationship to streets and public spaces—rather than focusing primarily on separating land uses. FBC relies on **visual standards, diagrams, and regulating plans** to implement a shared community vision for how places should look and function.

---

### Key Facts
- **Focus on form over use:** Regulates how buildings shape streets and public spaces, not just what activities happen inside them.
- **Regulatory tool:** FBC is adopted as law, not a voluntary design guideline.
- **Predictable outcomes:** Clear, visual standards reduce uncertainty for residents, developers, and staff.
- **Supports mixed-use & walkability:** Encourages compact, pedestrian-oriented development.
- **Community-driven:** Typically developed through public engagement, workshops, and visioning.

---

### Why It Matters Now (Latest Context)
- Many mid-sized cities are using FBC to support **downtown revitalization, housing diversity, and corridor redevelopment**.
- Research and practice increasingly link form-based approaches to **more sustainable, lower-car-dependence development patterns**.
- Cities similar in size (~90,000 residents) often adopt FBC **incrementally**, starting with downtowns or key corridors rather than citywide.

---

### Typical Components of a Form-Based Code
1. **Regulating Plan:** A map showing where specific building forms and street types apply.
2. **Building Form Standards:** Height ranges, build-to lines, frontage types, and massing.
3. **Public Space Standards:** Requirements for sidewalks, street trees, lighting, and plazas.
4. **Illustrations & Diagrams:** Visual explanations that clarify expectations and reduce interpretation disputes.

---

### Benefits
- Creates **predictable, high-quality development** outcomes.
- Supports **vibrant main streets and public spaces**.
- Enables **mixed-use and housing variety** without constant rezoning.
- Can **streamline approvals** by reducing discretionary reviews.

---

### Challenges & Limitations
- Requires **upfront effort** to draft detailed, context-sensitive standards.
- **Training needed** for staff, elected officials, and developers.
- May not be appropriate everywhere; often works best in **downtowns, centers, and corridors** rather than purely suburban areas.

---

### Form-Based Code vs. Traditional Zoning

| Topic | Form-Based Code (FBC) | Traditional (Use-Based) Zoning |
|------|----------------------|--------------------------------|
| **Primary Focus** | Physical form of buildings and public spaces | Separation of land uses (residential, commercial, industrial) |
| **Regulates** | Building placement, height, frontage, streetscape | Uses, setbacks, density, parking |
| **Visual Guidance** | Strong – diagrams, illustrations, regulating plans | Limited – primarily text-based |
| **Predictability of Design** | High – clearer expectations for built form | Lower – design outcomes often uncertain |
| **Mixed-Use Development** | Encouraged by default | Often restricted or requires variances |
| **Walkability** | Explicit goal through street and frontage standards | Indirect or incidental |
| **Approval Process** | Often more administrative and streamlined | Frequently discretionary and variance-driven |
| **Best Suited For** | Downtowns, corridors, activity centers | Single-use or low-change areas |

**Why Cities Shift from Traditional Zoning**
> Many cities find that traditional zoning produces inconsistent design outcomes, encourages auto-oriented development, and requires frequent variances for mixed-use or infill projects. Form-Based Codes are often adopted to provide clearer expectations, support walkable and mixed-use areas, reduce discretionary approvals, and better align development outcomes with adopted community plans and economic development goals.

---

### Open Questions for Council Consideration
1. Should Form-Based Code be applied **citywide or only in targeted areas** (e.g., downtown, corridors)?
2. How should FBC **integrate with existing zoning**—hybrid approach or full replacement?
3. What **staffing, training, or consulting support** would be required?
4. How might local developers and property owners **respond to form-focused standards**?
5. What level of **public engagement** is appropriate to ensure community buy-in?

---

### Two-Paragraph Explanation for Easy Use
**Form-Based Code is a zoning approach that focuses on what buildings look like and how they relate to streets and public spaces, rather than simply separating land uses.** By using clear diagrams and physical standards, it helps ensure that new development contributes positively to neighborhood character and creates walkable, human-scaled places. Instead of only regulating what is prohibited, it clearly communicates what is expected, giving developers and residents more certainty.

**For a community of about 90,000 residents, Form-Based Code can be a practical tool to guide growth, revitalize key areas, and support housing and economic development goals.** When implemented thoughtfully, it can improve design quality, encourage mixed-use development, and align new growth with community values. However, success depends on careful tailoring to local context, clear public engagement, and proper training for those who administer and use the code.
