# LMC Text Amendment -- Convenience Stores with Gas Sales (Wormald)

## Summary

Private applicant amendment to LMC Section 816, filed by The Wormald Companies, LLC (June 23, 2025), represented by McCurdy, Dean and Graditor, LLC (agent: Lisa L. Graditor, Esq.). Makes one targeted change: codifies explicit authority for the Reviewing Authority to determine the number of fuel dispensing pumps as part of the conditional use approval process. Announced at the April 2, 2026 public meeting; no vote taken. Will proceed to Planning Commission and a future public hearing.

## Key Points

Current Section 816 permits convenience stores with gas sales as a conditional use in NC, DB, and MU districts, subject to several conditions: arterial or collector street frontage, off-street parking compliance (no payment-in-lieu allowed), customer service area cap of up to 6,000 sq ft, and conformance with performance standards. The amendment makes two changes: (1) replaces the bracketed "[ZBA]" with "Reviewing Authority" -- a terminology update reflecting current LMC usage -- and (2) adds an explicit sentence giving the Reviewing Authority discretion over the number of fuel dispensing pumps permitted.

This is a narrow technical clarification. The Reviewing Authority arguably had this discretion implicitly under the existing conditional use framework; the amendment makes it explicit and certain. The amendment does not expand where gas stations are permitted, does not change the customer service area cap, and does not alter any other conditions.

Wormald is the applicant. They are an active Frederick developer -- The Wormald Development Company posted the surety released on the March 19, 2026 consent agenda (Monocacy Center Offsite Forest at Worman's Mill). They likely have a specific site or project in mind, though no project is identified in the application.

Watch items for the hearing stage: (1) Whether the Reviewing Authority actually exercises pump-count discretion in practice -- codification only matters if there is political will to use it. (2) How this interacts with NC-zoned sites adjacent to residential uses, where pump count has direct noise, traffic, and air quality implications. (3) Whether any companion design or operational standards should accompany the pump-count authority (hours of operation, canopy lighting, traffic study requirements).

## Relevance

Low direct connection to active legislative priorities. Worth monitoring because the conditional use process for gas stations in NC and MU zones touches neighborhood quality-of-life issues that can become constituent concerns, particularly in District 2's mixed residential-commercial corridors. The Wormald relationship is also worth noting given their broader development activity in the city.
