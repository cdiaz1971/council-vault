# LMC Text Amendment -- MU First-Floor Residential (GG Frederick Holdings)

## Summary

Private applicant amendment to LMC Section 838, Table 838-1, filed by GG Frederick Holdings, LLC (June 20, 2025). Seeks to add residential as a permitted first-floor use in Mixed Use Buildings in the MU-1 and MU-2 zones, limited to sites greater than 1 acre. Announced at the April 2, 2026 public meeting; no vote taken. Will proceed to Planning Commission and a future public hearing.

## Key Points

Current LMC Table 838-1 restricts ground-floor uses in Mixed Use Buildings to retail, office, and industrial. The amendment adds "Residential" to that list with an asterisk: the permission applies only in MU-1 or MU-2 for buildings on sites greater than 1 acre that contain a mix of uses. Second floor and above are unchanged (residential already permitted).

The applicant's justification argues that requiring a fully commercial ground floor on large mixed-use sites creates design, constructability, and financing challenges that discourage vertically integrated development. Allowing residential at grade on sites over 1 acre would permit a context-sensitive mix of residential and retail on the same level. Four Mid-Atlantic examples cited: The Otis (Richmond, VA), Union Wharf (Baltimore, MD), TENm.flats (Columbia, MD), and 19Nineteen (Arlington, VA).

The amendment applies city-wide across all MU-1 and MU-2 zones. GG Frederick Holdings is almost certainly pursuing a specific project -- the 1-acre threshold is not a neutral policy choice, it is a project-specific constraint that also happens to be a reasonable general rule.

Watch items for the hearing stage: (1) Which MU-1/MU-2 parcels over 1 acre are currently underdeveloped and would be affected. (2) Whether additional design standards are needed to ensure ground-floor residential does not hollow out street-level activation in mixed-use centers where retail is the planning intent. (3) Whether the 1-acre threshold is the right criterion or whether site context (proximity to transit, urban core vs. suburban edge) should be a factor.

## Relevance

Directionally aligned with housing production goals. The current code prohibition on first-floor residential in MU zones is a real constraint on certain mid-rise urban infill project types. Removing it could unlock units on sites that cannot pencil as purely commercial-retail ground floors. However, the amendment is applicant-driven and the council should evaluate it on its own merits as general policy, not just on the basis of one developer's project needs. Connect to housing production target resolution work and the broader question of what LMC reforms are needed to hit the 1,400-unit annual target.
