# Land Management Code

## Purpose

This page supports Council decisions about the City's Land Management Code (LMC): individual text amendments, a comprehensive review or rewrite, and how the code relates to the Comprehensive Plan and State law. Housing-specific zoning reform is covered in [[Zoning Reform]]; data center zoning in [[Data Center Regulation and Local Authority]]; site-specific rezoning and annexation in [[Annexation and Rezoning]].

## What the LMC does

The LMC is the City's zoning and development code. It sets zoning districts and the uses allowed in each (permitted, conditional, or prohibited), development standards, and the procedures and reviewing authorities (Planning Commission, Board of Zoning Appeals, Historic Preservation Commission, staff) for each kind of approval. Adoption date and current article structure: verify.

The LMC implements the City's 2020 Comprehensive Plan. State law requires each planning commission to review its comprehensive plan at least every ten years ([Land Use § 3-301](https://mgaleg.maryland.gov/mgawebsite/Laws/StatuteText?article=glu&section=3-301&enactments=false)).

## How the LMC changes

- **Text amendments** change the code's words citywide. They can come from private applicants, Council members, or staff, go to the Planning Commission for a recommendation, then follow Council's three-read sequence ([[Council Process and Boards and Commissions]]). LMC § 306(b) directs that Planning Commission and Council hearings on petitions for text amendments be scheduled "as soon as practicable," which allows timing to fit the complexity of the item.
- **State law now limits some local discretion over housing.** The Maryland Housing Certainty Act (HB 548, Chapter 591, effective October 1, 2026) governs a housing project's approval by the rules in effect when a complete application is filed and gives vested rights for at least five years ([HB 548](https://mgaleg.maryland.gov/mgawebsite/Legislation/Details/HB0548)). Council implemented it in Ordinance G-26-11 on September 17, 2026 ([[Hearing Minutes 2026-09-17]]). See [[Maryland Housing Law and Policy]].

## Environmental site assessment (§ 744)

The draft requires a Phase I environmental site assessment by an independent consultant for every major site plan; a Phase II if the Phase I finds recognized environmental conditions; and documented compliance with any continuing obligations. All documents become public on receipt, approval depends on complete documentation, and Planning may revoke an approval for fraud or material change. Fort Detrick is excluded as federal property ([[Brownfield Text Amendment Review -- LMC Sec. 744]]). Open issues include the draft's citation of ASTM E1527-05, which has been superseded by E1527-21; how the City's process meshes with the Maryland Department of the Environment's Voluntary Cleanup Program; enforcement; and the cost burden on small developers ([[Potential Gaps and  Concerns – LMC Section 719]]; [[Questions for Proponents – LMC Section 719]]).

## Comprehensive review

Three approaches are on the table:

| Approach | Structure | Cost and time | Key issues |
|---|---|---|---|
| **LMC Review Commission** | Advisory commission of 11 to 15 members appointed by the Council President with Council confirmation; nonvoting Council liaisons; reviews the whole LMC against the Comprehensive Plan and other City plans; dissolves three months after its final report ([[LMC Review Commission -- Draft Resolution Analysis -- 2026-04-12]]) | Staff support; volunteer time | Draft incomplete; developer representation uncapped; appointment authority needs counsel's review under Charter Art. III, § 6(c); equity goals lack targets |
| **Full modernization** (Council Member Taylor) | Four phases led by a term-limited Legislative Project Manager reporting to the Council President and a new Land Use Commission; resolve 5 to 10 code-versus-plan conflicts first; choose a regulatory model by resolution before hiring consultants ([[LMC Modernization -- Libby Taylor Proposal Analysis -- 2026-04-12]]) | About $1.39 million over three years; $276,650 FY2027 capital request | A 270-day pause on annexations, master plans, preliminary plats, and private text amendments once the draft reaches Council; little attention to affordability |
| **Targeted amendments** | A planning-and-zoning policy analyst drives specific text amendments instead of a rewrite ([[Policy Analyst Position as Alternative to Affordable Housing Commission]]) | One position | Changes arrive faster but piecemeal |

Questions the Council has not settled include scope, the relationship to the Planning Commission, Historic Preservation Commission, and Board of Zoning Appeals, conflict-of-interest rules for members, and whether to pause development during review ([[Land Management Code Review Commission Council Decision Points & Open Question]]; [[LMC Review]]).

## Regulatory models

| Topic | Form-based code | Use-based (Euclidean) zoning |
|---|---|---|
| Regulates | Building placement, height, frontage, streetscape | Separation of uses, setbacks, density, parking |
| Mixed use | Allowed by default | Often restricted or needs variances |
| Approvals | More administrative | More discretionary |
| Best suited to | Downtowns, corridors, activity centers | Areas expecting little change |

A hybrid applies form-based standards in targeted areas within a use-based code ([[Form Based Code]]).

## Authority map

| Lever | Who controls it | Council role |
|---|---|---|
| Text of the LMC | Council by ordinance, after Planning Commission recommendation | Direct |
| Comprehensive Plan | Planning Commission review; Council adoption | Shared |
| Approvals under the LMC | Reviewing authorities named in the code | Through the code's text |
| Housing approval rules and vesting | State (Housing Certainty Act) | Implementation only |
| Contaminated site cleanup | MDE | Local disclosure and sequencing |

## Decision framework

- Does the amendment change policy citywide or serve one site?
- Is it consistent with the Comprehensive Plan?
- Who benefits, and who bears costs, by neighborhood and income?
- Does State law already decide the question?
- Can staff administer and enforce it?

## Metrics

- Text amendments filed, by sponsor type, and time to decision.
- Variances and conditional uses granted, as signs of code friction.
- Housing units approved by zone and type.
- Code-versus-plan conflicts identified and resolved.
