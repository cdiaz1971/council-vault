# Annexation and Rezoning

## Purpose

This page supports Council decisions on adding land to the City and changing the zoning of specific properties: annexation resolutions and agreements, zoning map amendments, and amendments to past annexation terms. Citywide code changes are covered in [[Land Management Code]].

## Definitions

| Term | Meaning | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Annexation | Adding contiguous land to the City by resolution | Brings land under City zoning, services, and taxes |
| Annexation plan | Required statement of proposed land use, service extension, financing, and public facilities | The City's commitments and the developer's obligations |
| Annexation agreement or resolution terms | Conditions such as contributions, dedications, and phasing | Enforceable promises; can be amended later |
| Zoning map amendment (ZMA) | Rezoning a specific property | Site-specific; often tied to a project |
| Five-year rule | Limit on rezoning newly annexed land without County approval | Shapes what can be built in the first five years |
| APFO | Adequate public facilities requirements | Whether roads, schools, and utilities can serve the project |

## Maryland annexation law

- **Initiation.** By petition of at least 25% of registered voters and owners of 25% of the assessed value in the area, or by Council resolution with the same consents (Local Government §§ 4-402 to 4-404).
- **Contiguity.** The area must adjoin the City's boundary and may not create an unincorporated enclave (§ 4-401(b)).
- **Annexation plan.** Must address proposed land use, the schedule and financing for extending services, and land for schools, water and sewer, libraries, recreation, and public safety; it must be consistent with the City's comprehensive plan growth element (§ 4-415).
- **Notice and hearing.** Published notice four times (twice for 25 acres or less) at weekly intervals; hearing at least 15 days after the last notice; the County and State planning agencies receive notice to comment (§ 4-406).
- **Effective date and referendum.** An annexation takes effect at least 45 days after enactment. During that window, 20% of registered voters in the area, 20% of the City's voters, or the County governing body by two-thirds vote may petition it to referendum (§§ 4-407 to 4-410).
- **Zoning after annexation.** The City gains exclusive planning and zoning jurisdiction. For five years it may not allow a use substantially different from the County zoning or density more than 50% higher, unless the County expressly approves (§ 4-416).

Source for the above: [MML Annexation Handbook](https://planning.maryland.gov:443/Documents/OurWork/MML-Annexation-Handbook.pdf); [§ 4-416](https://mgaleg.maryland.gov/mgawebsite/Laws/StatuteText?article=glg&section=4-416&enactments=false).

## Frederick cases

### Gateway Property (2026)

On August 6, 2026 Council rezoned 24.33 acres of the Gateway Property from General Commercial to Institutional for a special events center and cricket stadium (PC24-984ZMA, 5-1), and amended the 2009 annexation resolution (PC24-983ANX, amending Res. 09-23, 6-0). The site is on the east side, north of Route 144, south of I-70, along the Monocacy River. The applicant, AcerInfra, plans 7,000 to 10,000 seats, up to 25,000 for occasional matches. The amendment raised the transportation contribution from $500,000 to $1.5 million, with no APFO credit, and replaced a 16-acre dedication with about 14 acres along the river for public recreation, including a boat launch and path. An events management plan is required ([[Gateway Property Zoning and Annexation]]). A Special Events Commission ordinance is being drafted to regulate events at such sites ([[Events Commission Ordinance Brief]]).

### Sage Way Farm (petition, 2025)

Owners petitioned in April 2025 to annex about 25 acres at 5509 Old National Pike, zoned agricultural by the County, for high-density residential development ([[Sage Way Farm annexation petition]]). Because the County zoning is agricultural, high-density residential within five years would need the County's express approval. Current status: verify.

## Authority map

| Lever | Who controls it | Council role |
|---|---|---|
| Annexation resolution and terms | Council, after consents and hearing | Direct |
| Referendum on annexation | Area voters, City voters, or County | None; can be petitioned |
| Zoning of annexed land, first five years | City, with County approval for major changes | Shared |
| Zoning map amendments | Council, after Planning Commission recommendation | Direct |
| Amending past annexation terms | Council by resolution | Direct |
| APFO and service capacity | City | Direct |

## Policy options

| Option | Addresses | Tradeoffs |
|---|---|---|
| Annexation standards in policy or code: affordability share, community benefits, fiscal impact analysis | Growth that does not serve City goals | May slow annexations; must survive legal review |
| Require independent fiscal impact analysis for annexations and large rezonings | Reliance on applicant numbers | Cost, which the applicant can bear |
| Tie MPDU or affordable housing commitments to residential annexations | Affordability | Negotiated case by case |
| Track annexation terms and payments on a public list | Commitments not delivered | Staff time |
| Seek County approval strategically within the five-year window | County zoning blocking City goals | County may refuse |

## Decision framework

- Is the area contiguous, and are the consents in place?
- What does the annexation plan commit the City to, and at what cost?
- Is the proposed use allowed under County zoning, or does it need County approval within five years?
- Are contributions and dedications enforceable, with dates?
- Who will live, work, or play there, and who bears traffic, noise, or infrastructure costs?

## Metrics

- Acres annexed per year and resulting zoning.
- Contributions due versus received, by agreement.
- Housing units and affordable units delivered on annexed land.
- Service extension costs versus revenue.

## Council checklist

- [ ] Consents and contiguity verified?
- [ ] Annexation plan complete and consistent with the comprehensive plan?
- [ ] Five-year rule addressed?
- [ ] Terms dated and enforceable?
- [ ] Referendum window noted?
