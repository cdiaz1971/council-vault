# Local Moratorium as the Post-Veto Strategy for Data Center Regulation

## Summary

After Maine's governor vetoed a statewide data center moratorium and the legislature failed to override, multiple municipalities moved to adopt their own local bans -- establishing local action as the fallback governance strategy when state-level regulation fails.

## Key Points

Maine Gov. Janet Mills vetoed a bill that would have banned data centers larger than 20 megawatts until November 2027. The legislature failed to get the two-thirds vote needed to override. In response, three municipalities -- Sanford, Westbrook, and Brunswick -- moved quickly toward local moratoriums. Sanford approved a 91-day pause on a 1,000-acre proposal along the Mousam River. Westbrook's housing and development committee advanced a moratorium to the full council. Brunswick's proposal went further than the state bill, covering any data center larger than one megawatt rather than 20.

The bill's sponsor, Rep. Melanie Sachs, explicitly encouraged municipalities to act locally after the veto failed: "communities should start looking at their own ordinances to decide whether they would like to welcome data centers or not." The pattern mirrors what happened with other state preemption failures -- local governments filling the gap the state left open.

Critically, the local moratoriums in some cases exceeded the state proposal. Brunswick's one-megawatt threshold is far stricter than the state's proposed 20-megawatt cap. Local action is not just a fallback -- it can be stronger than what the state proposed.

## Relevance

This is directly relevant to Frederick if Maryland ever faces a similar dynamic -- state legislation on data centers stalling or failing to pass, leaving localities to act. It also surfaces a structural argument César can use: local governments have both the right and the practical need to develop their own standards when state frameworks are absent or inadequate. The same logic applies to surveillance technology procurement.

The Westbrook mayor's framing is worth noting: the moratorium is about deciding whether data centers "fit for our community" -- an affordability and land use framing, not a technology framing. That is the correct register for a council conversation.
