# Cities Should Negotiate Data Center Deals From Leverage, Not Desperation

## Summary

An op-ed by Good Jobs First arguing that the framing of data center negotiations is deliberately rigged against localities. Companies present approval as a favor they are granting. The actual dynamic is the reverse: hyperscale data centers need specific infrastructure conditions that are increasingly scarce, and cities that understand this can negotiate accordingly.

## Key Points

The five largest tech companies announced over $710 billion in AI capital expenditures for 2026. Companies in that kind of buildout are not waiting for a county tax abatement to decide whether to build. They need the infrastructure. The public should negotiate from that fact.

The standard playbook -- LLC concealment, NDAs, consultant-manufactured urgency, threats to go elsewhere -- is designed to exhaust leverage before residents even know it exists. By the time a project becomes public, the site is selected, utilities are lined up, and zoning is nearly settled. The public's leverage has been spent before the public knew it could have any.

Good Jobs First documents that 14 states and scores of localities fail to disclose how much revenue they lose to data center tax abatements. States that do report show losses of $1B or more annually in Georgia, Virginia, and Texas. This is spending without a budget.

Elmo's reform sequence: (1) process transparency first -- no NDAs, no code names, no mystery LLCs, full disclosure of electricity/water use, backup generator count, subsidy requests, infrastructure requirements, and actual permanent job numbers before any vote; (2) stop subsidizing data centers entirely -- these are core infrastructure for trillion-dollar companies, not fragile startups; (3) real, enforceable community benefit agreements tied to actual project costs; (4) job quality standards for construction (PLA, prevailing wages, local hiring, apprenticeships) and permanent positions.

The 69% of Americans who say they do not want a data center near them is a leverage fact. Companies have fewer siting options than they want officials to believe.

## Relevance

Directly applicable to Frederick's data center zoning text amendment work. The process transparency argument maps directly onto the community benefit agreement framework. The framing -- treat these as transactions to be governed, not prizes to be won -- is exactly the posture Frederick needs going into any negotiation.
