# Executive Summary
City Council is considering the establishment of data centers in Frederick City. The process involves property annexation by City Council, with final zoning decisions resting with the County Council for transparency and public access. Prior to any legislative action, the recommendations from the Data Center Impact Study are crucial. Key concerns include power supply due to Maryland's dependence on imported power and the growing demand from hyperscale data centers in Adamstown, which could impact future Frederick City data centers. Water usage for cooling purposes is a significant concern, with potential solutions including evaporative cooling, glycol, chip chillers, and trapping heat in small water or other coolant compounds. Regulations are being proposed to address potential water-related problems, damages to neighboring properties, and upgrades to public treatment facilities.

# Key Points
- City Council determines property annexation for data centers; County Council has final say on zoning.
- The public prefers County Council's involvement for transparency and accessibility.
- Data Center Impact Study is crucial before enacting legislation.
- Maryland imports 40% of its power, with growing demand from Adamstown data centers affecting Frederick City.
- Only data centers with site plan approval have assurances of power availability.
- Additional transmission lines needed for power in Frederick City could take up to 10 years.
- Federal Energy Regulatory Commission (FERC) decreed data centers should provide their own generation.
- Data centers require water for cooling, with evaporative cooling being common but posing potential threats to efficient wastewater treatment.
- Newer technologies include adding glycol or other compounds, immersing computing equipment in coolants, and using chip chillers.

# Action Items
- Enact transparency and public access during the data center approval process through legislation.
- Establish rigorous requirements on issuing permits for new data centers.
- Mandate corporation-funded escrow accounts for data center-induced damages.
- Adopt amendments for less water-intensive cooling technologies in the future.

- How will the city ensure a stable power supply for data centers given Maryland's dependence on imported power and the growing demand from hyperscale data centers in Adamstown?
- Are there any potential environmental impacts from the use of evaporative cooling or other cooling technologies in the proposed data centers?
- What are the costs and timelines associated with upgrading public treatment facilities to accommodate the increased water usage from data centers?
