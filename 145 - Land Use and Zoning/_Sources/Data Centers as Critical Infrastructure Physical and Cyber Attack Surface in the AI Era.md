# Data Centers as Critical Infrastructure -- Physical and Cyber Attack Surface in the AI Era

## Summary

A CyberScoop op-ed by Claroty's Chief Strategy Officer arguing that the combination of AI workload growth and kinetic warfare precedents (drone attacks on Middle East data centers, Iranian-linked wipe of Stryker's global production via Microsoft Intune) has definitively moved data centers into critical infrastructure territory. The argument: if AI is integrated into military decision-making, the compute infrastructure that runs it is a high-value target -- for both cyber and physical attack.

## Key Points

Drone and missile attacks on cloud data centers in the Middle East in 2026 created downstream disruption across the companies and militaries depending on them. AI is now "totally integrated" into intelligence collection and military decision-making (NYT reporting). Disrupting AI infrastructure disrupts decision-making without conventional force.

The attack surface is broader than most organizations account for: power infrastructure (increasingly complex with on-site generation), building management systems (HVAC, access controls -- long depreciation cycles, often internet-exposed), liquid cooling systems for dense AI compute, and remote access pathways (vendors, contractors, system integrators).

The Stryker incident: a hacktivist group sympathetic to Iran gained access to Stryker's Microsoft environment and issued a wipe command via Intune, halting global production. No missile required.

Policy is moving toward treating data centers as critical infrastructure. CISA Secure by Design, IEC 62443, and the National Cybersecurity Strategy all reflect this direction.

Governance implication: resilience targets, IT/OT segmentation, remote access controls, and tabletop exercises for degraded operation are now baseline requirements for any organization dependent on data center infrastructure -- including municipalities.

## Relevance

Two angles for Frederick. First, for César's IT work: the Stryker/Intune vector is a concrete illustration of why Microsoft environment hardening and least-privilege remote access are not theoretical risks. Second, for council work: the critical infrastructure framing strengthens the argument for community benefit agreements and robust permitting standards on data center siting -- facilities that will be national security targets should not be approved through rushed, opaque processes.
