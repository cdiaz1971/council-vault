# Anti-Data Center Legislation -- State and Local Moratoriums Tracker

404 Media's Matthew Gault compiled a tracker of successful anti-data center legislative actions as of May 2026. Opposition is bipartisan and growing from Maine to California.

## Key Examples

**Colleton County, SC** -- Passed a six-month moratorium on data center construction in May 2026, with extension options, ahead of a planned 800-acre build that would have impacted 200 acres of untouched wetlands in the ACE Basin Estuary. This was the second attempted site for the same project after local opposition killed the first attempt in Georgia.

**Washington Township, MI** -- Prologis withdrew a 312-acre data center application after organized local opposition. The township clerk then added a temporary moratorium to create time for legal safeguards.

**Village of Caledonia, WI** -- Microsoft cancelled a planned 244-acre data center after 2,000 residents signed a petition opposing rezoning.

**Georgia** -- Considering a bill that would prevent cities from issuing permits to data centers until 2027.

**Maryland** -- A bill referred to committee would pause data centers until the legislature resolves how to provide power to them.

**Oklahoma** -- Considering a pause until 2029 pending research on water and utility rate impacts.

**Virginia** -- Considering a halt on new construction until specific power needs are met, significant given Virginia's massive existing data center concentration.

**Maine** -- A statewide moratorium passed but was vetoed by Governor Janet Mills, who wanted a carve-out for a data center already under construction. The veto illustrates that even sympathetic executives create political obstacles.

## Pattern

The organizing coalition that is taking shape cuts across party lines and is driving local civic engagement at a level not seen in decades. Residents are getting arrested at town halls for speaking too long; legislators are receiving death threats. The fight will shape American politics for years.

## Why This Matters for César

Frederick's data center zoning text amendment is happening in the middle of this national wave. This tracker provides peer precedents for moratoriums, permit pauses, and community benefit conditions -- all potentially applicable to the Frederick approach. Maryland's pending legislation is especially relevant as a state-level backstop or pressure point.
