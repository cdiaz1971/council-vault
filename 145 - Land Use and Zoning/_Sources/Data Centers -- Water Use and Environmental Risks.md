# Data Centers -- Water Use and Environmental Risks

From the December 19, 2025 council discussion. Water consumption is data centers' second major infrastructure impact after power, and it creates specific risks for Frederick's wastewater treatment system.

## How Data Centers Use Water

Data centers require large volumes of water primarily for cooling. The most common current approach is evaporative cooling: water is circulated to absorb heat from servers and then evaporated, removing the heat from the facility. This process consumes water rather than returning it to the treatment system, but the water that does return arrives in a chemically altered state that can disrupt biological treatment processes at wastewater facilities.

## Emerging Cooling Technologies and New Problems

Newer cooling approaches include glycol coolants (added to water to manage heat transfer), immersive cooling (computing equipment submerged in dielectric coolants), and chip chillers (direct liquid cooling at the processor level). These technologies reduce water consumption compared to evaporative cooling but introduce chemical compounds into wastewater that existing treatment facilities are not designed to handle. Upgrades to public treatment facilities to accommodate these compounds have cost and timeline implications that are not currently built into Frederick's capital planning.

## Legislative Requirements to Address

Legislation should require data center operators to disclose cooling technology and chemical inputs at permit application. It should mandate corporation-funded escrow accounts to cover data center-induced damages to neighboring properties and required upgrades to public treatment facilities. Amendments should anticipate less water-intensive cooling technologies and require operators to adopt them as they become available, rather than locking in older approaches.

## Regulatory Authority Questions

The December 19 discussion identified an open question about how cooling-related regulations interact with the county-level wastewater treatment authority. Frederick City's legislative authority over data center siting must be coordinated with the county's authority over treatment facilities. This coordination gap should be addressed in the Data Center Impact Study.

## Why This Matters for César

The wastewater treatment impact is the environmental risk that neighbors and environmental advocates will raise most concretely. Having a clear escrow and upgrade-funding requirement in the ordinance -- with the cost falling on operators, not taxpayers -- is the politically clean position. It also protects the city from being held responsible for treatment facility failures tied to data center activity it permitted.
