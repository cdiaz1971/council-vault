## Summary

The Neuron's May 27 issue frames the AI backlash not as an abstract cultural phenomenon but as a concrete political fight over who controls land, power grids, water supplies, and zoning decisions at the local level. Two institutions are responding in revealing ways: the US government is treating data center opponents as potential extremists, and Pope Leo XIV has issued an encyclical framing AI as a Babel-level civilizational risk.

## Key Points

WIRED obtained over 1,000 pages of DHS and FBI fusion center documents revealing that federal agencies are tracking "anti-technology extremists" -- a label applied to people opposing data center development. Critically, a nonviolent More Perfect Union video about data center impacts was flagged as a threat vector. Trump's AI Executive Order requiring 90-day advance model sharing with the government was scrapped at the last minute.

The risk is definitional: "anti-tech extremist" is an expandable category. What starts as surveillance of saboteurs can readily encompass peaceful zoning opponents, journalists, and local elected officials asking hard questions about community impact.

Pope Leo XIV's encyclical *Magnifica Humanitas* frames AI as a "Babel vs. Jerusalem" choice. His warning: AI touching jobs, credit, and services can exclude people without appeal mechanisms. His prescription: data carries social obligations, and AI rollouts require job protections and worker participation.

Goldman Sachs projects $800 billion in AI infrastructure spending by end of 2026. Anthropic's annualized revenue has reportedly grown 5x to approximately $45 billion.

## Why This Matters for Cesar

Frederick has an active data center policy track. The fusion center surveillance angle is directly relevant: if Frederick's council asks hard questions about data center community benefit agreements, participants in that process could potentially be caught in an expanding definitional net. The Pope's framework about AI exclusion without appeal is useful language for [[AI Governance and Public Sector Procurement]] -- it names what bad AI deployment looks like without requiring technical expertise.
