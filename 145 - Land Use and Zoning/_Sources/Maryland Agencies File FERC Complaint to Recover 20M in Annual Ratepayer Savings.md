# Maryland Agencies File FERC Complaint to Recover 20M in Annual Ratepayer Savings

## Summary

The Maryland Energy Administration, Office of People's Counsel, and Public Service Commission jointly filed a FERC complaint on July 2 seeking to end utility surcharges for PJM regional grid participation, now that Maryland law makes participation mandatory. The projected annual savings to ratepayers is $20 million. Utilities are expected to fight the change, potentially forcing extended litigation.

## Key Points

The Utility RELIEF Act, signed in May 2026, made utility participation in the PJM regional electric grid a legal requirement rather than a voluntary arrangement. The PSC chair's logic: "There's no reason to pay an incentive bonus for something that's a requirement in the law. Otherwise you're just taking money out of ratepayers' pockets for no good reason."

Ohio and California attempted the same move and were taken to court by utilities. Both the 9th and 6th Circuit Courts of Appeals ultimately agreed that utilities cannot levy the surcharge unless they participate voluntarily. Maryland is now on the same legal path, just earlier in the sequence.

BGE argued the state is not addressing the "primary driver of rising energy costs: Maryland's growing imbalance between electricity supply and demand," which is a reference to the data center buildout driving grid demand beyond available supply. BGE simultaneously put forward a rate increase proposal of $8/month for residential customers, which will go to the PSC for a decision by January 2027.

People's Counsel David Lapp estimated the litigation costs at "multiple hundreds of thousands of dollars" but said the $20 million in annual savings makes the fight worthwhile. The PSC is hiring two new attorneys focused on FERC.

## Relevance

This is a direct piece of the data center ratepayer harm argument. BGE's own statement names data centers as a driver of supply-demand imbalance and uses that as a rationale for raising rates. The logic runs exactly as David Lapp testified at the Frederick data center worksession: data centers impose costs on the grid, those costs flow through to ratepayers, and the surcharge mechanism is one channel for that cost transfer.

For the Frederick ZTA opposition record, this FERC fight is evidence that even Maryland's own regulatory agencies now consider the ratepayer cost of grid strain to be a live legal matter worth litigating, not an abstract concern. Add this to the Brandon Shores note and the grid failure note as evidence that the ratepayer harm vector is no longer theoretical.

The January 2027 BGE rate case timeline is also relevant: it will be active during the period when the Frederick Planning Commission ZTA proceedings are likely to resume.
