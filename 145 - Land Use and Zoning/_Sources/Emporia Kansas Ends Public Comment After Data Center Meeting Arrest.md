# Emporia Kansas Ends Public Comment After Data Center Meeting Arrest

## Summary
Emporia, Kansas arrested a high school teacher for clapping in support of data center opponents at a July 22 city commission meeting, then moved its August 5 and 19 meetings entirely virtual with no public comment period, citing death threats against commissioners over the proposed Flint Hills Digital Campus.

## Key Points
Physics teacher Lux Claridge was arrested and charged with disorderly conduct and interference with law enforcement after clapping roughly five times in support of a speaker opposing the proposed Flint Hills Digital Campus, a 1,000-acre gigawatt-scale data center. Commissioners had warned attendees that clapping would not be tolerated. Two weeks later, the city announced its August 5 and August 19 meetings would be held virtually with no public comment period at all, citing death threats against commissioners, though Claridge told 404 Media the threats appeared to come from outside the community rather than local residents. Commissioners voted 5-0 at the virtual meeting to refer the data center opposition petition to a Kansas court rather than act on it directly. Residents protested outside the (empty) auditorium during the virtual session. The city has not clarified how many threats it received or from where.

## Relevance
This is a sharp, citable example of the pattern named in the companion note on data center backlash becoming bipartisan politics: opposition escalating to the point that local governments restrict public participation rather than engage it.
