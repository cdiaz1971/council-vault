## Summary

A New Jersey municipality voted to ban all data centers after what GovTech describes as a "chaotic meeting" -- a case where community opposition, inadequate engagement, and a high-conflict public process produced the most restrictive possible outcome rather than a negotiated arrangement. The ban forecloses any community benefit agreement, any tax revenue capture, and any conditional approval framework.

This is the cautionary tail of the data center governance problem: when localities fail to build a proactive permitting framework *before* a contentious project arrives, the political dynamics can collapse into binary choices. Bans and blanket approvals are both failures of local governance capacity.

## Why This Matters for César

Frederick has active data center interest and an ongoing text amendment process. The New Jersey case is a direct argument for Frederick moving quickly toward a community benefit agreement framework and a clear conditional approval process -- before a chaotic public meeting forces a binary vote. The lesson is that engagement failure doesn't produce compromise; it produces maximalism on both sides.
