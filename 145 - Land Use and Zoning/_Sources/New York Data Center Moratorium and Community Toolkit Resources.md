# New York Data Center Moratorium and Community Toolkit Resources

## Summary

New York introduced the country's first statewide data center moratorium in July 2026 -- a one-year pause to develop environmental and grid protection rules. Next City compiled five community action toolkits for jurisdictions seeking to protect themselves from data center development without a moratorium in place.

## Key Points

**The moratorium.** New York's moratorium is one year, statewide, covering new data center construction. Purpose: give the state time to write rules protecting the energy grid and environment before approving additional capacity. At least 12 other states have issued temporary moratoriums. New York's is notable as the first statewide action.

**The scale context.** More than a third of Americans (38%) now live within five miles of at least one operational data center. Data center demand tripled over the last decade and is projected to triple again over the next decade. Newer construction is shifting toward rural areas, which often have lower water availability.

**Five community toolkits worth having.**

1. **Good Jobs First -- "What To Ask When A Data Center Wants To Come To Town."** Questions for public officials before approval: backup generator air pollution, water supply safeguards, NDA status. Addresses the LLC opacity problem (developers often use generic LLCs, making it hard to confirm the project is a data center). URL: https://goodjobsfirst.org/what-to-ask-when-a-data-center-wants-to-come-to-town/

2. **Brockovich AI Data Center Reporting.** Community awareness initiative from Erin Brockovich specifically tracking AI data centers (not all data centers). Includes a map of operational and under-construction AI facilities alongside community-submitted concerns and current legislation tracking. URL: https://brockovichdatacenter.com/

3. **Cuyahoga County Data Center Development Guide.** Local government guide covering the range of responses from moratoriums to community benefit agreements. Includes a checklist for officials. URL: https://cuyahogacounty.gov/county-news/county-news-detail/2026/06/10/cuyahoga-county-releases-data-center-development-guide

4. **MediaJustice -- "The People Say No: Resisting Data Centers."** 49-page organizing toolkit with glossary, slides, and step-by-step action guide. Targets Louisiana, Mississippi, Tennessee, Georgia, South Carolina -- but the framework is transferable. URL: https://mediajustice.org/resource/the-people-say-no-toolkit/

5. **Rio Grande Valley Broadband Coalition -- How Communities Can Negotiate For Local Benefit.** Framework for drafting a Community Benefit Agreement (CBA) or Public Benefit Agreement (PBA). Covers workforce security, tax abatements, sustainable cooling systems, zoning parameters. Includes examples of successful CBAs. URL: https://connecthumanity.fund/data-centers-are-coming-heres-how-communities-can-negotiate-for-local-benefit/

**The NDA problem.** Some local governments have signed NDAs with data center developers, making it legally impossible to track water usage and environmental impacts after approval. Good Jobs First recommends explicitly asking about NDA status before any approval, and refusing to sign them as a condition of permitting.

**Water as an unregulated resource.** Large data centers use up to 5 million gallons of water per day for cooling. There are currently no federal policies requiring data centers to use more efficient cooling systems or to report their water consumption. This is an active regulatory gap.

## Relevance

The Good Jobs First questions-to-ask guide is immediately usable for Planning Commission testimony on PC25-626ZTA. Several of the questions (backup generator emissions, water supply safeguards, NDA status) should be on the record before any ZTA vote.

The CBA framework from the Rio Grande Valley guide connects directly to [[Data Center Community Benefit Agreements -- Policy Models]] already in the vault. If the ZTA cannot be defeated, a CBA requirement should be the fallback position -- and having the toolkit in the record now establishes that Frederick had the resources to require it.

New York's moratorium is the strongest peer-jurisdiction precedent for a moratorium or interim review period. Twelve other state-level moratoriums are a growing body of evidence that responsible local governments are pausing before permitting, not rushing to approve.
