# Virginia Data Center Capital: Tax Breaks, Energy Costs, and Community Backlash

## Summary

Virginia became the world's largest data center concentration through compounding infrastructure investment, federal government proximity, and a 2008 sales tax exemption now costing $1.9 billion annually in foregone state revenue. Virginia is now navigating a new energy consumption tax, water regulations, community resistance, and a legislative study of whether to phase out the tax exemption -- a cautionary and instructive case study for Maryland and Frederick.

## Key Points

**How it happened.** AOL's 1997 arrival in Ashburn created anchor infrastructure. The Metropolitan Area Ethernet East exchange point followed. Compounding growth: infrastructure attracted companies, companies attracted more infrastructure. Federal government proximity (and its enormous data appetite) was the persistent underlying driver. A 2008 state sales and use tax exemption -- initially estimated to save the industry $1.5 million annually -- accelerated concentration. That exemption now costs $1.9 billion per year in foregone state revenue.

**The tax break politics.** A fight over whether to extend or phase out the exemption nearly caused a Virginia government shutdown in June 2026. Gov. Spanberger preserved the exemption (set to expire 2035 unless changed). The legislature added a new energy consumption tax: 1.1 cents per kilowatt-hour up to a cap, expected to generate $600 million annually. A work group is now studying phase-out options, with a report due November 2026.

**Loudoun County as the fiscal case study.** Loudoun has 71 of the state's 131 operating data centers. Data center revenue exceeded $1.3 billion in fiscal year 2027, allowing the county to cut homeowner real estate taxes every year for a decade and propose eliminating the vehicle license fee. This is the economic development argument proponents make -- real, but highly localized and highly context-dependent.

**The energy and water reality.** Dominion Energy alone reported data centers using 5,050 megawatts of peak-load power in 2023. The legislature approved new water use regulations for data-scarce areas and east of I-95 -- the first-ever state regulation of data center water consumption. Noise regulation also passed for the first time.

**Community resistance is growing.** Hundreds of residents now attend local government meetings to oppose specific projects near homes, drinking water supplies, and transmission lines. The pattern: zoning approval happens in meetings where municipalities can only ask whether the application meets code, not whether the project should exist. By the time community members mobilize, the technical approval threshold has already been cleared.

**What other states are watching.** Virginia is now the cautionary case: the tax incentive that seemed modest in 2008 became a $1.9B annual commitment as the industry scaled beyond anyone's projection. The energy consumption tax is an attempt to recapture some of that value. But the data centers are already built, already consuming, already locked in.

## Relevance

The Virginia trajectory is the most detailed available evidence of what Maryland risks if it permits data center concentration without the policy architecture Virginia is now scrambling to build retroactively.

For the ZTA opposition brief, the Virginia case supports two arguments directly:

1. **The tax commitment risk.** Virginia's "modest" 2008 exemption became a $1.9B annual liability as scale compounded. Maryland should not make zoning commitments now that will generate analogous fiscal lock-in later.

2. **The community resistance pattern.** Once data centers are in industrial zones, community members who oppose specific projects have almost no procedural leverage -- the zoning already permits them. The time to set conditions is before the ZTA passes, not after.

The energy consumption tax Virginia enacted (1.1 cents/kWh) is also a model for what Frederick and Maryland could require as a condition for any future approvals -- connecting to the FERC complaint and BGE rate case evidence already in the record.
