# Maryland Data Center Legislation -- 2026 Session Outcomes

## Summary

Of 17 data center bills introduced in the 2026 Maryland General Assembly session, only two passed -- including the Utility RELIEF Act -- while Gov. Moore and Democratic leaders backed a regulatory framework that stops short of blocking development.

## Key Points

The 2026 Maryland legislative session saw substantial attention to data centers: at least 17 related bills were introduced, but only two passed. The principal surviving legislation is the Utility RELIEF Act, introduced by House Speaker Joseline Peña-Melnyk. The act is designed to hold utilities and data centers accountable, strengthen long-term energy system reliability, and expand clean energy access.

Governor Wes Moore and state Democratic leaders publicly backed efforts to govern data centers rather than block them. Del. Julie Palakovich Carr (D-Montgomery) captured the dominant framing: the question isn't whether data centers should be built, but whether sufficient safeguards accompany them.

A separate energy bill -- which passed with bipartisan support -- included provisions incentivizing data centers that bring their own battery storage and non-emitting energy sources, and expanded a special utility tariff to require more data centers to pay for grid improvements their load demands.

## Relevance

The state legislative context shapes what local governments like Frederick can and cannot do through zoning and permitting. Understanding which bills passed -- and what they require -- is essential background for Frederick's own data center zoning text amendment work. The Utility RELIEF Act's accountability mechanisms and the expanded tariff provisions may create new leverage points or constraints at the municipal level.
