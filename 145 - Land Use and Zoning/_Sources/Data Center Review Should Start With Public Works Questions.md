# Data Center Review Should Start With Public Works Questions

## Summary

The American Public Works Association argues public works staff should shape data center reviews from the start, asking about water, power, roads, stormwater, phasing, and decommissioning before a project is locked in. APWA has released a Data Center Development Toolbox with the questions to ask.

## Key Points

APWA CEO Scott Grayson argues that data center proposals usually arrive framed around investment and economic development, while their infrastructure demands get examined late, after plans are substantially complete. Data centers draw water and power at levels unlike other buildings of similar size, and their construction and operation affect roads, traffic, stormwater, utilities, noise, lighting, and emergency access.

His core list of early questions: where the water comes from and how much is needed, whether existing systems can meet that demand while still serving residents, what utility extensions are required and who pays for them, how construction traffic affects local roads, what stormwater controls apply, and how the project fits land use and sustainability plans.

He also pushes review past opening day. Communities should understand phasing, how demand could grow over time, and what happens if the facility closes, with decommissioning plans and financial protections considered alongside construction approvals.

APWA's new Data Center Development Toolbox, built by its technology, engineering, sustainability, and water resources committees, takes no position for or against data centers. It gives public works staff a structured set of questions covering scale, location, water and power, transportation, stormwater, zoning, incentives, community benefits, outreach, and full life cycle.

## Relevance

The data center ZTA (PC25-626ZTA) is due back in November per the Watch List. A neutral, professional-association framework is useful because it cannot be dismissed as anti-development advocacy, and it makes the case that infrastructure questions belong at the front of review, not after a text amendment has already opened the door.

Two concrete asks it supports. First, a formal role for the Department of Public Works in evaluating any data center application before Planning Commission review, so water, sewer, and stormwater capacity are documented rather than assumed. Frederick's water supply constraints make this especially pointed. Second, decommissioning requirements and financial security, such as a bond, written into any approval, which fits alongside the community benefit agreement models already in the vault.

The APWA toolbox is a ready-made document to share with DPW staff ahead of the November revisit: https://www.apwa.org/resources/data-centers-and-public-works/
