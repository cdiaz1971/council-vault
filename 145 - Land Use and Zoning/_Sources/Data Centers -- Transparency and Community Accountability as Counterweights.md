# Data Centers -- Transparency and Community Accountability as Counterweights

## Summary

Virginia communities resisting data center overreach have found traction through FOIA requests, lawsuits, and organized local coalitions. Researchers note that politicians who sign NDAs or approve data centers without community input are beginning to face electoral backlash -- a sign that public accountability is a real counterweight to developer pressure.

## Key Points

The Culpeper case is the clearest example. After town planners coordinated privately with data center developers, residents filed FOIA requests and uncovered the communication. The town planning commission chair questioned the "unprecedented access" being given to developers. The community then formed the Coalition to Save Culpeper to advocate for noise, air quality, water, and visual impact protections.

The American Battlefield Trust pursued litigation in Prince William County and Orange County over zoning decisions made without proper process. Prince William County ultimately chose not to appeal two Digital Gateway rezoning losses -- a significant win achieved through sustained legal pressure.

The broader lesson from UVA's Ferreira: as more information about data center operations becomes publicly available, politicians who approved them without involving the community are facing voter backlash. Transparency is not just an ethical norm -- it's becoming a political liability not to have it.

Pushing data centers to less-regulated jurisdictions is not a good outcome. The goal is adequate regulation in Frederick, not displacement to somewhere with no standards at all.

## Relevance

Frederick's ordinance should include mandatory public participation requirements before any data center approval -- not as a courtesy, but as a procedural prerequisite. NDAs between the city and developers should be explicitly prohibited. This note supports the community benefit agreement framework and the case for early public hearings as part of zoning review.
