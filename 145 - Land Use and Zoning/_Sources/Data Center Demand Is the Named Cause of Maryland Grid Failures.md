# Data Center Demand Is the Named Cause of Maryland Grid Failures

## Summary

Two separate official sources -- PJM Interconnection's June 2026 affidavit and the Maryland People's Counsel's FERC complaint -- now formally name data center electricity demand as the primary driver of Maryland grid failures, transmission cost escalation, and the inability to retire fossil fuel plants on schedule. This creates an official record that local opposition to data center siting can cite directly.

## Key Points

PJM's senior manager of long-term transmission planning, Mark Sims, stated in a June affidavit that transmission upgrades required for Brandon Shores retirement have been delayed "in some instances by several years" due to land acquisition, permitting, and supply chain challenges -- all of which stem from the volume of transmission investment triggered by data center load growth.

PJM separately described the load growth driving these challenges as "unprecedented," and has asked FERC for a decision on the extension by early August.

David Lapp (Maryland People's Counsel) named the cause directly: "That other load growth is data center growth -- almost entirely outside the state of Maryland." His office's FERC complaint argues that over the last three years, PJM has unlawfully assigned Maryland ratepayers responsibility for $2 billion in transmission upgrades caused by data centers, in violation of cost allocation principles.

The solution Lapp raised -- and described as "the solution that no one is talking about" -- is telling data centers they cannot connect to the grid until capacity exists. This is the regulatory backstop that currently does not exist at the state or local level but which the ZTA opposition record could help build pressure toward.

Meanwhile, Trump administration federal dollars are flowing toward reopening the coal-fired Warrior Run plant in Western Maryland, and data center developers have expressed interest in building new natural gas generation at shuttered coal sites on the Potomac River. The pattern is: data center demand creates grid pressure, which creates political space to extend or reopen fossil fuel generation.

## Relevance

The June 11 worksession already had Lapp on the record about ratepayer harm. This article extends that record by confirming the mechanism is already producing observable damage in Maryland -- not as a projection but as a current cost being charged to ratepayers. The ZTA opposition can now argue: we have direct evidence, from the state's own utility ratepayer advocate and from PJM's own planning documents, that data center siting decisions impose real infrastructure costs on Marylanders with no local recourse.

The Warrior Run reopening and Potomac River gas generation interest are useful context for countering the "clean energy future" framing that data center developers sometimes use in their presentations.
