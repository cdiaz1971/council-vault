# Data Center Federal Legislative Landscape -- Congress Debates Rate Protection and Moratorium

## Summary

Community resistance to data centers has moved from local and state battles to Congress, where multiple competing bills seek to address energy cost impacts on consumers while the industry deploys $226M in lobbying. A Sanders/AOC moratorium bill has gained no traction. The Trump administration and EPA are moving to accelerate data center construction permitting. Grassroots opposition remains intense -- in May 2026, community members screamed and booed when Box Elder County, Utah commissioners approved resolutions supporting a 40,000-acre data center campus.

## Key Points

**Congressional bills in play:**

The GRID Act (Hawley-Blumenthal, bipartisan) would guarantee no consumer utility rate increases due to data centers and require data centers to use separate generation sources rather than drawing from the shared grid.

The Energy Bills Relief Act (148 Democratic sponsors, Reps. Levin and Casten) would ensure "facilities like data centers are paying for their own costs" rather than shifting expenses to household electric bills.

The Power for the People Act (Tonko, House and Senate Democratic support) directs FERC to ensure data centers pay for local transmission upgrades they require.

A Sanders/AOC moratorium bill would bar new AI data centers until "strong national safeguards are in place" covering safety, labor, energy, and environment. Called "suicidal for the country" by Republican members; no meaningful traction.

**EPA move.** On May 11, the EPA proposed making it easier for data centers to begin construction before receiving federal clean air permits. Framed as supporting AI infrastructure development and "affordable baseload power."

**White House Ratepayer Protection Pledge.** Major tech companies signed a March 2026 pledge to cover their own energy costs rather than shifting them to ratepayers -- described by critics as "a handshake and a promise" without enforcement mechanism.

**Industry lobbying.** OpenSecrets documented $226 million in lobbying by the electric manufacturing and equipment sector (including Microsoft and Oracle) in 2025, aimed in part at backing data center growth and addressing grid strain concerns.

**Data context.** Data centers used approximately 4.4% of total U.S. electricity in 2023 (Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory); that figure is projected to double or triple by 2028.

## Relevance

This is the federal backdrop for Frederick's data center text amendment, which is still at the Planning Commission. The political dynamics at the federal level -- ratepayer protection as the bipartisan touchstone, moratorium as a non-starter -- suggest that community benefit agreements and energy cost allocation are the viable policy levers at any level of government. A CBA framework that requires data centers to cover infrastructure costs and provide community benefits fits the GRID Act logic even if Frederick is acting locally.

The EPA permitting change is worth watching: if construction can begin before clean air permits, the window for community input during permitting is effectively shorter. That makes early community benefit agreement requirements more important, not less.
