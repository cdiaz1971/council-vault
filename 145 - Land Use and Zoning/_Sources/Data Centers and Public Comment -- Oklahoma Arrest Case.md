# Data Centers and Public Comment -- Oklahoma Arrest Case

## Summary
In February 2026, a resident attending a Claremore, Oklahoma city council meeting to oppose a proposed data center was arrested and jailed after going a few seconds over his allotted three-minute public comment period. The city charged him with trespassing. The incident is a sharp example of how data center opposition can be met with suppression of public participation, and it has direct relevance to how Frederick structures its own public comment and community benefit processes.

## Key Points
Darren Blanchard attended the Claremore City Council meeting on February 17, 2026, during a public hearing on Project Mustang, a proposed data center. When he went slightly over his three-minute limit, he was ordered arrested and transported to the county jail. The city charged him with trespassing based on the overage. Blanchard vowed to fight the charges.

The arrest is notable not just as an overreach but as a signal of how data center development can become politically charged to the point where municipal governments suppress dissent. The fact that this happened during a structured public comment process -- not a protest or disruption -- makes it particularly significant for anyone thinking about how to design accountable, community-responsive permitting.

## Relevance
Frederick is actively working on a data center community benefit agreement ordinance. This case illustrates what the failure mode looks like when local governments prioritize development over community voice. It strengthens the case for robust, protected public participation requirements baked into the ordinance rather than left to council discretion. It is also useful context for how other municipalities have handled data center opposition nationally.
