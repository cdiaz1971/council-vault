# DOE Emergency Order Keeps PJM Grid Online During October Heat

## Summary
The U.S. Department of Energy issued an emergency order letting PJM delay seasonal maintenance, require large users such as data centers to switch to backup power, and relax some environmental limits during an unseasonable mid-Atlantic heat stretch. PJM cites the regional data center boom as part of the strain.

## Key Points
- DOE emergency order, in effect through Friday of the week of October 13, 2026, during warm days (Baltimore forecast high of 87 on Wednesday).
- PJM Interconnection, operator for 13 states including Maryland, may delay or cancel seasonal transmission maintenance to keep lines active, and may demand that large-load customers such as data centers run on backup power. PJM also got temporary relief from some environmental restrictions.
- Energy Secretary Chris Wright framed it as ensuring "affordable, reliable, and secure electricity."
- PJM says the grid has been under pressure partly because of mid-Atlantic data center growth.
- Maryland regulators recently approved a $1.5 billion PJM transmission upgrade that allows the state's last coal plant to close.

## Relevance
Another documented instance of data center load named as a grid stressor in Maryland, now severe enough to trigger federal emergency action outside peak season. Note the mechanism: data centers ordered onto backup power means on-site diesel or gas generation running near neighborhoods, which bears on emissions and noise conditions in any Frederick data center ZTA.
