# Darren Blanchard arrested at data center public meeting -- bodycam footage and the chilling effect on public comment

Reported by Matthew Gault, 404 Media, June 25, 2026.

## What happened

In February 2026, farmer Darren Blanchard attended a Claremore, Oklahoma city council meeting focused on community concerns about a planned data center project called "Project Mustang." Residents opposed the project, which they felt had been approved without meaningful public input -- city officials had signed NDAs on behalf of the developers.

Blanchard went a few seconds over the three-minute public comment time limit. Two officers moved in immediately. He was handcuffed and removed while the crowd booed and shouted. He was charged with criminal trespass ($200 penalty). He has vowed to fight the charge and his legal team has filed a motion to dismiss and requested the city attorney recuse himself, as he was present and witnessed the arrest.

This is the first public release of the bodycam footage, shared directly with 404 Media.

## Blanchard's own framing

He names the chilling effect as his central concern -- not just his individual case:

> "If someone can be arrested after speaking at a public meeting, others may decide it is safer to stay quiet. That should trouble everyone, regardless of where they may stand on data centers."

On the process-as-punishment dynamic:

> "Even if my charges are dismissed and the arrest is deemed unlawful, the process I have endured is the penalty."

On the pattern of data center development:

> "A pattern is unfolding where these developers come in with promises of jobs and investment, public officials are swayed to move quickly, oftentimes incognito via nondisclosure agreements and the long-term costs are pushed onto residents who had little say in the process."

## Why this matters for Frederick / PC25-626ZTA

This case is a vivid national precedent for the chilling effect that aggressive data center governance can have on democratic participation. Several elements map directly onto the Frederick situation:

- NDAs between city officials and developers -- exactly the transparency concern raised in the Frederick ZTA debate
- Community members who feel decisions were made before the public entered the room
- The use of process and legal pressure to suppress opposition rather than engage it
- The arrest occurring at a public meeting over a procedural technicality -- a visible escalation of suppression tactics

As opposition strategy: this case is useful evidence that **the democratic legitimacy argument is not theoretical**. Communities attempting to exercise meaningful input on data center siting are facing active suppression. Frederick can position its ZTA opposition and any future governance framework as an affirmative stand for democratic process -- not just land use management.

The arrest also reinforces the argument in [[Community Interest Without Institutional Anchor Gets Buried]] that without proactive institutional support, community voices get extinguished by process even when they represent majority sentiment.
