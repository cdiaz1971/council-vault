# Anne Arundel Moratorium Grandfathers Adjacent Phases and Repeals Data Center Tax Break

## Summary

WMAR's report on the October 5 Anne Arundel vote adds details missing from earlier coverage: a 7-0 vote, a hard January 31, 2028 sunset, grandfathering of future phases of existing campuses onto adjacent parcels, and repeal of the county's personal property tax assessment reduction for data storage centers.

## Key Points

This is a supplement to [[Anne Arundel County Downgrades Data Center Ban to 15-Month Moratorium]], which covers the same vote. Only details not in that note are recorded here.

The Council voted 7-0 for the amended Bill 63-26. The moratorium ends January 31, 2028, or earlier if the county adopts comprehensive data center legislation, whichever comes first.

The ban-to-moratorium amendment was adopted in September, not at the October 5 meeting. October 5 was the final vote on the amended bill.

Grandfathering is broader than "projects already in development." Pending projects generally continue, and the amended bill also allows future phases of existing data centers on adjacent or adjoining properties. Microsoft, which is building at Annapolis Junction Business Park, testified that it supported regulations and a work group but wanted existing campuses to keep developing. The adjacent-phase clause gives it that.

The work group's scope is named: energy and water use, utility infrastructure, noise, air quality, environmental impacts, and economic development.

The bill also repeals a personal property tax assessment reduction for qualified data storage centers, pairing the pause with removal of an existing incentive.

## Relevance

Two drafting precedents for any Frederick response to PC25-626ZTA. First, the adjacent-phase exemption shows how a moratorium can quietly leave the door open for campus expansion. Any City moratorium language should decide that question deliberately rather than inherit it. Second, Anne Arundel paired the pause with stripping a tax incentive, which is a fiscal lever separate from zoning and less exposed to vesting claims under the Maryland Housing Certainty Act framework. Worth checking whether the City or County offers any comparable assessment break. Extends the Maryland county pattern tracked on the [[Council Watch List]].
