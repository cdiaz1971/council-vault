# Community Opposition to Data Centers -- Coalition Patterns and Tactics

The 404 Media tracker reveals a set of tactical patterns in successful community opposition to data center projects.

## What Works

**Sustained planning meeting presence** -- Communities that show up consistently at planning and board meetings create a visible record of opposition that makes approval politically costly. In Washington Township, MI, local opposition at planning meetings preceded Prologis withdrawing their application entirely.

**Petition campaigns** -- 2,000 signatures defeated a 244-acre Microsoft data center in Caledonia, WI. At scale, petitions signal electoral consequence to local officials.

**Legal action alongside legislative action** -- In Colleton County, SC, local landowners and the South Carolina Environmental Law Center were already suing before the county passed a moratorium. Legal and legislative tracks reinforce each other.

**Moratoriums as breathing room** -- Even a six-month moratorium with extension options creates time for communities to develop legal safeguards and frameworks. The moratorium is not the end goal but a tool for creating conditions for better outcomes.

**Bipartisan framing** -- The opposition cuts across party lines. Noise, water consumption, and property value concerns are not partisan. This is a tactical asset.

## What Fails

Companies that bypass early community engagement create their own obstacles. Nancy Pierce, a Village trustee in Caledonia, noted that not being allowed to engage directly with Microsoft early in the process became an obstacle at every stage. Companies that treat community engagement as a post-decision formality consistently face stronger opposition.

## Why This Matters for César

Frederick's data center zoning text amendment process should build in genuine early community engagement requirements as a policy condition, not just a nice-to-have. The evidence from peer jurisdictions shows that companies which skip this step generate exactly the kind of organized opposition that kills projects entirely. A community benefit agreement framework gives industry a legitimate path while protecting the community.
