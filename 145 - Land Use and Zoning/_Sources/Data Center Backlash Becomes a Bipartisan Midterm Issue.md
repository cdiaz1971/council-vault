# Data Center Backlash Becomes a Bipartisan Midterm Issue

## Summary
Data center opposition has moved from planning commissions to statewide races: 39 local ballot measures in eight states this November, a UMass poll with only 11% support for a local AI data center, and candidates of both parties retreating from past support. Industry PACs are responding with spending and contested astroturf tactics.

## Key Points
- **Polling:** UMass Amherst (Tatishe Nteta) found 11% of Americans would support an AI data center in their community; 65% oppose. Opposition crosses party lines.
- **Ballot measures:** 39 in eight states this November (Ballotpedia), 21 of them in Ohio. Four earlier 2026 local measures went overwhelmingly against data centers. Three Kansas communities, including Ottawa, vote on bans.
- **Grievances:** grid strain and rate increases, pollution, water use, noise, farmland conversion, private "behind-the-meter" gas plants, closed-door deals and NDAs with tech companies, plus objections to AI itself.
- **Scale:** U.S. data centers use about 49 GW today; planned or under-construction projects total nearly 350 GW, about a quarter of current national generation capacity. Ohio has 154 operating and 84 planned.
- **Politics:** Ohio Senate race (Husted attacked by Sherrod Brown as "the face of data centers"; an NRSC memo called the issue "the anchor around Husted's neck"); both Pennsylvania gubernatorial candidates backed away from past support; Texas Gov. Abbott issued a moratorium on new permits. Ottawa, KS: Democratic and Republican party officials allied against the project.
- **Industry response:** Leading the Future, a tech super PAC, funds candidates of both parties; it has been linked to an AI-bot "news" site soliciting quotes and to a dark-money group paying influencers to push China-competition messaging. Its co-leader argues backlash targets the worst speculative projects.
- Good Jobs First (Anthony Elmo): "If the industry you're trying to regulate immediately agrees with everything you're suggesting to regulate them, I'd suggest that those regulations are not extremely effective."

## Relevance
Direct ammunition for the PC25-626ZTA revisit in November: the opposition is bipartisan and electorally potent, so a restrictive ZTA is not a partisan risk. The Elmo line is a useful test for any "responsible development" amendments industry offers. The NDA and closed-door-deal grievance supports transparency requirements in any Frederick framework.
