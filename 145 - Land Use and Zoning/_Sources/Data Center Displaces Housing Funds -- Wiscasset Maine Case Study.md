# Data Center Displaces Housing Funds -- Wiscasset Maine Case Study

## Summary

In Wiscasset, Maine, local officials' attempt to redirect $240,000 in ARPA housing funds toward a prospective $5B data center resulted in the county rescinding the money -- before the data center ever broke ground. A concrete peer-city case study for the PC25-626ZTA record demonstrating how data center pursuit can directly eliminate existing housing investment.

## Key Points

**The sequence of events:** Wiscasset, a rural Midcoast Maine community of 4,000, had been allocated nearly $1 million in American Rescue Plan Act (ARPA) funds for affordable housing. A 300-acre former nuclear plant site on the Back River was the prospective location for those units. In September 2025, plans for a $5 billion data center on that same land became public. Local officials pivoted to pursue the data center instead.

**The consequence:** On March 3, 2026, Lincoln County reallocated Wiscasset's $240,000 in federal housing funds to neighboring communities -- Waldoboro and Newcastle -- for their own affordable housing and infrastructure projects. The data center has not broken ground. The housing money is gone.

**The broader pattern:** Two-thirds of new North American data centers are expected to be built in rural places (Pew Research Center, April 2026). The data center market in North America is projected at $135 billion by end of 2026, driven by AI demand. Rural communities face acute pressure to redirect land and resources toward data center development even before projects are confirmed.

**Community resistance:** A local group called Protect Wiscasset formed in opposition. The story is part of a wider pattern of data center proposals disrupting existing community development plans before a single shovel hits the ground.

**The framing:** As the Shelterforce article puts it, the data center story is not only about what gets built -- it is also about what does not. The opportunity cost is housing. In Wiscasset, that cost was $240,000 and a generation of affordable units that will not exist.

## Relevance

This is the cleanest available case study connecting data center pursuit directly to housing fund loss -- making it a strong addition to the Planning Commission record for [[Data Center ZTA PC25-626ZTA -- Opposition Master Strategy Note]]. The argument is no longer theoretical: a community lost federal housing dollars chasing a data center that never materialized. The structure of the Wiscasset case maps almost exactly onto the Frederick situation: industrial-zoned land, community housing need, and a large data center proposal displacing the prior use.
