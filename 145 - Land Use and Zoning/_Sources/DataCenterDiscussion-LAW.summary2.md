# Executive Summary
Discussion on Dec 19, 2025, focused on data centers coming to the City of Frederick involving city council members, scientists, and growth advocates. Key topics included property annexation, Data Center Impact Study, Maryland's power supply situation, types of data centers, water usage by data centers, environmental concerns, and related legislation.

# Key Points
- The City Council determines property annexation for data center sites but requires County Council's final vote on zoning due to transparency and public protection reasons.
- The Data Center Impact Study will provide economic and environmental data, recommending suitable locations based on Maryland's power issues.
- Maryland imports 40% of its power, and the hyperscale data center complex at Adamstown increases this number significantly.
- Only data centers with site plan approval have assurances of power availability; additional transmission lines may be needed for future data centers.
- Power needs to reach Doubs Substation to reduce voltage and supply data centers, which could take up to 10 years due to power allocation priorities in Northern Virginia.
- The Federal Energy Regulatory Commission (FERC) suggests that data centers should provide their own generation, making it unlikely for Maryland to attract the industry without existing generating resources.
- Server rooms and small data centers can be sited within the city, while hyperscale data centers are unsuitable due to population density and infrastructure requirements.
- Data centers require large volumes of water for cooling purposes, with evaporative cooling being common; this process may pose issues for wastewater treatment facilities.
- New technologies like glycol coolants, immersive coolants, and chip chillers are on the rise but may cause problems for wastewater facilities.

# Action Items
- The City Council should establish its authority for siting data centers through legislation prior to any applications.
- Legislation regarding data centers should not be enacted until the Data Center Impact Study Report is presented to the General Assembly and public.
- Rigorous requirements should be established on issuing permits for new data centers to address potential environmental impacts.

# Open Questions / Risks
- What measures can be taken to attract data center industries without existing generating resources?
- How can wastewater treatment facilities accommodate increasing water consumption from evaporative cooling processes and emerging technologies?
- Are there any alternative solutions for power supply that can support data centers in the city?
