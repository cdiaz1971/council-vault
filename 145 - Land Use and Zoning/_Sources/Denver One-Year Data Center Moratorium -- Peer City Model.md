# Denver One-Year Data Center Moratorium -- Peer City Model

## Summary

Denver city council unanimously approved a one-year moratorium on new data center permits to develop zoning standards around water use, energy consumption, and noise -- a direct peer city model for local data center regulation.

## Key Points

Denver's city council voted unanimously to pause all new data center permits for one year. The moratorium was prompted in part by a data center already under construction in the Elyria-Swansea neighborhood -- the DE3 site -- designed to consume up to 230,000 gallons of water and 18 megawatts of power per day at maximum capacity. The moratorium halts new permits while the city develops regulations specific to data center siting, energy demand, water consumption, and noise impact.

The framing is straightforward: the city lacked the regulatory infrastructure to evaluate what it was permitting, so it stopped permitting until it could build that infrastructure. The vote being unanimous signals this was not a partisan or ideological fight -- it was a governance gap the council recognized and closed proactively.

## Relevance

This is the strongest single peer city model for Frederick if a data center siting question surfaces. A unanimous moratorium framed around developing adequate standards -- not blocking data centers categorically -- is a defensible and moderate position. It buys time without committing to a permanent ban. The water and energy consumption figures from the DE3 site provide concrete benchmarks for what Frederick should be asking any applicant to disclose upfront.
