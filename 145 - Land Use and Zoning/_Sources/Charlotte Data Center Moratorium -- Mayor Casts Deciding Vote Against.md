# Charlotte Data Center Moratorium -- Mayor Casts Deciding Vote Against

## Summary

Charlotte's mayor cast the deciding vote to kill a data center moratorium, a peer-city precedent that illustrates the political fragility of moratorium strategies when executive leadership breaks from the council majority.

## Key Points

The Charlotte City Council attempted to pass a data center development moratorium. The mayor used executive tie-breaking authority to defeat it. The GovTech headline framed this as the mayor's vote "chilling" the moratorium, indicating the council was split and the mayor's position was determinative.

This is a cautionary precedent for any city council pursuing a moratorium on data centers: even a council majority is insufficient if the executive is not aligned. The vote structure matters -- some moratoriums require supermajorities or mayoral sign-off, creating executive veto points that opponents can use.

The Charlotte outcome contrasts with Michigan, where state-level action restricted 1,500 square miles from data center development entirely, removing the local political fragility problem by preempting it at the state level.

## Relevance

Directly relevant to Frederick's data center permitting policy track. The Charlotte case is a warning: if a moratorium strategy requires executive alignment and the mayor is not on board, the council is exposed. The more durable path may be community benefit agreements and permitting conditions rather than moratoriums, which are easily killed at a single vote.
