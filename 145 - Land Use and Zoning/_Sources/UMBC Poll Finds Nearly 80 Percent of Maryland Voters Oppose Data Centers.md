# UMBC Poll Finds Nearly 80 Percent of Maryland Voters Oppose Data Centers

## Summary
An October 2026 UMBC Institute of Politics poll finds roughly 8 in 10 Maryland voters oppose data center construction, both statewide and near their own community, and most voters hold a negative view of AI's effect on society.

## Key Points
- The poll, released October 8, 2026 by the Institute of Politics at the University of Maryland, Baltimore County (UMBC), has a margin of error of 3.5%.
- Respondents were split into two groups: half were asked about data centers built anywhere in Maryland, half about one built near their own community. In both framings, nearly 8 in 10 voters were opposed -- opposition does not meaningfully soften when the question is about someone else's backyard versus their own.
- Only about 23% of voters said they believe AI will have a positive impact on society; most hold a negative view of AI's societal effect.
- Todd Eberly, a political science professor at St. Mary's College of Maryland, framed the result as part of a broader nationalization trend: "Everything is nationalized these days, and we do have a national economy."

## Relevance
Adds a statewide, methodologically credible polling data point to the data center opposition case file alongside [[70 Percent of Americans Oppose Nearby Data Centers -- Becoming a Voting Issue]] and [[Poll Majority of voters oppose data center construction anywhere in Maryland]]-adjacent material -- useful as a Maryland-specific (not just national) number if a Frederick data center moratorium or zoning text amendment comes back to council, and as supporting evidence that opposition is not a NIMBY effect limited to directly affected neighborhoods.
