## Summary

On a Wednesday in late July 2026, Lux Claridge attended an Emporia, Kansas city meeting to speak against a proposed 1,000-acre data center. Before they could speak, a city commissioner ordered them arrested for clapping in support of a prior speaker. Claridge told 404 Media the clapping lasted only seconds, before the previous speaker had even returned to their seat, and that the timer for their own speech had not yet been reset. The arrest happened before any disruption to proceedings could reasonably be claimed.

## Key Points

- **Incident**: Public comment period, data center siting meeting, Emporia KS. Resident clapped for prior speaker. Commissioner ordered arrest. Claridge left in handcuffs before speaking.
- **Claridge's account**: "I don't know what I was disrupting exactly, but I was not clapping the entire time." The 51-second remaining count from the prior speaker was still visible when the arrest was ordered.
- **Scale of the proposed project**: 1,000 acres -- a significant land footprint that context suggests drove substantial community opposition.
- **Pattern match**: This is the second documented case (after Darren Blanchard in Frederick) of a data center opponent being removed or arrested at a public meeting during the comment period. The two cases together establish this as a recurring pattern, not an isolated incident.
- **National significance**: 404 Media covered this in the same weekly roundup as the Flock/ALPR commercial location data story -- both landing under a civil liberties frame. The proximity is not incidental; both involve government use of force to suppress civilian oversight.

## Relevance

This note directly extends the Darren Blanchard case study into a national pattern. For PC25-626ZTA opposition work, the Emporia case is useful in two ways: (1) it documents that suppression of public comment at data center hearings is happening in multiple jurisdictions, and (2) it provides a second case study for any consolidated opposition brief arguing that procedural fairness at public hearings is a live issue in data center siting fights.

Worth flagging in the synthesis note on chilling effects at data center public comment -- connecting Emporia, Darren Blanchard, and any Frederick-specific incidents into a documented pattern.
