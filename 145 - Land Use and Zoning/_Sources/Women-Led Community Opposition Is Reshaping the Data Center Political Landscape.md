# Women-Led Community Opposition Is Reshaping the Data Center Political Landscape

## Summary

National data center opposition has become a significant political force: $64 billion in data center projects have been terminated through community organizing, a Gallup poll finds 70% of Americans oppose data center construction in their communities, and the NAACP has built the first environmental-justice-centered framework specifically for data center fights. The opposition is explicitly bipartisan and is now producing first-time local candidates running specifically to defeat data center-friendly incumbents.

## Key Points

Three organizers profiled illustrate the strategic landscape:

**Abre' Conner (NAACP Center for Environment and Climate Justice)** built the NAACP Frontline Framework after the xAI Memphis lawsuit, in response to a flood of requests from communities facing data center proposals. The framework drew on the 17 environmental justice principles from 1991. Key observations: data centers are being disproportionately sited in communities with existing industrial footprints, histories of disinvestment, and significant Black populations. The pattern mirrors fracking and crypto mining -- a "this will fix all your problems" narrative deployed in communities with limited capacity to push back. Environmental harms cited: diesel backup generators as overlooked air pollution sources; water demand straining aging infrastructure.

**Maia Woluchem (Data & Society)** documented the Pennsylvania data center boom and its fiscal dynamics: municipalities facing disinvestment (closed satellite campuses, defunded hospitals, transit shutdowns) are positioned to accept data center investment because they cannot turn it down. Her policy brief argues that bypassing local governance harms communities. The comparison to the Manhattan Project -- used by developers to invoke national security urgency -- is a named tactic to watch.

**Jackie McGuire (Marana, AZ)** went viral opposing a data center rezoning of 600 acres, gathered 3,000 petition signatures, had her referendum rejected on a technicality she is now challenging in court, and filed for city council. Her framing: "I am not anti-growth, I'm not anti-AI. I am pro governments having enough knowledge from impartial, unbiased third parties to make informed decisions." The Working Families Party is actively recruiting local candidates to run against data center-friendly incumbents; 40% of applicants are women.

The Gallup data on opposition: 7 in 10 Americans oppose data center construction in their communities. Women are more likely than men to hold the strongest opposition. Women are also disproportionately impacted by rising electricity costs driven by data center energy demand.

## Relevance

Several elements are directly useful for the ZTA record:

The Gallup numbers are credible, independent polling that counter the assumption that data center opposition is a niche or fringe position. If the ZTA hearing draws community comment, this data supports the legitimacy of that opposition.

The NAACP Frontline Framework is a resource for Frederick Rapid Response Network and immigrant community partners who may be engaging the ZTA process. The pattern of siting in communities with industrial footprints and disinvestment histories maps onto some of Frederick's most affected neighborhoods.

Woluchem's framing of fiscal crisis creating data center dependency is the dynamic to name and preempt in the Frederick ZTA record. Frederick is not in fiscal crisis, has other options, and should not be positioned as a municipality that cannot say no.

McGuire's "impartial third party" argument is a clean formulation for why the Sage Policy Group analysis (paid for by the developer) is insufficient and why an independent fiscal analysis matters.
