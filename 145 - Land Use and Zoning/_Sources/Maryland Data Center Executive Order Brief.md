# Maryland Executive Order on Data Centers

## Summary
State-level Order creates a Maryland Data Center Accountability Task Force and a five-principle framework for reviewing large data center projects, with a coordinated permitting pathway for compliant projects and no fast track for those that aren't; directly relevant to Frederick's CDI/PC25-626ZTA work.

## Key Points
The Order sets one statewide standard and process for large data center development. It creates the Maryland Data Center Accountability Task Force, staffed by the Governor's Office, and requires every covered project to be measured against a five-principle framework before any state agency takes discretionary action. It does not ban or pause data centers and does not touch the PSC or Office of People's Counsel.

Coverage: data centers needing 25 MW or more at peak, or expansions adding 25 MW, or anyone meeting the Large Load Customer definition under Public Utilities Section 7-232. Facilities serving universities, hospitals, state agencies, or DoD can be excluded case by case. Only applies to requests made after the Order takes effect; anything already permitted or complete is grandfathered.

The five principles: Ratepayer and Grid Protection (full cost coverage, financial guarantees, curtailment commitments), Economic Benefit for Marylanders (jobs, wages, local hiring, net cost-benefit), Community Voice (early engagement, community benefits agreements, fit with local zoning; a project denied a required local approval cannot be found Aligned or Conditionally Aligned), Environmental Protection (water use, emissions, siting on already-developed land, decommissioning), and Transparency and Accountability (ownership disclosure, public Statement of Commitments, annual reporting).

Review process: within 60 days of a project asking the state for help, the Task Force does a baseline assessment and lists it on a public Dashboard. Within another 30 days it meets with the developer. After public comment it issues a Determination: Aligned, Conditionally Aligned, or Not Aligned, with an ongoing status of Performing, Under Review, or Non-Performing. Aligned and Conditionally Aligned projects get a Coordinated Review Pathway (state agencies review alongside local government, not after) plus incentive eligibility. Everything else waits until the developer already has local land-use approval, plan consistency, and water permits in hand.

Other mechanisms: a public Data Center Dashboard updated monthly, a ban on NDAs between state agencies and developers, annual reporting cross-checked against utility/PSC/PJM data, and agency directives, most notably that the Department of Planning will publish a model zoning guide for counties and towns, developed with MACo, MML, labor, and community groups.

## Relevance
The MDP model zoning guide will likely bear directly on PC25-626ZTA and any revisions to the text amendment allowing data centers in M1/M2. The Community Voice principle, which bars a state Alignment finding for any project denied a required local approval, gives local zoning decisions more leverage over state incentive eligibility than they'd otherwise have, which is relevant to Mobilize Frederick's push for caution on the local text amendment. The ratepayer protection fund and PJM/FERC cost-allocation directives connect to the transmission capacity concerns raised in the December 2025 council discussion on the Adamstown data center queue. Does not affect Planning Commission timing on PC25-626ZTA since the Order only applies going forward.
