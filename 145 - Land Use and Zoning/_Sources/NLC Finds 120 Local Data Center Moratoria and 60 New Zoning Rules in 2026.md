# NLC Finds 120 Local Data Center Moratoria and 60 New Zoning Rules in 2026

## Summary
NLC research counts at least 120 communities that adopted data center moratoria and 60 that passed new zoning rules in 2026, up from the 76 localities in its earlier tracker. Tucson and Cedar Rapids show two different paths: stronger guardrails after rejecting a hyperscale proposal, and negotiated agreements with public transparency.

## Key Points
The local response to data centers is accelerating. The moratorium count has grown well past the 76 localities the NLC tracker reported earlier, and 60 communities have rewritten zoning rules rather than pausing outright.

Tucson took the guardrails path after turning down a hyperscale proposal, moving toward rules that require disclosure of projected energy use, renewable energy plans, and water impacts. Cedar Rapids took the negotiated path, using development agreements with data center developers that fund a community fund, with the mayor stressing that a project only has a chance of success if the public knows everything going on.

NLC and the AAAS EPI Center published a "Key Questions to Ask When Considering a Data Center in Your Community" resource to help local officials evaluate proposals.

## Relevance
The moratorium wave is now large enough that a Frederick pause would be ordinary, not an outlier. The two models also map onto the existing PC25-626ZTA strategy: denial and guardrails first, with a Cedar Rapids or St. Louis style development agreement as the fallback. The NLC/AAAS key-questions resource is a ready checklist for the Planning Commission hearing.
