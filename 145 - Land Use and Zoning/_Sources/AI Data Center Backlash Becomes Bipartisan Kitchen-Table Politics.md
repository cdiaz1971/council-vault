# AI Data Center Backlash Becomes Bipartisan Kitchen-Table Politics

## Summary
Reporter Jasmine Sun's 10-day Wisconsin and Michigan reporting trip found data center opposition running around 70 percent locally and cutting across party lines, driven by power demand, water use, secrecy around NDAs, and distrust of promised jobs and tax revenue, with 100+ moratorium proposals now circulating nationally.

## Key Points
Sun's reporting, discussed on The Ezra Klein Show, found opposition to nearby data centers running around 70 percent in the communities she visited, with over 100 moratorium proposals circulating nationally. The complaints are consistent across sites: power demand that outruns local grids and raises rates for everyone to serve one large customer, water use (even with newer closed-loop recycling), construction noise, and buildings residents find ugly. Secret NDAs between developers and local governments have fueled distrust, and residents report not believing promised job and tax benefit figures. Sun's structural point is that AI infrastructure lacks a constituency the way housing has residents, auto plants have workers, or renewables have environmentalists behind them. Some deals do land better when developers put real money into visible community benefit, such as a $30 million site remediation tied to transparent upside-sharing. Sun frames the core political question for candidates heading into midterms as: who pays for AI, who decides where it gets built, and who gets the benefits.

## Relevance
This is useful framing and data for the consolidated data center opposition brief due before the October Planning Commission hearing on ZTA PC25-626ZTA. The bipartisan-opposition framing and the "AI lacks a constituency" argument are strong rhetorical tools for building the public case beyond the immediate vote math with Nash, Brehm, and Taylor. Worth citing alongside the existing note on Amazon's Calvert Cliffs withdrawal and Maryland's shifting political tide as evidence this is a national pattern, not a local outlier.
