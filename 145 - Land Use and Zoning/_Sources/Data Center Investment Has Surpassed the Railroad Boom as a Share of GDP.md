# Data Center Investment Has Surpassed the Railroad Boom as a Share of GDP

## Summary

A Columbia Business School analysis finds the $10 trillion AI data center buildout will reach nearly 4% of US GDP, almost double the historical peak of 19th-century railroad construction, and new Census data shows data center construction value has grown by $48 billion since 2024 while housing and apartment construction value dropped $29 billion over the same period.

## Key Points

- Columbia Business School professor Stijn Van Nieuwerburgh's report (for Brookings) projects $10 trillion in data center investment, reaching nearly 4% of GDP -- almost double the economic share driven by 19th-century railroad expansion, which consumed about $173 billion in today's dollars (about 2% of GDP over 20 years). Other historical booms -- canals, electrification, highways, and 1990s-2000s telecom/fiber buildout -- consumed 1% of GDP or less.
- Per a Stateline analysis of US Census Bureau construction data (as of August 2026): over the past two years, new data center construction value more than doubled to $85 billion annually, while all other construction value dropped 8%. New data center value grew $48 billion since 2024, while new housing and apartment construction value dropped $29 billion over the same period.
- Adam Shapiro, VP at the Federal Reserve Bank of San Francisco, called this a "pivotal shift" from residential investment toward computers (September 2026 LinkedIn post).
- Economist Paul Krugman (Oct. 11, 2026 analysis) argues data centers employ few workers, so the AI buildout will reduce labor demand even as it drives current construction hiring. Josh Bivens, EPI chief economist, called the economy's dependence on this "a narrow base of growth, basically one sector, and a pretty fragile one," warning that any shock to AI profitability expectations could trigger sharp cutbacks.
- States that welcomed data centers with tax abatements for ordinary internet-traffic infrastructure are now reconsidering as tax revenue losses and energy-affordability effects grow. Nevada (Gov. Joe Lombardo, Sept. 2026 executive order) trimmed abatements and will require data centers to cover energy and water infrastructure costs. Virginia passed an electricity consumption surcharge specific to data centers (effective July 2026) as part of a budget deal that preserved its tax breaks.
- Top projected three-year state data center capex (Industrial Info Resources): Texas ($638B), Virginia ($249B), Ohio ($169B), Georgia ($160B). Southern states dominate by project count; large numbers also proposed in Arizona, California, Florida, Indiana, Iowa, North Carolina, Oregon, Pennsylvania, and Washington (Pew Research, April 2026).
- Van Nieuwerburgh estimates the buildout will double electricity consumption. An Oregon report (Sept. 2026) found utility affordability was the single most frequent public-comment concern, leading to a continued freeze on data center environmental permits in that state. A joint Carnegie Mellon/NC State analysis (July 2026) found data centers and crypto mining combined could raise electricity bills 8% nationally and up to 25% in parts of Virginia.
- An IMF report this month (October 2026) warned that weaker or less durable AI investment/profitability than expected could trigger a stock-price correction.
- IIR's David Pickering: current profitability "is not a pretty picture right now," expecting it to improve once AI regulation matures and the sector settles. Financial historian Mark Higgins draws a direct parallel to 19th-century railroad financing booms and busts, where foreign capital flooded in, crashes followed, and distressed assets were bought up by those who could tell "which were the good ones."
- Some localities with heavy data center concentrations are considering pauses; at least one city has an outright moratorium (example cited: Loudoun County, VA board statement).
