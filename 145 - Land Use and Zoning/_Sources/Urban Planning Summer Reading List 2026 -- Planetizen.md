# Urban Planning Summer Reading List 2026 -- Planetizen

## Summary

Planetizen's Booked on Planning collaboration surfaced 10 recommended titles for summer 2026 spanning affordable housing, transportation, gentrification, community engagement, parking policy, and racial economic history. Several have direct relevance to Frederick's housing and transportation agenda.

## The List

1. **Road to Nowhere** by Emily Lieb -- How a Baltimore highway that was never built still damaged Rosemont, a Black middle-class neighborhood, by chilling mortgages and property values through uncertainty. Lesson: plans harm even when no bulldozer rolls.

2. **Messy Cities: Why We Can't Plan Everything** by Zahra Ebrahim, Dylan Reid, John Lorinc, Leslie Woo -- Essays reframing urban "messiness" as a source of inclusivity and connection rather than dysfunction. Draws on Toronto, Mexico City, Tokyo.

3. **Bittersweet Lane: Creating Home(s) in the American Affordable Housing Crisis** by Jamie Madden -- Memoir plus educational guide from someone who grew up in affordable housing and now builds it. Covers the "chain of consequences" behind housing decisions and the history of place that planners must reckon with.

4. **Paved Paradise: How Parking Explains the World** by Henry Grabar -- Parking as the operating system of the modern city: how parking policy shaped housing, transit, budgets, safety, and climate. Reportedly includes parking garages and mob activity.

5. **Before Gentrification: The Creation of DC's Racial Wealth Gap** by Tanya Maria Golash-Boza -- Challenges the narrative that racial wealth gaps stem from homeownership access failure; argues systemic disinvestment in Black neighborhoods after white departure is the mechanism.

6. **Gentrifier: a memoir** by Anne Elizabeth Moore -- Personal account of winning a "free house" in Detroit through a writer's residency and confronting the ethical complexity of benefiting from displacement-adjacent policies.

7-10: Not fully captured in available excerpt -- the Planetizen feature runs to 10 titles. Check https://www.planetizen.com/features/137790-10-great-urban-planning-summer-reads for the full list.

## Most Relevant to Current Work

**Road to Nowhere** is the most directly useful for the ZTA record: it documents that land use planning decisions harm communities even when the physical project never materializes. This is exactly the argument for why the ZTA process itself -- not just the eventual approval -- requires rigorous public accountability.

**Before Gentrification** is directly applicable to the Affordability Beyond Housing agenda and the racial equity framing of ALICE data for Frederick's Golden Mile neighborhoods.

**Bittersweet Lane** could inform the Housing Production Target Resolution framing -- specifically the "chain of consequences" concept that Madden describes as essential to housing planning.
