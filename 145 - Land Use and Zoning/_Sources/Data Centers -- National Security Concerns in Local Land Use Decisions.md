# Data Centers -- National Security Concerns in Local Land Use Decisions

## Summary

The township of Ypsilanti, Michigan is facing a situation where a data center tied to nuclear weapons infrastructure has prompted concerns about becoming a military target. The case illustrates how data center siting decisions can carry national security implications that local governments are poorly equipped to evaluate.

## Key Points

A data center in Ypsilanti township, Michigan -- described as connected to nuclear weapons infrastructure -- has prompted the township's attorney to warn that the facility places a target on the local community. The concern is that critical national security infrastructure co-located with civilian population centers creates risk that local governments did not sign up for when approving commercial development.

Local land use approvals are typically evaluated on zoning, traffic, utilities, and economic impact. They are not designed to assess whether a facility's tenants or functions create military targeting risk. This is an emerging category of externality that standard local permitting processes do not capture.

## Relevance

Frederick is actively developing data center policy. [[Data Center Policy]] should consider whether Frederick's ordinance addresses tenant use restrictions -- not just physical infrastructure standards like power draw and water use. A data center that passes all standard land use criteria could still create risk exposure the city has no framework to evaluate.

This is worth raising in the data center ordinance drafting process as a "what are we not asking" question.
