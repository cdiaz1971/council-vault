# City of Frederick Two-Year Data Center Moratorium Proposed

## Summary
A draft ordinance before the Frederick City Council would freeze for two years any application to amend city code to allow data centers, including applications already filed, while the city studies environmental, infrastructure, economic, and community impacts.

## Key Points
- The Council was scheduled to discuss the draft moratorium on Thursday, October 8, 2026. Coverage credits Council Member Derek Shackelford with leading it.
- Scope: the city could not accept, process, or approve any application seeking to amend the code to allow data centers. The draft explicitly reaches applications already submitted, which would capture the pending developer-driven text amendment (Trammell Crow, represented by land use attorney Bruce Dean).
- Purpose: a two-year window to complete a comprehensive study of environmental, infrastructure, economic, and community impacts before any rules are written.
- The draft lets the Council repeal or extend the moratorium on whatever terms it finds necessary for residents' health, safety, and welfare.
- Gap it fills: Frederick County's moratorium (now extended to July 1, 2027) does not cover land inside city limits.
- Advocates, led publicly by Steve Jakubczyk of Citizens for Responsible Growth, frame it as a timeout so that residents, council, and staff write the rules instead of a developer's lawyers. They want a citizen-led committee to shape any eventual regulations.
- Context: Baltimore City and 12 Maryland counties now have some form of data center moratorium. Frederick News-Post reports Trammell Crow continued pressing its zoning amendment before the Planning Commission even as the citywide moratorium surfaced.
- Public comment on the draft is open online via tinyurl.com/moratoriumcomment.

## Relevance
This moves the data center fight from the Watch List (ZTA revisit slated for November) to an active Council vehicle that would freeze PC25-626ZTA in place. Three things worth settling before a vote. First, whether the retroactive reach to already-filed text amendment applications is defensible, and whether the Maryland Housing Certainty Act vesting regime (now running locally under G-26-11) applies to a text amendment application at all, or only to development applications. That is a Rachel Gray question. Second, the coalition dynamics: Citizens for Responsible Growth is the loudest outside supporter, and on housing CRG has opposed ADU and production reforms. Supporting this moratorium without lending CRG credibility on housing will take some care. Third, the statewide polling in [[UMBC Poll Finds Nearly 80 Percent of Maryland Voters Oppose Data Centers]] and the national moratoria count in [[NLC Finds 120 Local Data Center Moratoria and 60 New Zoning Rules in 2026]] make the political ground favorable. The authority questions are already mapped in [[Data Center Regulation and Local Authority]].
