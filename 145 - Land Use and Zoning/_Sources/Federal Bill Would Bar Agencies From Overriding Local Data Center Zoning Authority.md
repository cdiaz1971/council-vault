# Federal Bill Would Bar Agencies From Overriding Local Data Center Zoning Authority

## Summary
Rep. Tom Barrett (R-Mich.) introduced the Protecting Local Control of Data Centers Act, which would bar federal agencies from overriding state and local land-use zoning and permitting laws related to data centers, and would prevent agencies from using federal construction funds to pressure local governments into ceding that authority.

## Key Points
The bill targets two mechanisms by which the federal government could otherwise pressure local land-use decisions on data centers: direct agency override of zoning and permitting rules, and conditioning federal funds on localities giving up that authority. Barrett framed the bill around utility bill impacts, land and water use, and residents' right to have the final say over proposed projects in their own communities. It is a protective measure for existing local authority rather than a new restriction on data centers themselves.

## Relevance
This bill reinforces the legal footing behind Frederick's opposition to the Data Center ZTA (PC25-626ZTA), currently on hold until the September revisit ahead of an October Planning Commission hearing. A federal statute foreclosing agency preemption or funding-based coercion removes one avenue that could otherwise be used against a Frederick moratorium or restrictive zoning text amendment down the line. It fits the broader pattern of state and local governments reclaiming zoning control documented in [[Data Center Federal Legislative Landscape -- Congress Debates Rate Protection and Moratorium]] and [[Data Centers -- Ohio Communities Use Moratoriums and Zoning to Reclaim Local Control]]. Worth folding into the consolidated opposition brief as evidence that federal sentiment is trending toward protecting, not undermining, local zoning authority over data centers.
