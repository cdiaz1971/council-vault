# Data Centers -- Maine Statewide Moratorium LD 307 (2026)

## Summary

On April 15, 2026, the Maine Legislature passed LD 307, a temporary ban on new data centers drawing 20 megawatts or more of power -- the first statewide data center moratorium in the country. The bill awaits Governor Janet Mills' signature. It also establishes the Maine Data Center Coordination Council to study risks and benefits and produce policy recommendations before the moratorium expires November 1, 2027.

## Key Points

The moratorium threshold is 20 megawatts, targeting large AI-scale facilities rather than small commercial deployments. The bill bans approvals from municipalities, state agencies, and quasi-independent entities -- not just state-level permits. The Legislature rejected an amendment that would have created an exemption process, meaning the ban is clean rather than case-by-case.

The bill arose from intense community backlash against two proposed projects: one in Jay (a former mill site) and one in Sanford. Governor Mills has expressed support for the Jay project due to the economic development argument for a struggling former mill town, and has signaled she may push for an exemption before signing. The Jay carve-out was not included in the bill as passed.

The political context is significant: proposals to impose data center moratoriums have been introduced in at least a dozen states. Maine is the first where one passed even a single chamber, let alone both. Voters in Festus, Missouri -- a St. Louis suburb -- replaced half of the city council this month in a backlash over a data center project. Bangor, Maine passed its own six-month city-level ban the day before the Legislature acted.

Community concerns driving opposition: electricity rate impacts on ratepayers, strain on the electric grid, voracious water consumption, and loss of local control over industrial siting decisions.

The Coordination Council is charged with strategic input, planning considerations, and policy tools to address data center impacts. The bill carries a $95,000 price tag for the council's operation.

## Relevance

High relevance to Frederick's active data center zoning text amendment track. Maine's moratorium is the national reference point for the most aggressive legislative response available to a municipality or state. The 20 MW threshold, the coordination council model, and the community benefit framework are all policy tools that could inform Frederick's approach -- whether or not Frederick goes as far as a moratorium. The fact that this passed in a state that is not a major data center destination, driven purely by community backlash, signals that the political dynamics around data centers are shifting faster than industry lobbying anticipated.

The Missouri electoral example (half a city council replaced) and Maine's legislative action together suggest that data center opposition is now capable of generating real political consequences -- directly relevant to how Frederick's zoning text amendment process should be handled and paced.
