# Data Centers -- Tech Lobbyists Use Critical Infrastructure Exemption to Roll Back Right to Repair

## Summary

Cisco, IBM, the Consumer Technology Association, and the National Electrical Manufacturers Association are pushing Colorado legislation that would gut that state's landmark right to repair law by exempting hardware designated as "critical infrastructure." The definition of critical infrastructure is self-assigned by manufacturers, making it effectively a blank check to opt out of repair obligations. The security rationale is a reversal of the actual evidence -- right to repair improves security response times, not the opposite.

## Key Points

**The mechanism**

Colorado passed one of the strongest right to repair laws in the US. The new legislation, which cleared a state senate committee, would exempt hardware from the law if it "is considered critical infrastructure." The problem: manufacturers self-designate. As repair advocate Louis Rossmann testified, if a laptop manufacturer knows the Pentagon buys their laptops, they can declare that entire product line exempt. If a networking company sells a $20 switch to a federal building, that hardware becomes critical infrastructure. It is a blank check.

**The security argument is backwards**

The "security risk" argument against right to repair has been litigated repeatedly. Greater access to repair parts, tools, and diagnostic software means broken equipment gets fixed faster, reducing the window of vulnerability. A security researcher testified that smaller organizations running equipment past its safe operating life because they cannot afford authorized repair are the actual security risk -- not independent repair.

**Who is backing the bill and why**

Legacy enterprise hardware manufacturers -- Cisco, IBM, Consumer Technology Association -- whose products are increasingly used in data centers. The real motivation is monopoly control over the service and repair market. As cybersecurity expert Paul Roberts testified, the big tech companies backing the bill are using a legitimate concern about critical infrastructure cybersecurity to lock in a monopoly on service and repair.

**The data center angle**

Lobbyists explicitly highlighted that their technology is used in data centers as justification for the exemption -- framing data center hardware as categorically different from consumer devices. This is the same rhetorical move used to resist community benefit agreements and local regulation of data center development.

## Relevance

Directly applicable to Frederick's data center permitting work. The "critical infrastructure" framing is a recurring rhetorical tool that large tech firms use to resist accountability -- whether for repair obligations, community benefit agreements, or local land use regulation. Understanding how this argument is constructed and defeated at the state level is useful preparation for local negotiations.
