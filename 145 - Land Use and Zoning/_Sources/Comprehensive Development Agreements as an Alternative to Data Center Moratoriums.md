# Comprehensive Development Agreements as an Alternative to Data Center Moratoriums

## Summary

Four cities, Festus MO, Joliet IL, Memphis TN, and St. Louis MO, have used negotiated comprehensive development agreements rather than moratoriums to extract enforceable community benefits from data center developers, with St. Louis's roughly $15 million deal covering digital inclusion, environmental justice, transportation, and hiring and wage commitments.

## Key Points

Per the National League of Cities' Data Center Local Action Tracker, moratoriums and zoning ordinances aren't the only tool local governments have used against data center projects. Four cities have instead negotiated comprehensive development agreements directly with developers as a condition of approval: Festus, Missouri; Joliet, Illinois; Memphis, Tennessee; and St. Louis, Missouri.

St. Louis is the most fully documented example: the city negotiated approximately $15 million in community benefits in exchange for approving a proposed data center project. The package included support for digital inclusion initiatives, environmental justice programs, and transportation improvements, and the developer separately agreed to meet documented targets on hiring, wages, job creation, and ongoing transparency reporting.

This approach sits between outright rejection and unconditional approval: it accepts that the project may proceed but treats approval as leverage to secure enforceable, monitored commitments rather than relying on a developer's voluntary goodwill. It's a materially different posture from a straight zoning denial or moratorium, and worth understanding as a distinct option rather than a fallback.

## Relevance

A concrete negotiating template for the PC25-626ZTA opposition brief: if the ultimate outcome for data center siting in Frederick shifts from denial to conditional approval at any point, the St. Louis-style development agreement (with named categories like digital inclusion, environmental justice, transportation, and enforceable hiring/wage targets) gives a specific, peer-tested structure to propose rather than negotiating from scratch.
