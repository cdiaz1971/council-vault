# Trump Warrior Run Coal Plant Reopening -- Federal Intervention in Maryland Energy Policy

## Summary

Trump administration announced hundreds of millions to restart retired coal plants including the Warrior Run facility near Cumberland, Maryland; energy economists say the plant is unlikely to be economically competitive and may not reduce consumer prices.

## Key Points

**The announcement:** Trump unveiled federal investment to fortify existing coal plants, build new ones, and restart retired plants. The Warrior Run facility near Cumberland, MD, deactivated in 2024; its owner AES signaled intent to reopen given high regional energy prices. AES filed with FERC in December for a waiver extension through 2026 to procure equipment and hire staff.

**PJM dynamics:** The independent market monitor for PJM Interconnection pushed back, arguing Warrior Run shut down because it could not provide energy economically -- and likely still cannot. Evergreen Action argues the plant won't bring consumer prices down; if it produces more expensive energy than competing resources, it could keep prices elevated.

**The data center connection:** Brandon Shores (Anne Arundel County), the last large-scale coal plant in Maryland, extended operations into 2029 specifically because of AI data center demand straining the regional grid. This is the same dynamic driving energy prices that allegedly make Warrior Run attractive to reopen. The federal push to restart coal is downstream of the data center buildout.

**Maryland's climate commitments:** Maryland is legally required to reduce emissions 60% by 2031 (from 2006 levels) and reach net-zero by 2045. The restart runs against that framework. Evergreen Action's preferred path: work with AES to generate clean energy on the Warrior Run site, potentially with battery storage.

**Political framing:** Republican legislators in Annapolis praised the announcement. The Maryland Republican Senate Minority Leader framed it as federal intervention against "hostile" Democratic climate mandates. Sen. McKay (Allegany/Garrett/Washington) called it the investment the region "deserved."

**Evergreen Action's counter:** "It is absolutely the White House reaching into states and jamming the policy they want down their throats."

## Relevance

The data center demand connection is the most relevant thread for Frederick. The same AI infrastructure buildout driving Frederick's data center zoning debate is what's keeping Brandon Shores online and making Warrior Run attractive to reopen. This is useful framing for any discussion of data center cumulative impacts -- the energy costs are not abstract, they are producing federal pressure to reverse Maryland's climate commitments.

Worth tracking if this surfaces in Frederick's franchise agreement context or in any future energy/climate policy discussion.
