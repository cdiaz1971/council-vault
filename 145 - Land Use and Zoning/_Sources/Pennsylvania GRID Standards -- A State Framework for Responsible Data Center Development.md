# Pennsylvania GRID Standards -- A State Framework for Responsible Data Center Development

## Summary

Pennsylvania Governor Josh Shapiro unveiled full GRID (Governor's Responsible Infrastructure Development) standards for data center development in May 2026, requiring developers to bring or build their own power supply, engage communities directly, hire and train local workers, and meet environmental protection standards. State tax benefits are tied to GRID certification, creating an enforcement mechanism. The framework represents a significant policy shift from Pennsylvania's earlier all-in posture toward a regulated development approach.

## Key Points

The GRID standards have four core pillars:

**Energy self-sufficiency.** Developers must build, bring, or buy the electric capacity their data center will consume. This directly addresses the core community concern: that data centers are adding enormous load to existing grids, raising ratepayer costs and straining infrastructure without contributing to new supply.

**Community engagement.** Developers must produce a formal community outreach plan, informing stakeholders about the project before it is approved. This shifts the default from post-hoc community opposition to pre-approval deliberation.

**Workforce development.** Local hiring and training requirements tie the economic benefit to the host community rather than allowing developers to import specialized labor.

**Environmental and ratepayer protections.** Standards include water use, environmental impact, and specific guardrails against cost-shifting to ratepayers.

The enforcement mechanism is the key innovation: GRID certification is the gate for state tax benefits. This means the standards have real teeth -- non-compliant developers simply do not receive public subsidy.

Industry responded with skepticism. The Data Center Coalition said the framework creates a "complicated framework" that will challenge future development. Environmental advocates called it an important step. Shapiro's evolution from "all in on AI" (June 2025, the $20B Amazon announcement) to this measured regulatory posture tracks the shift in public sentiment as communities began opposing projects on energy and environmental grounds.

## Relevance

Frederick's data center text amendment is still at the Planning Commission and has not yet come before the Council. When it does, the GRID framework offers a directly applicable peer model: the "bring your own power" requirement, the community engagement mandate, and the tax-benefit enforcement mechanism are all transferable to local community benefit agreement language. The [[Data Center Community Benefit Agreements -- Policy Models]] note is the right home for adapting these specifics. The GRID framework also demonstrates that state-level action is possible -- if Maryland's legislature were to follow Pennsylvania's lead, that would change the local policy environment significantly.
