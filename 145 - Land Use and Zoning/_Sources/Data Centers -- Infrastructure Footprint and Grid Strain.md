# Data Centers -- Infrastructure Footprint and Grid Strain

## Summary

The true land and infrastructure footprint of a data center is far larger than the building itself. When power transmission, substations, and water delivery infrastructure are included, the total footprint can be 100 times the building's physical size. Virginia's grid operator is processing applications for 70 additional gigawatts of capacity -- nearly tripling its all-time peak -- driven almost entirely by data center demand.

## Key Points

The Piedmont Environmental Council mapped an estimated 370 million square feet of existing and proposed data centers in Virginia, because no state overview existed. This gap in statewide oversight is itself a governance problem.

The energy numbers are staggering. Dominion Energy's applications to add 70 gigawatts would nearly triple its all-time peak energy load. The infrastructure investment required is "in the billions of dollars" according to William and Mary energy law experts. The key policy question is who bears those costs: the general ratepayer, or the data centers creating the demand.

Water consumption is analogous. Data centers require substantial water for cooling. The Virginia Mercury reporting on Culpeper shows data centers being sited near watersheds without adequate impact analysis.

The 18-to-24 month construction timeline for data centers is far shorter than the policy timeline for enacting adequate mitigations. Local governments are perpetually behind the development cycle.

## Relevance

Frederick's data center ordinance needs cooling and water use disclosure requirements before any approval is granted -- not as conditions on construction, but as threshold requirements for zoning review. The Virginia case shows that by the time a data center is approved, the window for meaningful mitigation has often already closed.

The infrastructure cost-shifting question is also relevant: if Frederick approves data centers, who pays for the water and power upgrades they require?
