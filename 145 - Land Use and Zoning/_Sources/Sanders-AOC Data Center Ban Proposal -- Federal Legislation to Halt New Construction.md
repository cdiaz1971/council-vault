## Summary

Senators Bernie Sanders and Representative Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez proposed federal legislation in March 2026 that would ban all new data center construction until comprehensive AI regulation is enacted. The proposal reflects growing progressive concern that the rapid proliferation of AI infrastructure is outpacing any democratic accountability for its impacts.

## Key Points

The proposal would place a moratorium on new data center permits at the federal level until AI-specific regulation passes Congress. The exact regulatory threshold -- what counts as sufficient AI regulation to lift the moratorium -- is unspecified in initial reporting.

The policy logic: data centers are the physical infrastructure enabling AI at scale. Controlling infrastructure buildout is one lever for slowing AI deployment without directly regulating model development, which is harder to define and enforce. This is similar to approaches taken with nuclear power, where facility permitting was used as a regulatory chokepoint before direct technology regulation matured.

Critics will argue this conflates AI infrastructure (which serves cloud computing broadly, not just AI) with AI development, and that a construction moratorium would disproportionately harm rural and economically distressed communities that have been competing for data center investment.

Separately, Pennsylvania's House passed legislation increasing power costs for data centers enabling AI, and Colorado is still grappling with who pays for data center power grid upgrades. These state-level actions are the more immediate policy landscape.

## Why This Matters for César

Frederick is actively engaged in data center permitting and community benefit agreement negotiations. A federal moratorium -- even if unlikely to pass -- signals a political environment where data center opposition is gaining mainstream progressive legitimacy. This strengthens Frederick's hand in demanding robust community benefit agreements as a condition of local permitting: if federal legislators are considering banning construction outright, the bar for what constitutes a responsible local approval is rising. This also connects to Frederick's position as a city that takes progressive governance seriously -- the City Council needs a coherent policy posture on data center development before the next application arrives.
