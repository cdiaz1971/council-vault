# Moore Data Center Order Defers to a Local No

## Summary
Governor Moore's September 23 order keeps final data center decisions with local governments, ties state incentives and fast permitting to Task Force alignment, and comes with a pledge to repeal the 2020 data center tax exemption and a signal he would sign a statewide moratorium.

## Key Points
Moore framed the order around one line of principle: the state will not route around a community that says no. Final siting authority stays local. The state's leverage is money and speed. Projects the Task Force finds "not aligned" get no incentives, grants, loans, or letters of support, and state agencies defer substantive permit review until the developer documents every required local land use approval. Aligned projects can get state advocacy before PJM and FERC.

Three things in the announcement go beyond the fact sheet. First, Moore will push the 2027 session to repeal the data center sales tax exemption created in 2020. Second, he told Punchbowl News he would sign a statewide moratorium bill if one reached him, while calling moratoriums a local jurisdictional issue and disclaiming authority to impose one himself. Third, the NDA ban covers developers, operators, and site-selection agents; existing agreements are honored but cannot be renewed or extended.

The order sets a 60-day clock for the initial assessment but no deadline for the final determination. Developers can seek reassessment within 30 days by adding commitments.

Reaction split along predictable lines. The Green Party candidate called the task force lip service and demanded a ban. Food and Water Watch welcomed the tax exemption repeal but wants a statewide pause and repeal of the 2024 law exempting diesel backup generators from PSC approval, which Moore supported. CCAN wants a 100 percent clean energy requirement. Senate Minority Leader Hershey called it recruiting an industry with one hand while taxing it with the other.

## Relevance
The practical effect for Frederick is that a local denial now carries state consequences: a project turned down locally loses its path to state incentives and coordinated permitting. That strengthens the case for getting PC25-626ZTA right, since the city's zoning decision is now also a gate on state support. The moratorium signal matters for the November revisit in the Watch List, and the tax exemption repeal is worth tracking against the county revenue estimates in [[Data Centers -- Frederick County Tax Revenue Estimates and Legal Conflict]].
