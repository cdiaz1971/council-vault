# Brandon Shores Extension Reveals Data Center Ratepayer Cost Is Not Theoretical

## Summary

Maryland's only remaining coal plant, Brandon Shores south of Baltimore, has had its reliability-must-run agreement extended through May 2031 -- six years past its original 2025 closure date and two years past the previously extended 2029 deadline. The proximate cause named by grid operator PJM and Maryland People's Counsel David Lapp is the same: data center electricity demand, mostly from facilities outside Maryland, is overwhelming the regional grid and delaying the transmission infrastructure upgrades that would allow the plant to retire.

## Key Points

The extension mechanism is a reliability-must-run agreement (RMR), which compensates plant owner Talen Energy to keep Brandon Shores and the neighboring H.A. Wagner oil plant online while transmission upgrades catch up. Maryland ratepayers currently pay $180M/year under the existing RMR. The cost of the new 2031 extension is not yet known, and any repairs Talen deems necessary to maintain the plant would add to that figure.

David Lapp, Maryland People's Counsel, named data centers directly: "That other load growth is data center growth -- almost entirely outside the state of Maryland." Lapp's office has also filed a complaint at FERC arguing PJM unlawfully assigned Maryland ratepayers responsibility for $2 billion in transmission upgrade costs driven by data center demand.

The transmission project chain has grown: PJM has added more projects to the list that must be completed before Brandon Shores can retire, including the controversial Maryland Piedmont Reliability Project, a 67-mile line through rural Central Maryland. One BGE transmission project assigned on an emergency basis has already doubled in cost to $1.5 billion.

Environmental and health costs are not abstract. Del. Lorig Charkoudian called the extension "devastating," citing increased childhood asthma hospitalizations and shortened lives from continued coal plant emissions. A 2024 Sierra Club analysis found battery storage paired with existing line upgrades could have substituted for continued Brandon Shores operation -- but PJM dismissed it, and a 2024 Maryland bill to evaluate battery storage at Brandon Shores passed the House and stalled in the Senate.

The current RMR cost structure has also been challenged by Lapp's office as inflated: the People's Counsel found the actual cost to keep the plants running is $83M/year lower than what ratepayers are being charged, alleging a Talen windfall that FERC should reject.

## Relevance

This is direct record-building material for the ZTA opposition. The argument that data centers impose real, quantified costs on ordinary ratepayers is no longer theoretical in Maryland -- it has a price tag ($180M/year, potentially higher to 2031) and a named government official (David Lapp, who testified at the June 11 worksession) making the argument on the record. The Brandon Shores story is the outcome Frederick is being asked to accept downstream: a planning decision made somewhere else, costs borne by Marylanders who had no vote on data center siting.

The Loudoun County cautionary case from Mike Turner's June 11 presentation focused on fiscal impact. Brandon Shores adds the ratepayer infrastructure cost layer. Together they build the argument that the ZTA's alleged fiscal benefits are offset by costs the city has no mechanism to control and no authority to recoup.

The $2B FERC complaint from Lapp's office is also worth tracking. If FERC rules in favor of the People's Counsel, it establishes a legal principle that states and localities can challenge unlawful ratepayer cost assignments -- potentially relevant to any future Frederick challenge to grid upgrade costs associated with a data center approved under the ZTA.
