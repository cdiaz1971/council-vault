# Frederick Golf Course Effluent Irrigation Data -- Water Use Comparison for Data Center Debate

## Summary

City of Frederick COO Zack Kershner provided annual effluent flow figures for the Clustered Spires golf course in response to a request from Councilmember Peter Brehm. The data was surfaced in the context of the proposed data center development at Frederick Commerce Center, where a community member (reportedly Bruce Dean or a Trammell Crow representative) had claimed golf courses use as much water as data centers. Brehm forwarded the data to Libby Taylor, César, and Sarah Hempel Irani on May 21, 2026.

## Key Points

### Golf Course Effluent Data (Total Annual Flow in Gallons)

| Year | Total Annual Flow (gallons) |
|---|---|
| 2020 | 17,826,315 |
| 2021 | 16,476,590 |
| 2022 | 16,386,077 |
| 2023 | 30,857,932 |
| 2024 | 24,313,789 |
| 2025 | 24,816,363 |

Note: 2026 partial (through March) shows 555,898 gallons. Usage is heavily concentrated June-October, consistent with irrigation seasonality.

### Context

The data was surfaced in direct response to a rhetorical move by data center proponents -- the argument that because golf courses use large amounts of water, data center water use is not a meaningful concern. This framing appeared at the May 16 community listening session hosted by techfrederick on behalf of the Nexus Frederick Commerce Center proposal.

Brehm's request also asked for comparable water use data on private businesses including a brewery, AstraZeneca (without naming them directly), and a dairy. Finance data on private business consumption was still pending as of May 21.

### What the Data Does and Does Not Show

This is effluent flow data -- reclaimed/treated wastewater sent to the golf course for irrigation, not potable water consumption. That distinction matters for the comparison: data centers typically consume potable water for cooling, not reclaimed effluent. A direct apples-to-apples comparison requires knowing what water source a proposed data center would draw from and whether any reclaimed water arrangement is part of the proposal.

## Relevance

This data is directly usable in the data center permitting and community benefit agreement debate. The golf course comparison argument is a known rhetorical tactic in data center advocacy (the "Carvana washes cars" equivalence mentioned in the email chain is another version). Having the actual numbers in hand allows César and council colleagues to respond specifically rather than generically. The pending Finance data on private industrial users will strengthen the comparison set further.

The email chain also confirms that community opposition (Steve Jakubczyk, Chad DiMarzo, NAC representatives) is organized and using email threads to coordinate pushback on the Nexus proposal. The developer sent the listening session invitation to a very wide distribution list -- over 100 recipients including all council members, labor unions, neighborhood advisory councils, state legislators, and media.
