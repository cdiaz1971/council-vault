# Data Centers -- Power and Infrastructure Constraints in Frederick

From the December 19, 2025 council discussion on data center legislation. Power availability is the central constraint shaping what kind of data center activity is feasible in Frederick City in the near term.

## Maryland's Structural Power Problem

Maryland imports approximately 40% of its power. The hyperscale data center complex already operating in Adamstown is compounding this dependency significantly. Frederick City sits downstream of these constraints: any new data center that requires dedicated transmission infrastructure will face a queue and timeline problem that cannot be solved locally.

Power reaching Frederick City depends on reduction through the Doubs Substation. Additional transmission lines to support significant new data center load could take up to 10 years to bring online, given power allocation priorities in Northern Virginia that take precedence in the regional grid.

## What This Means for Permitting

Only data centers that already have site plan approval have any assurance of power availability. This is not a minor administrative detail -- it means Frederick cannot assume that approving a data center use will result in an operating facility without a committed power supply. Legislation should require demonstrated power availability as a condition of permit issuance, not a post-approval consideration.

The Federal Energy Regulatory Commission (FERC) has taken the position that data centers should provide their own generation. For Maryland, which lacks the existing generating resources that attract hyperscale operators, this makes large-scale data center development structurally difficult without on-site generation commitments.

## What Scale Is Actually Feasible

Server rooms and small-scale data centers can be sited within Frederick City's existing infrastructure. Hyperscale data centers -- the large campuses typical of Northern Virginia -- are not suitable for Frederick's population density and current infrastructure. Legislation should define scale categories and apply different requirements accordingly.

## Legislative Gaps Identified

The Data Center Impact Study was not yet presented to council as of the December 19 discussion. Legislation should not be enacted until that study's findings and recommendations are on the record. The study will provide economic and environmental data and recommended siting criteria based on Maryland's power situation.

## Why This Matters for César

The 10-year transmission timeline is the single most important constraint to communicate to any developer or advocate who raises data center interest in Frederick. Approving zoning without resolving power supply creates expectation problems that will come back to council. The legislative sequencing -- study first, legislation second -- is the right approach.
