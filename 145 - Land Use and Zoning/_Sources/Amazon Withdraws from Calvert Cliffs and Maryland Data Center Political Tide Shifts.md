# Amazon Withdraws from Calvert Cliffs and Maryland Data Center Political Tide Shifts

## Summary

Amazon Web Services withdrew its proposal for a 2.4 million square foot data center campus at Calvert Cliffs after fierce voter backlash ousted three pro-data-center commissioners in the June 2026 primary, with a six-month moratorium vote scheduled for August 18. The story is less about one company's withdrawal and more about what is now a clear pattern: data center opposition has reached electoral consequences, and nearly 77% of Marylanders now live under some form of moratorium.

## Key Points

AWS had proposed multiple data center campuses at Calvert Cliffs covering 2.4 million square feet, submitted to Calvert County in early May 2026. The proposal was abandoned after the June 23 primary ousted all three county commissioners who had voted against a pause on data centers -- including the commission president and vice president. This is one of the most direct examples anywhere in the country of data center opposition translating into electoral consequences.

Calvert County will hold a public meeting August 18 to consider a temporary six-month moratorium on site plan approvals for data center projects. The proposed ordinance would allow time to review regulations and complete studies before any further approvals.

The Frederick County parallel is directly relevant: voters also ousted County Council Chair Brad Young after the council voted to expand an existing data center zone in Adamstown. County Executive Jessica Fitzwater subsequently enacted an executive order pausing data center development through end of year, though the county still plans to move forward with a 2,500-acre data center campus.

CCAN Action Fund reports that nearly 77% of Maryland's population now lives under some kind of data center moratorium. Counties with measures include Montgomery, Prince George's (two-year ban), Baltimore County, Baltimore City, Harford (no time limit), Queen Anne's, and Washington County. CCAN's Maryland director predicted the General Assembly will likely take a serious look at statewide moratorium bills during the 2027 session.

Constellation, which owns the Calvert Cliffs nuclear plant, released a statement maintaining that the opportunity for data centers in Maryland continues -- signaling Amazon's withdrawal does not close the door on the site entirely.

## Relevance

This is primary record material for the PC25-626ZTA opposition brief. The electoral consequences in both Calvert and Frederick Counties demonstrate that data center opposition is not a fringe position -- it is a voting issue that has already removed incumbents. The 77% moratorium statistic and the CCAN prediction of 2027 statewide legislation strengthen the argument that Frederick's ZTA process is running against the tide of state and local opinion, not with it.

The Calvert moratorium vote on August 18 is worth tracking. If it passes, it adds another Maryland county moratorium to the record before the Frederick Planning Commission hearing in October.
