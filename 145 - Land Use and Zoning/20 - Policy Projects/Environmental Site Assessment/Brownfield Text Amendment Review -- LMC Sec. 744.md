# Brownfield Text Amendment Review -- LMC Sec. 744

## Summary

CM Brehm proposes a new LMC Section 744 requiring Environmental Site Assessments as a condition of major site plan approval, with public disclosure of results. The amendment has genuine public health merit but contains a material drafting error (obsolete ASTM standard) and several structural enforcement gaps that should be resolved before introduction.

## What It Does

This LMC text amendment adds a new Section 744 titled "Site Soil, Water, and Air Characterization." It creates a three-stage cascade triggered at major site plan approval.

Stage one: every major site plan applicant must submit a Phase I ESA conducted by an independent consultant per ASTM standard E1527-05. Stage two: if the Phase I identifies Recognized Environmental Conditions (RECs), a Phase II ESA per ASTM E1903-19 is required. Stage three: if the Phase II finds contamination, the applicant must follow the continuing obligations identified in that assessment and document compliance with the Planning Department.

All submitted documentation is made publicly available on receipt by the Planning Department. Major site plan approval is contingent on receipt of all required documentation. The Planning Department receives explicit revocation authority if documentation was fraudulent, if conditions change materially, or if any provision of the section is violated. Fort Detrick is carved out as federal property outside city jurisdiction.

The justification statement frames this as a response to the gap between private environmental due diligence (which exists but is inaccessible to the public) and public awareness of site contamination risks.

## Concerns and Risks

**Obsolete ASTM standard citation (must fix before introduction).** The Phase I requirement cites ASTM E1527-05, a 2005 standard superseded by E1527-13 and then E1527-21. The current standard is E1527-21. Consultants and lenders use E1527-21 today. Codifying an obsolete standard creates interpretive problems from day one and is an avoidable embarrassment. The Phase II citation (E1903-19) is current.

**Scope gap -- major site plan trigger only.** The amendment applies only to major site plan approvals. Minor site plans and projects that proceed directly to building permit without a site plan are not covered. The justification specifically cites informal dumping grounds near residential areas, but small infill and some residential development may enter those areas without triggering this requirement at all. The coverage gap is real and should be surfaced in committee.

**No adequacy review standard.** The condition of approval is receipt of documentation, not adequacy of documentation. The Planning Department has no explicit authority to reject a deficient or incomplete ESA. A developer could submit a technically non-compliant Phase I and still satisfy the literal requirement. The amendment needs either a completeness review standard or a reference to ASTM's professional standards as the adequacy floor.

**Continuing obligations enforcement gap.** Once a major site plan is approved and construction begins, the city's only remedy for non-compliance with continuing obligations (say, groundwater monitoring) is revocation of the site plan -- a hollow remedy against a substantially built-out project. The amendment needs a tie to inspection or licensing authority, a financial assurance or bond requirement, or a referral mechanism to MDE.

**Public disclosure without redaction process.** All documentation is made publicly available on receipt. ESAs can contain CERCLA-sensitive materials and information that property owners, lenders, and insurers treat as confidential. The amendment does not address redaction authority or who makes that determination. At minimum it should acknowledge that public disclosure is subject to applicable state and federal law, including MPIA exemptions.

**No definition of "independent external consultant."** ASTM E1527-21 defines "Environmental Professional" with qualification standards. The amendment should either incorporate that definition by reference or establish its own independence standard (no financial interest in the project, no prior relationship with the applicant).

**MDE and state program interaction.** Maryland's voluntary cleanup and brownfields programs (BPSA, VCP) already impose ESA and remediation requirements for enrolled sites. The amendment creates a parallel local requirement without addressing how the two interact. A developer who has completed a state-approved cleanup under MDE supervision should not be required to satisfy this process independently. A provision recognizing MDE-accepted documentation as satisfying the city's requirements is needed to avoid duplicative and potentially conflicting obligations.

## Procedural Notes

Rachel Gray should review before introduction, specifically on whether the city's authority to impose ESA requirements as a condition of local land use approval is well-grounded under Maryland law, and on the MDE preemption question. Both are likely answerable in the city's favor but should be confirmed.

The development community will object on cost and delay grounds. Phase I ESAs run roughly $1,500 to $4,000; Phase II can run $10,000 to $50,000 or more depending on site conditions. For larger commercial projects this is marginal. For smaller major site plan projects it could be material. Expect this at public hearing.

The Fort Detrick carve-out is legally correct but the justification statement does not explain it, which could generate public confusion given the installation's documented contamination history.

## Political Assessment

The amendment has broad council support potential in principle. The public health rationale is solid and the transparency angle -- making private due diligence publicly accessible -- is politically compelling. The key pre-introduction fix is the ASTM standard citation. Everything else can be addressed through amendment or Planning's implementing regulations, but the standard must be clean on introduction.
