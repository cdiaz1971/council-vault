# LMC Review Commission -- Draft Resolution Analysis

## Summary

Draft resolution establishing a temporary advisory Land Management Code Review Commission, developed through two Government Operations Committee meetings and presented to full council in early April 2026. The document is substantive but unfinished -- three sections contain bracketed placeholders requiring completion before introduction. The Council President holds explicit appointment authority over all commission seats, subject to majority council confirmation.

## Key Points

**Structure and Status**

The resolution creates an 11-to-15 member advisory commission. It is advisory only -- no authority to adopt or enforce regulations. All final LMC decisions remain with the City Council. The commission dissolves three months after submitting its final report. The document header notes that bold text is new language and highlights represent areas needing additional work, confirming this is still a working draft.

**Appointment Authority**

The Council President appoints all members with advice and consent of the City Council. The resolution explicitly names the Council President as the appointing authority -- a significant structural fact. Council liaisons (one or two) participate fully but do not vote.

**Scope of Work (Section 2)**

The commission is tasked with evaluating LMC clarity and usability, assessing consistency with the Comprehensive Plan, reviewing zoning districts and approval processes for "predictability, effectiveness, and equity," identifying barriers to housing diversity and economic development, and recommending amendments. Section 2G includes an unusual mandate: analyze the LMC for opportunities for tax relief on residential accounts. The equity framing is present but thin -- "price appropriate housing" appears without AMI targets or affordability thresholds.

**Membership Composition (Section 3)**

The resolution calls for geographic balance (one member per council district as a goal), professional diversity across planning, development, real estate, housing, and environmental fields, nonprofit and civic representation, and members with lived experience. The committee ultimately declined to cap industry/developer representation, opting for flexibility instead, though this remains a live political question. The draft notes that architects, lawyers, land use developers, and urban planners were identified as desirable expertise.

**Reference Documents (Section 4)**

The commission is required to review a specific list of existing city plans: the 2020 Comprehensive Plan, Climate Action Plan, Active Mobility Plan, Let's Move Frederick, APA Housing Accelerator, ALICE Report, city and county housing studies, Parks Master Plan, small-area plans, and any existing form-based code drafts.

**Deliverables and Timeline (Section 7)**

Four key deliverables: a Planning Department policy memo within 60 days on the pros/cons of a development moratorium; an evaluation and review framework developed by the Government Operations Committee (affordability, equity, sustainability) to be presented to full council; a six-month internal status update to the Government Operations Committee; quarterly updates thereafter; and a final written report within 24 months of the commission's first meeting, with a possible extension by council resolution.

**Open Placeholders**

Three areas remain incomplete and require action before introduction: (1) the third WHEREAS clause needs housing study language -- César's housing production target resolution is the natural source for this; (2) Section 7 references a moratorium study without full language; (3) the evaluation framework referenced in Section 7B does not yet exist and is assigned to the Government Operations Committee to develop.

**Committee Discussion Notes (Post-Body Appendix)**

Six points were appended after the operative language -- these are deliberative notes, not resolution text, and should not travel into the final version. The most substantive: the committee flagged that significant legal and planning staff resources will be required, expressed strong interest in creative public engagement (including a full-day facilitated event), and noted Councilmember Atkins provided time-burden calculations for commission members and staff.

## Relevance

The LMC Review Commission is the vehicle through which housing production, affordability, and equity goals will either be embedded into Frederick's regulatory framework or sidelined. César's appointment authority gives him direct influence over who shapes the 24-month review. The missing AMI language in Section 2 is a gap to fill before introduction -- the housing production target resolution (1,400 units/year through 2035, 15% at or below 80% AMI, 33% sub-target at or below 60% AMI) provides ready WHEREAS language. The evaluation framework required under Section 7B is a parallel workstream that the Government Operations Committee owns -- if equity metrics are not locked into that framework early, the commission's recommendations may not be evaluated against affordability outcomes. See also [[LMC Modernization -- Libby Taylor Proposal Analysis -- 2026-04-12]] for the more detailed implementation proposal that runs parallel to this resolution.
