### Commission Role & Authority 

^bd02fb

The preferred structure is an **advisory commission with formal recommendations**, designed to maximize participation while retaining credibility. The Commission should:

- Be empowered to propose **new policy**, not just review existing code
    
- Coordinate closely with **Planning staff, Legal, the Planning Commission, and the Historic Preservation Commission**
    
- Maintain routine communication with **City Council**, including designated Council liaisons
    
- Clearly communicate its role to the **Board of Zoning Appeals**
    

The Commission is envisioned as setting a framework for future growth rather than addressing isolated technical fixes.  

^43546356




---

### Scope of Review

The review would cover the **entire Land Management Code**, with the understanding that not all sections will require changes. In addition to the Comprehensive Plan, guidance should be drawn from:

- Form-based codes and small-area plans
    
- Climate Action Plan
    
- Active Mobility Planning
    
- Parks Master Plan
    
- Racial equity considerations
    

---

### Membership & Composition

The Committee emphasized broad and inclusive representation while balancing manageability. Desired perspectives may include:

- Renters and homeowners
    
- Small landlords and developers
    
- Preservationists and environmental advocates
    
- Union and building trades representatives
    
- Business owners
    
- FCPS representatives
    
- Housing and ALICE advocates
    
- Realtors and land-use professionals
    
- Marginalized populations
    

Additional considerations include geographic balance, conflict-of-interest disclosures (with Legal guidance), and a transparent appointment process that allows Council members to propose candidates. Members would serve for the full duration of the Commission, with a defined job description and expected time commitment.

### Process & Timeline

- **Duration:** Approximately 2 years
    
- **Meeting frequency:** To be defined in advance; potentially up to twice per month
    
- **Format:** Public, primarily in-person, with possible hybrid options
    
- **Staff support:** To be determined
    
- **Training:** Provided incrementally on planning law, fair housing, preservation, and best practices using accessible, lay-person materials
    
- **Data inputs:** Housing studies, the Comprehensive Plan, and related analyses
    
- **Outside experts:** May be requested as needed
    
- **Agenda control:** Commission Chair
    
- **Public engagement:** Periodic check-ins, reporting meetings, and structured opportunities for stakeholder feedback
    

---

### Housing & Equity Considerations

The Committee stressed the importance of avoiding a performative process. Recommendations should be evaluated through explicit lenses, including:

- Housing supply impacts
    
- Affordability and cost impacts
    
- Equity and racial justice outcomes
    
- Accessibility, walkability, and mixed-use goals
    

The Committee also raised the question of whether development should continue unchanged during the review period and requested further analysis of potential moratorium options, including legal constraints and practical impacts.

---

### Deliverables & Accountability

Expected outputs include:

- A **written final report with recommendations**
    
- Potential redlined code language, if staff capacity allows
    
- Technical documentation supporting recommendations
    
- Analysis of implementation and cost impacts, including potential external consulting needs
    
- Identification of training and resource requirements
    

The Commission would dissolve upon completion of its final report, unless extended by Council. Unfinished recommendations or minority opinions would be documented in an appendix, with staff support for meeting minutes and recordkeeping.
