# Comparative International Models for City-Building

## Summary
A review of Benjamin Schneider's "The Unfinished Metropolis" highlights three international models cited as evidence other countries are out-executing the U.S. on city-building: Canadian transit built faster and cheaper than U.S. rail, European-style social housing now reaching states like Hawaii, and German baugruppen cohousing requiring building code changes to replicate.

## Key Points
Vancouver is cited as sustaining a "sense that the project of city-building never ended," largely through reliable, fast transit development at a fraction of typical U.S. rail costs, in contrast to many U.S. cities struggling with a post-pandemic transit ridership decline.

Social housing modeled on European approaches is described as migrating to U.S. states like Hawaii that are struggling to produce affordable housing through conventional channels.

German baugruppen, cohousing developments organized directly by resident groups rather than conventional developers, are cited as a model for more livable, walkable communities, contingent on building codes that currently do not accommodate them in most U.S. jurisdictions.

## Relevance
Adds concrete comparative case studies to the zoning and urban planning curriculum, and gives Frederick's own social housing and zoning reform threads, including [[Social Housing Requires Zoning Reform to Scale]] and [[Cambridge Social Housing Task Force -- Peer City Moving From Policy to Implementation]], additional peer precedent beyond the domestic examples already filed.
