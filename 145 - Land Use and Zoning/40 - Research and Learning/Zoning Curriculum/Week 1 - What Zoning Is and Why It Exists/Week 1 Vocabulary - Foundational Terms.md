# Week 1 Vocabulary

**Police power** -- the inherent constitutional authority of state governments to regulate private conduct and property for public health, safety, morals, and general welfare. Zoning's entire legal legitimacy rests on this doctrine.

**Enabling act** -- state legislation that delegates a specific power, in this case zoning authority, down to municipal governments. Cities have no independent right to zone; they exercise a power the state has granted them.

**Zoning commission** -- a body required under the Standard State Zoning Enabling Act to study and recommend zoning regulations before the legislative body adopts them. The ancestor of today's planning commissions.

**Use district** -- an area of a zoning map where only certain categories of use (residential, commercial, industrial) are permitted. NYC's 1916 ordinance used three: residential, commercial, and unrestricted.

**Bulk regulation** -- rules governing a building's size and shape independent of its use: height, setbacks, lot coverage, floor area. Distinct from use regulation, which governs what activity happens inside.

**Zoning envelope** -- the three-dimensional shape a building is allowed to occupy on its lot, defined by height limits, setback ratios, and lot coverage rules combined. The 1916 ordinance's envelope rules produced the stepped "wedding cake" building form common in older Manhattan towers.

**Lot coverage** -- the percentage of a lot's ground area that a building's footprint is permitted to occupy.

**Setback** -- the required distance a building must be set back from a lot line, street, or (under an envelope system) from an angled sightline that steepens as the building rises.

## A note on scope
This list covers only what week 1's readings need. The vocabulary that governs day-to-day zoning administration, variance, special exception, nonconforming use, spot zoning, arrives in week 4, once you've seen how a zoning code actually gets applied to a specific parcel.
