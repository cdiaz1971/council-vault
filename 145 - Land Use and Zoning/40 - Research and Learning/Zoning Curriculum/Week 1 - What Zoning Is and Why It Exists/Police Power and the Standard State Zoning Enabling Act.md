# Police Power and the Standard State Zoning Enabling Act

## The legal problem zoning had to solve
A city cannot regulate private land use just because it wants to. It needs a constitutional hook. That hook is the police power, the inherent authority of state governments to regulate for public health, safety, morals, and general welfare. Cities don't hold police power on their own. They are, as one legal outline puts it, creatures of the state, and they only get to zone because the state hands that power down to them.

After New York's 1916 ordinance, other cities wanted to copy it, but many state courts and legislatures had no clear framework for delegating that authority to municipalities. Zoning laws in this early period faced real legal uncertainty about whether they were a legitimate use of police power or an uncompensated taking of property rights.

## The Advisory Committee on Zoning
In September 1921, Secretary of Commerce Herbert Hoover formed an Advisory Committee on Zoning to write a model statute states could adopt wholesale. Edward Bassett, the same lawyer behind the 1916 NYC ordinance, sat on this committee alongside figures like Frederick Law Olmsted (the landscape architect's son, carrying the family name into planning) and Lawrence Veiller, a housing reformer.

## What the Standard State Zoning Enabling Act contains
First circulated in mimeograph form in August 1922, revised in January 1923, and formally printed in 1924, the SZEA runs nine sections. It:
- Grants zoning power to municipalities as a delegation of the state's police power
- Authorizes dividing a jurisdiction into districts
- States the purposes zoning may serve (health, safety, morals, general welfare)
- Lays out procedures for adopting and amending zoning regulations
- Requires a zoning commission to advise the legislative body before rules are adopted

More than 55,000 copies of the first printed edition sold. By the 1926 second printing, 19 states had adopted enabling legislation modeled directly on it. A companion model, the Standard City Planning Enabling Act, followed in 1928 and covered comprehensive ("master") plans, the document zoning is supposed to implement, which becomes directly relevant in Week 13.

## Why this is the load-bearing week 1 concept
Nearly every zoning code in the country, Frederick's included, ultimately rests on a chain that runs: state constitution grants police power, state enabling act delegates zoning authority to municipalities, municipal ordinance exercises that delegated authority. When a zoning decision gets challenged, this chain is where the legal argument starts. Week 3's case law, Euclid v. Ambler, is the Supreme Court blessing this whole arrangement as constitutional.
