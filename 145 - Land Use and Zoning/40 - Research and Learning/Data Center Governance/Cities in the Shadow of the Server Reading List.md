# Cities in the Shadow of the Server -- Reading List for Urbanists

## Summary

A curated reading list tracing the decades-long pattern of tech companies extracting land, water, subsidies, data, and democratic control from cities -- essential context for understanding the current wave of AI infrastructure demands on local governments.

## Key Points

Part of Next City's "In the Shadow of the Server" series. The core thesis: the fight over tech infrastructure in cities did not start with ChatGPT and the pattern -- grand promises of jobs and efficiency, followed by extraction of land, water, public subsidies, data, and democratic control -- has not changed.

The reading list:

**Sideways: The City Google Couldn't Buy** (Josh O'Kane, 2022) -- How Google's Sidewalk Labs tried to build a data-harvesting smart neighborhood on Toronto's waterfront and was beaten back by residents, privacy advocates, and local officials. The template for tech-city conflicts.

**Dracula Urbanism and Smart City Mania** (David Wilson and Elvin Wyly, 2024) -- Uses the vampire framework to argue that smart city development is seductive and parasitic, targeting vulnerable populations and punishing the poor, working class, immigrants, and homeless.

**Automating Inequality** (Virginia Eubanks, 2018) -- How automated decision-making in welfare, child protective services, and public housing systematically harms low-income communities. The AI-in-government cautionary text.

**The Smart Enough City** (Ben Green, 2019) -- Technology is not inherently progressive; it reflects and amplifies existing power structures. Cities need civic capacity and political will more than sensors and apps.

**World Eaters: How Venture Capital Is Cannibalizing the Economy** (Catherine Bracy, 2025) -- VC's extractive practices bleeding into all industries, undermining labor and housing markets.

**A City Is Not a Computer** (Shannon Mattern, 2021) -- The city-as-computer metaphor reduces place-based knowledge to information processing. Cities contain myriad local and indigenous intelligences that algorithms cannot capture.

**Grounding the Cloud** (Ali Fard, July 2026, forthcoming) -- Reveals the material foundations of global computing: mines, undersea cables, data centers on city edges. Counters the "shapeless cloud" mythology.

## Relevance

This reading list is direct background material for Frederick's active data center permitting work. The Sidewalk Labs case in particular is a useful reference point: a tech giant, a secretive deal, community organizing, and a fight over who controls the city's future. Frederick is not Toronto, but the structural dynamics are the same. The Eubanks book is also directly relevant to any future conversation about AI tools in city operations -- see [[AI for Housing Permitting Use Cases and Guardrails|AI for Housing Permitting -- Use Cases and Guardrails]].

The framing from "A City Is Not a Computer" -- that the city-as-computer metaphor erases local knowledge -- is a useful counterweight when vendors pitch algorithmic planning tools to council.
