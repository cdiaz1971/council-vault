# Data Center Governance -- Three Angles on the Same Problem

## The Convergence

Three pieces arrived in the inbox today from entirely separate sources, each approaching data center governance from a different angle. Together they constitute a more complete picture than any one of them provides alone.

**Angle 1 -- Negotiation leverage (Good Jobs First / Next City):**
Cities are negotiating data center deals from a position of manufactured weakness. The companies need the infrastructure -- land, power, fiber, regulatory access -- more than they let on. The standard playbook is designed to exhaust public leverage before the public knows it could have any: LLC concealment, NDAs, consultant-manufactured urgency, and threats to relocate that are largely bluffs. $710 billion in announced 2026 AI capex means these companies are not waiting for a county tax abatement to decide whether to build. They are racing to site. Cities should negotiate from that fact. See [[Cities Should Negotiate Data Center Deals From Leverage Not Desperation]].

**Angle 2 -- National security framing (CyberScoop / Claroty):**
The same facilities cities are being pressured to approve quickly and cheaply are being targeted by drone strikes and Iranian-linked cyberattacks. Data centers are now explicitly considered strategic military and economic infrastructure. CISA, the National Cybersecurity Strategy, and international standards frameworks are all moving toward treating them as critical infrastructure. This reframing matters for local governance: facilities that will be national security targets should not be sited through opaque, rushed processes that preempt public input. The security argument reinforces the transparency and process argument. See [[Data Centers as Critical Infrastructure Physical and Cyber Attack Surface in the AI Era]].

**Angle 3 -- Economic infrastructure embedding (AI Breakfast / Anthropic):**
Anthropic's reported $1.5B joint venture with Blackstone, Goldman Sachs, and other Wall Street firms positions AI as infrastructure for private equity portfolio optimization -- going through the owners of thousands of companies rather than waiting for adoption to happen organically. This is a different story than "AI company sells software." It is: AI compute infrastructure becomes embedded in the financial architecture of the economy. That has downstream implications for labor, local economic ecosystems, and how municipalities should think about vendor relationships with AI companies. See [[Anthropic Wall Street JV and the Enterprise AI Infrastructure Play]].

## The Synthesis

The three angles reinforce each other in a specific way: the leverage argument tells cities what they can demand, the security argument tells them why demanding it is legitimate, and the infrastructure-embedding argument tells them what the long-term stakes are.

A data center sited cheaply and opaquely, with no community benefit agreement, no job quality standards, and no transparency requirements, is not just a missed opportunity for local economic benefit. It is a national security facility approved without appropriate scrutiny, built by companies whose AI infrastructure is becoming structurally embedded in the economy in ways that will be very difficult to renegotiate later. The moment of siting is the moment of leverage.

## Implications for Frederick

Frederick's data center zoning text amendment is the right vehicle. The process transparency requirements, community benefit agreement standards, and job quality provisions that belong in that amendment are not anti-growth provisions. They are the governance framework that makes data center siting consistent with Frederick's interests -- and, per the security framing, consistent with national interest.

The amendment should address at minimum: disclosure requirements (no LLCs, no NDAs, full infrastructure impact disclosure before any vote), community benefit agreement enforceability, utility rate protection (data centers should not be subsidized through residential rate cross-subsidy), and job quality standards for construction and permanent employment.
