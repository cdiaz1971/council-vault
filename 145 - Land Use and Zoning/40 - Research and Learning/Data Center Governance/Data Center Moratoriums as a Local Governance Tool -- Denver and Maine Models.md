# Data Center Moratoriums as a Local Governance Tool -- Denver and Maine Models

## Summary

Two concurrent 2026 cases -- Denver's unanimous city council moratorium and Maine municipalities acting after a state veto -- establish the local moratorium as the primary practical tool for data center governance when regulatory frameworks are absent or state-level action fails.

## The Pattern

Both cases share a common structure: a data center proposal (or wave of proposals) arrives before the municipality has adequate regulatory standards to evaluate it. The local response in both cases is the same -- pause permitting, develop standards, then resume. The moratorium is not a ban; it is a governance gap repair.

Denver acted proactively, before a formal application triggered a crisis. The DE3 site in Elyria-Swansea, designed for 230,000 gallons of water and 18 megawatts of power per day at maximum capacity, made the stakes visible enough to generate unanimous council support. The moratorium lasts one year while the city builds zoning standards around siting, energy, water, and noise.

Maine municipalities acted reactively, after state-level regulation failed. Gov. Mills vetoed a 20-megawatt moratorium bill; the legislature couldn't override. Sanford, Westbrook, and Brunswick then moved independently -- and in some cases more aggressively than the state proposal. Brunswick's threshold was one megawatt, not twenty.

## Implications for Frederick

Frederick has no data center zoning framework. If a proposal surfaces, the council would be in the same position Denver was: no standards to apply. A moratorium framed around developing those standards -- not blocking data centers categorically -- is both defensible and precedented. The unanimous Denver vote shows this is not ideologically contested territory when framed correctly.

The Maine pattern adds a second argument: when state frameworks are absent or blocked, local action is legitimate and necessary. Maryland has no data center-specific municipal ordinance framework to César's knowledge -- that gap is the same gap Maine municipalities faced.

The community benefit agreement framework ([[Data Center Community Benefit Agreements -- Policy Models]]) is what comes after the moratorium period -- the standards the moratorium buys time to develop.
