# FY2026 Q3 Budget Update Analysis

## Reading context

This analysis was prepared in August 2026 but covers FY2026 performance through March 31, 2026. Its fiscal findings, staff references, and proposed next steps retain that reporting context. Use [[City Budget and Fiscal Condition]] for the core reference and [[Budget and Finance - In-Progress Plans]] for questions requiring updated figures.

## Summary

Walkthrough of the FY2026 Q3 budget update (through 3/31/26, 75% of fiscal year elapsed) for a Gov Ops committee audience with limited budget background, plus a comparative benchmark showing Frederick's General Fund is more property-tax-reliant than peer Maryland cities.

## Fiscal Year Confirmation

City Charter, Section 2: fiscal year runs July 1 to June 30. Confirmed against the Q3 report's own math (March 31 = 75% of year elapsed, nine of twelve months) and against the FY2026 budget adoption ordinance language. No ambiguity here going forward.

## Overall Fiscal Health

Solid through Q3. Operating revenues ($153.6M, 77.4% of amended budget) are outpacing operating expenditures ($140.8M, 61.1%), both ahead of last year's pace. General Fund specifically: 88% of revenue collected versus 85% at this point last year, 65% of expenditures incurred, consistent with last year. No red flag requiring council action.

Total budget across all funds: $264.0 million (Governmental $159.1M, Enterprise $58.9M, Special Revenue $4.6M, CIP $41.5M).

## Key Findings

**Housing and Human Services underspend.** HHS is at 60% of budget spent with 75% of the year gone, the lowest functional area outside debt service. The Housing sub-line specifically sits at 50%. HHS's staffing share has also declined from 10% to 8% of citywide FTEs since FY2023, even as total city staffing grew 9.8%. Worth a direct question to Stuart Campbell or HHS staff on what's driving the pace (vacancies, contracting delays, grant timing) before assuming it's benign.

**MPDU in-lieu-of fee revenue down sharply year over year.** $366,318 collected this year versus $690,697 at the same point last year, roughly a 47% decline. Not budgeted (developer-triggered), so no % of budget to compare, but the year-over-year drop is real. Adds supporting weight to the Track 2 MPDU reform case (raising the in-lieu rate from $2/sq ft to $12/sq ft): a lower per-unit fee needs more triggering development activity just to hold revenue flat, and that activity isn't showing up.

**Rental licensing fee revenue running at 577% of budget.** $1.73M collected against a $300,000 projection, attributed by staff to inexperience budgeting a new program rather than a surge in rentals. Directly relevant to Brehm's rental licensing fee rewrite, which would reduce this fee and shrink Housing Fund revenue. Worth getting the cost-reconciliation numbers from Katie Barkdoll or Lacey Gordon before that fee debate moves.

**Overtime running hot for a second year.** Premium Overtime at 120% of full-year budget already spent, Regular Overtime at 142%. Both exceeded last year's rates too, a pattern rather than a one-off.

**Interest earnings elevated citywide**, General Fund miscellaneous revenue at 261% of budget, driven by interest at 385%. Reflects the current rate environment on city cash balances, a one-time-feeling benefit rather than a durable revenue source.

**Fund balance presentation needs a plain-language answer from staff.** The General Fund's $35.4M beginning fund balance is budgeted down to $2.65M by year-end, including a $16.4M "Rainy Day Fund" transfer that also appears elsewhere in the report as a Fund Balance/Reserves expenditure line. Unclear from the report alone whether the true unrestricted year-end cushion is closer to $2.65M or closer to $19M. Get this from Katie Barkdoll before any future budget-capacity conversation.

## Comparative Context: Property Tax Reliance

Frederick's General Fund draws 75.5% of its revenue from real property tax alone, 83.8% including all tax types. That's higher than Hagerstown, the closest true peer (standalone Maryland city, not a wealthy DC suburb), which has run 64.9% to 70.2% property-tax reliance in its General Fund over the last two budget cycles. It's also higher than Gaithersburg's roughly 40-44% of total city revenue from property tax, though Gaithersburg is a different kind of comparison (wealthier DC-suburb tax base, different fund denominator).

National city-finance averages (25-45% of general revenue from property tax, per Census data) aren't a fair comparison, since they typically include education funding and state aid that Maryland municipalities don't touch (counties run schools here). Strip that out and Maryland municipal reliance on property tax runs structurally higher across the board. Even so, Frederick sits toward the high end even among Maryland peers.

Practical implication: Frederick's fiscal health rides more heavily on the same tax base that's contested in every housing, zoning, and development conversation already underway, with less of a cushion from a diversified revenue mix than a city with meaningful sales or utility tax revenue would have. Personal income tax collection is also lagging (58% of budget through Q3, versus 96% for property tax), a second data point in the same direction.

## Watch List for Gov Ops (2026-08-10)

Housing and Human Services spending pace and staffing decline. MPDU in-lieu fee revenue decline as context for Track 2 reform. Rental licensing fee overcollection as context against Brehm's fee rewrite. True year-end General Fund reserve position given the Rainy Day Fund presentation. Overtime trending above budget for a second consecutive year.
