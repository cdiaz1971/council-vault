## Concept – Tax Increment Financing (TIF)

## Reading context

This January 2026 learning note describes the general TIF mechanism. Its statement that TIF can avoid tapping the General Fund is not a guarantee for a particular agreement: risk allocation depends on the actual financing terms and revenue performance. Use [[Revenue, Taxes, and Debt]] for the decision reference and [[Chicago Tax Increment Financing Study Finds Minimal Growth Attribution]] as a dated counterpoint on benefits and distribution.

**Tax Increment Financing (TIF)** is a financing tool in which the City uses the **future increase in property tax revenue from a defined area** to pay for public improvements in that same area.

### Core idea

1. A **TIF district** is created.
2. Current property tax revenue in that district is recorded as the **baseline**.
3. Public improvements and private redevelopment increase property values.
4. The **increment** (tax revenue above the baseline) is captured and used to:
   - repay bonds, or
   - reimburse eligible project costs.

TIF is essentially **borrowing against future growth** in tax revenue.

### What TIF funds

- streets, sidewalks, streetscape
- utilities and stormwater
- parking and structured parking
- environmental remediation
- public plazas and infrastructure supporting redevelopment

### How TIF bonds are repaid

- Bonds or other debt are issued upfront.
- **Only the increment** (new tax revenue above baseline) is pledged to repayment.
- Once bonds are fully paid off, increment revenue returns to the City's general fund.

### Why cities use TIF

- jump-start private redevelopment
- fund infrastructure without raising tax rates citywide
- align cost of improvements with those who benefit
- redevelop underutilized or blighted areas

### Major risks

- **Growth risk:** increment may be lower than projected
- **Concentration risk:** revenue tied to a small number of parcels or projects
- **Equity risk:** potential gentrification or displacement
- **Transparency risk:** complex deals can obscure real obligations

### Risk-management tools to ask about

- conservative revenue forecasts
- developer guarantees or special assessments
- debt coverage covenants
- clear reporting requirements and annual performance reviews

### Questions for council consideration

- What is the **baseline revenue** and how is it calculated?
- What **growth rate** is assumed and why is it reasonable?
- Who bears the risk if the increment is insufficient?
- What **public benefits** are guaranteed (housing, jobs, infrastructure)?
- What happens when the TIF district expires?

### Bottom line

TIF can be a powerful tool to fund redevelopment **without tapping the general fund**, but it depends on **future tax growth actually happening** and requires strong oversight to ensure public benefit.
