# City Budget and Fiscal Condition

## Purpose

This page supports Council decisions on the annual budget, mid-year changes, and the City's fiscal health: what Council controls under the Charter, how City funds work, and where structural risks lie. Revenue sources are covered in [[Revenue, Taxes, and Debt]]; budget method reform in [[Priority-Driven Budgeting]].

## Charter rules for the budget

All citations are to City Charter Art. VII.

- **Fiscal year:** July 1 to June 30 (§ 2).
- **Proposal:** The Mayor submits a proposed budget and overview, including five years of pending capital projects, by April 1, and may revise it through May 14 (§ 3).
- **Council's power:** From May 15, Council alone may revise the budget. It may insert, increase, or decrease items, but any change to total spending must be matched by a change to anticipated revenue (§§ 3(a), 5(c)).
- **Hearings:** At least two public budget meetings, with 10 days' notice of the first; department heads must attend on request (§ 5(a), (b)).
- **Adoption:** By special ordinance with at least four votes, before June 20. If Council does not adopt in time, or the Mayor vetoes and Council does not override with five votes, the Mayor's proposed budget takes effect (§ 5(d), (e); Art. II, § 16.1).
- **Spending:** No money may be spent without appropriation. The Mayor may move funds between classifications within a department; Council may move funds between departments in the last quarter at the Mayor's request. Unspent appropriations lapse (§ 6).
- **Contingency fund:** Council sets its size; the Mayor allocates it (§ 4(c)).
- **Fees:** Set by Council ordinance (§ 8).
- **Audit:** Council retains an independent auditor at least annually (§ 15).
- **Administration:** The Mayor oversees financial administration and purchasing (§§ 1, 7).

## Fund structure

| Fund type | Examples | Council leverage |
|---|---|---|
| Governmental | General Fund, Weinberg Center, City Housing Fund | Direct appropriation; where budget negotiation happens |
| Enterprise | Water and Sewer, Airport, Parking, Stormwater, Golf Course, Rental Operations | Self-supporting by rates and fees; less discretionary |
| Special revenue | Community Development, Community Health Center, Controlled Dangerous Substance, Opioid | Restricted by law to their purposes |

The City Housing Fund is a governmental fund, so its size is a Council appropriation choice ([[FY 2027 Budget -- City Fund Types Reference]]).

## FY2027 adopted budget

- Council adopted the FY2027 budget, tax rate, and fee schedule 6-0 on May 28, 2026 ([[FY2027 Budget Tax Rate and Fee Schedule Passed 6-0]]).
- The proposed General Fund was $160.6 million, up 8.6% from $147.8 million in FY2026 ([[FY2027 Budget -- Cycle Index]]).
- **One-time money for ongoing costs.** The proposal drew $37.0 million from reserves to balance: Rainy Day Fund $17.3 million, capital $12.9 million, capital equipment $5.5 million, and rental licensing $1.3 million. Recurring spending is outpacing recurring revenue (same source; adopted amounts: verify).
- **Revenue growth** came from rising assessments (+$3.4 million real property tax), personal income tax (+$3.1 million), and investment interest (+$2.4 million), not a rate increase.
- **Mayor's office cluster.** Equity and Inclusion was cut 29%, while the Office of Opportunities and Transformation grew 54% and a new Office of Vendor Engagement and Compliance was funded at $529,387, including a $300,000 one-time disparity study, without performance measures ([[FY2027 Budget -- Equity and Inclusion (1112)]]; [[FY2027 Budget -- Office of Opportunities and Transformation (1115)]]; [[FY2027 Budget -- Office of Vendor Engagement and Compliance (1116)]]; [[Why Local Governments Still Need Diversity in Public Contracting]]).
- Internal audit was budgeted at $50,000; the City Clerk's budget added a $10,000 stipend for residents serving on boards and commissions.

## FY2026 performance (through March 31, 2026)

- All funds totaled $264.0 million (governmental $159.1 million, enterprise $58.9 million, special revenue $4.6 million, capital $41.5 million). General Fund revenue was 88% collected and spending 65%, consistent with the prior year ([[FY2026 Q3 Budget Update Analysis 2026-08-10]]).
- Housing and Human Services had spent 60% of its budget at 75% of the year, the Housing line 50%; its share of City staff fell from 10% to 8% since FY2023.
- MPDU fee-in-lieu revenue fell about 47% from the prior year; rental licensing fees ran at 577% of a budget set for a new program.
- Overtime exceeded full-year budgets for a second year (premium 120%, regular 142%).
- Interest income ran far above budget, a one-time effect of the rate environment.
- The General Fund's true unrestricted year-end balance is unclear from the report, somewhere between about $2.7 million and $19 million: verify with Finance.

## Outside pressures

- Maryland faces a projected $3 billion structural gap, with counties warning of aid cuts, mandated costs, and an income tax distribution correction ([[Maryland Faces a Projected 3 Billion Dollar Structural Budget Gap]]).
- FY2028 is the first year Blueprint for Maryland's Future costs outrun the revenue dedicated to fund it, requiring an estimated $2.6 billion in general fund support that year; the digital ad tax meant to help pay for the Blueprint remains tied up in litigation, and the State has already asked agencies to model 10% cuts ([[Blueprint Costs Exceed Dedicated Revenue in FY2028]]). A gap this size tends to reach counties and municipalities through reduced state aid and shifted costs, which belongs in the background for Frederick's FY2028 budget cycle.
- Federal grants, especially in health and human services, carry termination risk.

## Authority map

| Lever | Who controls it | Council role |
|---|---|---|
| Proposed budget | Mayor, through May 14 | Hearings and questions |
| Final budget | Council, from May 15 | Direct, if balanced |
| Within-department transfers | Mayor | None |
| Between-department transfers | Council, last quarter, on Mayor's request | Approval |
| Fees | Council by ordinance | Direct |
| Independent audit | Council | Direct |
| Purchasing and administration | Mayor | Oversight |

## Decision framework

- Is ongoing spending funded by ongoing revenue?
- What is the true unrestricted fund balance, and what is the reserve policy?
- Which items are one-time, and are any one-time sources funding recurring costs?
- Does each new office have measurable outcomes?
- Are underspent lines a sign of vacancies or stalled programs?

## Metrics

- General Fund structural balance (recurring revenue minus recurring spending).
- Unrestricted fund balance as a share of spending.
- Budget execution by department at each quarter.
- Overtime against budget.
- Audit findings and their resolution.

## Council checklist

- [ ] Fund type identified?
- [ ] Revenue change matches any spending change?
- [ ] One-time versus ongoing sources stated?
- [ ] Department head available to answer?
- [ ] Four votes in hand, and five if a veto is likely?
