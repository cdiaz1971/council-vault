# Priority-Driven Budgeting

## Purpose

This page supports Council decisions about how the City builds its budget: whether to move from incremental budgeting (last year plus adjustments) to priority-driven budgeting (PDB), and how to apply PDB questions in the meantime. Vice President Nash introduced PDB at the Government Operations Committee on April 6, 2026.

## What PDB is

PDB, documented by the Government Finance Officers Association, starts from what revenue is available and what results the community needs, then funds programs in order of how well they produce those results ([[Priority-Driven Budgeting -- Concept Reference]]; GFOA, *Anatomy of a Priority-Driven Budget Process*). The eight steps:

1. **Identify available resources,** separating ongoing revenue from one-time sources.
2. **Identify priorities** as measurable results stated from residents' point of view.
3. **Define the results precisely** with cause-and-effect maps for this community.
4. **Prepare decision units,** either existing programs or new service offers, each roughly 1% to 10% of a fund.
5. **Score units** against the results, with evidence.
6. **Compare scores** in a ranked list.
7. **Allocate resources** down the list until revenue runs out; discuss programs near the line.
8. **Create accountability** by measuring whether funded programs deliver.

## How it applies to Frederick

- **Structural balance.** Step 1 would name the FY2027 reliance on $37.0 million in one-time reserves before any allocation ([[City Budget and Fiscal Condition]]).
- **No link from priorities to spending.** The City has a strategic plan (CommUNITY 2030; verify current status) but no process connecting program funding to its goals ([[Priority-Driven Budgeting -- Frederick Application]]).
- **Equity.** The 29% cut to Equity and Inclusion alongside growth in two newer offices without performance measures would be visible under scoring against an equity result.
- **Evidence for priorities.** United Way ALICE data, showing about 43% of City households below the ALICE threshold, can define a basic-needs priority.
- **Capital projects.** Capital projects can be scored the same way, which would surface the geographic concentration of the capital list raised at the April 8 work session.
- **Grant risk.** Federal grants can be treated as uncertain sources needing contingency plans.
- **SPARK, the administration's own priority exercise.** Staff introduced SPARK at the September 24, 2026 work session. Each council member allocates 100 points across the six CommUNITY 2030 strategic goals as input to the FY2028 budget. Allocations are due to Ericka Rohrbaugh by October 9, 2026, and results return to Council October 22. It ranks goals rather than scoring programs, so it is a first step toward PDB, not PDB itself, and a low-cost place to put points behind housing and equity results.

## Applying PDB questions now

Without formal adoption, Council can ask at any hearing:

- What priority result does this program serve?
- What evidence shows it is producing that result?
- Is it funded from ongoing or one-time sources?

## Authority map

| Lever | Who controls it | Council role |
|---|---|---|
| Budget format and contents | Council by ordinance (Charter Art. VII, § 4(a)) | Direct |
| Budget preparation | Mayor and staff | Requires administration cooperation |
| Priority results | Council, with resident input | Direct |
| Program scoring | Staff teams | Oversight |

Because Council may set the budget's contents by ordinance, it can require ongoing and one-time revenue to be shown separately and programs to be tied to stated results, even before a full PDB process.

## Implementation options

| Option | Tradeoffs |
|---|---|
| Ordinance requiring the budget to separate ongoing and one-time revenue and report program outcomes | Modest staff work; first step toward PDB |
| Pilot PDB in one fund or function | Tests the method; partial picture |
| Full PDB adoption with resident input on priorities | Largest change; needs executive cooperation and several cycles |
| PDB questions in hearings only | No cost; no structural change |

## Risks

- Scoring can be gamed if evidence standards are weak.
- Without executive cooperation, Council can require format changes but cannot run the process itself.
- Priorities set without resident input repeat the problems PDB is meant to fix.

## Metrics

- Share of spending tied to a stated priority result.
- Share of programs reporting outcome measures.
- Structural balance by year.
