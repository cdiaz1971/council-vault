# Priority-Driven Budgeting -- Frederick Application

## Reading context

This April 2026 application note preserves its original analysis of the proposed FY2027 budget. Its statement that the full $37 million draw came from HHS fund balance differs from the reserve-pool breakdown in [[FY2027 Budget -- Cycle Index]] and [[City Budget and Fiscal Condition]]; reconcile that attribution and adopted amounts with Finance before use. ALICE measures cited here mix City and other geographic populations; consult [[Cesar Context -- Council]] for their geographic and reporting-year context. Use [[Priority-Driven Budgeting]] for the core reference. These proposals do not establish adopted budget methodology or current legislative status.

## Summary

How the PDB framework maps onto Frederick's specific fiscal and political context. PDB is not an abstract reform here — it directly addresses the two analytical frames driving César's FY2027 budget work: structural fiscal risk and equity misalignment. Several findings from the current budget cycle are already PDB critiques in practice.

## Where Frederick's Current Budget Fails the PDB Test

**Structural imbalance is invisible in the Mayor's proposed budget.** PDB Step 1 requires that one-time sources be explicitly separated from ongoing revenues before any allocation begins. The Mayor's FY2027 proposal draws $37M from HHS fund balance to support ongoing operations. Under PDB logic, this would be identified and named as a structural problem at step one — before any program gets funded. The current incremental process allows it to be obscured within departmental line items.

**Programs are not scored against priority results.** The city has no mechanism for evaluating whether a funded program actually advances a stated community priority. The CommUNITY 2030 strategic plan exists and could serve as a priority framework, but there is no budget process that connects program funding to those priorities. Money flows based on last year's allocations, not demonstrated outcomes.

**The equity inversion finding is a PDB diagnosis.** The Equity and Inclusion office was cut 29% in FY2027 while two equity-adjacent new offices received over $1.3M combined with no performance accountability framework. Under PDB, both would be scored against an equity priority result using evidence of outcomes. The scoring would expose the inversion explicitly and publicly. The current incremental process makes it a footnote.

**Federal grant dependency is not risk-weighted.** PDB Step 1 distinguishes reliable ongoing revenues from uncertain one-time sources. Federal grants — particularly in HHS — are treated as stable in the current budget but carry real termination risk under the current federal environment. PDB would flag these as one-time sources requiring contingency planning.

## Where PDB Amplifies César's Active Agenda

**Housing production target resolution.** Once introduced, the 1,400-unit annual target resolution establishes a measurable, citizen-facing outcome — precisely the form PDB uses for priority results. That resolution becomes the anchor for scoring every land use and housing-adjacent budget item. If the Mayor's budget does not fund new housing production, a PDB-informed council can show that the city is spending below the funding line on its own stated housing priority.

**ALICE threshold as a scoring criterion.** The United Way ALICE data — 43% of city households below threshold, 72% of children in rental units, 35% of Black veterans — is the kind of evidence PDB Step 3 uses to define what a "basic needs" or "economic security" priority actually means for this community. This data is already in hand and can be deployed in any priority-definition exercise.

**Equity and Inclusion office.** The office's work is directly scoreable against an equity priority result. A PDB framework gives the council a principled, process-based argument for restoring that funding that goes beyond advocacy — it is a budget process argument.

**Geographic equity.** The downtown-focused capital project list challenged at Work Session 1 (April 8) by César and Nash is another PDB application. Under PDB, capital projects are scored against priority results the same way operating programs are. Projects serving District 2 and other underserved areas would need to compete on their merits against priority outcomes — not historical allocation patterns.

## What PDB Cannot Fix in Frederick

PDB is a process reform, not a power shift. The Mayor proposes the budget. Unless the council successfully compels a change in the budget development process — which would require cooperation from the executive branch — PDB can inform council analysis and questioning but cannot restructure how the Mayor's office allocates resources internally. The council's leverage is in the amendment process (additions must be offset by cuts) and in the political record built through hearing testimony.

A second limit: PDB does not guarantee equity outcomes. If the priority-setting process is not deliberately inclusive, stated priorities will reflect whoever participates. In Frederick, that risk is real given the demographics of typical civic engagement participants versus the demographics of ALICE households and immigrant residents.

## Practical Application in FY2027 Hearings (Now)

Even without a formal PDB implementation, three questions drawn from the PDB framework can be asked of every department in remaining hearings:

1. What specific community outcome does this program advance, and how do you measure whether it is achieving that outcome?
2. Is this program funded from ongoing revenues or one-time sources, and what happens to service levels when that source expires?
3. If this program were scored against the city's stated equity priorities, what evidence would you cite?

Departments that cannot answer these questions are exhibiting exactly the accountability gap PDB is designed to close.
