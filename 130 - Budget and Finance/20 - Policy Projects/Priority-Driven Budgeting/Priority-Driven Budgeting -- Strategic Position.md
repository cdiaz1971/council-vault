# Priority-Driven Budgeting -- Strategic Position

## Reading context

This April 2026 strategy note preserves the recorded preference to co-champion PDB while Nash controls the pace. References to remaining FY2027 hearings and staff roles belong to that date. [[Cesar Context -- Council]] records Katie Barkdoll as CAO and Richard Roark as CFO following June 2026 appointments. Confirm present roles and coordination before acting. Use [[Priority-Driven Budgeting]] for the core reference and [[Budget and Finance - In-Progress Plans]] for planning questions; this note does not confirm adoption or implementation.

## Summary

César's strategic decision on PDB: whether to co-champion the initiative alongside VP Nash or to engage as a supportive follower. This note captures the reasoning behind the preferred position and the concrete actions that follow from it.

## The Decision

Nash introduced PDB at the April 6 Government Operations Committee. It is her initiative. The question is not whether to support it -- PDB aligns directly with César's equity and fiscal accountability agenda and is worth supporting. The question is what role to play.

**Option A: Let Nash lead, follow when she moves it.**
Nash controls the timeline and the committee process. César engages when it comes to a vote or a work session, signals support, and does not expend capital or bandwidth on a process reform that is not his to drive. Lower cost, lower risk. PDB is not a constituency priority -- District 2 residents do not organize around budget methodology.

**Option B: Active co-champion, Nash controls the pace.**
César understands PDB well enough to deploy it analytically during hearings now, connects it publicly to equity and housing priorities when the opportunity arises, and makes clear in committee that he intends to use it. Nash still owns the process. César adds political weight and a clear equity framing without taking over the initiative or overextending on bandwidth.

**Option C: Co-driver.**
César takes an active role in designing or accelerating the implementation timeline alongside Nash. Higher cost, higher ownership, higher risk if it stalls or if executive resistance sharpens the friction with O'Connor.

## Preferred Position: Option B

Active co-champion, Nash controls the pace. The reasoning:

PDB is the most durable tool available to the council for addressing structural fiscal risk and equity misalignment -- the two frames driving César's FY2027 work. If equity and housing are named as priority results when Frederick adopts PDB, every future budget allocation gets scored against those outcomes. That embeds César's policy agenda into the budget process itself in a way that outlasts any individual administration. The political cost of Option B is low. The benefit of being clearly on record as a PDB supporter -- and as the member who connected it to equity and housing -- is meaningful.

Option A is not wrong, but it leaves value on the table. Option C risks overextension during an already full legislative and budget cycle.

## What to Avoid

Do not attempt to drive the implementation timeline. Do not propose formal PDB adoption as a council resolution without coordinating with Nash first -- it is her initiative. Do not let PDB become a political conflict with O'Connor before Nash has had the opportunity to build executive buy-in on her own terms.

## Three Concrete Actions (No Deadline Pressure)

These do not require PDB to be formally on the agenda. They can happen in the normal course of current work.

**1. Use PDB questions in remaining FY2027 hearings.** Ask every department: what priority result does this program serve, what evidence shows it is achieving that result, and is this funded from ongoing revenues or one-time sources? No formal process required. Builds the record and demonstrates fluency.

**2. Connect PDB to the housing production target resolution when it is introduced.** Frame the resolution explicitly as establishing a PDB-style priority result -- measurable, citizen-facing, scoreable. This ties the two initiatives together and gives PDB a concrete application in César's most visible legislative priority.

**3. Propose a program inventory request to the Budget Manager for FY2028 budget development.** This is the GFOA's own recommended first step -- a "quick win" that does not require executive buy-in to initiate as a council request. Ask CFO Barkdoll or Budget Manager Gordon to develop a fully costed General Fund program inventory as a trial run. Frame it as a council oversight tool, not as PDB adoption.

## Key Relationship Notes

**Nash:** She owns this. Coordinate before any public move on PDB. Do not get ahead of her on the committee process.

**O'Connor:** PDB implicitly critiques how his administration has been proposing budgets. Full implementation requires his cooperation. Do not make this a conflict with the Mayor before Nash has had a chance to build executive support.

**Barkdoll / Gordon:** The Budget Manager and CFO are the staff partners for any program inventory or process reform request. Both have been engaged productively in FY2027 hearings so far.
