# FY2027 Budget -- Legal Services (1121)

**Department head:** Rachel Gray, City Attorney. Gray is also the preemption review gatekeeper for the council's legislative agenda -- the junk fees ordinance, any future tenant protection measures, and CVA-adjacent work all require her sign-off before introduction.

## Summary

Legal Services is proposed at $1,281,615 for FY2027, up $41,189 (3.3%) from the FY2026 adopted budget of $1,240,426. This is a modest, primarily salary-driven increase with no significant new programs or structural changes. Two anomalies in the detail warrant a question: a police chiefs association membership buried in the Legal dues line, and a notable shift in retirement plan costs suggesting personnel turnover.

## Department Overview

The Legal department provides in-house counsel for the city, handles public information act requests, manages outside counsel relationships, and supports all city departments including the Council. The department generates revenue from legal review fees charged to development applicants -- $10,050 budgeted in FY2027, flat from FY2026. FY2025 actuals of $33,674 in legal review fees suggest significant underbudgeting of this revenue line.

## Key Budget Lines

**Salaries:** Full-time productive time $835,587 (up from $789,280, +5.9%). The department has grown substantially -- FY2023 actuals were $401K, now proposed at $836K. This reflects genuine headcount growth over four years, not just wage increases.

**Outside counsel ($95,000):** Budgeted for administrative hearings, consultations, and litigation. Held flat from FY2026. FY2025 actuals were only $10,282 -- another significant underrun. The $95K budget may be conservative given FY2024 actuals of $107,302.

**Software and licenses ($29,900):** Includes WestLaw research subscriptions ($12,300 combined), PIA (Public Information Act) software ($6,800), Legal Edge matter management ($4,200), social media archiving ($4,500), and Adobe ($2,100). The PIA software expenditure is directly relevant to CVA implementation -- the city's capacity to handle records requests from immigration enforcement or any other party depends on this infrastructure.

**Retirement line anomaly:** The 25-Year Plan line increases from $31,205 to $39,934 (+28%), while the 30-Year Plan drops from $47,733 to $17,284 (-64%). This pattern -- a large drop in the longer-vesting plan and a rise in the shorter-vesting plan -- indicates that a more senior staff member departed or retired and was replaced by a less senior hire. Routine, but worth confirming to understand the department's institutional knowledge retention.

**Maryland Chiefs of Police Association dues ($150):** Buried in the Legal travel and dues line. The city attorney's office has no operational reason for this membership. It likely belongs in the Police Department budget if it belongs anywhere.

## Questions to Ask at Hearing

- Why does the city attorney's office carry a Maryland Chiefs of Police Association membership?
- What is the staffing change reflected in the retirement line shift -- who departed and has the position been backfilled at equivalent seniority?
- Why is the legal review fee revenue budgeted at $10,050 when FY2025 actuals were $33,674?

## Flags

**Misaligned spend:** $150 MCPA membership in Legal has no apparent justification. Small dollar amount, but signals loose budget hygiene.\
**Personnel change:** Retirement line shift suggests senior departure. Worth confirming institutional knowledge is being maintained given Rachel Gray's central role in the council's legislative pipeline.\
**Revenue underbudgeting:** Legal review fees consistently underbudgeted. If actuals run at FY2025 levels, the city is leaving ~$23K in unbudgeted revenue.

## Relevance

Legal is directly relevant to César's legislative agenda. Rachel Gray's department is the gateway for preemption review on the junk fees ordinance, the housing production target resolution, and any future tenant protection or immigrant protection measures. The department's capacity and institutional knowledge directly affect the council's ability to move legislation. A senior departure in Legal is worth tracking -- a less experienced department could mean slower turnaround on preemption opinions.
