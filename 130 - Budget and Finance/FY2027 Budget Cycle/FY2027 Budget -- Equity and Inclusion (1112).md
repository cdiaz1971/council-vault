# FY2027 Budget -- Equity and Inclusion (1112)

## Summary

The Equity and Inclusion office is proposed at $252,277 for FY2027, a reduction of $101,948 (29%) from the FY2026 adopted budget of $354,225. This is a significant cut. At least one staff position has been eliminated or reclassified. The cut is happening in the same budget year that two new equity-adjacent offices are being created and expanded at a combined cost of over $1.3M. This pattern warrants a direct public question about the Mayor's equity priorities.

## Department Overview

The Equity and Inclusion office (formerly a larger function peaking at $330K in FY2025 actuals) houses the city's DEI infrastructure -- training, consultants, affinity group support, and the GARE membership. The 29% cut is driven almost entirely by a personnel reduction: productive time drops from $182,707 adopted to $106,121 requested, a reduction of $76,586. Benefits lines drop proportionally, confirming a position loss rather than a reclassification of hours.

## Key Budget Lines

**Salaries:** Productive time $106,121 (down from $182,707, -42%). This is the dominant driver of the overall cut. The FY2025 actuals were $153,352 and FY2024 actuals were $118,862, meaning the office has been staffed at a higher level in both prior years than what is proposed here.

**DEI consultants, speakers, and training ($60,000):** Held flat from FY2026. This is the programmatic spending line. It has been consistently unspent -- FY2026 actuals through the report date were only $410, and FY2025 actuals were $49,795 against a $60K budget.

**GARE membership:** Retained within the dues and registration fees line ($8,000 total). Government Alliance on Race and Equity membership is a meaningful commitment worth preserving.

**Affinity groups budget ($5,000):** New line within general supplies. This is a small but positive programmatic addition -- supporting employee affinity groups directly.

## The Equity Inversion Problem

In the same FY2027 budget:
- Equity and Inclusion (1112): cut $102K (-29%)
- Office of Opportunities and Transformation (1115): increased $284K (+54%), now $812K
- Office of Vendor Engagement and Compliance (1116): created from zero at $529K

The Mayor is cutting the office explicitly named for equity while standing up two new offices that claim equity-adjacent missions. Whether this is a strategic reorganization with a coherent theory of change, or equity work being diffused and de-prioritized under new branding, is a legitimate and important public question.

## Questions to Ask at Hearing

- What position was eliminated from this office and why?
- Is the work that position performed being absorbed elsewhere, or is it simply gone?
- How does the Mayor describe the relationship between this office's mission and the new Vendor Engagement and Opportunities offices?
- Why is the DEI training line consistently unspent ($410 in FY2026 actuals vs. $60K budget) and what is the plan to change that?

## Flags

**Position cut:** At least one FTE eliminated. The office is being run leaner in a year when equity infrastructure should arguably be growing.\
**Equity inversion:** The named equity office shrinks while equity-adjacent offices balloon. The council should name this pattern publicly.\
**Underutilized training line:** $60K budgeted for DEI consultants and training, $410 spent through most of FY2026. That is a structural problem with program execution, not just a budget number.

## Relevance

For César, this is both a values question and a political opportunity. Restoring the cut position in Equity and Inclusion -- offset by a reduction elsewhere in the Mayor's cluster -- is a concrete amendment that is easy to explain and defend publicly. The training utilization failure also deserves accountability: what is the plan to actually spend the $60K on programming that reaches staff?
