# FY2027 Budget -- Finance (1221)

**Department head:** Katie Barkdoll, Chief Financial Officer. Also oversees the Budget department (1223) and is the central staff voice on fiscal sustainability questions across the full budget cycle.

## Summary

The Finance department is proposed at $2,334,111 for FY2027, up $104,120 (4.7%) from the FY2026 adopted budget of $2,229,991. The increase is primarily salary-driven and largely routine. Four items cut across the routine: the credit card processing fee structure is regressive; internal audit is underinvested for a budget of this size; a new software subscription jumped the licenses line 66%; and the $37M fund balance draw is the single largest fiscal risk in the entire budget and requires a direct answer from CFO Barkdoll on the record.

## Department Overview

Finance covers accounting, payroll, treasury, debt management, accounts payable, audit coordination, and financial reporting. FY2026 actuals were $1,454,721 against a $2,230,000 adopted budget -- a $775K underrun, though much of this reflects timing of large payments rather than genuine savings. FY2025 actuals were $1,800,157.

## Key Budget Lines

**Salaries:** Full-time productive time $1,197,423 (up from $1,108,525, +8%). The department has grown staffing steadily -- FY2023 actuals were $594K in productive time, now proposed at $1.2M. Significant headcount growth over four years.

**Professional services ($429,150):** The largest non-salary line. Components:
- Audit fees: $125,000
- Banking services: $75,000
- Credit card processing fees: $125,000
- Other (actuarial, arbitrage, software): $54,150
- Internal audit services: $50,000

**Credit card processing fees ($125,000):** This cost is passed directly to residents as a surcharge when they pay utility bills, taxes, or fees by credit or debit card. Lower-income residents are statistically more likely to pay by card because they lack checking account access or use prepaid cards for financial management. Absorbing this cost into the operating budget and eliminating the surcharge would be a concrete equity intervention. The cost is already in this budget -- the city is paying it and then charging it back to residents on top.

**Internal audit services ($50,000):** For a city with a $160M+ General Fund budget plus multiple enterprise funds, $50,000 in internal audit coverage is thin. Internal audit is the primary institutional tool for catching fraud, waste, and program mismanagement before it becomes a public crisis. Comparable cities of Frederick's size typically budget $100-150K for this function. This is the one line in Finance where spending more is clearly justified.

**Capital asset software ($15,000 new recurring):** The licenses line jumped 66% from $9,895 to $29,400, driven by this new standalone capital asset tracking software. No prior-year actuals, no competitive procurement reference in the narrative. Needs confirmation of how this was procured.

## The $37M Fund Balance Draw

The FY2027 General Fund budget proposes drawing $36,987,995 from fund balance to balance. This is structured as four designated uses:
- Rainy Day Fund draw: $17,261,261
- CIP: $12,930,481
- Capital equipment: $5,462,147
- Rental Licensing Program: $1,334,106

Drawing from the Rainy Day Fund to cover operating and capital costs means recurring expenses are outpacing recurring revenues. This is not a one-year anomaly -- it is a structural trend. CFO Barkdoll should be asked at the hearing to provide the projected fund balance after this draw, the city's policy-defined reserve floor, and a multi-year sustainability analysis. If recurring revenue growth does not catch up to recurring expense growth, the FY2028 and FY2029 budgets will face harder choices with depleted reserves.

## Questions to Ask at Hearing

- Has the city evaluated eliminating credit card surcharges and absorbing processing costs as a cost of doing business?
- Is $50,000 for internal audit adequate given the city's budget size and enterprise fund complexity?
- What is the capital asset software, was it competitively bid, and what does it replace?
- What is the projected fund balance after the $37M draw, and what is the city's reserve policy floor?
- What is the multi-year fiscal sustainability picture -- does the FY2028 budget face a structural gap?

## Flags

**Regressive cost:** $125K in credit card processing fees passed to residents. Disproportionate burden on lower-income households who rely on card payments.\
**Underinvested:** $50K internal audit for a $160M+ budget is below comparable peer cities. Fraud and waste risk.\
**Structural risk:** The $37M fund balance draw is the budget's load-bearing problem. One-time money papering a recurring gap.\
**Procurement:** New $15K capital asset software with no visible competitive procurement trail.

## Relevance

The fund balance draw is the most consequential item in the entire April 8 hearing and arguably in the whole budget cycle. César should ask CFO Barkdoll this question directly, on the record, at the hearing -- not in a follow-up. The credit card fee question is a concrete equity ask that has a clear mechanism and a known cost ($125K). Increasing internal audit from $50K to $100-125K would be a defensible addition with a clear rationale.
