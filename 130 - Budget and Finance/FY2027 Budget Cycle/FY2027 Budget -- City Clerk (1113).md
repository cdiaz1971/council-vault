# FY2027 Budget -- City Clerk (1113)

## Summary

The City Clerk's office is proposed at $470,183 for FY2027, up $100,059 (27%) from the FY2026 adopted budget of $370,124. The increase is driven by two legitimate one-time projects (charter re-codification and historical document scanning) and a new recurring commission participation stipend program. The stipend is an equity win worth supporting. The Granicus software cost warrants a competitive procurement review.

## Department Overview

The City Clerk manages legislative records, board and commission administration, public records, and meeting management infrastructure. FY2026 actuals were $256,698 against a $370K adopted budget -- significant unspent appropriation. FY2025 actuals were $304,261.

## Key Budget Lines

**Salaries:** Productive time $192,183 (up from $177,252, +8.4%). Includes an overtime increase from $500 to $3,200, likely tied to election and meeting workload.

**One-time professional services ($55,000 new):**
- Re-codification of City Charter and Code: $30,000. The City Code should be re-codified periodically for legal clarity and public accessibility. This is overdue and defensible.
- Scanning historical ordinance books and minutes: $15,000. Preservation of historical legislative record. Appropriate.
- Commission participation stipend: $10,000 recurring. This is the most significant new policy item in this budget.

**Commission participation stipend ($10,000 recurring):** A new program to pay citizens for serving on boards and commissions. This is a direct equity intervention. People who cannot afford to lose work hours for unpaid civic service are structurally excluded from participatory governance. A stipend program directly addresses that barrier. Small dollar amount, high policy significance. Worth defending and potentially expanding in future years.

**Granicus software ($45,000 recurring):** The city's legislative management and public-facing agenda platform. $45K/yr is a substantial recurring cost. This platform has near-monopoly pricing in the municipal market. The question is when it was last put to competitive bid -- alternatives like CivicPlus (which the city already uses for Municode) and others may offer lower cost.

**Docusign ($42,500 recurring):** Electronic signature platform. Costs have climbed steadily -- FY2023 actuals were $18,000, now at $42,500. Worth asking whether the city is on a scalable enterprise contract or paying per-seat at higher rates.

## Questions to Ask at Hearing

- What are the eligibility criteria and payment structure for the commission participation stipend?
- When was Granicus last put to competitive bid and have alternatives been evaluated?
- Has the city reviewed its Docusign contract structure given the significant cost growth?

## Flags

**Equity win:** Commission participation stipend is a meaningful access intervention. Support it and ask about eligibility to ensure it reaches lower-income residents.\
**Vendor lock:** Granicus at $45K/yr with no visible competitive review history is a soft target for cost reduction.\
**Docusign cost growth:** $18K in FY2023 to $42.5K proposed -- 136% increase over three years. Needs contract review.

## Relevance

The commission stipend is directly aligned with César's citizen participation priority. If resources are available later in the budget cycle, expanding the stipend amount or eligibility scope is worth considering. The Granicus and Docusign cost reviews could generate savings that offset additions elsewhere. See [[FY2027 Budget -- April 8 Hearing Index]].
