## Structural Context (applies to all hearings)

**General Fund proposed:** $160,590,483 vs. $147,832,296 adopted FY2026 (+$12.8M / +8.6%)

**Fund balance draw:** $36,987,995 drawn from reserves to balance the budget. Pools: Rainy Day Fund ($17.3M), CIP ($12.9M), capital equipment ($5.5M), Rental Licensing Program ($1.3M). This is the budget's central structural problem -- recurring expenses outpacing recurring revenues, papered over with one-time money. Track CFO Katie Barkdoll's responses across hearings on this.

**Revenue drivers:** Real estate tax growth +$3.4M (assessments, not rate increase), personal income tax +$3.1M, investment interest +$2.4M.

**Budget manager:** Lacey Gordon, Manager of Budget. CFO: Katie Barkdoll.

## Key Themes to Track Across the Cycle

**Equity inversion in the Mayor's cluster:** Equity and Inclusion (1112) cut 29% while Office of Opportunities and Transformation (+54%) and Office of Vendor Engagement and Compliance (new, $529K) both expand. Watch whether this pattern repeats in other clusters.

**New office accountability:** OOT and OVEC are growing fast with no performance metrics in the budget. Track whether any department heads present outcome data at hearings.

**Regressive cost opportunities:** Credit card processing fees ($125K, Finance) passed to residents. Worth identifying similar cost-shifting patterns in other funds.

**Internal audit coverage:** $50K for internal audit on a $160M+ budget is thin. Flag if similar underinvestment appears elsewhere.

**Disparity study follow-through:** OVEC is funding a $300K disparity study. Track whether the council can extract a binding commitment to procurement reform before approving it.

**Commission participation stipend (City Clerk, $10K):** New recurring program paying citizens for board/commission service. A model worth expanding. Watch whether similar access interventions appear in other departments.
