# FY2027 Budget -- Office of Opportunities and Transformation (1115)

## Summary

The Office of Opportunities and Transformation is proposed at $812,441 for FY2027, up $283,655 (54%) from the FY2026 adopted budget of $528,786. This office is new -- FY2024 actuals were zero, FY2025 actuals were $132,958. It is growing extremely fast with no performance data or outcome metrics visible in the budget document. The workforce programming is directionally defensible but the office structure, the VR equipment purchase, and the misplaced bus lane maintenance line all warrant challenge.

## Department Overview

The office was stood up in FY2025 and runs youth workforce and employment programming under the Mayor. It did not exist in FY2024. By FY2027 it is proposed at $812K -- a nearly 6x increase from its first year of actuals. Staff salaries more than double from $190K adopted to $387K requested, adding FTEs. The part-time position from FY2026 ($25K) is eliminated, suggesting the work is being absorbed into new full-time hires.

## Key Budget Lines

**Salaries:** Productive time $386,919 (up from $190,554 adopted, +103%). Significant staff expansion in a two-year-old office with no documented outcomes.

**Professional services ($219,000):** Five vendors --
- Frederick County Workforce Services summer job program: $45,000
- Mayor's Fellowship Program: $44,000
- Youth Apprenticeship Stipends: $30,000
- Youth Employment Services: $50,000
- Workforce Services (Flying Cows): $50,000

The youth employment and apprenticeship programs are the strongest lines in this office. They are the direct public service. The fellowship and Flying Cows contracts are less transparent.

**VR Goggles and Subscription ($10,000 one-time):** Listed as a one-time general supplies purchase for a workforce development office. No explanation of how VR equipment connects to the office mission in a city where 43% of households fall below the ALICE threshold.

**Scotty's Bus Lane maintenance ($42,000 recurring):** A Public Works-type expenditure sitting inside a workforce opportunity office budget. No narrative explains why this belongs here rather than in DPW or Streets.

**BGov bill tracking software ($6,000):** Legislative bill tracking software in a workforce office is also anomalous -- this belongs in the Mayor's political office or the Council office.

## Questions to Ask at Hearing

- What measurable outcomes has this office produced in FY2025 and FY2026? Number of youth placed, wages earned, retention rates?
- What does the VR equipment do specifically in the context of workforce development?
- Why is Scotty's Bus Lane maintenance in this office's budget rather than Public Works?
- What does the Flying Cows contract deliver and how is it evaluated?

## Flags

**No accountability:** Office grew from $0 (FY2024) to $812K (FY2027 proposed) with no performance data in the budget.\
**Questionable priority:** VR equipment purchase signals poor priority-setting in a high-poverty service environment.\
**Misaligned lines:** Bus lane maintenance and legislative bill tracking software do not belong in a workforce office budget.

## Relevance

The workforce programs themselves align with César's priorities -- youth employment and apprenticeships in an ALICE-majority city are exactly the right investments. The accountability gap is the problem. Conditioning full funding on submission of a Year 1/Year 2 outcomes report is a reasonable council ask. Misaligned line items (bus lane, BGov) should be moved or eliminated. See [[Budget Brief 2026-04-08|FY2027 Budget -- April 8 Hearing Index]] for full hearing context.
