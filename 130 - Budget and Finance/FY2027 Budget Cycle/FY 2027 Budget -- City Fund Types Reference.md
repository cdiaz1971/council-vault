# FY 2027 Budget -- City Fund Types Reference

## Summary

The City of Frederick organizes its budget into three fund categories: Governmental, Enterprise, and Special Revenue. Understanding which fund a program or department sits in determines how it is financed, who controls appropriation, and what levers the council has to act on it.

## Key Points

### Governmental Funds

Tax-supported activities. This is the core of the municipal budget and where council appropriation authority is most direct. FY 2027 governmental funds include:

**General Fund** -- The main operating budget. Covers most city departments: Police, Public Works, Parks and Recreation, Planning, Housing and Human Services, General Government, and Economic Development. The largest single fund. All council priority add-ons and amendments are negotiated here.

**Weinberg Center for the Arts** -- Separately tracked from the General Fund but still tax-supported. Treated as its own governmental fund because of its distinct operational character.

**City Housing Fund** -- Exists in law as a dedicated fund for housing purposes, but has historically been thinly capitalized. It sits in the governmental bucket, meaning its funding level is a direct council appropriation decision -- not fee-driven or legally restricted. The Mayor's FY 2027 materials do not call out a specific appropriation to the City Housing Fund, making the April 9 Housing and Human Services session the right moment to press on what, if anything, is proposed for it.

### Enterprise Funds

Business-type activities financed primarily through fees for service rather than taxes. The FY 2027 budget proposes all enterprise funds be self-supporting -- meaning no General Fund subsidy. If an enterprise fund runs a deficit, it is expected to cover it through its own rates and fees. The council has less direct discretionary leverage here than in governmental funds. FY 2027 enterprise funds:

- Water and Sewer
- Airport (Frederick Municipal Airport)
- Parking
- Stormwater
- Golf Course
- Rental Operations

### Special Revenue Funds

Resources held for a specific, legally or regulatorily defined purpose -- typically restricted by law and not available for general appropriation. The council cannot redirect these funds to other uses. FY 2027 special revenue funds:

- Community Development
- Community Health Center
- Controlled Dangerous Substance Fund
- Opioid Fund

## Relevance

The fund type determines council leverage. Governmental funds are where budget negotiations actually happen -- the General Fund and City Housing Fund are both in this bucket, so housing investment decisions are fully within council authority to contest. Enterprise funds are essentially off the table for discretionary reallocation. Special revenue funds are legally restricted and not available for other uses.

When the Mayor cites fiscal constraints as a reason to resist council priorities, he is primarily defending the General Fund. The City Housing Fund's thin capitalization is a policy choice, not a structural inevitability -- it is a governmental fund and can be appropriated to. That is the argument to make on April 9.
