# Maryland State Property Tax Rate Held at 11.2 Cents -- FY2027

## Summary

Maryland's Board of Public Works voted unanimously to keep the state property tax rate flat at 11.2 cents per $100 of assessed value -- unchanged since 2007. Despite the flat rate, homeowners will likely pay more because residential assessments increased by an average of 13% in the most recent cycle, continuing eight consecutive years of rising assessments.

## Key Points

The rate covers commercial and residential properties. Utility rate remains at 28 cents per $100. The current rate has been unchanged since 2007.

Despite the flat rate, almost 93% of the 789,000+ residential properties reviewed in the December reassessment cycle saw increased values. Average residential assessment increase was 13% this year, down from 20.1% last year and 23.4% in 2023. Increases are phased in over three years, which softens the immediate impact but ensures sustained upward pressure on bills.

The revenue from the state property tax does not cover the state's full debt service burden. In FY2027, the state expects to collect nearly $1.2 billion in property taxes, approximately $320 million short of its debt service obligations. Shortfalls are covered by general fund revenues and are projected to grow to $362-416 million by 2030-2031.

The Board also approved a $615,000 construction contract for a monument on the State House grounds honoring Black Marylanders who fought in the Revolutionary War.

## Relevance

The state-local fiscal relationship matters for Frederick's budget picture. State property tax policy shapes the overall tax burden residents feel, which affects the political headroom for any municipal-level tax or fee conversations. The structural shortfall in state debt service is worth tracking as Maryland's fiscal position tightens under federal funding reductions.
