# Blueprint Costs Exceed Dedicated Revenue in FY2028

## Summary
FY2028 is the first year Blueprint for Maryland's Future costs outrun dedicated revenue, inside a projected $3 billion state gap of which about $1.8 billion is education, making the Blueprint a likely 2027 session target while its oversight board proposes additions.

## Key Points
MACo's figures put the FY2028 state shortfall at about $3 billion, roughly $1.8 billion tied to the Blueprint. The gap will have to come from the general fund, because the digital ad tax meant to help pay for the plan is tied up in litigation. Budget officials have already asked agencies to model a 10 percent cut.

The Accountability and Implementation Board is moving the other direction on some items. Staff recommendations reviewed September 17 include making automatic Tier I prekindergarten eligibility for multilingual learners and students with disabilities permanent (it lapsed after FY2026), easing certification for private pre-K providers, more National Board Certification pathways, more college and career readiness funding, and a study on raising base per-pupil funding. Several carry costs. The board may vote October 15.

Moore praised the Blueprint on the campaign trail while saying it needs to be "effective but also scalable." Sen. Carozza (R) plans legislation to let districts shift money between Blueprint pillars, for example from CTE to pre-K. Worcester County's superintendent openly opposes the automatic $10,000 National Board salary increase.

## Relevance
A state structural gap of this size tends to land on counties and municipalities through reduced aid and shifted costs, so it belongs in the background for the FY2028 city budget cycle that SPARK is feeding. The pre-K eligibility piece also touches the childcare and affordability thread: pre-K access for multilingual families is part of the same care-infrastructure argument in [[Childcare and the Care Economy]].
