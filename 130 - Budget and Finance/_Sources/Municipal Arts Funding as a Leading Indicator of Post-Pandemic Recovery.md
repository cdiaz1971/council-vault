# Municipal Arts Funding as a Leading Indicator of Post-Pandemic Recovery

## Summary
A study of more than 4,400 nonprofit arts organizations across Atlanta, Cleveland, Des Moines, Houston, Los Angeles, New York City, Philadelphia, Phoenix, Sacramento, and Seattle found that cities with sustained municipal arts investment had measurably healthier cultural sectors by 2024, even though public dollars make up only a small share of any individual organization's revenue.

## Key Points
The defining finding is divergence. Average arts sector revenue varied 117 percent more across the ten cities in 2024 than it had five years earlier, and six of the ten cities saw sector-wide revenue decline by more than 20 percent. Cleveland, with the highest per-capita local arts funding in the group, saw strong revenue growth. Phoenix started from a lower funding base but posted the largest increase in per-capita public arts funding and made major gains in operating surplus. Philadelphia saw the steepest decline in per-capita local arts funding and the largest workforce cuts of any city studied.

The divergence also ran along three internal fault lines within cities. Smaller organizations were the most agile, able to scale down and back up as pressures shifted, while mid-sized organizations were hit hardest because they carry high fixed staffing costs without the reserve buffer that larger organizations have. Performing arts organizations rebuilt their audiences but their finances still lag, with operating surplus nearly vanishing by 2024, while visual arts organizations held revenue better but saw audiences return more slowly. Organizations in downtown cores saw steeper declines than those elsewhere in their cities, tracking the broader falloff in weekday foot traffic from remote work.

## Relevance
This reframes municipal arts funding as an investment with a measurable return in sector stability rather than a discretionary line item, which is directly useful language for the priority-driven budgeting work with VP Nash. It is also relevant background for the Art in City Hall program mentioned in the State of the City speech, since the study's core claim is that consistent public support, not the size of the check, is what determines whether a city's cultural organizations come out of a downturn intact.
