# Maryland Is One of Nine States Without Local Sales Tax Authority

## Summary
Maryland is one of nine states that does not authorize any local sales tax, and a legislative panel is now studying alternative local revenue tools such as restaurant taxes and land use tax changes as counties look beyond property and income tax.

## Key Points
Local governments in Maryland rely almost entirely on property and local income tax, with no authority to levy a local sales tax. Part of the structural reason is that Maryland does not collect sales tax by point of origin, making it hard to even determine how much sales tax revenue originates in any given jurisdiction. A legislative panel is currently studying alternative local revenue tools, including local restaurant taxes and changes to land use taxes, along with the idea of a local component added to the state sales tax. Baltimore's Mayor floated a 2 percent city sales tax increase last year, which stalled. It is unclear whether the panel will produce recommendations before the General Assembly reconvenes in January, and expanding local taxing authority runs into the state's own interest in preserving its sales tax base.

## Relevance
Directly relevant to any future conversation about diversifying Frederick's revenue base beyond property tax, particularly if the WAVE Fines Act and other utility side relief measures increase pressure on the general fund. Worth flagging if a local revenue tools bill emerges from this panel in the 2027 session.
