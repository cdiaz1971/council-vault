### Real Rent Ordinance
**Status:** Active -- may be rolled into broader tenant protection package
July 9 council workshop completed. Public input surfaced two concerns: (1) implementation timeline, and (2) potential disproportionate impact on small landlords compared to large ones. Real Rent Ordinance may be rolled into a broader tenant protection package rather than advanced as a standalone ordinance. Next action: determine scope and structure of tenant protection package.

**Rockville Chapter 18 as comparative model (added 2026-07-27):** Ryan Trout, Director of Housing for the City of Rockville and a Frederick resident, shared Rockville's comprehensive Chapter 18 rewrite (Rental Facilities and Landlord-Tenant Relations, effective January 1, 2027). It is the most directly relevant peer model available for scoping a broader Frederick tenant protection package. Key provisions worth evaluating for Frederick:

- **Algorithmic rent pricing and price collusion ban** -- prohibits use of algorithmic pricing tools to set or coordinate rents. Politically easier to defend than rent stabilization because it targets market manipulation rather than price setting. Not currently in the Real Rent Ordinance draft.
- **Fee restrictions (enumerated list) and fee transparency** -- full disclosure required in advertising, prior to lease signing, and in the lease agreement. Directly overlaps with the Real Rent Ordinance's core fee provisions. Rockville's enumerated list provides a peer citation for Frederick's approach.
- **Repair and deduct** -- allows tenants to make repairs at their own cost and deduct up to two months' rent when a landlord fails to act within a defined window (24-28 hours for emergencies, 15-30 days for other repairs). Not in Frederick's current code. Operates through contract remediation rather than rent control; lower preemption risk.
- **Two-year lease term requirement** -- landlord must offer a two-year term at initial signing and renewal. Framed as a mutual benefit: tenant stability and lower landlord vacancy risk.
- **Relocation assistance (temporary and permanent)** -- most ambitious and most contested provision. Permanent assistance triggered by demolition, redevelopment, substantial renovation, license revocation, or displacement exceeding six months. Payment includes security deposit return, prorated rent, three months' fair market rent equivalent, moving costs, and supplements for elderly, disabled, or dependent households. Strongest likely landlord pushback but protects lowest-income tenants facing worst outcomes.
- **Eviction history ban** on rental applications, with landlords retaining access to public records. Keeps screening focused on ability to pay rather than past financial hardship.
- **Tenant organizing protections** -- prohibits landlords from charging tenant organizations fees for use of meeting rooms or common areas.

Rockville's rollout is happening in real time and can be cited as a live peer implementation. A phased Frederick approach could lead with fee restrictions and the algorithmic pricing ban -- provisions least burdensome to small landlords -- and phase in relocation assistance and repair and deduct with longer lead times, directly addressing the two concerns surfaced at the July 9 workshop.

### ADU Ordinance (PC25-614ZTA)
**Status:** Active -- discussion item at August 13 work session, no vote scheduled
Eliminates the owner-occupancy requirement in LMC Section 802, reduces parking (transit-proximity exemption, smaller stall dimensions), and raises the detached ADU cap from 800 to 1,200 SF via Planning Commission modification. Zero detached ADUs permitted since the 2021 amendment -- the core production rationale. Planning Commission voted 4-0 in favor on March 17, 2026, but with conditions the draft does not adopt: retain owner-occupancy, add a DB/DR/DBO parking exemption, and consider a deed restriction if occupancy is eliminated anyway. Owner-occupancy elimination matches [[LMC Zoning Amendments -- Personal Strategy Brief (2026-05-13)]] Track 1, Amendment Three.

Public comment on the item is split three ways: a supportive account from an existing ADU owner; a critical letter from Citizens for Responsible Growth (Marjorie Rosensweig) arguing the size increase and occupancy removal favor absentee investors over Section 802's affordability purpose; and a 15-year ALICE-income rent-and-inspection covenant proposal from Kevin Sellner. Sellner is a CRG member and resident of Brehm's District 3, not an ally César typically considers a good-faith actor on housing.

Developed position going into the work session: defend the draft's owner-occupancy elimination, countering the Planning Commission's community-character rationale with a Portland case study (owner-occupancy requirement there produced fewer owner-occupants, 35% vs. 90% citywide, and speculative investment did not materialize without it) and California data showing no disproportionate corporate ownership share among ADU-producing properties. Reject Sellner's covenant proposal on the grounds that it carries no offsetting incentive (unlike LIHTC, MPDU bonus density, or PILOT abatement) and imposes an inspection burden roughly 6-7x Chapter 12.5's citywide standard; the empirical record on uncompensated mandates (San Francisco rent control and inclusionary zoning, Seattle's voluntary bonus program) shows this design suppresses production rather than increasing it. Maryland HB 1466 (effective October 1, 2025, local compliance deadline October 1, 2026) prohibits deed restrictions that unreasonably limit ADU development -- not cited anywhere in the packet, worth confirming with Rachel Gray whether this is why the draft strikes the existing deed-restriction requirement, and whether it also forecloses both the Commission's suggested deed-restriction fallback and Sellner's covenant.

Full analysis, sourced counters to both letters, and drafted talking points in [[ADU Ordinance Brief 2026-08-13]] and [[ADU Talking Points 2026-08-13]]. Next action: deliver position at the August 13 work session; follow up with Rachel Gray on HB 1466 exposure.

## Completed

### Resolution 26-15, Affordable Housing Action
**Completed:** 2026-10-01. Passed 6-0 as written, with no floor amendments. Formerly the Housing Production Target Resolution, rewritten July 20 as the Resolution on Affordable Housing Action. Adopts the Mayor's Affordable Housing Task Force report (July 2026) and the TPMA Housing Data Report (February 2026) as the City's evidence base, declares housing essential infrastructure and a human right, adopts the task force's goal of 4,000 additional units over ten years, and lists nine priority recommendations (annual housing priorities process, Affordable Housing Navigator and implementation team, tiered AMI-based APFO exemptions, MPDU threshold 25 to 10 with fee revisions, 8 to 10 month expedited review, financing tools, City land disposition, missing-middle and ADU zoning, a production dashboard). Adds an anti-displacement section, commits resources to a Frederick County AMI standard, requires an HHS annual Housing Production Report starting FY2028, and calls on the County to set comparable goals. Codifying the targets and report is Item 2 of [[Frederick Affordability Agenda 2026-2029]]. Full analysis in [[Hearing Brief 2026-10-01]].

### Maryland Housing Certainty Act Implementation Ordinance (G-26-11)
**Completed:** 2026-09-17. Passed 5-0 at the Regular Public Meeting. Interim, uncodified ordinance introduced September 3 through City Attorney Rachel Gray, aligning City review and approval processes with HB548/SB325 ahead of the Act's October 1, 2026 effective date: defines "complete application" and requires a 30-day completeness determination, vests development rights under the law in effect at complete-application submission for 5 years (phased projects treated as separate projects), and restricts impact-fee collection to no earlier than 30 days before final inspection. Fiscal impact is a timing shift only, not a revenue loss. Full analysis in [[Hearing Brief 2026-09-17]]. Unresolved risk carried into the vote: interaction with the LMC review moratorium question, see the updated LMC Review Commission entry above.

### Mixed Use Vertical LMC Amendment (G-26-10)
**Completed:** 2026-08-20. Passed 6-0, no public comment, confirmed against official minutes in [[Hearing Minutes 2026-08-20]]. Effective date August 30, 2026. Amends LMC Section 604 (Building and Urban Design Standards), Section 838 (Mixed Use Development, Vertical), and Section 1002 (Definitions) to allow residential uses on the first floor of mixed-use buildings in MU-1 and MU-2 zones.
