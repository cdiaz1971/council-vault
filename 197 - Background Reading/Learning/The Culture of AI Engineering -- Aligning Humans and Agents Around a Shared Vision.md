# The Culture of AI Engineering -- Aligning Humans and Agents Around a Shared Vision

The core argument: AI engineering is not primarily a technical discipline -- it is a cultural one. The real challenge is building shared understanding of what you are trying to accomplish across humans and AI agents operating in the same environment. Without that alignment, technical capability goes in multiple directions at once and compounds rather than compounds productively.

This is paywalled content; the substantive argument is drawn from the teaser. The framing maps onto a broader pattern: every organization that tries to adopt AI at scale runs into the same problem -- the technology is ready before the people and structures around it are.

## Why this matters for César

This is the theoretical counterpart to [[Beyond Tech -- Four Keys to Successful Government Modernization]] and [[Charlotte NC Requires AI and Cloud Certifications for IT Staff]]. All three point at the same underlying problem from different angles: technology adoption fails not because the tools don't work but because the organizational and cultural infrastructure for using them well is absent. For Frederick, this matters when thinking about how AI gets deployed in city services -- the question is never just "can the tool do this" but "are the people and workflows around it aligned well enough to produce good outcomes."
