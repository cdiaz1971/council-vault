# Federal Teen Pregnancy Prevention Grant Cancellations Hit Maryland

## Summary

The Trump administration abruptly terminated $3M in teen pregnancy prevention grants to Maryland mid-program, cutting off Baltimore City Health Department and six rural counties without notice, as part of a broader $68M national termination framed as ideological.

## Key Points

Maryland lost two grants: $1.4M to the Maryland Department of Health (supporting six rural counties in partnership with Johns Hopkins University Center for Adolescent Health) and $1.8M to the Baltimore City Health Department. Both were canceled on June 26 with no advance notice, three years into five-year grant cycles.

Baltimore's funding would have supported comprehensive teen pregnancy prevention education for 11,500 youth and 11,500 adults across classrooms, clinics, and communities. The state funding supported evidence-based programs, Youth Advisory Boards, and professional development for rural health and education systems.

The administration's stated justification was that 53 of 67 programs were "normalizing sexual activities for teens." HHS did not provide specific evidence for Maryland's or Baltimore's programs. A federal court previously struck down a similar 2025 policy change. A coalition including county governments and progressive organizations filed suit in July arguing the termination violates the program's statutory purpose.

Teen birth rates in Maryland have declined substantially -- from 17.8 per 1,000 in 2014 to 10.9 per 1,000 in 2024, below the national average. Public health researchers credit comprehensive sex education as a significant contributor.

The Maryland Family Institute welcomed the terminations, calling the programs "indecent" and "immoral."

## Relevance

This is part of a pattern of abrupt federal grant terminations affecting Maryland jurisdictions and local public health infrastructure -- the same pattern as SNAP cuts, HUD homelessness grants, and other federal funding withdrawals. The mechanism is the same: mid-program cancellation without notice, no appeal process, politically motivated framing as justification.

For Frederick specifically: if MDH's six rural counties include Frederick County or nearby jurisdictions, there may be downstream effects on county health department partners that serve Frederick residents. Worth checking with Stuart Campbell (HHS) whether Frederick-area programs were affected.

The lawsuit framing -- that terminations violate the statutory purpose Congress set in 2009 -- is the same legal theory being used in other federal funding fights and is worth tracking as a template.
