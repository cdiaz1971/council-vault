# Philadelphia Philly Stat 360 -- Data-Driven Neighborhood Revitalization

## Summary

Philadelphia's Philly Stat 360 program, led by Kristin Bray under Mayor Parker, is using public data dashboards to drive accountability for the city's revitalization of Kensington -- a neighborhood with entrenched poverty, an open-air drug market, and inadequate housing and services. The approach makes the city's pain points visible to the public in real time, using Esri's ArcGIS platform.

## Key Points

Kensington has been historically underinvested, with high poverty, limited employment, and poor housing conditions. Mayor Parker is treating its revitalization as a signature priority. To track progress and hold agencies accountable, the city launched a dashboard suite that:

- Maps where city efforts are working and where gaps remain
- Includes contact information so residents can provide direct input
- Shows data on services, complaints, and agency activity in a single view

The transparency rationale is straightforward: by making the city's problems visible publicly, it creates accountability pressure that keeps officials honest and lets residents engage meaningfully. Bray specifically tied the transparency to trust-building.

The platform is built on Esri ArcGIS, which Frederick already uses. That's a practical detail worth noting.

## Why This Matters for César

This is a peer-city model for how a city can use existing GIS tools to drive equity-focused accountability. The Kensington dashboard is a real-world example of what [[Municipal Technology Strategy]] could look like applied to Frederick's own equity challenges -- whether that's the Golden Mile, housing conditions, or LMC implementation tracking. The framing of "transparency builds accountability" is directly usable in public-facing communications about data and governance.
