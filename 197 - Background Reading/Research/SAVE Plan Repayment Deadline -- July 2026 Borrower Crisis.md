## Summary

With July 1 approaching, the Debt Collective's publication *In the Red* reports that roughly 7 million borrowers currently enrolled in the SAVE income-driven repayment plan will soon receive 90-day notices requiring them to choose a new repayment plan. For many borrowers, switching plans would double or triple monthly payments. The situation is compounded by conflicting and alarming information from the Trump administration's Department of Education, and by errors by student loan servicers including MOHELA and Nelnet that are preventing borrowers from receiving entitled relief.

## Key Points

- SAVE plan dismantlement timeline: July 1 notices trigger a 90-day window to select a new plan
- Switching plans could double or triple monthly payments for millions of borrowers
- Servicers (MOHELA, Nelnet) are making account errors preventing appropriate relief
- Trump DOE communications described as "alarming and conflicting"
- Debt Collective profiled Joseph Strafaci, PhD, whose Reddit post about navigating the system drew wide attention, framing the student loan system as "a dumpster fire by design"
- Debt Collective is soliciting borrower experiences via survey (BIT.LY/SAVEPlan26) to pressure lawmakers for a payment pause

## Relevance for Frederick

This is primarily a federal policy matter, but it has direct relevance to Frederick constituents. The SAVE plan termination is the single largest bill increase most working families will see in 2026, falling disproportionately on lower-income households and people of color. The Debt Collective framing -- a "dumpster fire by design" -- aligns with the structural affordability argument running through the council housing and budget tracks. The July 1 deadline and 90-day window make this time-sensitive for any constituent outreach or public statement opportunities. It also connects to the immigrant community, where DACA recipients and mixed-status families often carry federal student debt without access to full federal protections.
