# HR 2616 Don't Say Trans Bill -- Federal Threat to School Inclusivity

H.R. 2616, sometimes called the "Don't Say Trans" bill, would deny federal education funds to any school that acknowledges transgender people exist -- whether in a class lesson, a library book, or by simply using a student's preferred name and pronouns. The bill also mandates forced outing: schools would be required to notify parents if a student identifies as transgender, regardless of the student's safety or wishes.

The House Education Committee advanced the bill in late April 2026. A floor vote is expected within weeks.

## Mechanism

The funding threat functions like a compliance lever. Schools that lose federal Title I or Title IV dollars face cascading budget consequences. The effect would be to make trans-inclusive practice financially untenable in most public school districts, even where state law does not require exclusion.

## Relationship to other legislation

H.R. 2616 is part of a coordinated federal package alongside bathroom bans and trans athlete restrictions. It expands the logic of Florida's HB 1557 (the original "Don't Say Gay" law) to apply nationally and to trans identity specifically.

## Why this matters for César

Frederick City schools operate under Frederick County Board of Education jurisdiction, so direct municipal authority is limited. But the council's [[City Values Act]] and constituent relationships with immigrant and LGBTQ+ families mean the political and human stakes are real. If H.R. 2616 passes, expect local families and advocacy partners like [[Centro Hispano de Frederick]] to look to city leadership for a response.
