# AI-Generated CSAM in Schools -- How Deepfakes Tore Radnor High School Apart

## Summary

A Pennsylvania high school student used a paid app to generate sexually explicit deepfakes of five female classmates -- and despite state law criminalizing malicious deepfakes since 2024, Radnor's administration failed the victims, raising unresolved questions about Title IX liability for off-campus AI-generated abuse.

## Key Points

A freshman at Radnor Township High School (ranked among Pennsylvania's top schools, ~1,000 students) paid $250 for a subscription to an app called Movely, obtained from the Apple App Store, and used it to superimpose five female classmates' faces onto nude bodies. He created the content outside school hours on his personal device, but sent messages about it on a school-issued device and discussed it with friends on Snapchat.

Pennsylvania criminalized malicious deepfakes in 2024. Despite this, school administrators' initial response was to offer victims the option to leave class early and eat lunch alone -- isolating the harmed students rather than the perpetrator. Governor Josh Shapiro put the school's response in the spotlight. Parents organized at school board meetings and worked with state representatives.

The central unresolved policy question: does what happens on students' personal devices off-campus fall within a school's Title IX obligations, when the relationship (and thus the harm) exists entirely because of the school community? One victim's parent framed it cleanly: "My daughter would not know this other boy if they were not in school together."

The technology angle: the app was commercially available on the App Store. The $250 price point and subscription model indicate this is a developed consumer market, not a fringe technical capability.

## Relevance

This is a direct AI governance and youth safety issue that touches council work. Frederick City Council has no direct jurisdiction over schools (FCPS is county), but AI harm in schools is an emerging policy space where city values leadership and public statement are appropriate. The Title IX off-campus question will be litigated nationally over the next several years -- staying current matters. Also relevant to any conversations about AI policy at the state level or through NLC/municipal networks.

The app store distribution question -- Apple selling a product that enables CSAM generation -- is a platform accountability angle that connects to broader AI governance debates.
