# Hispanic Serving Institution Designation Under Federal Defunding Pressure

## Summary

Montgomery College -- Maryland's only HSI -- lost access to $350M in federal HSI grants after the Trump administration ended funding for minority-serving institutions, while internal task force recommendations for improving Latino student outcomes remain only partially implemented.

## Key Points

Montgomery College earned the federal HSI designation in 2021, when Latino enrollment reached 25%. Today nearly 30% of students identify as Latino -- the school's largest minority. The U.S. Department of Education deleted the HSI webpage in 2025 and ended $350M in grants, claiming the funds were discriminatory and unconstitutional. Montgomery College had two grants in the pipeline that were cut off.

The college's 2024 HSI Task Force report called for disaggregated data-driven learning strategies, recruitment of Latino faculty and staff, multilingual supports, and regular community accountability. One co-author says they have received little feedback on implementation since the report was delivered. The college's formal strategic planning documents -- the Academic Master Plan, Strategic Plan, Student Enrollment Plan -- contain no mention of "Hispanic Serving Institution," "Latino," or "Hispanic."

The student outcome data is striking: 49.1% of Hispanic students received a D, F, or withdrew from Biology 150 in spring 2025 -- an entry-level course for healthcare career pathways. That is a structural equity failure in one of the highest-value credential pathways for immigrant and first-generation students.

Undocumented students are navigating a climate of heightened fear. One student described being stopped from distributing know-your-rights cards on campus.

## Relevance

Montgomery College's experience is a bellwether for what Maryland's broader Latino higher-education pipeline looks like under federal defunding pressure. For Frederick specifically: Frederick Community College serves a significant Latino population in a county with 1.4x the county average in foreign-born residents. If FCC does not hold HSI designation, the question of what supports are in place for Latino students is worth asking.

The federal defunding argument -- that race-conscious grants are unconstitutional -- is the same legal theory being applied across federal equity programs. This is the higher education front of the same fight the City Values Act addresses in local government services.

The Task Force's public accountability gap (report delivered, implementation invisible) is a governance pattern worth naming: equity plans that lack public reporting mechanisms become invisible. César can apply that lesson to any equity-related initiative at the city level.
