# Illinois School Cell Phone Ban -- 2026 State Legislative Model

## Summary

Illinois is poised to join a majority of states restricting school cell phone use; the bill bans phones in elementary and middle schools all day and gives high schools discretion during instruction time, with equity protections against punitive discipline and exceptions for medical, translation, and family care needs.

## Key Points

Governor Pritzker-backed legislation passed the House in spring 2026 and appears headed for Senate approval. The bill would take effect in 2027. A prior version passed the Senate unanimously in 2025 but failed in the House due to concerns about implementation costs and punitive discipline.

The revised version addresses those concerns: schools cannot suspend or expel students over phone infractions. Exceptions are built in for students with medical needs, English learners using phones for translation, and students who care for family members.

Implementation concerns remain. The Illinois Federation of Teachers raised cost and logistics objections, citing Peoria's $250,000 expenditure on phone pouches. The union also noted that some immigrant families rely on student phones for contact during stepped-up immigration enforcement near schools. The House sponsor countered that low- and no-cost options exist (disabling apps, existing lockers).

RAND Corporation research cited in the article found a cell phone ban in a large Florida district led to test score and attendance improvements, particularly in middle and high school grades.

## Relevance

Low direct relevance to Frederick's active policy tracks. Noted as a peer-city state legislative model in case Frederick Public Schools or Maryland legislators take up similar legislation. The equity and discipline protections in the Illinois bill (no suspensions for phone infractions, exceptions for immigrant families) are worth flagging if this issue ever comes to council.
