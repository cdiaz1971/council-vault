# H.R. 1 -- Federal Benefit Program Changes and State Administrative Burden

## Summary

H.R. 1 imposes sweeping new requirements on state agencies administering SNAP, Medicaid, and other federal benefit programs. States must upgrade IT systems, tighten identity verification, expand work reporting requirements, and absorb new administrative costs -- all under compressed timelines and with reduced federal support in some areas.

## Key Points

H.R. 1 reshapes benefit eligibility at the federal level in ways that cascade directly onto state and local administrative systems. The major changes include expanded work requirements for SNAP that eliminate prior exemptions for veterans, homeless individuals, and former foster youth. States lose waiver flexibility they previously used to adapt requirements to local labor market conditions.

FEMA discretionary funding is cut by nearly $10 billion, with staffing reductions that will impair state and local disaster response. For state IT teams, the practical consequences are updating eligibility systems, reconfiguring data pipelines, and ensuring platforms can handle increased federal scrutiny around payment error rates and program integrity.

The reporting and data-sharing obligations that accompany H.R. 1 are substantial. States that cannot demonstrate compliance risk federal funding clawbacks. AI tools are being evaluated to help agencies meet system update deadlines, but the timeline pressure is severe.

From a city government standpoint, Frederick does not administer SNAP or Medicaid directly, but residents who rely on those programs -- including many in the Latino community and those served by Centro Hispano and SSCM -- will be affected by Maryland's implementation decisions. Any service disruptions, eligibility losses, or benefit delays will surface as constituent needs at the city level.

## Relevance

This connects directly to [[City Values Act]] through the lens of how federal enforcement posture affects residents who interact with city-adjacent services. If SNAP work requirements are tightened and exemptions removed, low-income immigrant and non-immigrant residents may face benefit loss simultaneously with any federal immigration enforcement escalation. The city's role is not administrative but it is political and support-oriented.

Watch for how Maryland responds to the state waiver restrictions -- this is a potential area for public comment or coalition communication with [[Spanish Speaking Community of Maryland]] and [[Centro Hispano de Frederick]].
