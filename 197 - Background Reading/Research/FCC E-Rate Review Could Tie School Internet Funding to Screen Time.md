# FCC E-Rate Review Could Tie School Internet Funding to Screen Time

## Summary
The FCC is reviewing the $3 billion E-Rate school internet subsidy, with options on the table ranging from elimination to requiring screen-time limits or parental opt-outs as a condition of funding; public comment closes October 13.

## Key Points
E-Rate is funded through telephone bill fees and covers 20 to 90 percent of schools' internet costs, making it the fifth largest source of federal school support. FCC Chair Carr has asked whether it has served its purpose. The review draws on NTIA Administrator Arielle Roth's screen-time listening sessions and revives older conservative critiques of the program, including a stalled Cruz bill conditioning E-Rate on screen-time policies.

Districts worry about three outcomes: elimination (which advocates doubt the FCC can do alone), restriction to rural areas, and mandatory opt-out or tech-limit policies. Superintendents point out that school internet runs security, HVAC, attendance, and payroll, not just instruction, and that losing the subsidy would hit high-poverty districts hardest. Even screen-time advocates like Fairplay object to tying curriculum questions to basic connectivity. The contradiction the article highlights: the same administration is promoting AI in education.

## Relevance
Peripheral to council work, since schools are a county function, but it is a clean example of using infrastructure funding as leverage over content policy, the same pattern seen in federal grant conditions on immigration cooperation. Also relevant from the IT side: connectivity is operational infrastructure for any institution, and treating it as a discretionary content subsidy misreads what it pays for.
