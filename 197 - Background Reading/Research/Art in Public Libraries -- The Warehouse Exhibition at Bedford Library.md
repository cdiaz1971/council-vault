# Art in Public Libraries -- The Warehouse Exhibition at Bedford Library

Artist Vic Liu's exhibition "The Warehouse," created with author James Kilgore, opened April 4 at Bedford Library in NYC and runs through June 27. The exhibition uses large-scale paintings to examine resistance and survival inside prisons, the emotional and political distance between inside and outside, and what abolition might look like in practice.

Mariame Kaba, who organized the exhibition at Bedford, articulates a deliberate choice to site it in a public library rather than a gallery: the library is where people live, not just visit. Placing art about mass incarceration in a public library brings together "one of the worst things the state does with one of the best."

## On art and organizing under authoritarianism

The newsletter includes a substantial interview with Vic Liu on the relationship between art and organizing. The core argument: art is always political because it is an articulation of self, and self is always political. Art under authoritarianism matters because fascism "is constantly and deliberately shrinking the definition of who is considered a person," and art -- especially art made by and for marginalized communities -- does the opposite.

"Art holds a mirror to who we are as a people. Crucially, because it holds a mirror not only to our flaws but also to our possibilities, it allows us to dream of what we can be."

## Maryland note

Maryland Eastern Shore Public Libraries are losing access to Hoopla, a popular digital media lending service, per WBOC. Worth monitoring for equity implications in digital access to library resources.

## Why this matters for César

The intersection of public libraries, civic space, and community-led cultural programming is relevant to Frederick's [[Parks and Infrastructure]] work and the broader question of what civic institutions do under political pressure. The Warehouse model -- meeting people in the spaces they already use -- echoes the community engagement approaches César has found most effective.
