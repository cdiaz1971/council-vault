# Dēmos Think-and-Do Tank Model -- Race-Forward Strategy Under Authoritarianism

## Summary

Heather McGhee reflects on Dēmos's organizational evolution from a small experiment in policy advocacy into a multi-issue "think and do" tank that centers racial equity across all its work. The piece argues that the organization's founding instinct -- looking past the immediate political fight to the structural trends shaping the future -- is again the correct posture as the country navigates an authoritarian turn.

## Key Points

Dēmos was built around the premise that the long-term trends in American democracy were legible even when they were politically unpopular: an increasingly diverse electorate, rising inequality, and growing gaps between what people needed and what institutions delivered. Under McGhee's leadership, the organization moved beyond neutral policy analysis to explicitly center racial equity, treating the racial impacts of policy as an analytical lens applied across all issue areas, not a separate program.

The "think and do" framing is deliberate. Dēmos has positioned itself not as a pure research institution but as an organization that builds strategy alongside the movements and communities it serves. Current president Taifa Smith Butler is carrying that posture forward with a specific focus on Black and brown communities and cross-sector alliance building.

The "looking to the horizon again" framing in this piece is a signal that Dēmos sees the current authoritarian moment not as a reason to go defensive but as a reason to accelerate long-horizon investment: countering cynicism, building participatory capacity, and developing strategies that outlast the current political cycle.

## Relevance

The Dēmos model -- think-and-do, race-forward, horizon-oriented -- is a useful frame for how progressive municipal leaders should position themselves right now. The instinct to chase every immediate political fight often comes at the cost of the structural groundwork that produces durable wins. This is directly relevant to how César thinks about housing, immigrant protection, and democratic governance in Frederick: short-term responsiveness is necessary, but the horizon work (LMC reform, municipal ID, Housing Production Target) is what actually changes conditions.

The Power Scorecard work Dēmos has released is also worth tracking as a benchmarking tool for state-level conditions affecting Black and brown families -- useful context for Maryland legislative work.
