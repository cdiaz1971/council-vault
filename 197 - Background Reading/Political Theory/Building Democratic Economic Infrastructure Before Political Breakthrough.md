# Building Democratic Economic Infrastructure Before Political Breakthrough

## Summary

Nakagawa argues that democratic movements historically fail when they win political power without prior economic transformation -- the sequencing that works is building cooperative institutions, labor density, alternative financial infrastructure, and democratic ownership structures during the "war of position," not after breakthrough.

## Key Points

Nakagawa opens with a question from a foundation briefing: when pro-democracy forces win, what kind of economy are they consolidating into? Most movements don't ask this question until it's too late.

Historical cases he draws on:

**Spain after Franco:** Democratic opposition won political power but left Francoist capital intact. That capital purchased the new democracy and shaped it for decades.

**South Africa post-apartheid:** The ANC won the state but the economic architecture of apartheid -- mines, banks, land -- survived almost entirely undisturbed. South African democracy is now in crisis.

**Nordic counter-model:** The Swedish labor movement built cooperative institutions, union density, and worker-ownership infrastructure *before* achieving political dominance. When Social Democrats took power in the 1930s, the skeleton of a democratic economy already existed and could be scaled.

The infrastructure priorities Nakagawa identifies, in order:

1. **Labor density** -- unions, worker centers, sectoral bargaining
2. **Alternative financial infrastructure** -- credit unions, CDFIs, public banking at state/municipal level (Bank of North Dakota model; California has enabling legislation)
3. **Democratic ownership** -- worker cooperatives, ESOPs, business succession as opportunity (half of U.S. small businesses changing hands this decade), platform cooperatives
4. **Land and housing** -- community land trusts remove land from speculation permanently; CLT residents are among the most civically engaged people in their neighborhoods
5. **Democratic information infrastructure** -- local news cooperatives, reader-supported journalism, data cooperatives

The Mondragon cooperative network was built during Franco's dictatorship as preparation for democracy, not as a retreat from politics.

## Relevance

Highly relevant to César's work at the intersection of progressive governance and civic organizing. The public banking and community land trust arguments connect directly to Frederick's housing policy agenda. The labor density and democratic ownership arguments inform how to think about economic development policy and community benefit agreements in the data center ordinance work. The framing of economic institution-building as political organizing is useful for how César thinks about the relationship between council work and movement building.
