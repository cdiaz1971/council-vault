# Assembly-Based Movement Organizing -- PAH Model

## Summary

The Platform for People Affected by Mortgages (PAH), a Spanish housing justice movement with 200+ chapters, offers a detailed model for assembly-based organizing: horizontal decision-making, committee structures answerable to assemblies, facilitation discipline, and building commitment that outlasts individual cases.

## Key Points

PAH was founded in Barcelona in 2009 in response to the mortgage crisis. It now has over 200 local chapters, received Spain's national human rights award in 2013, and has kept thousands of families in their homes through direct action, legal support, and legislative advocacy. This is an excerpt from their handbook, "Yes, It's Possible! A Handbook for Building Power from Below."

**Structural principles:**
- Decisions are made at open weekly or fortnightly assemblies. Committees handle operational and thematic work but are accountable to the assembly and authorized only to decide on specific matters.
- The agenda is shared in advance, displayed at the meeting, and each item has a time limit. Assemblies end at a fixed time -- this is non-negotiable.
- Facilitation is active: facilitators keep discussion on track, use sign language to manage pacing, and distinguish between urgent items and items that can be revisited.
- Consensus is preferred over majority voting. Dissenting voices are respected and valued as contributions to collective intelligence.

**Horizontality as ongoing practice:**
Horizontality is not a structure you install -- it requires constant vigilance. Leadership patterns emerge naturally; the response is not to eliminate leaders but to ensure they don't accumulate too many roles. Someone who serves as spokesperson, committee member, and assembly chair is a single point of failure and a structural risk to horizontality.

**Building commitment:**
Most people who come to PAH with a housing problem don't stay for the long term. But the ones who do are motivated not by gratitude for their own case resolution but by solidarity with others in similar situations. Informal spaces -- Telegram groups, after-meeting drinks -- are as important as formal assemblies for building the bonds that sustain long-term commitment.

**Adapting to change:**
When the nature of housing problems shifted from mortgage defaults to rental and squatting cases, PAH had to adapt its model. Rental cases move faster, leaving less time to build relationships. PAH treats adaptation as a core competency -- if something isn't working, it gets raised at assembly and changed.

## Relevance

The PAH model is relevant to Frederick's community organizing context in two ways. First, for thinking about how to structure ongoing community engagement around housing policy -- the assembly model is a tested alternative to the one-time public hearing. Second, the commitment-building section is useful for thinking about organizations like [[Centro Hispano de Frederick]] and [[Spanish Speaking Community of Maryland]] and how they sustain member engagement over time beyond individual service interactions.

The facilitation discipline -- time limits, agenda sharing, sign language consensus checks -- is also directly applicable to how César runs council sessions.
