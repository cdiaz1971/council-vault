# Block and Build -- Why Defeating Authoritarianism Is Only Half the Work

## Summary

The core argument: movements that defeat authoritarian regimes without building the democratic infrastructure to consolidate gains will either slide into left-wing authoritarianism (trying to hold power without sufficient institutional capacity) or get overrun when the authoritarian movement returns, having studied its defeat. Block and Build are not two tracks -- they must be one integrated project.

## Key Points

**The distinction:**
- Block: stopping authoritarian consolidation. Federal lawsuits, mass mobilization, boycotts, civil disobedience, legislative and legal pushback, defiant journalism. Loud, visible, urgent.
- Build: constructing the institutional, economic, civic, and analytical infrastructure democracy requires. Distributed economic power, civil rights enforcement architecture, mass-membership civic institutions, multi-racial governing coalition with positive content, workers' power, adequate public institutions. Slower, less visible, not viral.

**The breakthrough trap:**
The most consistent pattern in modern democratic history: movements that win the block round without winning the build round lose the long game. Examples:
- Hungary 1989: celebrated democratic transition collapsed into Orbán's authoritarianism within 20 years because economic power was not redistributed and civic infrastructure was not built.
- U.S. Reconstruction after 1865: multiracial constitutional revolution collapsed into Jim Crow within 30 years for substantially the same reasons.
- Trump's second term as evidence: the movement that defeated Trump's first term didn't build consolidation infrastructure to keep the door closed.

**What block-only failure looks like:**
A movement holding power against concentrated capital, captured judiciary, and a regrouping opposition, without institutional capacity to govern from a positive program, reaches for the only available tools: purges, court-packing, restricting civic participation, emergency powers that outlast emergencies. This is desperation, not intention.

**What build-only failure looks like:**
Long-term institutional construction can't survive in a context consolidating into authoritarianism. The infrastructure gets dismantled before it can mature. The American left's error in the 1990s-2000s was assuming institutional construction could proceed at its own pace because formal democracy was secure.

**What integration requires:**
- Civil resistance training must include analytical content about distributed power.
- Economic redistribution work must be in conversation with civil resistance, not in a separate funder portfolio.
- Communities best at build -- Black Southern organizing, indigenous sovereignty, disability rights, immigrant mutual aid, Latino farmworker organizing -- must be at the center of block work, not adjacent.
- Stop sorting block and build into separate program areas. Democracy work, racial justice work, and economic justice work are the same work from three angles.

**Historical successes:**
Brazil's Workers' Party (strongest periods), Portugal's Carnation Revolution, South Korea's candlelight movement, parts of post-apartheid South Africa: these produced consolidation by integrating block and build.

## Relevance

This is a direct intellectual frame for how César should think about his own role at the municipal level. His council work is both block (resisting preemption, protecting immigrant community, defending democratic norms locally) and build (housing production, LMC reform, Municipal ID, community benefit agreements). The essay gives language for why both matter and why they can't be separated into different buckets.
