# Community-Centered Research as a Model for Equitable Policy

## Summary

Dr. Brittany Lewis's Equity in Action (EIA) model offers a concrete alternative to extractive research. Rather than extracting data from communities and returning with predetermined solutions, it positions community members as co-producers of knowledge: they help name the problem, define the research questions, and chart the course of action. The model requires long-term institutional commitment, not transactional engagement.

## Key Points

Lewis founded Research in Action (RIA) in 2019 specifically to build this alternative model. The core reorientation is epistemic: community members are not survey respondents or focus group participants -- they are holders of expertise that professional researchers lack. The EIA model structures institutions to act on that recognition.

In practice, this means:

Partner organizations build ongoing relationships with community members before any research begins. They do not arrive with questions already formed. Community members help define what the problem is, not just respond to an institution's framing of it. Trust is built through shared values and shared understanding, not through a single engagement process. The result, Lewis argues, is a breakthrough in outcomes that extractive methods cannot produce -- because the solutions are grounded in how people actually experience their situation.

The model explicitly addresses the "dominator culture" behaviors that undermine authentic partnership: paternalism, power hoarding, perfectionism, binary thinking, and avoidance of conflict. These are not individual failings but institutional norms that must be named and actively countered.

Lewis also challenges the credentialing hierarchy that gives academic researchers more authority to describe someone's situation than that person themselves. Lived experience is treated as evidence, not anecdote.

## Relevance

This is directly applicable to how Frederick structures its community engagement in housing and immigration policy. The Municipal ID program, the City Values Act, and any housing production strategy touching immigrant and low-income communities all face the same structural question Lewis is asking: are we designing for these communities, or with them?

The EIA model also connects to César's work with Centro Hispano, SSCM, and AACF. These organizations have long-term community relationships that city government often lacks. A genuine partnership model would position them as co-designers of policy, not just community liaisons who relay city decisions.
