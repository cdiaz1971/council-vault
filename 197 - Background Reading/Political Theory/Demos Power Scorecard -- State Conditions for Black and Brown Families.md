# Demos Power Scorecard -- State Conditions for Black and Brown Families

## Summary

Demos released a Power Scorecard evaluating whether states create the conditions for Black and brown communities to build power and thrive economically. The headline finding: zero states currently provide optimal conditions. As people of color become the majority in the United States, this analysis frames structural barriers as a governance failure, not a demographic inevitability.

## Key Points

The scorecard evaluates states against a set of conditions tied to economic power, political representation, and community infrastructure. The framing is explicitly about power-building for Black and brown communities, not just service delivery. The fact that no state scores optimally suggests that even the most progressive states have not aligned their policy environments with the needs of communities that are already or soon will be the majority population.

The analysis connects to a broader Demos argument: that building power for communities of color is not a niche equity agenda but the central challenge of democratic governance as demographics shift. States that fail on this metric are leaving political and economic capacity on the table.

## Relevance

Frederick sits in Maryland, which has a strong progressive governance reputation but likely does not score at the top of this index given persistent racial wealth and opportunity gaps in the state. As Council President, César can use this framing to push back against the idea that equity programming is optional or supplementary -- it is a precondition for a functioning, majority community. The scorecard is also a useful rhetorical tool: "zero states get this right" reframes local equity gaps as part of a national structural problem, not a Frederick-specific failure.
