# Climate Fascism -- The Walled-Garden Response to Climate Collapse

Nakagawa names a specific variant of disaster authoritarianism that is particularly relevant to the climate crisis: climate fascism. It is not climate denial as such -- it is the political program of a narrow, racially exclusive coalition that accepts the physical reality of climate catastrophe but responds by building walls, both literal and figurative, to protect the inside from the outside rather than addressing the crisis at its roots.

The logic is internally coherent: if there is not enough for everyone, the job of government is to ensure there is enough for us. The definition of "us" is the authoritarian project's most important product. Climate fascism is already visible in European far-right politics, in the "great replacement" ideology that frames migration as existential threat, and in the American right's combination of domestic climate denialism with military preparation for the geopolitical instability a destabilized world produces.

## The Connection to Immigration and Scapegoating

Climate-driven migration is one of the primary engines of this politics. Tens of millions are already displaced; hundreds of millions are projected by mid-century. Each wave of migration creates an opportunity for authoritarian movements to activate threat and exclusion politics: to militarize borders, to treat human beings in motion as invaders rather than as people in need.

Water scarcity, food system disruption, and grid fragility all function similarly -- they are machines for producing the kind of zero-sum resource politics that authoritarianism runs on. The politics of scarcity are almost always the politics of exclusion.

## Why This Matters for César

Climate fascism is the long-game version of the politics Frederick already faces in microcosm: who counts as a member of the community, who gets access to resources, who bears the costs of decisions made by others. The City Values Act and immigrant protection work are not tangential to climate politics -- they are a local version of the democratic resistance this analysis calls for.
