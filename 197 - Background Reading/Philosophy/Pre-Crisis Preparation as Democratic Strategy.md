# Pre-Crisis Preparation as Democratic Strategy

Nakagawa's core strategic argument is that trigger moments reward whoever is most prepared. Authoritarian movements are increasingly good at pre-positioning: they have narratives that can be activated quickly when a disaster strikes because the groundwork has been laid for years. Democratic movements tend to be reactive -- good at mobilizing in response to outrages, but not consistently good at being ready before the moment arrives.

## What Preparation Requires

Nakagawa identifies four concrete forms of democratic preparation:

**Scenario planning** -- not abstract but operational: What happens if a major climate disaster hits your region in the next eighteen months? Who gets scapegoated? What emergency powers will be invoked and on what legal basis? Who are the decision-makers who can be supported in refusing authoritarian demands? What is the narrative you want telling in the first 48 hours?

**Narrative preemption** -- building frames before the crisis that explain who is actually responsible for the conditions that made the disaster possible. Climate change must be framed as a democracy issue, not just an environmental one: the denial of shared reality is itself a democratic threat.

**Mutual aid infrastructure** -- food networks, emergency communication, neighborhood-level support that crosses racial and class lines. Communities with this capacity are less dependent on the authoritarian state and less susceptible to the dependency relationships authoritarian governments use to consolidate loyalty. Mutual aid is not charity; it is material expression of the democratic principle that we are responsible for each other.

**Coalition infrastructure** -- the relationships built across difference before the crisis arrives. The labor union in solidarity with the immigrant rights organization, the faith community in relationship with the racial justice group. These are not just morally important; they are strategically necessary because disaster authoritarianism depends on the isolation of targeted communities.

## Five Characteristics of Democratic Crisis Response

When the trigger moment arrives, the effective democratic response names the political dimension immediately, actively contests scapegoating in the first hours, makes solidarity visible and contagious, uses the moment to build lasting infrastructure rather than just survive the emergency, and connects local response to national strategy.

## Why This Matters for César

The Frederick Rapid Response Network, Centro Hispano, SSCM, and the coalition around the City Values Act are exactly the pre-crisis infrastructure this analysis describes. The question worth asking is: what is the narrative Frederick can deploy within 48 hours of a major crisis, and who are the messengers?
