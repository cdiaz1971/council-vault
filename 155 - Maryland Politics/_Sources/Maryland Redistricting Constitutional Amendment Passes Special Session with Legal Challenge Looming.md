# Maryland Redistricting Constitutional Amendment Passes Special Session with Legal Challenge Looming

## Summary

HB 2100, a constitutional amendment allowing Maryland to pursue mid-decade congressional redistricting, passed the special session 96-38 in the House and 32-13 in the Senate and now goes to voters in November. Republicans threatened an immediate legal challenge aimed at keeping the measure off the ballot entirely, citing procedural grounds and arguing the bill insulates future maps from court challenge.

## Key Points

The amendment, if approved by voters, would override a 2022 ruling by Judge Lynne Battaglia that applied state constitutional compactness and contiguity requirements -- previously interpreted to apply only to legislative districts -- to congressional maps. Democrats argued the Battaglia ruling was a novel interpretation that hamstrung congressional redistricting. Ferguson, who blocked an earlier redistricting effort this year citing that ruling, ultimately supported this approach.

Republicans argue the bill sidesteps a law requiring constitutional amendments to be submitted in early July for public review, and that it creates a scenario where future congressional maps could not be challenged in most state or federal courts. Senate President Ferguson disputes this interpretation, saying the bill simply confirms that federal law governs congressional maps as it always has.

Senate Minority Leader Hershey and Minority Whip Justin Ready (R-Frederick and Carroll) both signaled intent to seek legal action quickly -- the ballot language had to reach elections officials by August 5 to make the November ballot.

If voters approve the amendment, the legislature could take up new congressional maps as early as January. House Majority Leader David Moon framed this as positioning Maryland to respond symmetrically as other states engage in mid-decade redistricting. The current 7-1 split (seven Democrats, Rep. Andy Harris the lone Republican) would be the baseline for any new map.

Governor Moore held a bill signing ceremony despite the amendment not requiring his signature -- a deliberate signal of support.

## Relevance

Senate Minority Whip Justin Ready represents Frederick and Carroll counties. His opposition to the amendment and his threat of legal challenge are worth tracking as indicators of the local Republican posture heading into the November ballot. Frederick County voters will be deciding on this amendment alongside other fall races.

The redistricting thread connects to broader democratic resilience work -- the amendment is a direct response to the national pattern of hyperpartisan redistricting that began with Trump pushing Texas to redraw maps ahead of midterms. If it passes and maps are redrawn, Maryland's congressional delegation picture changes significantly.
