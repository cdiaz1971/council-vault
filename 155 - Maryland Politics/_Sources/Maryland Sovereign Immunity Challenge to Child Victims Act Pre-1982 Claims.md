# Maryland Sovereign Immunity Challenge to Child Victims Act Pre-1982 Claims

## Summary

The Maryland Supreme Court agreed to hear the state's challenge to whether the 2023 Child Victims Act waived sovereign immunity for claims arising before the Maryland Tort Claims Act took effect in 1982. Roughly 1,300 of the 12,000 CVA lawsuits predate 1982; the state argues the CVA lifted time limits but not sovereign immunity. A ruling favorable to the state would eliminate those claims and cap exposure at over $1 billion.

## Key Points

The 2023 Child Victims Act eliminated statutes of limitation for civil lawsuits alleging institutional child sexual abuse. The wave of resulting litigation targeted the Catholic Church but also state institutions. The state faces potential liability of at least $11 billion across all 12,000 suits, and as much as more depending on how "occurrence" is defined.

The state's argument: the MTCA of 1982 ended absolute sovereign immunity, but only prospectively. Conduct before 1982 was never waivable. The CVA extended the time window to sue but did not expressly waive pre-1982 immunity. "The State's sovereign immunity is not a 'time limitation' at all. It is a structural protection."

Circuit courts have split. Frederick County and Baltimore City courts agreed with the state. The Prince George's County court rejected the immunity claim. The Maryland Supreme Court taking the case will produce a uniform standard when it hears arguments starting in September 2026.

AG Anthony Brown is named as a defendant. Gov. Moore is not.

## Relevance

This is primarily background tracking on Maryland state liability and judicial dynamics rather than a direct Frederick action item. It's worth knowing because: (1) Frederick County Circuit Court already ruled in the state's favor on this issue, meaning Frederick courts are not outliers; (2) the overall fiscal pressure on the state from this litigation reinforces the budget environment heading into 2027 session, which affects what the state can fund locally; (3) the sovereign immunity doctrine itself is relevant to any council consideration of municipal liability in the context of expanded tenant protections, sanctuary infrastructure, or civil rights enforcement.
