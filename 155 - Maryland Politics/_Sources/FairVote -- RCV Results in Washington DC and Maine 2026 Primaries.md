## Summary

Washington DC used ranked choice voting for the first time in its June 2026 primary. Maine used RCV for governor and Congress. In both cases, RCV delivered majority winners and meaningfully counted more votes.

A SurveyUSA poll of DC voters found:
- **78%** said their RCV ballot was simple to complete (87% among those who had already voted)
- **74%** support ranking candidates on their ballots
- **69%** ranked two or more candidates in at least one race

## DC results

Two Democratic Council races went to RCV counts because no candidate earned a majority of first choices. Across those two races, 90% of voters expressed a preference among the finalists. In the at-large Council primary, about 30,000 voters whose first choice was eliminated still had their ballot count for one of the finalists. Turnout exceeded 139,000 votes -- the highest since 1994.

## Strategic relevance

This is live evidence for RCV advocacy. The DC turnout spike and the strong voter approval numbers (78-74%) give concrete counterweight to "voters will be confused" arguments. The Maine governor's race adds a high-profile statewide precedent.

The combination of majority winners + higher turnout + strong post-election approval mirrors FairVote's research predictions and is directly citable in any Maryland or Frederick electoral reform advocacy context.
