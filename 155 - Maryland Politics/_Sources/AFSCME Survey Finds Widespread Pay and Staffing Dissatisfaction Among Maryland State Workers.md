# AFSCME Survey Finds Widespread Pay and Staffing Dissatisfaction Among Maryland State Workers

## Summary
AFSCME Council 3 released its first survey of state workers, drawing more than 2,500 responses. Nearly eight in ten respondents said they had considered leaving their jobs, and nearly sixty percent said they work more than one job to make ends meet. Union president Patrick Moran called on Governor Wes Moore to deliver on pay, staffing, and safety commitments before his term ends in January.

## Key Points
The union's demands include across-the-board pay increases, raises specifically for correctional officers, guaranteed cost-of-living increases, and back pay to make up for missed scheduled increases. It also called for improved heat and air quality safety standards and better training. Notably, the union also wants rules governing mandated AI tool use. One board member quoted in the piece said workers are being required to use AI in their jobs without any input on how it affects their work or the clients they serve, tying labor conditions directly to the AI procurement and workforce preparation questions already active in the GovTech thread.

Vacancy data shows real but uneven progress. Statewide vacancy rates fell from 13.4 percent in 2023 to about 9.3 percent in May 2026, and average hiring time dropped from 188 days in 2024 to 109 days. But contractual positions increased from 1,125 to 1,161 over the same period, moving in the opposite direction from Moore's stated goal of converting contract jobs to state positions, and some agencies, including the Department of Public Safety and Correctional Services and Maryland State Police, still show only marginal improvement.
