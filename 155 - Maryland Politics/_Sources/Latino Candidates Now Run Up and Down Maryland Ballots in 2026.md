# Latino Candidates Now Run Up and Down Maryland Ballots in 2026

## Summary

The Maryland Democratic Party is tracking 21 Latino candidates for state and local office in November 2026, including county council nominees in Prince George's, Montgomery, Frederick and Baltimore counties, a generation after Ana Sol Gutierrez became the first Latina state legislator.

## Key Points

The arc runs from Ana Sol Gutierrez, a Salvadoran-born systems engineer who served on the Montgomery County school board from 1990 and became Maryland's first Latina delegate in 2002, built on hand-carried voter registration in restaurants and shops. She remains active fielding bilingual calls for the League of Women Voters election hotline.

Current numbers from the state party: 21 Latino candidates this fall. Nine are incumbents, six are new candidates in safe Democratic seats, two are in competitive races, and four are running in Republican-held seats. All won primaries for county council seats in Prince George's, Montgomery, Frederick and Baltimore counties, including some seats Democrats are trying to flip.

House Speaker Joseline Peña-Melnyk, the first Afro-Latina and first immigrant speaker, elected unanimously in December 2025, co-founded the Maryland Legislative Latino Caucus in 2014 with Del. David Fraser-Hidalgo. It now has 100 members, eight Latino and 92 associate. She credits the caucus with energy bill relief, prescription drug cost controls through the Prescription Drug Affordability Board, and coverage for undocumented pregnant women, and emphasizes the growth of Latino staff across state government. She is on a seven-day statewide get-out-the-vote tour and hosted an August candidate meetup at a College Park restaurant.

Candidates profiled:
- **Ruben Amaya**, 25, Baltimore County Council District 2 nominee, favored in a heavily Democratic district. Would be the county's first Latino and first Gen Z elected official. Third vice chair of the state party and Young Democrats of Maryland president. Priorities: housing affordability, economic and food security, basic county services.
- **Jessica Nichols**, Howard County Council nominee, unopposed, expected to be sworn in December 7 as the first African American woman and first Latina in the seat. 2024-25 Howard County Teacher of the Year and co-founder of Columbia Community Care, a food security nonprofit.
- **Barbara Ebel**, Montgomery County register of wills nominee, favored against Republican T. Dolores Reyes. 26 years at the state Labor Department's workforce office.

Peña-Melnyk closed with a call for cross-community solidarity on immigrant rights.

César is the first Latino Council President in Frederick's history, and this piece documents the statewide pipeline he is part of. The Frederick County Council nominee named in the party's count is not identified in the article, which is a gap worth closing before November 3: knowing who the Latino nominees are in Frederick County matters for coalition building and for the City-County relationship after the election.

Amaya's platform (housing affordability, food security) mirrors the Frederick Affordability Agenda, and the Latino Caucus's 92 associate members are a natural audience for City-level asks on immigrant protections and housing. Peña-Melnyk's statewide tour and stated interest in visiting every district is an opening worth pursuing for Frederick.
