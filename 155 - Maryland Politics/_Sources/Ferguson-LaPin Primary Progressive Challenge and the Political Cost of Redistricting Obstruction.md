# Ferguson-LaPin Primary -- Progressive Challenge and the Political Cost of Redistricting Obstruction

## Summary

Bobby LaPin, a Baltimore sailboat captain and social media personality, ran to Ferguson's left in the June 23, 2026 Democratic primary for Maryland Senate District 46, challenging Senate President Bill Ferguson on redistricting obstruction, affordability, and immigration. Ferguson held the seat, but required over $300,000 in outside PAC money and significant independent expenditure support to do so. LaPin's campaign demonstrated that redistricting obstruction carries real electoral cost, even when it does not flip a seat.

## Key Points

Ferguson blocked redistricting earlier this year on legal grounds, resisting pressure from Gov. Moore and House Minority Leader Jeffries. A redistricting plan that could have flipped Maryland's only Republican-held congressional seat died in the Senate under Ferguson's leadership. He has since reversed course, signaling openness to a summer special session after the Supreme Court's Voting Rights Act ruling.

LaPin's self-described pitch: "I'm a Bernie Sanders. He's a Chuck Schumer." He leveraged a TikTok and Instagram following into a grassroots campaign, knocking 6,000+ doors, making tens of thousands of phone calls. His own polling showed him trailing 61-32 before positive biography messaging -- but flipping to 52-45 after. Ferguson's internal polling showed a 53-28 lead. The gap between those numbers suggests a race that was closer in intensity than in final margin.

The outside money picture is notable: Ferguson benefited from $290,000+ from American Future (a PAC funded primarily by sports betting interests including FanDuel and DraftKings), plus $55,000 from the Maryland Realtors IEC. LaPin raised nearly $188,000 with $30,000 cash on hand at the end. The resource asymmetry was decisive.

The progressive organization non-endorsement of LaPin is the most politically interesting part of the story. Groups including CASA in Action looked at the "totality" of Ferguson's tenure rather than his redistricting obstruction, declining to endorse LaPin out of fear of burning their relationship with the Senate president. LaPin named this directly as a pattern of choosing access over values -- and it will be a live tension in Maryland progressive organizing for the next cycle.

LaPin was a vocal Community Trust Act supporter, publicly emotional about Ferguson's obstruction of the bill. CASA's decision not to endorse despite this was felt as a betrayal by ICE-affected communities in his district.

## Relevance

Maryland legislative dynamics matter for Frederick's council work, particularly on housing preemption, the Community Trust Act implementation, and any future redistricting that affects the Western Maryland congressional picture. Ferguson's continued leadership of the Senate is the operating environment for all of that.

The progressive access-vs-values tension LaPin named is also a useful frame for thinking about Frederick's own coalition work. The Institutional Solidarity Pact thread asks civic organizations to commit to mutual defense in ways that may cost them institutional access. That is the same choice CASA faced -- and chose differently than LaPin's supporters hoped.
