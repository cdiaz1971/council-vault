# Maryland June 2026 Primary Final Results and Legislative Implications

## Summary

Final ballot counting from the June 23 Maryland primary confirmed five incumbent losses: Senate Majority Leader Nancy King (Montgomery), Sen. Dalya Attar (Baltimore City), and Dels. Frank Conaway Jr., Kevin Hornberger, and Stephanie Smith. The results set the legislative landscape for the 2027 session.

## Key Points

The most consequential loss is Nancy King, Senate Majority Leader and a reliable obstacle to progressive housing and tenant protection legislation. Her loss opens the majority leader position and shifts caucus dynamics in the 2027 session.

Tiffany Alston won the Senate District 24 seat being vacated by Joanne Benson with 59% of the vote over Kevin Ford Jr. Alston had previously served in the House. LaTasha Ward finally won a delegate seat in District 24 after finishing fourth in 2018, fourth in 2022, and losing a central committee appointment earlier in 2026. Ward's persistence in a grassroots campaign without central committee backing is notable.

Stephanie Smith lost the fourth seat in Baltimore City District 45, apparently after Sen. Cory McCray organized against her. Smith publicly named McCray; McCray offered a diplomatic non-denial. No Republican or unaffiliated candidates filed in that district, so the Democratic primary was effectively the election.

Del. Darrell Odom Sr. in District 27A survived a close race, reversing an early six-vote deficit to win by 277 votes over Yonelle Moore Lee.

Certification is set for July 23.

## Relevance

King's loss matters most. She was a consistent brake on tenant protection and housing legislation in the Senate. Her successor as majority leader will shape what passes in 2027, including any state-level junk fee or rent stabilization legislation that Frederick's Real Rent Ordinance might need to survive preemption challenges.

The broader pattern of incumbents losing to progressive challengers, combined with the redistricting special session, signals a more assertive legislative majority in 2027. That environment is generally favorable for Frederick's housing and immigration agenda at the state level, though the specific vote math on any given bill depends on who fills leadership positions.
