# AFSCME Demands Highway Worker Safety Reforms After Two Maryland Deaths

## Summary

Two Maryland State Highway Administration workers were killed in work zone incidents within 72 hours in late April 2026. AFSCME Council 3 President Patrick Moran met with Transportation Secretary Kathryn Thomson on May 1, calling the meeting "very productive" and outlining a set of demands before next steps could proceed. Between 2020 and 2025, Maryland averaged more than 1,308 work zone crashes annually.

## Key Points

**The deaths:**
- Dipakkumar Patel, 70: killed April 28 on Route 13 in Somerset County while inside his work vehicle, struck by a minivan.
- Robert Dempsey, 40: killed approximately April 25 on the Capital Beltway in Prince George's County while setting up traffic cones.

**AFSCME's demands:**
- More enforcement (drivers treat road crews as fine-exempt, unlike police; automated cameras could change this)
- Vehicle-mounted cameras to automatically ticket drivers who fail to move over
- Increased fines and jail time for killing a highway worker in a work zone
- Better protective equipment and barriers
- More in-person safety training

**Context:** Maryland already passed tiered work zone speeding fines after a 2023 incident where six workers died on I-695 (including a father and son). Fines now range from $60-$1,000, doubling when workers are present. State Police conducted 440 work zone enforcement initiatives last year, issuing 3,112 citations.

**Enforcement gap:** The current system depends on police presence. Moran's key point is that drivers respond to troopers but ignore road crews. Automated enforcement closes that gap without requiring a trooper at every work zone.

## Relevance

Maryland labor and safety policy; relevant as context for state legislation tracking and any constituent conversations about worker protection. Also connects to Frederick's own infrastructure and parks work -- city crews doing road and parks work face analogous exposure. Worth checking whether Frederick has any local work zone safety protocols or move-over enforcement posture.
