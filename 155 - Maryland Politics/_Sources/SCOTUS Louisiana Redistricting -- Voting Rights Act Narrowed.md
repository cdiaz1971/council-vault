# SCOTUS Louisiana Redistricting -- Voting Rights Act Narrowed

In a 6-3 decision issued April 29, 2026, the Supreme Court struck down Louisiana's congressional map, which had been drawn to include two majority-Black congressional districts. Justice Alito's majority opinion held that the Constitution "almost never permits" race-based discrimination, and narrowed Section 2 of the Voting Rights Act of 1965 to require a "strong inference that intentional discrimination occurred" -- a significantly higher bar than previous interpretations.

Justice Elena Kagan dissented, writing that the ruling rendered Section 2 "all but a dead letter."

## Significance

The VRA's Section 2 has been the primary tool for challenging racially discriminatory redistricting since the 1982 amendments. This ruling makes it substantially harder for minority communities to challenge maps that dilute their voting power, even when those maps produce demonstrably unrepresentative outcomes.

This is a landmark decision that narrows one of the last federal enforcement mechanisms protecting minority political participation.

## Why this matters for César

Frederick's immigrant and minority communities -- including constituencies served by [[Centro Hispano de Frederick]] and [[Spanish Speaking Community of Maryland]] -- already face structural underrepresentation at state and federal levels. A weakened VRA accelerates that dynamic. The ruling is also relevant to César's broader thinking on democracy infrastructure and [[Municipal ID Program]] as a participation tool.
