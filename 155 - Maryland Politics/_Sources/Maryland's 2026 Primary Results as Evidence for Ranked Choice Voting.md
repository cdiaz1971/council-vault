# Maryland's 2026 Primary Results as Evidence for Ranked Choice Voting

## Summary
Maryland's crowded 2026 primaries produced plurality winners across the board, including Adrian Boafo's 32% win for Hoyer's seat and Dan Cox's 45% win for governor, giving fresh empirical backing to the case for ranked choice voting.

## Key Points
The author, an independent who re-registered Democratic just to vote in the primary, describes having to vote strategically in two races this cycle rather than for a preferred candidate: a 24-candidate 5th Congressional District primary and a splintered Anne Arundel County executive primary. The statewide pattern backs that experience up. In the crowded race to succeed Steny Hoyer, Del. Adrian Boafo won with roughly 32% of the vote, meaning more than two-thirds of primary voters chose someone else. Anne Arundel Councilmember Allison Pickard won her county executive primary with 42.5%. On the Republican side, Dan Cox won the nine-candidate gubernatorial primary with about 45%, with the remaining 55% split across the field. Only two counties in the entire state avoided a plurality winner this cycle.

The piece argues ranked choice voting fixes this by letting voters rank preferences rather than force strategic voting to block a disliked candidate, producing winners with a genuine majority mandate. Takoma Park has used RCV since 2007, Greenbelt voters approved it last fall, and Berwyn Heights just passed a ballot measure to explore it with 76% support.

## Relevance
Direct fresh evidence for the active ranked choice voting and charter reform thread, where Nash, Taylor, and Brehm have been circulating a charter amendment reference list without a formal proposal yet. The Boafo and Cox plurality numbers are concrete, current-cycle data points worth having on hand if that proposal formalizes, alongside the existing FairVote research already in the vault.
