# Baltimore School Board Candidates Span the Democratic Left

## Summary
After Baltimore voters rejected the only Republican in the June primary, the four remaining city school board candidates are all Democrats ranging from a DSA-aligned incumbent to a self-described moderate liberal, competing for two elected seats on an otherwise appointed board.

## Key Points
The race is nonpartisan in form, and the top two vote-getters win. Only two adult members of the Baltimore City Board of School Commissioners are elected; the rest are appointed.

Ashley "Ash" Esposito (incumbent) calls herself "blue-green," a Democratic socialist and former Greater Baltimore DSA leader. Endorsed by the Baltimore Teachers Union, AFL-CIO, Progressive Maryland, AFT Maryland, Greater Baltimore DSA, Charm City Indivisible, and others. Focus on working-class families, students with disabilities, foster youth, English learners, and homeless students.

Kwame' Jamal Kenyatta-Bey (incumbent) describes himself as a statesman over a partisan, centers community need, and emphasizes the arts. Endorsed by State Sen. Antonio Hayes. No contributions reported.

Jamar Day (challenger) is a self-described progressive Democrat who previously ran for City Council. Endorsed by Gov. Wes Moore, former Mayor Jack Young, SEIU, LGBTQ+ Victory Fund, and Run For Something. Priorities are healthcare, wages, housing, student transit reliability, and teacher planning time.

Domonique Flowers (challenger) is a former special education attorney who identifies with "moderate liberalism," focused on equitable school funding, special education funding, and juvenile crime.

## Relevance
Mostly background on Maryland's progressive bench. The useful signals are that Moore is endorsing in down-ballot local races, and that the DSA and labor-backed lane and the Moore-backed lane are competing within the same Democratic field, a dynamic that shows up in Frederick's own coalition politics. Low priority.
