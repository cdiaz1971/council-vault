# Maryland Muslim and Jewish Heritage Month Bill -- Governor Desk 2026

## Summary
The Maryland General Assembly approved legislation establishing Muslim American Heritage Month in January and Jewish American Heritage Month in May, rejecting amendments that would have added a Christian Heritage Month. The bill passed the House 126-7 and is headed to Governor Moore for signature. It was championed by the newly formed Legislative Muslim Caucus and the Jewish Caucus.

## Key Points

**What passed:** House Bill 560 / Senate Bill 308 designates January as Muslim American Heritage Month and May as Jewish American Heritage Month as official state commemorations.

**The vote:** House passed 126-7 after rejecting calls to add a Christian Heritage Month to the measure. The debate reflected the broader question of whether a state commemorative month requires one-to-one religious symmetry or whether it addresses specific communities that have faced documented discrimination and underrepresentation.

**Who drove it:** The Legislative Muslim Caucus, which launched February 16, 2026 on Muslim Lobby Day in Annapolis, made this one of its first legislative priorities. Del. Sean Stinnett (D-Baltimore City) is chair; Del. Sarah Wolek (D-Montgomery) and Del. Caylin Young (D-Baltimore City) are the other members. The Jewish Caucus co-sponsored via Sen. Shelly Hettleman (D-Baltimore County).

## Relevance
Frederick has a growing Muslim community and a significant Jewish community. While this is state-level symbolics rather than policy, Heritage Month designations create opportunities for community recognition events, proclamations, and visibility that Frederick's council can mirror or align with at the municipal level. The rejection of a Christian Heritage Month amendment and the reasoning behind it -- that the bill specifically recognizes communities facing discrimination -- is useful framing for any future Frederick proclamations or equity-adjacent recognition work.
