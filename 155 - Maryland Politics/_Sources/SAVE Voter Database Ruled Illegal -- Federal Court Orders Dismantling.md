# SAVE Voter Database Ruled Illegal -- Federal Court Orders Dismantling

## Summary

Judge Sparkle L. Sooknanan of the D.C. District Court ruled the Trump administration's SAVE voter database illegal, finding agencies knowingly violated federal privacy laws in order to comply with an executive order. The database was built by combining SSA citizenship data and other federal records into a mass voter verification system -- an action federal law explicitly prohibits. States that ran voter rolls through SAVE incorrectly flagged citizens as non-citizens and canceled their registrations.

## Key Points

SAVE was originally a benefits verification database for legal immigrants. The administration repurposed it, combining it with Social Security Administration data and other federal sources, to create a national voter citizenship verification system. Federal agencies knew this repurposing violated the Privacy Act, the Social Security Act, and the Administrative Procedure Act -- internal records reviewed by the court showed agencies were "scrambling" to comply with the executive order despite that knowledge.

The resulting system used unreliable citizenship data. When states ran their voter rolls through it, some voters were incorrectly identified as non-citizens and had their registrations canceled. Sooknanan's ruling used pointed language: "The federal government has knowingly trampled on the privacy rights of American citizens in a manner that threatens the sacred right to vote."

The case was brought by the League of Women Voters and the Electronic Privacy Information Center (EPIC). The ruling vacates the modified SAVE system and the associated data-sharing notices.

The ruling reinforces a principle that federal privacy statutes explicitly prevent the executive branch from repurposing Americans' personal data across agencies in ways not authorized by Congress. That constraint applies even when the stated purpose is citizenship verification or election integrity.
