# Maryland 2026 Sine Die -- Legislative Session Overview

## Summary

Maryland's 448th General Assembly session concluded at midnight on April 13, 2026. The session was defined by a heavy focus on resisting Trump administration federal policy, a tense budget environment, and a last-minute sprint on Sine Die that included heated floor debate. Several major bills crossed the finish line; others died.

## Key Points

**Voting Rights Act of 2026** passed with minutes to spare on Sine Die, after heated arguments and outbursts from both sides of the aisle. House Speaker Joseline Peña-Melnyk (D-Anne Arundel/Prince George's) presided through the final session -- her first as Speaker.

**Utility RELIEF Act** passed and was sent to Gov. Moore. Described as a consumer utility cost relief measure; details not fully in source but cited as one of the session's finish-line accomplishments.

**Budget:** The General Assembly wrestled the state's budget deficit to what sources describe as a temporary resolution, with patches still being applied heading into the final stretch. The structural deficit remains unresolved long-term.

**Immigration legislation** dominated Sine Die floor time (see [[Maryland 2026 Sine Die -- Immigration Bills Passed]] for detail on the Community Trust Act and police identification bill).

**Congressional redistricting** was unresolved. Senate President Ferguson believes the filing deadline passing renders a new map effectively impossible for 2026 elections, though Gov. Moore and Speaker Peña-Melnyk have not conceded. An alternate redistricting plan B from the Moore administration was reported but unconfirmed.

**Ranked-choice voting** -- no passage noted this session; RCV elimination at the federal level continues to be pushed by Heritage / Project 2026 framing (see [[Project 2026 -- Heritage Foundation's Post-2025 Authoritarian Agenda]]).

Session ran January 14 through April 13, 2026 (90 days). Most new laws take effect October 1, 2026.

## Relevance

The state legislative session outcome shapes what Frederick can and cannot do locally on housing, immigration, and utility costs for the remainder of 2026. The Voting Rights Act passage is relevant to César's broader governance work. The budget deficit outcome has downstream implications for municipal aid and state grants Frederick relies on.

The failure to pass redistricting maps before the filing deadline is a live political situation -- worth monitoring as the Moore administration's reported Plan B may surface in coming weeks.
