# Louisiana v Callais -- SCOTUS Guts Voting Rights Act Section 2

## Summary

The Supreme Court's 6-3 decision in Louisiana v. Callais on April 30, 2026 effectively rendered Section 2 of the Voting Rights Act unenforceable. Written by Justice Alito, the majority held that race cannot be considered in drawing district maps even to correct demonstrated racial vote dilution -- categorizing any race-conscious remedy as itself unconstitutional. Justice Kagan's dissent, joined by Sotomayor and Jackson, called it "a dead letter."

## Key Points

**What the ruling does:** Plaintiffs must now prove that lawmakers intentionally discriminated on the basis of race, not merely that maps have racially discriminatory effects. Because racial and partisan motivations are nearly impossible to disentangle -- and partisan gerrymandering is unreviewable in federal courts -- this effectively closes off VRA Section 2 challenges entirely.

**Immediate consequences:** Louisiana's governor declared a "state of emergency" and suspended active primary elections (100,000+ absentee ballots already sent, early voting days away) to allow new maps to be drawn. Democrats filed suit arguing the Constitution assigns election timing to state legislatures, not governors. Florida passed a new partisan gerrymander the same day. Tennessee, Mississippi, and Alabama are considering redistricting.

**Scale of damage:** Fair Fight analysis projects Republicans could create 19 additional safe US House seats across 10 Southern states, potentially locking out minority political power at state and local levels for a generation.

**The dissent's core argument (Kagan):** The VRA was created precisely because facially race-neutral mechanisms -- including district line drawing -- were used to systematically nullify Black voting power after the 15th Amendment. The majority's rule forecloses the specific remedy the law was designed to provide.

**Democratic response:** Blue states are considering counter-gerrymanders. Virginia's redistricting ballot measure passed April 21 with a narrow margin (pending judicial stay). Hakeem Jeffries vowed to "fight back."

## Relevance

Directly relevant to Frederick and Maryland electoral politics. A ruling that enables minority vote dilution at the state and congressional level has downstream effects on representation in Annapolis and DC. Local majority-minority communities -- Frederick's Latino population, the Black electorate -- have a stake in what happens when these map-drawing norms shift.
