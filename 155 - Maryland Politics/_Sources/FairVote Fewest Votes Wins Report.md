# FairVote Fewest Votes Wins Report: 2026 Primary Plurality Winners

## Summary
FairVote counted a record 125 congressional and statewide primary winners in 2026 who won with under 50% of the vote, and argues ranked choice voting and proportional representation are the fix.

## Key Points
The 125 plurality winners are the highest count since FairVote began tracking in 2018. Twenty-one likely new U.S. House members were nominated without majority support and will win uncompetitive seats in November, so they will effectively be chosen by a fraction of a fraction of voters. FairVote estimates they will make up 20% to 40% of the incoming House freshman class.

The report ties the increase to the record number of congressional retirements and to mid-decade gerrymandering, both of which produced crowded open primaries in safe seats.

The same email flagged several related items. A University of Maryland survey found 66% of Americans in battleground states and districts support RCV for congressional elections, including 70% of Democrats and 64% of Republicans. FairVote also published an analysis of the updated California Voting Rights Act, which strengthens voter protections and opens the door to more municipal election innovation, plus an explainer on Alaska's RCV results timeline.

## Relevance
This is national data that supports the Maryland primary argument already captured in [[Maryland's 2026 Primary Results as Evidence for Ranked Choice Voting]]. The University of Maryland polling is a useful bipartisan number for any local or state RCV conversation, and the California Voting Rights Act analysis is a model for how state law can enable municipal reform. Fits the democratic resilience work tracked in [[Voting Rights and Elections]].
