# Maryland Green Party 2026 General Election Nominees

## Summary

The Maryland Green Party announced eight nominees for 2026 general election races, led by gubernatorial candidate Andy Ellis, with ballot retention dependent on reaching 1% of the vote; the party's platform centers on data center bans, divestment from Israel, and democratic expansion.

## Key Points

The party held its own online primary after being excluded from the state-run primary. All eight nominees ran unopposed within the party.

**Statewide:**
- Governor: Andy Ellis
- Lieutenant Governor: Owen Andrews

**Congressional:**
- 4th District: Sam Husseini (vs. Glenn Ivey, D, and George McDermott, R)
- 6th District: Moshe Landman (vs. April McClain Delaney, D, and Robin Ficker, R)
- 8th District: Nancy Wallace (vs. Jamie Raskin, D, and Cheryl Riley, R)

**Local:**
- Howard County Executive: Nater Kane (vs. Vanessa Atterbeary, D; no Republican)
- Baltimore 45th District delegate: Renaud Brown
- Charles County 4th District commissioner: Artiium Arbyummi

The party must reach 1% of the gubernatorial vote to retain ballot status without petition signatures in the next cycle. In the last gubernatorial election, the Greens fell short at 0.73%.

Key platform planks: banning data centers, divesting from Israel, expanding democracy. Ellis framed the goal as building a 25-year foundation for the party.

With only ~6,100 registered Greens in a state of 4.3 million registered voters, the 1% threshold is a high bar.

## Relevance

The data center ban position is the most directly relevant item. If the Green Party manages meaningful ballot presence in 2026, their platform reinforces the salience of data center opposition as an issue with cross-partisan appeal beyond the traditional left. Ellis's framing of data centers as a flagship issue is worth noting as the PC25-626ZTA opposition brief is being developed for the October Planning Commission hearing.

The Howard County Executive race (Nater Kane vs. Atterbeary) is worth monitoring -- Howard County is a peer jurisdiction on several policy fronts and the race has no Republican, making the Green presence more consequential.
