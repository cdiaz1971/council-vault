# Maryland PERB Rules State Violated Bargaining Agreement on Telework Records

## Summary

The Maryland Public Employee Relations Board ruled July 1 that the Department of Budget and Management failed to bargain in good faith with AFSCME Council 3 when it refused to produce records about telework eligibility. The board ordered the state to provide the information within 14 days.

## Key Points

AFSCME Council 3 represents roughly 50,000 state workers. The union requested telework records as part of contract negotiations to understand how telework policies were applied across agencies. DBM denied the request, arguing it did not maintain individual telework records and was not required to create new ones.

The PERB rejected that argument, finding DBM did not carry its burden to show the information did not exist, could not be obtained, or would be unduly burdensome. The ruling is one of three unfair labor practice complaints filed by AFSCME in December 2025. Two others are pending: one alleging a violation of overnight pay rate agreements, and one alleging the Maryland Department of Health used video surveillance to track employees beyond the "security purposes" allowed under the collective bargaining agreement.

The ruling adds to an already strained relationship between Gov. Moore and AFSCME. The union endorsed another candidate over Moore in 2022, did not endorse in the 2026 primary, and publicly criticized the governor for wage increases below inflation. Moore and AFSCME failed to reach a contract agreement in January 2026.

## Relevance

This is primarily background context on Maryland state labor relations rather than a Frederick council action item. Its relevance is twofold. First, César is co-championing collective bargaining with binding arbitration legislation alongside Nash. The PERB ruling illustrates what good-faith bargaining obligations look like when codified and what happens when a public employer ignores them. It's a useful reference point for framing the binding arbitration bill. Second, the video surveillance complaint at MDH is directly adjacent to the surveillance governance thread: public sector employers using surveillance tools against their own employees outside the agreed purposes, with no pre-authorization framework. The parallel to Flock ALPR mission creep is worth noting.
