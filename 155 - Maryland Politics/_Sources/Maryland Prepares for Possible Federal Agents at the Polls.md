# Maryland Prepares for Possible Federal Agents at the Polls

## Summary

With the Trump administration refusing to rule out ICE or other federal agents at polling places, Maryland officials have war-gamed responses, set up reporting hotlines, and coordinated with local police and the National Guard. Federal law bars armed agents from polls, but it remains unclear how the state would actually enforce that line.

## Key Points

**The federal posture.** In May 2026 Trump said he would do anything necessary to ensure honest elections when asked about sending the Guard or ICE to polls. FBI Director Kash Patel and DHS Secretary Markwayne Mullin later left the door open. An unnamed DHS spokesperson said ICE has no planned polling-place operations but has not ruled them out. Joint Chiefs chair Gen. Dan Caine told lawmakers in August he had no plans to send troops. The pretext is noncitizen voting, though a Reuters investigation found only 129 noncitizens charged with voting over three decades.

**The legal line.** Federal law bars armed federal agents or troops from polling places except to repel armed enemies of the United States. DOJ election monitors are separate and lawful. They observe from a fixed spot and cannot talk to voters. DOJ is sending monitors to Prince George's County, where they have previously checked Spanish-language compliance.

**State preparation.** Gov. Moore says the state has been war-gaming scenarios and will enforce the law, without saying how. Elections Administrator Jared DeMarinis, who is being sued by DOJ for refusing to hand over the state voter database, has coordinated with the Department of Emergency Management, the Attorney General, the State Prosecutor, and the Maryland National Guard. He says federal agents and Guard members have no legitimate business inside a polling place and that the aim of the threats is to disenfranchise and sow doubt. Poll workers are trained to report disturbances to county election directors or the state board. AG Anthony Brown's office says it is working with election officials on voter safety.

**Local preparation.** Election offices are establishing lines with local police, with 911 as the default for immediate threats inside the perimeter. Montgomery County Police, Baltimore Police, and Anne Arundel County Executive Steuart Pittman all describe plans to support elections without a visible presence that could deter voters. Alysoun McLaughlin of UMD's Election Resilience Lab is a source on local protocols.

**Civil society.** Common Cause Maryland added de-escalation and scenario training for roughly 250 volunteer poll monitors. Voters can report federal agents at polls or other interference to 866-OUR-VOTE (866-687-8683) by call or text. Common Cause and the state board also want reports of suspected AI-generated election misinformation.

As of the start of early voting, there were no reports of federal law enforcement at Maryland polling places.

## Relevance

The concern is explicitly about intimidation of Black and Latino voters, which puts Frederick's Latino and immigrant residents at the center. Together with [[Immigration Enforcement Erodes Maryland Latino Republican Support]], it describes enforcement pressure working as a turnout suppressant whether or not agents ever show up.

For Frederick specifically, the useful questions are what the Frederick County Board of Elections and Frederick Police have agreed on, and whether the City has any role in communicating voter rights in Spanish. The Common Cause hotline is the number to share through RISE, Centro Hispano, and council channels before Election Day.
