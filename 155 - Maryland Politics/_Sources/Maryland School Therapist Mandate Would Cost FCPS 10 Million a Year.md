# Maryland School Therapist Mandate Would Cost FCPS 10 Million a Year

## Summary
A new Maryland law requiring a full-time therapist in every public school would cost Frederick County Public Schools about 10 million dollars a year, and most school board candidates surveyed say schools need more resources while some push parental responsibility instead.

## Key Points
**The law.** A bill effective July 2026 requires every Maryland public school to have a full-time therapist, a big shift for schools that had part-time coverage. Estimated added annual cost starting FY2027: Frederick County about 10 million dollars for a full-time therapist in every school; Anne Arundel 7 million and 55 more therapists; St. Mary's 2.9 million and 25 more therapists. The Maryland Community Health Resources Commission announced 96.3 million dollars in June to expand school behavioral health services statewide.

**The need.** Maryland averaged one counselor per 309 students last year against an ASCA recommendation of one per 250. The share of Maryland high schoolers reporting persistent sadness or hopelessness peaked during COVID and has stayed above 30 percent since.

**Candidate survey.** Of 66 school board candidates who answered the Local News Network questionnaire, 45 said schools need more mental health resources and 8 said resources were sufficient.
- Rae Gallagher (Frederick County board member, running for reelection): personnel and resources are in place but more funding is needed, and new or reallocated money is unlikely given constrained state and local budgets.
- Tyler Walker (former teacher, Frederick County candidate): schools should support families, but parents should come first in mental health decisions; cites FCPS counselors and community partnerships.
- Other districts: Baltimore County's Lisa Mack says cutting psychologist contracts from 12 to 10 months drove staff away; Cecil and Carroll candidates argue schools should stay focused on academics with parents and outside providers responsible; Wicomico's Gene Malone doubts resources can ever be enough.

## Relevance
The unfunded or underfunded cost lands on the county and FCPS, not the City, but it competes for the same constrained local dollars and shapes the County budget conversation the City depends on. The Gallagher versus Walker split is the Frederick County version of the resources-versus-parental-rights framing showing up in school board races statewide.
