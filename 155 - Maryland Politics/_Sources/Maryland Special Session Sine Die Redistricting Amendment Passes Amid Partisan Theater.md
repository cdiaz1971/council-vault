# Maryland Special Session Sine Die Redistricting Amendment Passes Amid Partisan Theater

## Summary

Color piece on the close of Maryland's August 2026 special session: extended bipartisan floor tributes to outgoing Senate Majority Leader Nancy King (D-Montgomery), who lost her primary to first-time candidate Amar Mukunda; clean management of the 50-person testimony process Republicans had predicted would be manipulated; and a small balloon-and-confetti sendoff in the House marking the end of a two-day session.

## Key Points

Senate Majority Leader Nancy King is leaving after more than two decades -- elected to the House in 2002, Senate since 2007. She lost her primary to Amar Mukunda. The floor tributes ran more than 40 minutes and included Republicans, with Sen. William Folden (R-Frederick) among those speaking warmly of her. Folden said he had attended only two Democratic fundraisers in his career -- one for King.

The 50-person testimony limit for the redistricting hearing drew Republican criticism as a potential manipulation tool. In practice, exactly 50 people signed up during the 10-hour registration window -- no one was turned away, and no committee discretion was required.

The session closed with House delegates tossing a handful of balloons across the floor -- a stripped-down version of the balloon-and-confetti tradition that ends the regular 90-day session. Speaker Peña-Melnyk closed with brief remarks thanking members for making time during the summer.

## Relevance

Primarily texture and context for the redistricting thread. The Folden tribute to King is a minor but useful data point on cross-aisle relationships in the Frederick-area delegation. Folden represents Frederick County in the Senate and has been a reliable conservative vote; his warmth toward King suggests the kind of personal relationship that occasionally matters on close procedural questions.
