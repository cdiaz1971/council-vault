# Maryland Faces Opt-In Decision on Federal Scholarship Tax Credit

## Summary
A Maryland Matters op-ed urges Gov. Moore to opt into the new Federal Scholarship Tax Credit, which lets taxpayers redirect up to $1,700 in federal taxes to K-12 nonprofits starting January 2027. Teachers unions oppose it and Moore has sent mixed signals, making it a live test of his labor alignment.

## Key Points
The Federal Scholarship Tax Credit takes effect January 2027. Taxpayers can claim a dollar-for-dollar credit of up to $1,700 for donations to scholarship-granting nonprofits. States must opt in for those nonprofits to operate within their borders. About 30 states have already done so. If Maryland stays out, residents can still claim the credit but only by giving to organizations in participating states.

The op-ed makes the pro-opt-in case. It argues the money can fund tutoring, technology, transportation, and special education services for public school students, that it is additive rather than drawn from state education funding, and that opting out sends roughly $1.8B out of Maryland. It pairs that with slow MCAP progress (barely half of students reading at grade level statewide, under a point of gain per year, wider gaps for Black, Latino, and special needs students) and cites a poll showing 68-21 voter support, including 78% among Black voters.

Moore has sent mixed signals. In June he said he would not leave money on the table for kids. At the AFT convention in August he tied the program to Republicans and called it a tax scheme. He has consistently said he opposes private school vouchers.

This is an advocacy piece. It does not address the main counterarguments: that scholarship funds can flow to private school tuition, that the credit is a federal revenue loss wherever it is spent, that the poll sponsor is unnamed, and that the teachers union objection is about the program's school-choice design rather than tutoring as such.

## Relevance
Mostly a state-level watch item, since Frederick City has no role in the decision. The political dimension is what matters for César. It is another pressure point between Moore and organized labor, alongside the AFSCME disputes tracked in [[AFSCME's Widening Conflict with the Moore Administration on Pay, Staffing, and Safety]], and the Democratic coalition split it exposes (union opposition against strong Black and Latino voter support) may surface in local races and endorsements. Worth watching for the governor's decision and how FCPS and the Frederick County Teachers Association respond.
