# Baltimore City Community College Faculty Ratify First Union Contract

## Summary

Baltimore City Community College faculty, represented by United Academics of Maryland Local 6628 (AFT-Maryland), unanimously ratified their first-ever contract after 19 months of bargaining, winning raises and successorship protections, following a state audit that found widespread mismanagement including aid to fictitious students.

## Key Points

The contract includes a 3.25 percent raise plus a $1,000 bonus in year one, at least 8.25 percent in cumulative wage increases over the three-year term, clearer promotion criteria, and a successorship agreement guaranteeing faculty keep their union through any merger or reduction in force.

BCCC becomes the seventh Maryland institution represented by United Academics of Maryland to ratify a contract, made possible by Senate Bill 746 and House Bill 894, which granted community college faculty collective bargaining rights in 2021.

The ratification lands shortly after a June 3, 2026 state audit found BCCC had awarded $264,000 in financial aid to 145 potentially fictitious or "ghost students," failed to follow some state procurement laws, and did not adequately verify certain payments. Union leaders framed the contract as validation of concerns faculty had raised for years, following a September 2025 rally over late pay and missing classroom supplies.

## Relevance

A concrete, recent example of Maryland public-sector collective bargaining winning real terms, relevant background for Nash's Collective Bargaining with Binding Arbitration legislation (dormant, revisit fall) and for tracking how the 2021 community college bargaining law is playing out in practice across the state.
