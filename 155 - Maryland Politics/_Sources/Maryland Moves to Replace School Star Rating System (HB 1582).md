# Maryland Moves to Replace School Star Rating System (HB 1582)

## Summary

Maryland HB 1582 authorizes the state to replace its one-to-four-star school rating system with a new accountability framework that better reflects school quality and doesn't shortchange high-poverty schools -- a proposal expected to result in a new system by the 2027-28 school year.

## Key Points

**What HB 1582 does:** Gives the Maryland State Department of Education the flexibility to replace the current one-to-four-star school rating system. The bill adds new school quality indicators including staffing adequacy and completion of a well-rounded curriculum. It requires a public update by July 1, 2027 on final metrics, including analysis of how they correlate with student poverty trends.

**Timeline:** Bill takes effect July 1, 2026 (pending governor signature, expected in one of two remaining signing sessions in May). The Accountability Advisory Committee will reconvene to develop a new framework, present it for public comment later in 2026, and submit a proposal to the U.S. Department of Education early in 2027. New system targeted for the 2027-28 school year based on 2026-27 data.

**Why the change:** State officials said the current star system does not adequately reflect school quality and actively penalizes schools serving large numbers of low-income students. The new framework is intended to align with the Blueprint for Maryland's Future education reform plan.

**Sponsor:** House Ways and Means Chair Jheanelle Wilkins (D-Montgomery).

**Other education legislation from the 2026 session:**
- SB 311 (sponsored by Senate President Bill Ferguson) makes minor revisions to the Blueprint -- extends hold-harmless funding protections for multilingual learners, students in poverty, and special education students through FY2028; extends principal National Board Certification deadline to 2034; extends dual enrollment authority through FY2030.
- SB 359 / HB 635 require criminal background checks for childcare workers in direct contact with children, centralize background check processing by June 30, 2027, and allow appointment of a deputy state superintendent for early childhood education.

## Why This Matters for César

Frederick City schools fall under Frederick County Public Schools, which is accountable to the state rating system. The shift away from star ratings matters locally because the current system has been criticized for disadvantaging schools with high concentrations of poverty -- a pattern present in Frederick's lower-income areas. A more nuanced accountability framework could change how resources and interventions are directed. Worth watching what indicators the Accountability Advisory Committee proposes, particularly around school staffing and curriculum breadth.
