# RNC v Mi Familia Vota and Documentary Proof of Citizenship

## Summary

The U.S. Supreme Court will hear Republican National Committee v. Mi Familia Vota on December 8, 2026. The case centers on two Arizona laws. One requires documentary proof of citizenship (DPOC) to register using state forms. The other lets officials remove eligible voters from the rolls when they lack that documentation. The legal framing is preemption, meaning whether the National Voter Registration Act limits what states can demand for federal elections.

## Key Points

- The NVRA has eased voter registration for more than 30 years.
- Demos estimates 21.3 million Americans lack documentary proof of citizenship or ready access to it, with the burden falling hardest on low-income voters and voters of color.
- If Arizona prevails, other states could adopt the same requirements, weakening Congress's authority over access to federal elections.
- Demos filed an amicus brief supporting the respondents on October 20, 2026.

## Why This Matters

This is the registration side of the same pattern as the SAVE database purges and the Callais ruling on Section 2. Citizenship verification is becoming the tool for shrinking the rolls, and mixed-status and naturalized Latino households in Frederick are the people most likely to be caught by document demands. Maryland is unlikely to adopt DPOC, but a ruling for Arizona would shape federal registration rules and embolden neighboring states. Worth watching for any effect on the Maryland Voting Rights Act and on city outreach around registration.

Decision expected by June 2027.
