# Maryland Politics - In-Progress Plans

This page collects options and questions already recorded in the Maryland Politics core pages. It does not establish a new sponsored initiative, introduction, approval, funding, staff assignment, or implementation date. Check the live Notion Legislative Roadmap for current legislative status. State and federal developments supply context; City authority must be assessed separately.

## City election methods and representation

[[Voting Rights and Elections]] records options for counsel's review of the City election structure under State voting-rights law and ranked choice voting through a City Charter amendment.

Before advancing either option, confirm the governing Charter and State-law requirements, the actual district and at-large structure, demographic and election evidence, administration costs, voter education, and the amendment process. [[Callais VRA Ruling and Frederick Minority Voting Power]] retains an earlier political synthesis; its gap list does not replace the core page's later State-law analysis. [[Maryland's 2026 Primary Results as Evidence for Ranked Choice Voting]] and the FairVote notes are dated supporting material, not proof of City-specific outcomes.

## Voter participation and public positions

[[Voting Rights and Elections]] records voter outreach under the City's registration rules and possible resolutions on State measures. [[Civic Participation and Co-Governance]] supplies the City participation and eligibility reference.

Confirm the decision sought, actual City authority, current measure text and stage, and the audience before preparing a public position or outreach proposal. Distinguish a symbolic resolution from a change in election law. Municipal ID or civic-belonging proposals do not themselves establish eligibility to vote. The dated election-administration notes can inform communication questions without establishing a City incident or newly assigned response project.

## City employee bargaining framework

[[Public Sector Labor in Maryland]] records design questions for a City bargaining ordinance: employee units, subjects of bargaining, impasse resolution, budget interaction, City representation at negotiations, and dispute review.

These are design questions, not confirmation that a general-employee ordinance is being drafted or introduced. Establish the present legal framework, affected employees, fiscal implications, personnel responsibilities, and review process before choosing an option. [[AFSCME's Widening Conflict with the Moore Administration on Pay, Staffing, and Safety]] and [[AFSCME Reaches Deal With Moore and USM to Restore Withheld Pay Raises]] provide dated State context; State arrangements do not automatically govern City employees.

## State advocacy and City dependencies

[[Maryland Legislature and State Landscape]] identifies City dependencies involving local revenue authority, data center policy, cybersecurity support, and immigration data sharing.

Use the relevant subject references for scope and authority: [[Revenue, Taxes, and Debt]], [[Data Center Regulation and Local Authority]], [[Municipal Cybersecurity]], and [[ICE Surveillance and Data]]. Confirm current State text, session stage, City position, fiscal effects, and the precise request before treating any dependency as a new advocacy commitment. Topic-specific legislative work and project records remain in their subject folders.
