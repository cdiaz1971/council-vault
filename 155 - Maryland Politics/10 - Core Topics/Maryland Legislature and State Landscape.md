# Maryland Legislature and State Landscape

## Purpose

This page supports the City's work with Annapolis: who holds power, what the 2026 session and elections changed, and what State action the City depends on. Topic-specific State law is on each topic page (for example [[Maryland Housing Law and Policy]] and [[Data Center Regulation and Local Authority]]).

## The 2026 session

- The regular session ended April 14, 2026. Members earn $55,526; the Senate President and House Speaker $72,119 ([[Maryland 2026 Legislative Session Ends April 14]]).
- Major outcomes affecting the City include the Maryland Voting Rights Act ([[Voting Rights and Elections]]), the Housing Certainty Act ([[Land Management Code]]), the Utility RELIEF Act, a 287(g) ban, and the Data Privacy Act. Progressive Maryland's debrief lists wins and losses on housing and immigration ([[Progressive Maryland -- 2026 Legislative Session Debrief]]).
- The General Assembly passed bills creating Muslim American Heritage Month and Jewish American Heritage Month, rejecting a Christian Heritage Month amendment ([[Maryland Muslim and Jewish Heritage Month Bill -- Governor Desk 2026]]).
- A constitutional amendment to tie special elections for vacant Delegate and Senate seats to the regular statewide primary and general election passed each chamber in its own version but ran out of time for the Senate to ratify the House bill before adjournment, so it did not reach the November ballot ([[Maryland Special Session Needed for Redistricting and Democracy]]).
- A special session August 3 to 5 passed the congressional redistricting amendment now on the ballot ([[Maryland Special Session on Congressional Redistricting August 2026]]; [[Maryland Special Session Sine Die Redistricting Amendment Passes Amid Partisan Theater]]).

## Leadership and turnover

- Senate President Bill Ferguson held his seat against progressive challenger Bobby LaPin with heavy outside spending ([[Ferguson-LaPin Primary Progressive Challenge and the Political Cost of Redistricting Obstruction]]). House Speaker is Joseline Peña-Melnyk.
- Five incumbents lost primaries, including Senate Majority Leader Nancy King ([[Maryland June 2026 Primary Final Results and Legislative Implications]]). At least 25 legislators will not return, including Del. Barrie Ciliberti (R-Frederick) ([[Maryland 2026 Legislative Turnover -- Key Departures and Races]]; [[Maryland 2026 Legislative Primary Landscape -- Senate and Delegate Races]]).
- Governor Moore named Will Tilburg chief legislative officer amid friction with Senate leadership ([[Moore Names Will Tilburg Chief Legislative Officer]]).
- Republican delegates posted a video accusing Del. Chao Wu, a Chinese-born delegate, of spying, drawing bipartisan condemnation ([[Maryland Delegates Racist Video Targeting Del. Chao Wu]]).
- The Maryland Legislative Latino Caucus, co-founded in 2014, has grown to 100 members (8 Latino, 92 associate) under House Speaker Joseline Peña-Melnyk, the first Afro-Latina and first immigrant speaker. The state party is tracking 21 Latino candidates for state and local office in the November 2026 election, including county council nominees in Frederick, Prince George's, Montgomery, and Baltimore counties; the specific Frederick County nominee is not yet identified in public reporting ([[Latino Candidates Now Run Up and Down Maryland Ballots in 2026]]).

## November 3, 2026 general election

- Governor: Wes Moore (D, incumbent) faces Dan Cox (R, nominated with 45%), with the Green Party's Andy Ellis also on the ballot ([[Maryland's 2026 Primary Results as Evidence for Ranked Choice Voting]]; [[Maryland Green Party 2026 General Election Nominees]]).
- Sixth Congressional District: Rep. April McClain Delaney (D) defeated David Trone in the primary and faces a competitive general election in a district registered 39% Democratic and 33% Republican ([[April McClain Delaney Wins MD-6 -- Frederick-Area Federal Delegation Picture]]).
- Ballot questions: Question 1, binding arbitration for State employees; Question 2, temporary appointments to the Commission on Judicial Disabilities; Question 3, congressional redistricting ([State Board of Elections](https://www.elections.maryland.gov/elections/2026/2026_Ballot_Question_SBE_Letter.pdf)).
- Frederick's State legislative seats and candidates: verify from the State Board of Elections candidate list.

## Fiscal pressure

Maryland faces a projected $3 billion structural gap, Blueprint education costs that exceed dedicated revenue from FY2028, and pressure to shift costs to local governments ([[City Budget and Fiscal Condition]]). The Governor plans to seek repeal of the data center sales tax exemption in 2027.

The Strategic Energy Investment Fund has become a budget-balancing source. Alternative compliance payments, which electricity suppliers make when they cannot buy enough renewable energy credits and pass through to ratepayers, rose from $77 million in FY2022 to $365 million in FY2025, more than half of SEIF revenue. For FY2027 the Governor proposed moving about $292 million from SEIF to close the budget gap and $100 million to a one-time residential bill credit ([Maryland Matters](https://marylandmatters.org/2026/02/01/climate-groups-caught-by-surprise-last-year-fight-to-protect-maryland-energy-fund-in-2026/)); the amount enacted: verify. Republican critics now frame rising electricity prices as a state leadership failure and cite more than $500 million diverted from SEIF over two years ([[Maryland SEIF Diversion and Renewable Compliance Payments Drive Electricity Costs]], opinion). Expect that message in the 2026 and 2027 cycles. The budget-integrity point, that money collected from ratepayers for relief and clean generation should go there, is one the City can make on its own terms because energy costs fall hardest on ALICE households.
