# Public Sector Labor in Maryland

## Purpose

This page supports Council decisions on collective bargaining for City employees, with the State labor landscape as context.

## City authority and current law

- The Charter authorizes Council to "provide for collective bargaining for City employees, in appropriate units, of wages, hours and other terms and conditions of employment" by ordinance (Charter Art. VIII, § 7).
- Council has done so only for police. City Code Chapter 17, Article II (§§ 17-21 to 17-29) recognizes two units, sworn officers at sergeant and below and at lieutenant and above. A recognized organization bargains over listed subjects including wages, benefits, insurance, layoff seniority, agreement term, and drug testing. Agreements go to Council for ratification or rejection, and State, federal, or City law prevails over any conflicting term (§ 17-26).
- A three-member labor relations panel, chaired by an attorney or arbitrator chosen jointly, decides prohibited-practice complaints and grievance appeals, with appeal to Circuit Court (§§ 17-27, 17-28). Strikes are prohibited (§ 17-29).
- Other City employees have no bargaining rights under City law.

## State landscape

- **AFSCME and the Moore administration.** AFSCME Council 3, the largest State employee union, sued the Governor and the University System of Maryland in July 2026 over withheld raises and layoffs, then settled in September: USM will pay about 5,700 non-faculty employees their withheld raises from $13.8 million in reserves, and the Governor will build that amount into the FY2028 budget ([[AFSCME Sues Moore and USM Over Withheld Raises and Layoffs]]; [[AFSCME Reaches Deal With Moore and USM to Restore Withheld Pay Raises]]). A union survey of over 2,500 State workers found nearly 80% had considered leaving and nearly 60% work more than one job ([[AFSCME Survey Finds Widespread Pay and Staffing Dissatisfaction Among Maryland State Workers]]). After two highway workers were killed within 72 hours, the union pressed for work zone safety measures ([[AFSCME Demands Highway Worker Safety Reforms After Two Maryland Deaths]]; [[AFSCME's Widening Conflict with the Moore Administration on Pay, Staffing, and Safety]]).
- **Layoffs.** The University System cut jobs at College Park, Bowie State, and UMBC, citing a 10% State funding cut over two cycles and federal research cuts ([[Federal and State Budget Cuts Force University Layoffs Despite Collective Bargaining]]).
- **First contracts.** Baltimore City Community College faculty ratified their first contract after 19 months of bargaining ([[Baltimore City Community College Faculty Ratify First Union Contract]]).
- **Binding arbitration on the ballot.** Question 1 on November 3, 2026 would let either side in State employee wage talks request binding arbitration if no agreement is reached by October 25, and require the Governor to include arbitrated wage costs in the proposed budget, though the legislature keeps final funding authority ([WYPR](https://www.wypr.org/wypr-news/2026-05-01/maryland-voters-will-weigh-in-on-state-employee-wage-negotiation-process-in-november)). It does not apply to City employees.

## Design questions for a City bargaining ordinance

- Which employees and units: general employees, supervisors, specific departments?
- Which subjects are mandatory, permitted, or excluded?
- How impasses are resolved: mediation, fact-finding, or binding arbitration, and whether arbitration awards bind the budget given the Charter's budget rules ([[City Budget and Fiscal Condition]]).
- Who represents the City at the table, given that the Mayor administers personnel and Council ratifies.
- Who hears disputes: extend the police labor relations panel or create a citywide board.
