## Major City Issues (with Priority Ratings)

- **Community Living Options for Deaf Senior Citizens (Priority: 9/10)**  
  Participants strongly emphasized the need for Deaf seniors to remain connected to the Deaf community rather than being isolated in general nursing homes or assisted living facilities. Insurance barriers and lack of accessible providers were identified as major obstacles. There is interest in developing clustered or designated living arrangements within existing senior housing so that Deaf residents can age in place with language access, cultural continuity, and peer support. This aligns closely with past Council priorities on aging-in-place, housing equity, and accessibility.

- **Access to Senior Resources and Programs for Deaf Residents (Priority: 8/10)**  
  Deaf seniors reported limited access to retirement housing, senior programs, and recreational or social services. A previously proposed task force to coordinate these efforts stalled due to lack of follow-up. The issue reflects systemic gaps in outreach, communication access, and coordination between service providers and the Deaf community. Renewed municipal leadership could re-establish momentum and ensure Deaf seniors are included in existing City senior services.

- **Mental Health Services for the Deaf Community (Priority: 9/10)**  
  Significant gaps exist in mental health care due to the lack of ASL-fluent providers. Most services rely on interpreters rather than direct communication, creating barriers to therapy, diagnosis, and medication management. Participants identified an urgent need for a dedicated mental health provider who is fluent in ASL and knowledgeable about Deaf culture, ideally working in partnership with the Maryland Deaf Community Center (MDCC). This connects directly to Council initiatives on public health, behavioral health access, and underserved populations.

- **Movie Theater and Public Venue Accessibility (Priority: 6/10)**  
  Concerns were raised about inconsistent access to captioned movies. Deaf patrons often must wait for special open-caption showings, and closed-caption devices are limited in number. Theaters cite concern about hearing audiences' experiences, which restricts broader adoption. While not as urgent as housing or health services, this issue reflects broader ADA compliance and inclusion challenges in private venues operating within the city.

- **Recognition of Deaf Residents as a Distinct Community in City Services (Priority: 7/10)**  
  Participants stressed that Deaf individuals should not be grouped generically within disability services designed primarily for hearing populations. Policies and programs often fail to account for language access and cultural needs unique to the Deaf community. This issue has implications for how the City designs outreach, allocates resources, and structures accessibility policies.

- **Traffic Signal and Emergency Vehicle Accessibility (Priority: 5/10)**  
  A question was raised about reinstating or expanding blinking traffic signals to allow emergency vehicles to pass more efficiently. While not specific to the Deaf community, it reflects public safety and infrastructure concerns raised during the forum. This intersects with transportation planning and emergency response efficiency.

- **Concerns About Utility Impacts from Proposed Data Center Project (Priority: 5/10)**  
  Participants requested clarity on whether the proposed data center project would affect local utilities and whether residents should expect increases in water and electricity costs. While not unique to the Deaf community, the question reflects broader community anxiety about development impacts and transparency.

- **City vs. County Tax Burden (Priority: 4/10)**  
  One question focused on why city taxes are higher when county taxes already exist. This represents a recurring concern among residents about fiscal structure and equity but was not a dominant theme of the meeting compared to social service and accessibility issues.

## Recommended City Council Action Items (in Sequence)

1. **Request a staff briefing on Deaf senior housing and service access.**  
   Direct staff to assess current City programs serving seniors and identify gaps in accessibility for Deaf residents, including housing, recreation, and social services.

2. **Convene or revive a Deaf Senior Services Task Force.**  
   Re-establish a coordinated working group including City staff, MDCC, senior service providers, and Deaf community representatives to develop concrete recommendations.

3. **Explore partnerships for ASL-fluent mental health services.**  
   Initiate discussions with Frederick County Health Department, MDCC, and regional providers to evaluate options for contracting or supporting an ASL-fluent mental health professional serving city residents.

4. **Audit accessibility practices in public venues.**  
   Ask staff to review compliance and best practices related to captioning and accessibility in movie theaters and large public venues operating in the city.

5. **Review City policies on disability classification and language access.**  
   Examine whether current policies sufficiently distinguish Deaf residents' needs and whether changes are warranted to better reflect language-based access requirements.

6. **Request analysis of traffic signal options for emergency vehicle priority.**  
   Have transportation or public works staff report on feasibility, cost, and safety impacts of blinking or priority traffic signals.

7. **Seek formal briefing on data center utility impacts.**  
   Request clear information on projected water and energy usage and potential rate implications for residents.

8. **Prepare a public-facing summary for constituents.**  
   Develop a short communication outlining what the City can act on locally versus what requires county or state intervention, to manage expectations and show responsiveness.
